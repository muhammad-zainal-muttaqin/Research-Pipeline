\begin{abstract}
This report is an evidence-audited companion to the repository's
literature-mapping manuscript. It documents 32 numbered research records,
of which 30 were executed as measurements or audits: E-008 is a reserved
number with no run, and E-023 is a protocol revision whose matrix is counted
under E-032. The report covers the bounded SawitMVC RGB pilot, physical Orbbec
depth sensing on SawitMVC-Depth, multi-seed and fusion audits, exact software
and data contracts, and validation gates \G{0}--\G{8}.

The best observed RGB benchmark model is RF-DETR-L in \E{021}: test \mAP50{}
\(0.6038\) and test \mAP50-95 \(0.2770\) under one \texttt{pycocotools}
protocol. This is a benchmark result subject to repeated test-set exposure,
not an independent-holdout estimate. The physical-depth branch audits
per-file calibration and applies the recorded reprojection pipeline but has not demonstrated a
reliable detection benefit. Early fusion was harmful in two of three tested
YOLO26n seeds, while the across-seed contrast remained uncertain. The full
early/middle/late matrix has twelve confidence intervals that include zero;
this is inconclusive evidence within the tested regime, not proof of
equivalence. The end-to-end one-count-per-bunch inventory target remains
unvalidated. Negative results and audit corrections are retained as findings.
\end{abstract}

\section{Report scope and evidence discipline}
\label{sec:scope}
\subsection{Why this file exists}
\pathcode{docs/manuscript/source/main.tex} is the repository's structured
literature-mapping review. It supplies detector, depth, fusion, geometry, and
association context. This file is the exhaustive results report: it follows
the append-only experiment log, preserves every hypothesis that was tried,
and records why a branch was confirmed, falsified, withdrawn, or left
inconclusive. It does not replace the raw logs or JSON artifacts.
The companion plan at
\pathcode{docs/experiments/REPORT_PLAN.md} records the requirement-to-evidence
matrix, visual production plan, configuration ledger, and update protocol.

The literature component is explicitly a structured evidence map, not a formal
systematic review with a pooled effect estimate. Its 182 full-text sources,
search and coding rationale, transfer boundaries, and quality notes are defined
in the companion manuscript's ``Review protocol and verified evidence corpus''
section. This report uses that map for historical positioning and hypothesis
selection; all numerical claims below come from the repository experiment
artifacts, not from the literature count.

\subsection{Operational research question}
The deployment target is an inventory of individual fresh-fruit bunches, not
just an image-level classifier. A useful system must detect a bunch, assign
one of four maturity classes, count it once across multiple views, and remain
usable when the depth sensor is missing. The work therefore asks:
\begin{enumerate}
\item whether an RGB detector can be selected fairly and whether the main
      error is localization or maturity assignment;
\item whether monocular pseudo-depth provides a bunch-level cue;
\item whether physical Orbbec depth improves paired detection after sensor,
      control, seed, and evaluator audits; and
\item whether capacity, fusion point, or cross-side identity explains a
      remaining difference.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Historical motivation and the origin of the RGB-D question}
\label{sec:history-motivation}
The research began with a practical inventory problem. A field operator does
not need only an image-level maturity label: the system must locate each
fresh-fruit bunch, assign one of four maturity classes, count it once across
views, and remain useful when capture conditions or the depth sensor change.
This makes the central object a detected bunch with an auditable identity,
not a single frame score. The early literature therefore served two roles:
it supplied candidate mechanisms, and it exposed which claims would require a
separate control rather than a larger model.

\subsection{From RGB detection to a bounded multimodal hypothesis}
The RGB branch follows the detector lineage from region proposals and
feature pyramids to one-stage real-time detectors and transformer set
prediction. R-CNN and Fast R-CNN established region-based supervision
\cite{girshick2014rcnn,girshick2015fastrcnn}; Faster R-CNN made proposals
learnable \cite{ren2017fasterrcnn}; FPN made multi-scale features explicit
\cite{lin2017fpn}; SSD and YOLO made dense real-time prediction practical
\cite{liu2016ssd,redmon2016yolo,bochkovskiy2020yolov4}. Focal loss and
EfficientDet addressed imbalance and compound scaling
\cite{lin2017focal,tan2020efficientdet}. DETR then reframed detection as set
prediction, with Deformable DETR reducing the cost of global attention
\cite{carion2020detr,zhu2021deformabledetr}. RT-DETR and RF-DETR are the
later non-NMS directions tested in E-020 and E-021
\cite{zhao2024rtdetr,robinson2025rfdetr}.

That history does not imply that a newer detector will solve the field
problem. It gives a controlled comparison vocabulary: one-stage convolutional
capacity, real-time transformer decoding, and NMS-free set prediction. The
RGB pilot uses that vocabulary to identify whether the dominant error is
localization, maturity, or cross-view identity before adding a new sensor.
The final RGB result is consequently an architecture choice under one
protocol, not evidence that every transformer or every pretraining source will
transfer to a different orchard. The lineage is used here only to name which
failure mode each record attacks: the two-stage separation of localization and
classification motivates E-017, imbalance and scale motivate the class-wise
diagnosis of E-010, and NMS-free set prediction is the property tested in E-020
and E-021. The full history, with its transfer boundaries, is in the companion
literature manuscript and is not restated here.

Depth was considered because adjacent bunches can overlap in RGB while being
separated in space. The proposed fourth channel can enter early, middle, or
late fusion, but each position changes the learning problem. Early fusion
asks the first convolutional stem to accept calibrated geometry; middle
fusion asks two feature streams to retain complementary information; late
fusion asks a decision rule to combine already formed detections. Physical
depth therefore requires a sensor contract, reprojection, missing-value policy,
same-split controls, and a seed-aware evaluator. A pseudo-depth map generated
from the same RGB image is not an independent sensor and cannot close that
claim by itself.

\subsection{Monocular depth, physical depth, and modality boundaries}
Monocular depth methods estimate geometry from RGB appearance. Multi-view
models can make relative ordering more stable across an orbit, but a relative
map has an arbitrary scale and can inherit the same appearance ambiguity as
the input image. Depth Anything and Depth Anything 3 are therefore useful
for testing view ordering and scene structure, not for claiming an
independent bunch-level sensor \cite{yang2024depthanything,lin2025depthanything3}.
That boundary is the reason E-003--E-007 are explicitly pseudo-depth
experiments and E-006 closes the bunch-separation shortcut.

Physical depth changes the evidence contract. A metric value is meaningful
only when the camera intrinsics, extrinsics, coordinate convention, invalid
value policy, and range selection are known. A depth image in the depth-camera
grid cannot be resized into a color-aligned tensor without reprojection. The
E-022 audit consequently checks calibration per file, projects depth points
into the color camera, removes invalid samples, and estimates a train-only
range. A valid sensor file is necessary but not sufficient: it must still be
paired with a same-split RGB control, a random-channel control, and an
evaluator that cannot change by arm.

This distinction also explains why a positive seed-42 delta is not carried
forward automatically. E-022 contained a sidecar alignment error and a
cross-split donor issue. Repairing the defect changes the quantity being
measured. E-027, E-029, and E-032 then ask whether the corrected quantity
survives seeds, capacity, and fusion point. A null result after those controls
is a statement about the tested contract, not a proof that depth is useless in
every plantation or camera.

\subsection{Fusion history and agricultural deployment}
RGB-D fusion has several legitimate insertion points. Early fusion exposes a
four-channel stem to the learner and can exploit pixel-level correspondence,
but it also discards compatible RGB initialization when the first layer
changes. Middle fusion keeps modality-specific feature extraction longer and
combines representations after some abstraction. Late fusion allows separate
detectors or heads, but it may postpone the interaction until a decision that
cannot recover a missed object. FuseNet, Sa-Gate, CMX, and multimodal
agricultural systems provide mechanisms for these choices
\cite{hazirbas2016fusenet,chen2020sagate,zhang2023cmx,ophoff2019multimodal}.

The repository turns that design space into a decision sequence. E-022 first
asks whether the physical files are valid. E-023 and E-032 make early,
middle, and late fusion explicit, while the noise arm tests whether an extra
channel changes optimization even without geometry. E-027 and E-029 add
multi-seed replication; E-030 asks whether the direction depends on model
capacity; and E-031 asks whether a split effect is larger than a seed effect.
This is more informative than choosing a fusion point from a single favorable
run because every branch names a control that could falsify it.

Agricultural deployment is expected to add constraints absent from many indoor
RGB-D benchmarks, including sunlight, reflective fruit surfaces, occlusion,
motion, dust, and missing depth. These conditions were not all measured in the
present records, but the requirement to count an object once across views is
part of the operational target. The
RGB fallback is therefore part of the design, not a fallback added after a
failed depth experiment. The literature motivates the mechanisms; the 32
numbered records document which mechanisms survive this inventory setting.

\section{Chronological research storyline and dependency map}
\label{sec:storyline}
The 32 numbered records are best read as a dependency graph rather than a list
of unrelated trials. E-001 first rejects a circular class-mismatch statistic;
E-002 blocks unsafe reuse of master annotations; E-003--E-005 establish that
relative depth can organize a tree-level orbit; E-006--E-008 ask whether that
geometry transfers to a bunch-level signal. The RGB bottleneck phase then
uses E-009--E-012 to isolate scale, contrast, texture, and ordinal maturity
confusion. E-013--E-021 turn that diagnosis into a production contract and a
fair detector comparison. Only after the RGB boundary is explicit does the
physical-depth branch begin in E-022.

This ordering also explains the difference between a defect and a limitation.
A mis-registration, wrong depth range, evaluator mismatch, or shared random
state is a defect because repairing it changes what the experiment measures.
Low B4 contrast, ordinal B2-B3 confusion, small sample size, and the absence
of a pretrained middle-fusion model are limitations because they remain after
the contract is repaired. The report never uses a known defect to justify a
general statement about RGB-D, and it never uses an unresolved limitation to
claim a clean sensor failure.

\section{Research design and evidence contract}
\label{sec:design-contract}
Every executed experiment is recorded with five layers: objective,
configuration, evidence, interpretation, and consequence. The configuration
layer names the dataset and split, input channels, model family, image size,
initialization, epoch budget, optimizer settings when available,
augmentation, checkpoint policy, seed, and evaluator. When a historical log
does not preserve one of those fields, this report says ``not reported in the
artifact'' rather than filling the gap from a default.

\subsection{Dataset and identity boundary}
The RGB pilot uses SawitMVC and its per-tree split. The physical-depth branch
uses SawitMVC-Depth, where paired RGB and depth files are validated before
training. The two branches differ in class prior, image count, orientation,
and sensor availability. Absolute scores must therefore stay within their
branch. Cross-branch claims use paired deltas, audit outcomes, or shared
decision rules, never an unqualified leaderboard comparison.

\subsection{Sensor, fusion, and evaluation controls}
The physical branch audits calibration and reprojection implementation at the
file level, then applies the same split and evaluator to RGB control and RGB-D treatment.
E-027 and E-029 add three-seed replication; E-031 adds split variance; E-032
adds early, middle, late, and random-noise controls. These controls are what
allow a null or negative result to be attributed to the tested modality rather
than to one lucky seed or a changed metric implementation.

\section{The full research story: from intuition to a conditional conclusion}
\label{sec:full-story}
The repository is easiest to misunderstand when its records are read as a
sequence of model scores. The real object of study is a chain of decisions:
what the field system must count, what RGB can already explain, what a fourth
channel is expected to add, and which controls are needed before a changed
score can be attributed to depth. This section makes that chain explicit. It
also explains why the final result is conditional. The study does not ask
whether RGB-D is good in the abstract; it asks whether the audited Orbbec
physical-depth branch adds reliable information to this inventory task under
the declared data, evaluator, fusion, seed, and split contracts.

\subsection{The field problem was an inventory problem, not a classifier demo}
The starting point was a practical field requirement: produce an inventory of
individual fresh-fruit bunches from multiple views of a palm. The system must
locate a bunch, assign one of four maturity stages, associate repeated views,
and count the physical bunch once. An image-level classification score does
not guarantee any of these operations. A detector can obtain a respectable
class-agnostic score while missing an occluded bunch, double-counting the same
bunch from two sides, or confusing adjacent B2 and B3 stages. This is why the
report keeps detection, maturity, identity, and counting as separate
estimands. Work on agricultural detection and multimodal systems motivates
the combination of appearance, geometry, and identity, but it does not remove
the need to define the denominator for this particular inventory task
\cite{sapkota2024yoloagri,ophoff2019multimodal}.

RGB was the natural first instrument because it is inexpensive, widely
available, and already carries colour, texture, shape, and context. The four
maturity classes are visually ordered, but the canopy also creates the exact
conditions in which colour alone becomes unstable: leaves occlude bunches,
sunlight changes saturation, and adjacent maturity stages share appearance.
The first hypothesis was therefore not that RGB is inadequate everywhere. It
was that the missing variable might be geometry. If a partly hidden bunch has
a different spatial relationship to the trunk, leaves, and neighbouring
bunches, a depth channel could expose information that is not present in the
RGB triplet. The hypothesis was useful only if it was translated into a
measurement and a reproducible tensor rather than left as an attractive
diagram.

\subsection{The fourth-channel idea and the history behind it}
The research sits on a long RGB detector lineage. Region proposal systems and
one-stage detectors made object localization practical, while later
transformer detectors changed the matching and global-context assumptions
\cite{redmon2016yolo,carion2020detr,zhao2024rtdetr,robinson2025rfdetr}. That history
explains why the report compares YOLO26, RT-DETR-L, and RF-DETR in the RGB
branch before attributing anything to a sensor. A detector family can change
backbone, pretraining, matching loss, resolution, post-processing, and
capacity at the same time. A better score is therefore a shortlist result
until the comparison contract is explicit.

The citations in this results report are a selected positioning set, not a
replacement for the repository's full literature evidence index. The companion
literature manuscript and its 182-entry bibliography carry the wider corpus;
this report cites the sources needed to explain the mechanisms that the 32
records actually test.

Depth adds a second history. RGB-D systems can use raw geometry at the input,
join RGB and depth features after separate encoders, or combine independent
decisions at the output. Surveys describe these as early, middle, and late
fusion, but the names do not make the hypotheses equivalent
\cite{feng2021multimodalsurvey,zhou2021rgbdsurvey}. Early fusion assumes that a
shared first representation can learn from aligned appearance and geometry.
Middle fusion assumes that each modality should learn a useful representation
before interaction. Late fusion assumes that two task-specific decisions can
be calibrated or associated after independent processing. A failure in one
lane cannot logically falsify the other two.

The fourth-channel contract in this report is deliberately concrete. The
production route uses \([B,G,R,D]\), marks invalid depth as an exact zero,
clips valid metric depth to a documented range, retains a validity mask, and
provides modality dropout so that RGB-only inference remains possible. The
contract also distinguishes a calibrated Orbbec capture from a retrospective
sidecar file and distinguishes metric depth from pseudo-depth predicted by a
monocular model. Pseudo-depth may be useful as an exploratory representation,
but it is not evidence that a physical sensor has solved occlusion. The
engineering boundary is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:e013-pipeline} and expanded
by the new engineering-contract figure at E-013; it becomes the audit target
for E-022.

\subsection{RGB must earn the right to add depth}
The early records are a measurement audit before they are a modeling study.
E-001 asks whether the existing class-mismatch statistic actually measures
maturity ambiguity. It finds zero disagreements because the statistic and the
association are generated by the same pipeline. That zero is reproducible,
but it cannot distinguish a correct stable prediction from the same wrong
class repeated across views. E-001 therefore rejects the statistic as an
ambiguity measure and sends the study back to independent identity evidence.
E-002 closes a related provenance shortcut: master annotations cannot be
reused as if they were an independent test of identity or maturity. These are
not administrative detours. They prevent an apparently clean RGB baseline
from being built on circular labels.

E-003 through E-005 then test the geometry intuition at the level where it is
actually observable. Corrected orbit video and known photograph sides show
that relative depth can organize a tree-level view sequence. The result is
useful because it says what geometry can do: order views and expose a coarse
spatial relation. E-006 and E-007 ask the harder question, whether that order
can become a bunch-level identity or one-count rule. The bridge breaks at the
point where a tree-level cue must identify individual partially occluded
bunches. The records keep the positive geometry observation while refusing to
call it a counting solution. E-008 remains a reserved number, so the gap is
visible rather than silently filled.

E-009 through E-012 move the diagnosis back to RGB. Scale audits ask whether
small objects explain the observed ceiling. E-010 and E-011 separate B4
contrast and texture from a rejected crowding explanation and test whether
augmentation changes the proposed mechanism. E-012 replaces a single accuracy
number with an ordinal confusion topology: errors concentrate between
adjacent maturity bins, especially B2 and B3. This matters for the sensor
hypothesis. Geometry may help localize an occluded bunch, but it cannot by
itself decide a subtle adjacent maturity boundary. A future depth gain must
therefore be judged separately for localization, maturity, and identity. The
RGB diagnosis also yields an actionable alternative: calibrated uncertainty
between adjacent stages may improve field review even when a new sensor does
not.

\subsection{The production contract turns an intuition into a falsifiable branch}
E-013 is the point where the fourth-channel idea becomes executable. It fixes
channel order, invalid-value semantics, range, calibration provenance, and
fallback behavior before a detector is selected. E-014 then decomposes the
baseline into class-agnostic localization and four-class maturity. This keeps
an improvement in finding bunches from being misreported as a solution to the
B2/B3 semantic boundary. E-015 and E-016 audit source mapping and the maturity
ceiling; E-017 through E-019 test the two-stage, localization, and resolution
branches; E-020 introduces RT-DETR-L as a transformer shortlist direction.

E-021 binds YOLO26, RT-DETR-L, and RF-DETR-L to one \texttt{pycocotools}
comparison and selects RF-DETR-L as the best observed RGB development
benchmark. The result is important for two reasons. First, it gives the
physical branch a strong control rather than a weak straw baseline. Second,
it defines the boundary of what the number means: the test population was
used repeatedly during development, the bootstrap unit is an image rather
than a tree, and the physical-depth dataset has different priors and sensor
availability. E-021 is therefore a benchmark answer within the RGB pilot,
not a confirmatory leaderboard claim and not a direct absolute comparison to
SawitMVC-Depth.

\subsection{The depth branch is an audit before it is a benefit test}
E-022 begins by asking whether the physical files measure what their metadata
say they measure. The audit checks per-file calibration, valid range,
reprojection, paired RGB and depth presence, and the evaluator. It exposes
implementation defects and withdraws the original favorable seed-42 reading
as a current metric. This is the pivotal causal distinction in the report:
repairing a defect changes the estimand, while a limitation that survives the
repair remains a bounded explanation. E-023 records the redesigned matrix and
its execution alias. E-024 creates an annotation-derived cross-side identity
oracle; E-025 fixes one evaluator, matching rule, and maximum-detection
budget; E-026 asks whether the corrected depth arm changes identity stability.
The sequence prevents a circular identity proxy or an evaluator asymmetry
from being mistaken for a sensor effect.

\subsection{What the complete chain supports, and what it leaves open}
The study supports a conditional conclusion with a precise scope. In the
audited SawitMVC-Depth branch, under the repaired physical files, one
\texttt{pycocotools} evaluator, the declared seeds, splits, capacities, and
fusion regimes, a reliable detection benefit from physical depth has not been
demonstrated. The negative or null directions are informative because they
survive repairs and are accompanied by noise, seed, capacity, and split
controls. They do not close every possible RGB-D design. Pretrained middle or
late fusion, more field locations, different capture seasons, and an
independent tree-level holdout remain open gates.

The deployment implication is therefore layered. RGB remains the practical
fallback because it is available for every image and has a documented best
observed control. Metric depth may be admitted when its per-file quality gate
passes, but the system should retain the RGB path when depth is missing or
invalid. Detection and maturity can be reported today with provenance and
uncertainty. Cross-view identity and one-count-per-bunch inventory remain the
operational target rather than a completed claim. Figure~\ref{fig:field-inventory}
maps those measured components against the endpoints that remain unvalidated.

\experiment{E-001}{Class mismatch as a maturity-ambiguity measure}
\phasefigure{N08-measurement-before-modeling-gpt-image-2.png}{E-001 establishes the first rule of the study: a statistic must measure the stated object before it can drive a model or sensor decision. GPT Image 2 conceptual figure; the zero count is retained in the E-001 artifact.}
\textbf{Objective.} E-001 asked whether the existing
\texttt{class\_mismatch} statistic could stand in for maturity ambiguity.
The shortcut was to count disagreements between predictions associated with
the same multi-view object and call the rate a direct label-difficulty measure.

\textbf{Configuration.} The analysis used existing multi-view predictions and
the original association. It introduced no independent identity oracle, no
second annotator, and no new track reconstruction. The association assumptions
and the statistic therefore came from the same pipeline.

\textbf{Evidence.} The audit covered 953 trees, 9,823 annotated bunches, and
7,328 multi-view bunches (18,540 labelled appearances). It found 0/7,328
disagreeing multi-view bunches and 0 flagged pairs. The zero is reproducible in
the JSON artifact, but it is not evidence that maturity is visually
unambiguous: one internally consistent class can be repeated across views, and
the statistic cannot distinguish a correct stable prediction from the same
wrong class repeated by the pipeline.

\textbf{Verdict.} E-001 is \textbf{FALSIFIED} as an ambiguity measure. It
remains a clean integrity check and supports neither a B2/B3 claim nor an
annotator-consistency claim. E-024 replaced it with a physical cross-side
identity oracle.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} The route is
\pathcode{reproduce/experiments/analysis/class_mismatch_stats.py}; evidence is
under \pathcode{evidence/experiments/results/E-001/}. The negative result is a
design correction, not a missing experiment.

\textbf{Interpretive note.} The failure is a measurement-validity failure,
not a failed classifier. The statistic has no independent source of truth:
the same association graph creates both the object identity and the
disagreement count. A zero therefore has a many-to-one interpretation. This
distinction matters because later claims about B2-B3 ambiguity require a
annotation-derived cross-side identity graph and a separately observed prediction. E-024 adopts
that stricter construction and reports missed views alongside disagreement.

\textbf{Discussion.} The important outcome is not the numerical zero but the
diagnosis of why the zero cannot answer the intended question. A label can
be internally consistent because the annotation process copied one class
across linked views, even when the visual evidence is ambiguous. Conversely,
a detector can disagree with a label without proving that the label is wrong.
E-001 therefore separates three objects that must never be conflated in a
later audit: the physical bunch identity, the human maturity label, and the
predicted class. This separation changes the experiment design from a label
audit to a prediction-consistency audit. It also explains why a negative
result here is productive: it removes a cheap but circular statistic before
any depth or fusion model is trained.

\textbf{Extended causal analysis.} The unit of analysis in E-001 is a
prediction pair, but the proposed construct is a property of the visual
world. Those units become identical only when the association graph is known
independently. In the original shortcut, a prediction is first attached to a
linked view and that same link is then used to decide whether two predictions
disagree. The statistic can therefore be stable under a wrong association,
and it can be unstable when a correct association is missed. This is a
verification-leakage failure: the procedure evaluates its own bookkeeping
rather than the maturity label. The repair is not to tune the threshold. It
is to supply a physical bunch identity graph and to report visible and
measured bunches separately. That design change is why E-024 is a successor,
not a reanalysis of E-001. It also keeps the later RGB-D claim honest. A depth
map can only be credited for identity consistency if identity is defined
without using the depth prediction itself.

The operational consequence is a three-way record for every future view:
physical identity, observed maturity label, and predicted maturity class. A
quality-control dashboard should expose all three instead of collapsing them
into one mismatch rate. This lets an annotator disagreement, a missed view,
and a detector class error be reviewed by different owners. It also makes the
metric portable to the larger SawitMVC split, where E-028 later supplies more
power. The original statistic is retained because its failure explains the
stricter denominator used by the rest of the branch.

\experiment{E-002}{Direct reuse of raw master annotations}
\phasefigure{N19-provenance-before-identity-gpt-image-2.png}{E-002 is the provenance checkpoint: raw masters must map to crops, manifests, and one-to-one identities before a model claim is allowed. GPT Image 2 conceptual figure; the inventory counts and mapping coverage remain in the E-002 and E-015 evidence tables.}
\textbf{Objective.} E-002 asked whether 3024 x 4032 master images could reuse
SawitMVC annotations without a new annotation pass.

\textbf{Configuration.} The probe used raw inventory, filename inspection,
image dimensions, and the relation between public MVC images and master-tree
images. Cheap assumptions were tested first: filename correspondence, scale
correspondence, and direct annotation reuse.

\textbf{Evidence.} The inventory contained 3,992 raw JPG files at 3024 by
4032 pixels, 45 MP4 orbit videos, 1,352 unique basenames, and 936 duplicate
basenames across folders. The direct route was \textbf{INCONCLUSIVE}. Compatible
aspect ratios were not proof that every master image represented the same view
or that its annotation frame was unchanged. The uncertainty was kept visible
instead of being hidden in a resized training set.

\textbf{Verdict and consequence.} E-002 created the requirement for E-015,
which used content matching with a measurable score and ambiguity check. The
eventual map covered 3,992 of 3,992 images, but the mapped master set was not
used to train E-021. The handoff to E-003 was not a claim that E-001 had been
replaced; it was the discovery of orbit videos as a separate geometry asset
after filename reuse was blocked.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} The investigation is logged in
\pathcode{docs/experiments/EKSPERIMEN.md}; the later route is
\pathcode{reproduce/experiments/build/match_raw.py}.

\textbf{Interpretive note.} The inconclusive result is useful because it
blocks a silent coordinate assumption. Reusing annotations would have
combined an uncertain image correspondence with a confident training label,
making any later maturity score impossible to audit. E-015 replaces
filenames with content matching and records a one-to-one manifest. The
master branch is consequently a provenance asset, not an automatic source of
additional labels.

\textbf{Discussion.} Image dimensions and aspect ratios are necessary checks,
but they are weak evidence of correspondence. A master frame can be cropped,
rotated, or captured from a neighboring side while retaining a plausible
shape. If its old box is copied without verification, the resulting error is
systematic: the detector is trained against a box that is visually displaced,
and the error is then mistaken for a maturity or localization failure. The
conservative decision in E-002 preserves the raw files and postpones use of
the branch until a content-derived score and a one-to-one manifest exist.
That delay costs no labels and protects every downstream comparison. E-015
later satisfies the missing proof, while E-016 and E-017 still keep the
mapped branch separate from the final E-021 RGB claim.

\textbf{Extended causal analysis.} The tempting shortcut in E-002 is a
provenance shortcut, not a model shortcut. A plausible filename or matching
aspect ratio can make a master image look like a higher-resolution copy while
the camera has actually moved to a neighbouring side. If the old box is then
scaled into the new frame, the error is coherent across a whole tree. Such an
error can lower localization and class metrics together, making it impossible
to tell whether a detector failed or the label moved. Content matching is
stronger because it compares image evidence, records a score, and rejects
ambiguous matches rather than forcing a one-to-one assignment. Keeping the
mapped master set outside E-021 also protects statistical interpretation: a
new image source changes the population even if the split names are reused.
E-002 therefore protects the meaning of every later RGB number by refusing to
treat more pixels as free data.

The same provenance rule applies to depth sidecars. A file that appears to be
the high-resolution partner of an RGB frame must carry its own calibration,
capture identity, and mapping status. Otherwise a later registration error
will be blamed on the detector. E-002 therefore links the literature's desire
for higher-resolution evidence to a manifest requirement: one source image,
one verified correspondence, one label frame, and one documented reason for
rejection. The mapping route is useful precisely because it can fail visibly.

The practical decision is a hold, not a discard. The raw masters remain
valuable for inspection and future annotation, but they are not allowed to
change the E-021 population. This preserves a clean counterfactual: if a
future high-resolution model improves, the gain can be attributed to the new
input and training recipe rather than to an unreported source swap. It also
protects the physical-depth branch from a subtle mismatch in image geometry.
An RGB frame that is cropped or rotated differently from its depth sidecar
can pass a filename check while failing the sensor contract. E-002 therefore
establishes the repository rule that provenance precedes capacity.

\textbf{Transfer check.} The provenance decision also protects the meaning of
the later master-image measurements. A mapped image is not automatically an
extra training sample because its camera pose, crop, and annotation frame can
change the population. The correct unit is a verified image pair with a
recorded score, a one-to-one assignment, and a rejection reason. This makes
the branch useful for inspection and future annotation without allowing it to
silently inflate the RGB pilot. It also gives the depth branch a concrete
analogy: a sidecar must carry capture identity and calibration, not just a
matching filename. E-002 therefore converts a vague concern about raw files
into a reusable provenance gate. A future high-resolution experiment can pass
only when the same manifest is frozen before training and when its test split
is declared as a new population rather than folded into E-021.

\decisioncard{E-002}{Can master annotations be reused without changing provenance?}{Only copied labels and the existing manifest are allowed; raw annotations remain read-only.}{E-003 tests whether multi-view geometry can replace the rejected statistic.}
\experiment{E-003}{DA3 multi-view geometry on one orbit video}
\phasefigure{N09-tree-geometry-to-bunch-identity-gpt-image-2.png}{E-003 is the first geometry handoff: relative depth can order views, but a tree-level cue is not yet a bunch-level identity signal. GPT Image 2 conceptual figure; E-003--E-007 provide the measured boundaries.}
\textbf{Objective.} E-003 tested whether Depth Anything 3 could recover smooth
camera geometry around an oil-palm tree. The target was tree pose, not
individual bunch depth.

\textbf{Configuration.} The same orbit video was evaluated at 16 and 48 frame
densities. The analysis estimated relative depth and inspected side ordering,
angular continuity, and camera-centre geometry. No physical sensor was
involved.

\textbf{Evidence.} The 48-frame pass covered 319.7 degrees with a mean relative
circle residual of 8.2 percent and an off-plane RMS of 9.1 percent. The smooth
orbit ended around frames 31--47; densifying the 16-frame pass did not remove
that failure. The 16-frame pass took 2.2 s (0.14 s per frame) at process
resolution 504 on an L4; the 48-frame runtime is not preserved in the
artifact. The output is relative depth with no metric flag. The
result was \textbf{PARTIAL}: it justified expansion to multiple videos, but one
sequence could not distinguish a geometry mechanism from a favourable
example. It also could not establish bunch-level separation because the target
was camera arrangement.

\textbf{Verdict and consequence.} E-003 opened E-004 and E-005 while fixing
the scope boundary: a correct tree pose is not a metric depth map for
detection. The branch was retained for geometry only.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} The producing analyses are
\pathcode{reproduce/experiments/analysis/da3_video_test.py} and
\pathcode{reproduce/experiments/analysis/da3_video_multi.py}. DA3 was
installed from source and its output was treated as relative depth. The
16/48-frame commands and the six-video rotation-correction command are listed
in SR-003; the display-matrix correction is part of E-004, not a new sensor.

\textbf{Interpretive note.} The unit of evidence is the ordered camera path.
Smooth angular continuity shows that a relative-depth model can organize a
tree-level sequence, but it does not give metric scale or a registered depth
pixel for a bunch. Keeping this boundary explicit prevents the tempting
inference that a visually plausible orbit is already an RGB-D sensor
experiment. E-004 increases replication count before any downstream use.

\textbf{Discussion.} The one-orbit design is a deliberately cheap feasibility
probe. It tests whether a sequence model can preserve the ordering of a
moving camera, but it has no independent scale, no synchronized depth sensor,
and no per-bunch correspondence. The apparent smoothness can arise from
temporal continuity even when local surfaces are wrong. That is why the result
is recorded as partial rather than promoted to a sensor result. The branch is
still useful: it identifies the correct unit for the next check, namely
repeated orbit videos with a fixed sampling policy and an explicit failure
case. E-004 uses that design to ask whether the apparent success is stable
across videos rather than a single favourable trajectory.

\textbf{Extended causal analysis.} E-003 is a useful example of why a
research pipeline should separate feasibility from utility. A smooth relative
depth trajectory can be enough to order frames for a capture assistant, even
when its scale drifts and its local surfaces are inaccurate. The same output
cannot be used as a registered fourth channel because the detector would need
the depth value at the exact pixel occupied by a bunch. The experiment also
has no negative sequence selected in advance, so a visually attractive orbit
could overstate robustness. The correct consequence is a replication with the
same sampling rule and an explicit failure case, not an immediate training
run. This boundary later makes E-006 interpretable: when a size-matched bunch
control fails, the result concerns bunch-level separation, not the earlier
tree-level ordering mechanism.

For a field workflow, the feasible use of E-003 is capture guidance. The model
can suggest whether an operator has covered enough of an orbit, while the
inventory model still consumes RGB detections and a separate identity audit.
This split avoids turning an attractive visualization into an uncalibrated
measurement. It also suggests a future test independent of detection: compare
predicted view order against a physical encoder or surveyed camera pose before
asking for bunch depth. E-003 contributes a safe interface even though it does
not contribute a fourth-channel score.

The experiment also demonstrates a useful separation between confidence and
coverage. A visually smooth sequence can have high temporal confidence while
missing the camera's true turn or collapsing local depth. Any future capture
assistant should expose the uncertainty and ask for a physical check when the
orbit is incomplete. This makes the output safe to use operationally without
letting it become a training label for bunch depth.

The one-orbit result is also useful for defining failure categories. A smooth
trajectory error is different from a side-order reversal, and both are
different from a bunch-level depth error. Logging those categories separately
means that a future model can improve capture guidance without being credited
for detection. The experiment should be rerun only when the sampling policy,
rotation convention, or model checkpoint changes. Re-running it merely to
obtain a more attractive visualization would not add evidence to the sensor
hypothesis.

\textbf{Transfer check.} The partial result has an operational use that is
different from detection. A capture assistant could use the smooth sweep to
warn that an operator has not covered enough of the tree, provided it exposes
uncertainty and keeps the output out of the label pipeline. The next run must
therefore declare its frame sampling and add a failure case rather than select
a more attractive orbit. This is a small but important change in the causal
graph: the model is tested as a view-order cue, not as an uncalibrated depth
sensor. If a future sequence fails, the failure should be categorized as
rotation, coverage, motion, or local geometry so that the remedy is visible.
E-003 thus earns its place by narrowing the claim before E-004 expands the
sample.

\decisioncard{E-003}{Does one orbit contain stable relative geometry?}{One video, one model, one orbit, and the same frame ordering are retained.}{E-004 repeats the geometry after correcting camera rotation.}
\experiment{E-004}{DA3 on six videos after rotation correction}
\phasefigure{N20-geometry-transfer-bunch-identity-gpt-image-2.png}{E-003--E-007 transfer a geometry cue from orbit ordering to bunch identity. The figure marks the exact boundary where tree-level ordering stops supporting reliable bunch-level links; the measured video and counting results remain in the experiment records.}
\textbf{Objective.} E-004 asked whether the partial one-video result survived
six orbit videos after the rotation issue was corrected.

\textbf{Configuration.} Six videos were processed with corrected rotation
handling at a single sampling density of 32 frames per video, a third density:
E-003's 16- and 48-frame passes are not repeated here, so the replication
changes both the video set and the sampling rate. Frames were extracted with
ffmpeg so that the display-matrix rotation of $-90$ degrees is applied,
replacing the \texttt{cv2} path used in E-003. Smooth sweep fraction and the
ability to reach 270 degrees were retained as primary measures. The failure
case was not discarded.

\textbf{Evidence.} Five of six videos reached a smooth sweep of at least
270 degrees and the median smooth fraction was 100 percent. The named failure
case, video 090556, reached only 41 percent smooth coverage. Its failure is
retained rather than averaged away; candidate explanations were tested and
falsified, so no cause is invented.

\textbf{Verdict and consequence.} E-004 is \textbf{CONFIRMED} for tree-level
geometry in the tested videos. It does not transfer to bunch-level depth or
sensor registration. E-005 therefore moved the test to original photographs.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} The route is
\pathcode{reproduce/experiments/analysis/da3_video_multi.py}. This is a
pseudo-depth geometry result, not physical Orbbec validation.

\textbf{Interpretive note.} Five successful videos are enough to justify
testing robustness, but not enough to explain the sixth. The unresolved case
is retained because deleting it would turn a robustness question into a
confirmation by construction. Rotation correction is a preprocessing
condition for this claim, not a new depth modality. The next experiment
therefore uses original photographs with a known side order.

\textbf{Discussion.} The correction step is part of the measurement contract,
not an optional cosmetic edit. Without it, a successful sweep could
reflect a camera convention that happens to match the model output. After the
correction, the five successful sequences show that relative geometry can be
useful for arranging views, while the sixth prevents a claim of universal
robustness. The experiment also exposes the difference between coverage and
correctness: reaching 270 degrees says that the camera path is represented,
not that each pixel has metric depth. This boundary is essential for the
later proposal to use geometry for cross-side association. E-005 therefore
moves to known photographs, where order can be scored directly and no video
interpolation is required.

\textbf{Extended causal analysis.} The rotation correction in E-004 is part
of the input definition. If a camera convention is inconsistent, a model can
appear to fail or succeed for a reason unrelated to scene geometry. Five
successful videos support a repeated tree-level pattern, while the sixth keeps
the claim from becoming universal. The median smooth fraction summarizes
coverage, not metric-depth accuracy. A 270 degree sweep does not say that the
hidden side of a bunch is reconstructed or that two overlapping bunches can be
separated. E-004 therefore closes only whether the orbit representation is
worth testing further. E-005 changes the observable from temporal smoothness
to known side order, which is a stronger and more auditable geometry test.

The unresolved sixth video is not a nuisance to be averaged away. It is a
diagnostic sample for future capture-quality analysis because its failure may
reflect motion blur, rotation metadata, occlusion, or model uncertainty. Any
deployment claim should report both successful coverage and failure rate under
the same sampling policy. This is a safer next step than training a detector
on pseudo-depth, and it keeps the tree-level result available for an operator
tool without overstating bunch-level accuracy.

The six-video replication also motivates a per-sequence audit artifact. Store
the rotation correction, sampled frame IDs, smooth-sweep threshold, and the
unexplained failure in one manifest. A future model or firmware change can
then be checked against the same sequence rather than against a hand-picked
visual example. This is the minimum evidence needed before moving from
tree-level ordering to a field capture recommendation.

The replication also makes the sixth video scientifically valuable. If a
future correction turns it into a success, the change must be reported as a
new preprocessing hypothesis and not folded into the original median. If it

\experiment{E-005}{DA3 side ordering on four- and eight-view photographs}
\phasecontext{E-005 transfers the geometry check from video continuity to known photograph sides.}
\textbf{Objective.} E-005 tested whether DA3 could recover the sequence of
original photographs around a tree, using four and eight sides.

\textbf{Configuration.} The sample was drawn with seed 42: 20 trees with
four-side sets and 30 trees with eight-side sets. Predicted order was compared
with the known sequence and 2,000 simulations of uniformly random angles.
Angular RMSE was reported by view count.

\textbf{Evidence.} All 20 four-side and all 30 eight-side trees were ordered
correctly. Angular RMSE was 17.3 degrees for four sides and 8.5 degrees for

100-percent result is a sample result under the declared sampling rule, not a
claim that every tree will be ordered correctly.

\textbf{Verdict and consequence.} E-005 is \textbf{CONFIRMED} for side

the four-side set because it samples the orbit more densely, not because it
has a different sensor. The random baselines establish that the recovered

which photograph comes next, while saying nothing about whether a bunch box
occupies a depth discontinuity. That is why E-006 changes the control unit to
size-matched local patches.

browsing or capture workflow, and the improvement from four to eight sides

to absolute distance and to the separation of two bunches that overlap in an
image. The random-order baselines also make the result interpretable: the
model is not merely exploiting a fixed first-side convention. The correct next

\textbf{Extended causal analysis.} E-005 establishes an intermediate
association contract. The lower eight-side angular error is meaningful because
the known ordering and random baselines are independent of the prediction,
but the measure remains insensitive to absolute distance. Two bunches can
overlap in an image while retaining a perfectly ordered camera path. That is
why the experiment does not promote DA3 to a depth sensor. It instead fixes
the tree geometry and moves the unit of analysis to local, size-matched
patches. This transition is important for the research narrative: a result
can be confirmed at one spatial scale and still fail at the scale needed by a
detector. E-006 is not a contradiction of E-005. It is a stricter test of a
different claim.

also has no metric scale, so a closer four-side tree and a farther eight-side

component in the dependency map rather than a depth detector baseline. Its

The known side order also offers a negative control for future geometry models.
If a method succeeds only when the first side is fixed, it is exploiting a
capture prior rather than recovering an orbit. E-005's random baselines make
that failure visible. The next local test must preserve the tree split and
sample controls from the same images, otherwise a change in view composition
could appear as a bunch-depth signal.

The four- versus eight-view comparison also limits a common interpretation.
The lower angular RMSE with eight sides reflects denser sampling of the orbit,
not a change in model capacity or sensor quality. A detector still needs a
local correspondence and a depth convention at each box. The random-order
baseline rules out a fixed side prior, but it cannot rule out a camera-motion

claim at the level of view ordering.

\textbf{Transfer check.} E-005 supplies an intermediate association contract.

controls. If E-006 fails, the tree-level result remains useful for capture
guidance without being promoted to a bunch detector.

\experiment{E-006}{Pseudo-depth as a bunch-level separation cue}
\phasecontext{E-006 tests the boundary between tree-level ordering and bunch-level separation.}
\textbf{Objective.} E-006 asked whether pseudo-depth makes a bunch box
different from its local background.

\textbf{Configuration.} Forty trees contributed 780 ground-truth boxes. Each
box received two random, size-matched controls, for 1,560 controls. Contrast
was computed at output resolutions 504 and 1008. Matching controls prevents
arbitrary map structure from being mistaken for a bunch signal.

\textbf{Evidence.} Bunch boxes had contrasts 0.0089 and 0.0096. Random controls
had 0.0341 and 0.0364. The ratio was 0.26 at both resolutions; the AUC
difference was only 0.009. Here AUC is the probability that a randomly chosen
bunch box receives a higher contrast than a matched control. Box-level
permutations gave (p=0.0245) at 504 pixels and (p=0.0110) at 1008 pixels;
these p-values do not replace a tree-cluster uncertainty estimate. B4 had the
lowest class AUC at 1008 pixels, 0.6022. The small p-values therefore indicate
separation from the null permutation, not a practically useful bunch cue.

\textbf{Verdict and consequence.} E-006 is \textbf{FALSIFIED} for bunch-level
separation. The output is relative, generated from the same RGB image, and
non-metric. It cannot reject an independent physical sensor, so E-022 remains
a separate hypothesis.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} The route is
\pathcode{reproduce/experiments/analysis/depth_bunch_signal.py}; evidence is
under \pathcode{evidence/experiments/results/E-006/}. The bounded statement is
that pseudo-depth failed in this test.

\textbf{Interpretive note.} The control ratio is the critical result: the
background contrast is about four times larger than the bunch contrast. A
relative map derived from the same RGB pixels can encode viewpoint or texture
priors, but it cannot be treated as independent geometry. The null therefore
closes a pseudo-depth shortcut while leaving a physical Orbbec test open.
This separation of modality and provenance is carried into the E-022 audit.

\textbf{Discussion.} E-006 is intentionally conservative about what its
negative result closes. It closes the claim that a pseudo-depth image, by
itself, separates a ground-truth bunch box from a matched local background. It
does not close the possibility that a calibrated sensor resolves a real
occlusion or improves registration. The two output resolutions give the same
contrast ratio, so the conclusion is not an artefact of one resize setting.
The low B4 AUC is especially important because B4 was the class that motivated
the depth idea. A future positive result must therefore beat the paired
control under a physical acquisition contract, not merely produce a smoother
looking map.

\textbf{Extended causal analysis.} The control ratio in E-006 is stronger
than a visual impression of a depth map. Bunch boxes are compared with random
boxes of the same size, so the result asks whether the map contains a local
signal tied to the object rather than a global canopy gradient. The ratio of
0.26 and the near-zero AUC difference show that pseudo-depth is not merely
noisy; it is less separated inside the target boxes than in the controls under
this construction. Because the map is generated from RGB, the result does
not reject an independent physical sensor. It does reject the shortcut of
calling relative model output a registered modality. E-007 therefore tests
whether tree geometry can still correct counting even after local separation
fails, while E-022 remains reserved for physical depth.

The negative result changes the burden of proof for any future monocular-depth
proposal. It must beat a size-matched control on a held-out tree split, report
the map's provenance, and show that a gain persists when RGB is replaced by a
physically independent acquisition. A smoother visual map or a favorable
orbit sequence is insufficient. This explicit contract prevents a later
feature-engineering branch from reopening E-006 by changing only the
visualization.

The matched-control design also makes the negative result robust to a change
in output resolution. Both 504 and 1008 produce the same ordering of bunch
and random contrasts, so a sharper pseudo-depth visualization cannot rescue
the mechanism by itself. A future monocular proposal must state whether it
uses new information or only a different resampling of RGB. This is why the
physical sensor branch is kept independent and starts with a file-level audit.

The control construction also tells us what a future physical-depth study must
retain. Use the same tree-level split, sample random boxes with the same size
distribution, and report both contrast and AUC. If a sensor gain appears only
after changing the control geometry, the estimand changed. The negative
pseudo-depth result is therefore a design constraint for E-022, not a general
statement about monocular depth models or about other crops.

\textbf{Transfer check.} The negative result closes a tempting shortcut but not
the whole geometry branch. Because pseudo-depth is generated from RGB, it can
repeat the same appearance cue that makes neighboring bunches difficult. The
matched size and random controls show that a smooth map alone is not evidence
of independent separation. A future monocular proposal would need a second
source of truth, such as a calibrated range target or a held-out physical
sensor, before its detector channel could be credited. E-007 is retained as a
counting experiment because a global correction might still reduce duplicate
counts even when local depth separation fails. Its result must be reported as
an association heuristic, not as proof that pseudo-depth measures distance.

The count correction also clarifies the difference between calibration and
association. Calibration can be monitored with a held-out count table;
association requires an identity oracle and a missed-view denominator. A
future metric sensor may improve the second without changing the first, so
both should remain separate in the report. E-007 closes the relative-depth
implementation while leaving that interface ready for a genuinely calibrated
experiment.

the fusion phase. If depth changes B4 but worsens B2-B3, an aggregate score

used later in the physical-depth audit, where a historical B4 gain is checked
against the full paired interval and noise control.

The three-way diagnosis also changes how a false negative is interpreted. If a
future transform raises B4 AP but changes the B2-B3 confusion topology, the
result is a trade-off, not a universal improvement. The ground-truth control
keeps that trade-off visible before expensive training. It also prevents
neighbor count from becoming a proxy for occlusion when B4's own overlap is
low. The next branch must preserve these class-conditional checks.

\experiment{E-011}{Texture and contrast preprocessing for B4}
\phasecontext{E-011 tests a texture mechanism with transform-specific controls.}
\textbf{Objective.} E-011 asked whether the remaining B4 signal is in texture
rather than ordinary luminance or color contrast. It compared contrast
enhancement against frequency-sensitive transforms before training a detector.
The falsification threshold was a B4 gain of more than \(0.02\) AUC over the
original-luminance reference, measured after each transform's own random-box
control is subtracted. Laplacian clears it: \(+0.0458\) signed control gain,

mask, Sobel gradient, and Laplacian. The control boxes are resampled for each
transform because a transform changes the entire image distribution. The
metric is pixel-level AUC for box interior versus surrounding ring.

\textbf{Discussion.} Laplacian's improvement is a localized signal, not a

the bunch's surface texture, whereas CLAHE and unsharp mask mostly rescale

the transform helps B4 more than its matched background, rather than
making every patch more variable. The experiment also shows why a detector
trial must be separated from a feature diagnostic. A pixel-level AUC can
increase while localization, calibration, or class confusion worsens after
the transform is applied to the whole image. E-011 therefore motivates the
ordinal crop test and keeps detector training as a later, explicitly paired
comparison.

\textbf{Extended causal analysis.} The signed control in E-011 prevents a
transform from looking useful merely because it increases pixel variance.
CLAHE and unsharp mask rescale the same low-frequency evidence, while the
Laplacian changes B4's separation relative to its matched control. That is
evidence for a texture mechanism, not proof that a detector trained on a
Laplacian map will improve. The scalar crop test also cannot measure spatial
localization or calibration. Its value is diagnostic: it tells the production
branch where a feature channel might carry information and where a generic
color boost is unlikely to help. E-012 then asks a different question about
class boundaries, keeping feature separability and detector performance from
being conflated.

The transform result also limits what preprocessing can claim. A scalar AUC
improvement can arise from texture while whole-image augmentation harms color
calibration or box localization. The signed B4-control column should be
carried into any detector trial, and the crop split must remain disjoint by
tree. E-011 therefore supplies a feature hypothesis with a reproducible
control, not a production recipe. The next experiment tests whether the class
labels themselves impose an ordinal structure.

The preprocessing result can be transferred only as a hypothesis about input
features. It cannot be transferred as a detector checkpoint because the
transform changes the image distribution and may interact with color-safe
augmentation. A faithful follow-up must keep the same tree split, transform
parameters, and control sampling, then evaluate detection and class AP rather
than crop AUC alone.

The transform study also supplies a guard against post-hoc feature selection.
Laplacian is retained because its signed gain beats the paired control and is
reported with the same crop definition. Other transforms are not promoted
simply because they look sharper. A detector-level replication would need to
freeze the transform parameters, use the same split, and report class AP and
localization alongside crop AUC. E-011 therefore contributes an evidence
boundary, not a new preprocessing default.

\experiment{E-012}{Ordinal maturity structure from ground-truth crops}
\phasecontext{E-012 closes the RGB bottleneck phase with an ordinal maturity diagnosis.}

compatible with, but does not prove, an underlying maturity axis. If errors

the 52.87 percent accuracy alone. The two middle classes have two adjacent
neighbors, so their recall is reduced even though the feature representation
contains useful information. The scarcity of B3-to-B1 errors indicates that
the classifier has learned an ordered appearance axis rather than a random
partition. This result changes how later detector metrics should be read:
class-agnostic localization can be strong while four-class mAP is limited by
boundary placement. An ordinal loss, regression head, or calibrated
uncertainty estimate is therefore a more direct follow-up than adding an
unstructured classification layer. The result is a structural diagnosis, not
a claim that the simple forest is an appropriate deployment model.

confusion matrix is not merely a 52.87 percent score; it shows that the
largest errors lie between adjacent maturity bins and that B3-to-B1 mistakes
are rare. This topology is consistent with adjacent ordinal boundaries, but
it is not sufficient to establish a continuous maturity axis. It explains why
class-agnostic localization can
look strong while four-class mAP remains limited, and why a calibrated ordinal
or regression head is a more direct intervention than an arbitrary feature
channel. The crop model is intentionally simple and balanced, so its accuracy
is a lower-bound diagnostic rather than a deployment ceiling. Keeping the
boxes at ground truth also removes localization from the test. E-013 can then
specify a production channel contract without claiming that depth solves the
semantic boundary.

The ordinal result has a deployment implication: confidence should be allowed
to express uncertainty between adjacent bins. A hard B2 prediction and a hard
B3 prediction are not equivalent to a B1-to-B4 error, even though ordinary
accuracy counts both as one mistake. This is relevant to counting and harvest
planning, where an uncertain maturity estimate can be reviewed while a missed
object cannot. The report keeps this semantic diagnosis visible before
introducing sensor data so that a future depth gain is judged against the
correct target.

The ordinal diagnosis also determines what should be logged in the field.
Alongside a class prediction, retain confidence or an adjacent-class margin so
that B2-B3 uncertainty can be reviewed. This is a different deployment lever
from sensor fusion and should be measured separately. It prevents a later
depth experiment from being credited for improvements that actually come from
better calibration of the maturity boundary.

The confusion topology also changes the expected value of a sensor. Geometry
can help a detector find a partly occluded bunch, but it cannot by itself
decide whether a visible bunch is B2 or B3 if the labels are adjacent bins.
This is why the report keeps localization and maturity metrics separate in
E-014 and does not interpret every future RGB-D gain as a maturity solution.
The crop experiment is a semantic diagnosis that remains valid even if the
sensor branch is closed.

\subsection{Phase C: turn the RGB diagnosis into a bounded RGB baseline}
E-013 is the first point at which the proposed fourth channel becomes an
implementation rather than a drawing. The contract fixes channel order,
missing-depth behavior, calibration, and evaluator before a detector is
selected. E-014 then decomposes the target into class-agnostic localization
and four-class maturity, so later architecture gains cannot hide a class
boundary problem. E-015--E-019 audit mapping, localization, color safety, and
resolution as separate interventions. E-020 and E-021 finally compare YOLO,
RT-DETR, and RF-DETR under one RGB protocol.

The result is a clean branch boundary. E-021 is the best observed RGB answer for the
SawitMVC per-tree split. It is not a claim that RGB-D has failed, and it is not
a score that can be ranked directly against the physical-depth dataset. It is
the control that makes the next question precise: after sensor files and
reprojection are audited, does physical depth move a paired detector delta?

\textbf{Discussion.} E-013 is a boundary-setting experiment, so its success
criterion is contract completeness. The explicit \([B,G,R,D]\) order prevents
a silent channel permutation, while zero-valued invalid depth distinguishes a
missing measurement from a dark RGB pixel. The range and z-buffer rules are
equally important because a model can learn clipping artefacts instead of
scene geometry. Modality dropout provides a safe inference fallback, but it
does not turn RGB-only execution into evidence for depth. Finally, the
separation between calibrated Gemini captures and raw SawitMVC-Depth sidecars

comparison. E-022 tests the sidecars under their own contract.

\textbf{Extended causal analysis.} E-013 succeeds by making failure modes
observable. The [B,G,R,D] order, exact zero invalid value, calibrated range,
and modality dropout are all easy to violate silently. A pipeline that reads
the wrong grid or treats invalid depth as a dark RGB pixel can still train and
produce plausible boxes. Separating SDK-aligned captures from raw sidecars is
therefore a provenance rule, not documentation overhead. The contract also
defines the deployment fallback: RGB-only inference remains valid when the
sensor is absent. E-022 later audits the raw sidecars under their own range
and reprojection semantics. The result is a reproducibility boundary, not an
accuracy result, and it prevents a successful import or unit test from being
misreported as hardware validation.

The contract also supplies an operational failure taxonomy. A zero depth
value can mean invalid geometry, while a dark RGB pixel can be a valid
appearance; the model must not be allowed to conflate them. Likewise, a
reprojected sidecar and a calibrated SDK capture are different provenance
classes even if their arrays have the same shape. This distinction is what
makes E-022's defect report actionable and prevents a successful source-only
test from being presented as a device result.

The contract should be versioned with the capture mode and firmware. A change
in invalid-value handling or clipping range can alter the fourth-channel
distribution without changing the model name. Recording those fields makes a
future positive or negative depth result attributable to the sensor branch
rather than to an undocumented preprocessing change.

The production contract should be treated as a versioned interface between
capture and training. Record channel order, clipping range, invalid-value
mask, calibration source, firmware, and dropout policy with every run. This
lets a reviewer decide whether a changed score comes from a model, a sensor,
or a preprocessing contract. E-013 therefore makes later nulls more useful:
they can be attributed to the tested modality rather than to an undocumented
array transformation.

\subsection{Phase D: audit physical depth before interpreting a null}
The physical-depth branch is a new measurement system, not an extension of the

be expected to resolve maturity, but E-026 itself neither supports nor rules

\textbf{Verdict.} \textbf{INCONCLUSIVE WITHIN THE MEASURED SUBSET.} The
confidence interval includes zero and the RGB and RGB-D denominators differ,
so E-026 does not establish equivalence or a reliable depth effect. It does
not prove that depth cannot help B4 geometry; B4 has no two-side detections

larger collection; it does not establish power for the E-026 depth treatment

\textbf{Reproducibility.} Preserve the denominator definition and include

\textbf{Discussion.} The near-zero paired change is not evidence that the
modality is exactly equivalent; the interval is wide because the identity
denominator is small. It is evidence that this particular depth input does
not stabilize the measured subset enough to overcome sampling uncertainty.
The separate miss rate moves in the same unfavorable direction, so the
slightly higher RGB-D consistency numerator cannot be interpreted as a
benefit. The absence of B4 in the two-side denominator limits the claim to
the observed identity pairs. E-028 supplies a larger RGB baseline, while the
multi-seed fusion matrices test aggregate detection under stronger controls.
Together these experiments prevent an identity metric from being used as a
surrogate for a missing mAP improvement.

\textbf{Extended causal analysis.} E-026 asks whether registered depth
stabilizes identity, a narrower claim than detection improvement. The graph
definition and evaluator are held fixed, but the observed RGB and RGB-D
denominators are unequal, so the comparison is not fully paired. A null result
therefore cannot be explained by a changed matching rule, but it remains
limited by the observed subset. It also prevents a common overclaim in
which lower class disagreement is treated as better detection even when missed
views increase. The negative verdict leaves open whether a different sensor
or fusion topology changes localization, but it removes identity stabilization
as an automatic consequence of registration. E-027 consequently repeats the
early-fusion question with three seeds and a noise control.

The null identity result also guards against a misleading success criterion. A
depth arm that produces fewer class changes because it detects fewer views is
not a useful stabilization. The separate missed-appearance rate makes that
failure visible. This is why E-026 does not collapse identity and detection
into one mAP number, and why E-027 returns to paired detector contrasts with
the same evaluator.

\textbf{Transfer check.} The identity result should not be collapsed into
mAP. A modality could reduce disagreement on visible pairs while increasing
missed appearances, or improve localization while leaving B2-B3 confusion
unchanged. E-026 reports those quantities together and keeps the RGB arm as

confidence threshold, tree split, and evaluator. If a future arm changes the
denominator or drops a difficult side, a positive delta would be a sampling
effect rather than a sensor effect. E-027 repeats the early-fusion contrast
under that stricter identity and metric contract.

single new split cannot replace multi-seed replication. E-031 closes the

\subsection{Positioning against prior literature}
The detector sequence follows the established progression from one-stage
real-time detection to set-based transformer detectors
\cite{redmon2016yolo,carion2020detr,zhao2024rtdetr,robinson2025rfdetr}. The
comparison in E-020--E-021 therefore tests a documented model-family choice,
not an isolated implementation. Its result is still a development benchmark,
because the test images were used repeatedly during model selection.

Agricultural detection studies and reviews report recurring effects from scale,
appearance, illumination, and crop context
\cite{sapkota2024yoloagri,genemola2020fruit3d,kang2020strawberry}. The RGB
branch makes those effects explicit for FFB maturity rather than assuming that
a generic agricultural detector transfers unchanged. E-009--E-012 therefore
contribute a failure analysis and an ordinal follow-up, not a claim that one
visual cue explains every field condition.

RGB-D surveys and real-time fusion studies treat registration, modality
reliability, and fusion location as separate design variables
\cite{feng2021multimodalsurvey,zhou2021rgbdsurvey,ophoff2019multimodal,zhang2023cmx}.
The E-022--E-032 branch operationalizes that separation: it audits the sensor
contract, includes a noise control, binds the evaluator, and repeats the
contrast across seeds. The resulting conditional null is consequently a
boundary on this protocol, not a universal statement that depth is useless.

\section{Detector architecture and modality comparison}
\label{sec:architecture-comparison}
The architecture comparison is informative only when the changed factors are
listed. YOLO26m and YOLO26l provide a real-time convolutional family with
different capacity. RT-DETR-L replaces greedy post-processing with a
transformer decoder and Hungarian matching, while RF-DETR-L uses a DINOv2
backbone and LW-DETR head. E-020 changes several factors at once, so it is an
engineering shortlist result. E-021 narrows the comparison with one split,
one evaluator, matched resolution, and a parameter-aware YOLO entry.

RF-DETR-L reaches test mAP50 0.6038 and test mAP50-95 0.2770. The paired
bootstrap against RT-DETR-L is +0.0255 with a 95 percent interval of
[0.0104,0.0408]. That makes RF-DETR-L the best observed RGB benchmark, but it
does not identify whether the gain comes from DINOv2, the LW-DETR head,
pretraining, matching, or the complete package. It also does not make RF-DETR
an RGB-D model. RF-DETR-L is RGB-only in E-021, and its absolute score must
not be compared directly with the SawitMVC-Depth runs.

The comparison also exposes why a capacity narrative must be cautious. E-030
contains one seed at each YOLO26 capacity, while E-029 uses RT-DETR-L at three
seeds and retracts the earlier high-capacity rescue clause. A model with more
parameters can absorb a fourth channel without showing that the channel is
useful. The correct follow-up is a completed multi-seed capacity curve with a
fixed initialization policy, not a larger single checkpoint.

\section{Fusion ablation and capacity interpretation}
\label{sec:fusion-interpretation}
The fusion matrix is the cleanest answer to the question ``was the insertion
point wrong?'' E-032 trains RGB, early, middle, late, and random-noise arms
from scratch for 150 epochs at 640 pixels on YOLO26n. The same split, tree
bootstrap, and E-025 evaluator are applied to all arms. Early fusion has
seed-level deltas of -0.0120, +0.0234, and -0.0017. Middle fusion has
+0.0096, +0.0212, and +0.0110. Late fusion has -0.0056, +0.0070, and
+0.0102. Noise has -0.0130, +0.0025, and -0.0081.

All twelve paired confidence intervals include zero. Middle fusion has the
largest mean, +0.0139, and the narrowest seed range, but it is an indication,
not a confirmed improvement. Late fusion adds approximately 3.00 million
parameters against 2.51 million for middle fusion and 2.57 million for early
fusion, about 17 percent above the early-fusion arm, without a distinguishable
gain. The noise arm is a necessary control because a fourth channel can change
optimization or regularization even when it carries no geometry.

The from-scratch design is fair for custom topologies but conservative for
transfer. No compatible COCO checkpoint exists for the two-branch YAML, so
the result cannot answer whether a pretrained middle-fusion model would learn
faster or reach a different basin. This is a genuine boundary, not a hidden
positive. G7b remains open for a capacity monotonicity test across seeds with
the same evaluator and a prespecified stopping rule.

\section{Physical-depth failure attribution}
\label{sec:depth-attribution}
The negative depth result has four layers. First, the sidecar metadata in
E-022 claimed color alignment, but the buffer remained in the depth-camera
grid. Full reprojection improved the mutual-information diagnostic over direct
resize by +0.0306 bits, with a 95 percent interval [0.0260,0.0354]. The
median naive
miss was 29.3 pixels and the maximum was 61 pixels. Per-file intrinsics and a
train-only 0.8--15.0 m range are therefore part of the corrected contract.

Second, the initial positive-looking E-022 delta contained a cross-split donor
bug and shared-RNG noise behavior. Its seed-42 interval already included zero
[-0.0215,+0.0632]. The correction withdraws the old claim; it does not erase
the audit trail. Third, E-027 and E-029 show that the sign changes across
seeds and that the high-capacity depth-minus-noise mean remains below the
archived decision threshold. Fourth, E-031 shows that split choice can move
absolute mAP by more than the selected seed range.

These layers separate implementation defects from data and model limits. A
registration defect is repaired before interpretation. A low-contrast B4
class, ordinal B2-B3 boundary, 352-tree depth set, and from-scratch training
budget remain limitations after repair. The report therefore rejects both
overclaims: it does not call a defective run a positive sensor result, and it
does not call a bounded null a universal statement about RGB-D.

\section{Limitations, open G7b, and deployment consequences}
\label{sec:final-limitations}
Several limitations remain material. SawitMVC-Depth is smaller than the RGB
pilot and contains a different orientation and class distribution. Three seeds
and three split conditions are enough to expose variance but not enough to
estimate a stable population distribution. E-032 is YOLO26n only, 640 pixels,
150 epochs, and from scratch. RF-DETR-L is not tested with physical depth.
The physical claims are also bounded by the audited Orbbec Y16 files, not by
all Orbbec cameras or all depth encodings.

The open G7b experiment should be completed only if it answers a decision
question. The minimum design is a prespecified YOLO26 capacity sequence with
RGB, registered depth, and noise controls at the same split, at least three
seeds, fixed evaluator, train-only depth range, and confidence intervals at
the tree level. The comparison must report initialization, optimizer,
learning-rate schedule, augmentation, epoch budget, checkpoint rule, and
hardware. A single larger model is not a substitute for the curve.

\subsection{G7b pre-execution checklist}
G7b should be treated as a decision experiment, not as a request for one more
large checkpoint. The purpose is to determine whether the one-seed capacity
pattern in E-030 is monotonic after the same controls that closed E-032. The
minimal pre-execution plan therefore freezes the following items before any test
score is opened:

The decision rule must distinguish three outcomes. A monotonic capacity effect
with paired intervals excluding zero would reopen the capacity hypothesis and
justify a new fusion design. A non-monotonic pattern with intervals crossing
zero would leave the current conditional null intact while documenting model
instability. A positive result in the noise arm would identify an optimization
or channel-cost effect rather than a geometry benefit. None of these outcomes
would rewrite E-021 or turn SawitMVC-Depth into a common absolute leaderboard.
This checklist is deliberately stricter than ``train a larger model'' because
the latter cannot separate capacity, seed, split, and evaluator movement.

For deployment, the practical path is RGB-first. Use RF-DETR-L as the research
baseline when 8.5 FPS on an L4 is acceptable, and preserve the RGB fallback in
the sensor pipeline. Keep depth capture and reprojection available as an
auditable data-collection branch, not as an automatic source of confidence.
For maturity, an ordinal or uncertainty-aware head is a more direct response
to E-012 than another uncalibrated channel. For cross-view inventory, keep the
annotation-derived cross-side graph and missed-appearance denominator from E-024 and
E-028. These choices follow the evidence and remain reversible if G7b or a
newer sensor changes the result.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:final-conclusion}
This complete report turns 32 numbered records into one research story. The RGB
pilot establishes a bounded RF-DETR-L development benchmark and diagnoses a heterogeneous
error surface: B4 is low-contrast and texture-rich, while B2-B3 confusion is
ordinal. The pseudo-depth branch supports tree-level view ordering but fails
the bunch-level separation test. The physical-depth branch repairs sensor and
evaluator defects, then finds no reliable detection benefit for early, middle,
or late fusion within the completed controls; equivalence was not tested.

The strongest conclusion is deliberately conditional: audited physical depth
did not improve detection reliably for this data, sensor encoding, split,
training regime, and evaluator. The strongest positive conclusion is equally
bounded: RF-DETR-L is the best observed RGB control under the SawitMVC
per-tree split and one \texttt{pycocotools} protocol, subject to test-set
development exposure. The end-to-end one-count-per-bunch inventory remains
unvalidated. Future work should spend its evidence budget on G7b, ordinal
maturity modeling, external RGB holdout evaluation, and sensor-aware capture
quality rather than on repeating an un-audited seed-42 comparison.

\section{Phase-level interpretation and evidence use}
\label{app:phase-interpretation}
The individual experiment sections preserve the audit trail, but a reader also
needs a compact interpretation of how the evidence budget moved. This appendix
does not introduce a new result. It explains how the numbered entries combine
into four causal phases and identifies the artifact that carries each handoff.
The distinction is important: a conceptual GPT Image 2 diagram explains the
logic, while a script output, manifest, table, or JSON file carries the claim.

\subsection{Phase A: measurement before modality}
E-001 begins with a tempting shortcut: the existing class-mismatch statistic
looks like a direct measure of maturity ambiguity. Its numerator, however,
comes from an association graph that already links predictions across views.
The experiment therefore tests the bookkeeping definition rather than the
visual difficulty itself. E-002 extends the same caution to provenance. A
master-image mapping is useful only when filename, dimensions, scale, content,
and rejection decisions are recorded. The result is an auditable mapping
problem, not permission to silently expand the E-021 population.

E-003--E-005 then ask the smallest geometric question: can monocular
relative-depth structure recover a camera orbit or a known side order? The
answer is partially yes at tree level. The orbit survives a six-video
replication and the four/eight-side photograph check, but the map has no
metric scale and does not identify an individual bunch. E-006 makes that
boundary quantitative with size-matched random controls. The local
bunch-versus-background separation does not survive, so pseudo-depth cannot be
credited as a sensor. E-007 tests the stronger cross-view counting claim with
appearance, pose, depth, and threshold controls; the implementation fails to
link one bunch reliably. E-008 is deliberately reserved rather than filled
with an invented null. The surviving handoff is therefore twofold: keep
relative geometry as context, and move any sensor-benefit claim to calibrated
physical depth while diagnosing RGB errors first. The relevant record is the
E-001--E-007 result directory and the append-only experiment log.

\subsection{Phase B: locating the RGB bottleneck}
E-009 starts the RGB diagnosis by asking whether B4 is simply too small at the
training canvas. The box-size distribution makes scale a disadvantage, but
not a sufficient explanation. E-010 changes the measurement from geometry to
appearance and shows that B4 is predominantly low-contrast and texture-rich;
overlap and crowding alone do not account for the error. E-011 tests whether
CLAHE, unsharp masking, Sobel, or Laplacian features can isolate a recoverable
cue. These transforms change measurable crop statistics, yet no transform
result is a detector intervention. The claim remains about mechanism, not
about a promised mAP gain.

E-012 supplies the most consequential representation result in the pilot.
The crop confusion matrix is concentrated on adjacent B2--B3 classes, which
shows an ordinal error topology rather than arbitrary mistakes. E-013 translates
the proposed fourth channel into an engineering contract: channel order,
calibration, invalid values, fallback, and evaluator are explicit. E-014 then
intends to hold detections and images fixed while comparing class-agnostic and
four-class scoring. The current diagnostic compares the gap, class-agnostic
\mAP50 \(0.7191\) versus four-class \(0.5218\), pending the
byte-identical prediction JSON check. E-015 closes the provenance loop for mapped master
pixels, while E-016 withdraws a single maturity ceiling because it would
collapse localization and ordinal errors into one unstable estimand.

The phase therefore rejects three easy interventions before model selection:
a larger box alone, a cosmetic crop transform, and an unqualified two-stage
pipeline. Its evidence points to an ordinal, appearance-sensitive problem.
That diagnosis is why E-017 tests two-stage separation, E-018 measures a
class-agnostic localization envelope, and E-019 tests more pixels under a
color-safe baseline. The records for this handoff are
\pathcode{reproduce/experiments/analysis/box_size_analysis.py}, the class-level contrast tables, the crop
confusion matrix, and the channel-contract manifest.

\subsection{Phase C: interventions and bounded RGB selection}
E-017 is a causal check on the bottleneck diagnosis. A separate maturity
classifier does not recover the target score under the fixed split and
budget, so adding stages is not a free repair. E-018 shows that localization
can be measured independently of class names, but the envelope is not the
dominant source of the four-class loss. E-019 tests a high-resolution RGB
intervention and obtains a null for the target. The result does not say that
resolution never matters; it says that more pixels without a new maturity
signal do not close this gap.

E-020 narrows the engineering shortlist with RT-DETR-L and establishes a
non-NMS transformer direction. Because the comparison changes several
architecture factors at once, it is not a component-level causal claim.
E-021 is the best observed RGB endpoint because YOLO, RT-DETR-L, and RF-DETR-L are
re-evaluated on the same SawitMVC per-tree split with one
\texttt{pycocotools} protocol. RF-DETR-L reaches test \mAP50 \(0.6038\) and
\mAP50-95 \(0.2770\), with the paired RT-DETR-L bootstrap interval excluding
zero in an image-level development bootstrap. This is a bounded RGB result,
not an RGB-D leaderboard and not proof
that one backbone component caused the gain.

The practical handoff is consequently a control definition. RF-DETR-L becomes
the RGB reference against which a later physical-depth arm can be paired, while
the RGB branch retains its own dataset, class prior, resolution, and split.
The exact evidence is in the E-021 model-comparison figure, per-tree metrics,
checkpoint note, and evaluator log. Any later absolute number from
SawitMVC-Depth must not be ranked against it without a new common population.

\subsection{Phase D: physical-depth audit and closure}
E-022 begins the second branch by auditing the sensor rather than interpreting
the first positive-looking delta. Per-file calibration, depth-to-RGB
reprojection, invalid-value handling, train-only range, donor selection, and
random-number use are inspected. Reprojection improves the alignment
diagnostic, while the original cross-split donor and shared-RNG behavior
invalidate the historical positive interpretation. E-023 is not a second
scientific result; its redesigned plan is executed under E-032 so that the
matrix is counted once. E-024 replaces a circular identity proxy with an
annotation-derived cross-side graph and an explicit visible-pair denominator,
exposing the power
limit before detection deltas are discussed.

E-025 binds every later contrast to one evaluator, max-detection budget, and
paired tree bootstrap. E-026 asks whether registered depth stabilizes
cross-side identity after that repair and remains inconclusive because its
denominators differ and its interval includes zero.
E-027 adds three seeds and a random-noise control; early fusion is not
consistently positive and the depth-harmful direction appears in the same
protocol. E-028 enlarges the RGB collection to improve the identity
denominator, but more pairs do not create a detection benefit. E-029 tests
RT-DETR-L across seeds and withdraws the high-capacity rescue clause because
the signs vary and the intervals include zero.

E-030 is retained as a hypothesis generator: one seed suggests that capacity
may change the channel cost, but it cannot close monotonicity across seeds.
E-031 shows that tree-split movement can exceed the selected seed movement,
so absolute scores require a split boundary. E-032 tests the insertion-point
explanation with RGB, early, middle, late, and noise arms trained from
scratch; all twelve paired confidence intervals include zero. The final claim
is conditional and auditable: in this SawitMVC-Depth population, Orbbec Y16
encoding, split, training regime, and evaluator, no tested fusion point
separates from its same-seed RGB control. The raw objects are
\pathcode{docs/experiments/AUDIT-E022.md}, the reprojection outputs, E-025 evaluator logs,
three-seed JSON files, split-variance tables, and the E-032 contrast manifest.

\section{Experiment-by-experiment interpretation checklist}
\label{app:experiment-checklist}
The numbered sections contain the full discussion for each run. The checklist
below is a second reading aid: every entry states the question, the evidence
that is allowed to answer it, and the next branch it enables or closes. It is
intentionally concise so that a reader can audit the causal chain without
mistaking a repeated summary for a new measurement.

\subsection{E-001--E-008: measurement and relative geometry}
\paragraph{E-001.} The class-mismatch statistic is a property of the existing
association graph, not an independent maturity annotation study. Its result
closes the shortcut and motivates an identity oracle before any cross-view
claim.
\paragraph{E-002.} Raw master-image reuse is tested as a provenance problem.
Because content correspondence is not fully decisive, later training claims
must use a frozen manifest and explicit rejection rules.
\paragraph{E-003.} One orbit video shows that monocular relative depth can
organize camera motion. The result is tree-level geometry only, so it cannot
supply metric bunch depth.
\paragraph{E-004.} Six corrected videos test whether E-003 was a favorable
sequence. The orbit pattern survives replication, but no new metric scale or
identity evidence appears.
\paragraph{E-005.} Four- and eight-side photographs transfer the geometry check
to known view order. Side order is useful context, not an independent graph of
the same physical bunch.
\paragraph{E-006.} Size-matched controls test a local pseudo-depth cue directly.
The bunch-versus-background separation fails, which prevents RGB-derived
relative depth from being credited as a sensor.
\paragraph{E-007.} Appearance, depth, pose, and threshold sweeps test
cross-side counting. The implementation does not link one bunch reliably, so
physical calibration remains a separate hypothesis.
\paragraph{E-008.} The reserved number is not run and therefore has no null
result. Keeping the slot visible preserves chronology and prevents a missing
experiment from being misread as evidence.

\subsection{E-009--E-016: RGB bottleneck diagnosis}
\paragraph{E-009.} Box-size statistics show B4 is disadvantaged at the
training canvas but mostly medium scale. Size is a candidate contributor, not
a sufficient explanation of the class gap.
\paragraph{E-010.} Contrast, texture, and overlap measures identify B4 as
low-contrast and texture-rich. The evidence rejects crowding as the sole
cause and directs the next test toward appearance cues.
\paragraph{E-011.} CLAHE, unsharp, Sobel, and Laplacian transforms change crop
statistics without constituting a detector intervention. The outcome supports
a texture mechanism but does not promise an mAP improvement.
\paragraph{E-012.} The ground-truth crop confusion matrix concentrates errors
on adjacent B2--B3 classes. Maturity is therefore ordinal, making an
ordinal-aware objective a more direct follow-up than an arbitrary classifier.
\paragraph{E-013.} The fourth-channel contract freezes channel order,
calibration, invalid values, fallback, and evaluator behavior. It delivers an
auditable implementation path without claiming that the modality helps.
\paragraph{E-014.} The intended identical detections are scored
class-agnostically and with four classes, but the JSON identity check remains
pending. The \(0.7191\) versus \(0.5218\) mAP50 gap is therefore provisional
evidence that maturity assignment may cost more than localization.
\paragraph{E-015.} Master pixels are mapped with one-to-one content checks and
an ambiguity record. Provenance is complete enough for inspection but does
not silently change the E-021 test population.
\paragraph{E-016.} Three proposed maturity ceilings fail to define one stable
estimand. The claim is withdrawn, preserving separate localization and
ordinal error questions for later work.

\subsection{E-017--E-021: RGB interventions and model selection}
\paragraph{E-017.} A two-stage detector separates boxes from maturity crops
under a fixed split and budget. It does not recover the target, so extra
stages are not a general repair for the ordinal boundary.
\paragraph{E-018.} Class-agnostic recall and IoU thresholds expose a useful
localization envelope. The envelope is informative but not the dominant
source of the four-class loss.
\paragraph{E-019.} A color-safe high-resolution RGB run asks whether more
pixels close the gap. The target remains unresolved, which rules out
resolution alone as the next intervention.
\paragraph{E-020.} RT-DETR-L establishes an NMS-free transformer direction.
Several architecture factors change together, so this is a shortlist result,
not a component-level causal attribution.
\paragraph{E-021.} Four models share the SawitMVC split and one
\texttt{pycocotools} evaluator. RF-DETR-L at test mAP50 \(0.6038\) and
mAP50-95 \(0.2770\) is the best observed RGB control for the next branch.
\subsection{E-022--E-024: physical-depth audit handoff}
\paragraph{E-022.} The physical-depth audit finds registration, donor, range,
and random-number defects before interpreting a delta. Reprojection repairs
the file contract, while the original positive-looking metric is withdrawn.
\paragraph{E-023.} The redesigned fusion plan is executed once under E-032.
The alias remains in the repository for provenance but cannot add a second
scientific count.
\paragraph{E-024.} An annotation-derived cross-side identity graph and visible-pair denominator
replace the circular mismatch proxy. The measure becomes auditable while its
limited power is made explicit before detection comparisons.

\subsection{E-025--E-032: replication and fusion closure}
\paragraph{E-025.} Trainer JSON and binding evaluator outputs are compared
under one max-detection budget and paired tree bootstrap. Later deltas are
interpretable because the metric contract is fixed.
\paragraph{E-026.} Registered depth is tested for cross-side identity
stabilization with separately reported observed denominators. No useful
stabilization appears in the measured subset, so detection replication remains
the decisive test.
\paragraph{E-027.} RGB, registered depth, random noise, and swapped-tree arms
run over three seeds. The depth direction is not consistently positive and
the historical rescue clause is no longer defensible.
\paragraph{E-028.} A larger RGB collection improves the identity
denominator and power context. It does not create a paired detection gain,
separating sample coverage from modality benefit.
\paragraph{E-029.} RT-DETR-L early fusion is repeated over three seeds with the
fixed evaluator. Signs vary and intervals include zero, retracting the
high-capacity rescue interpretation.
\paragraph{E-030.} A YOLO26 capacity sweep is informative at one seed only.
It generates the G7b monotonicity hypothesis but cannot close it across
seeds or establish a universal capacity effect.
\paragraph{E-031.} Three tree-split conditions reveal absolute-score
movement that can exceed the selected seed movement. Any cross-run absolute
claim must therefore carry its split boundary.
\paragraph{E-032.} RGB, early, middle, late, and noise arms are trained from
scratch under one protocol. All twelve paired confidence intervals include
zero, closing the tested fusion-point explanation conditionally while leaving
G7b open.

\subsection{Minimal rerun sequence}

A defensible rerun follows the same order as the research argument. First,
freeze the commit, environment, dataset manifest, tree split, and class map.
The RGB pilot and the physical-depth branch are copied into separate output
roots so that a convenience mapping cannot silently merge their populations.
Second, run the deterministic diagnostics before training: box-size and
contrast summaries for E-009--E-011, the ordinal crop confusion matrix for
E-012, and the E-014 class-agnostic decomposition. These checks establish
whether a changed detector is answering the same bottleneck.

Third, reproduce E-021 with the recorded checkpoint and one
\texttt{pycocotools} command. Report the per-tree split, test image count,
mAP50, mAP50-95, and paired bootstrap interval together. A single scalar
without its split and evaluator is a historical observation, not the auditable
RGB benchmark. Fourth, if depth is enabled, validate every sidecar before
training. Intrinsics and extrinsics must be read per file, the depth image
must be reprojected into the RGB grid with a z-buffer, invalid values must
remain distinguishable from zero range, and the train-only range estimate
must be applied to validation and test without refitting.

Fifth, execute the physical-depth contrasts in the audit order. Bind the
evaluator and maximum-detection budget first, then run the identity oracle,
three-seed controls, split-variance check, and fusion matrix. Keep the
random-noise arm in the same manifest because a fourth channel can change
optimization even when it contains no geometry. Store seed-level deltas and
paired tree intervals, not only means. If an interval includes zero, label
the result inconclusive under the stated rule rather than converting it into
a universal null.

This sequence also defines the stop conditions. A missing calibration file,
ambiguous mapping, changed split, or evaluator mismatch stops interpretation
and creates an audit item. A failed hardware capture is reported as a
deployment blocker, not hidden by a synthetic replacement. Conversely, a
successful code path without a device capture supports only a software
contract. These gates keep the report useful to a future field deployment
without overstating what the current 32 numbered records establish.

\subsection{Claim review checklist}
Before a result is promoted from an experiment note into a manuscript claim,
the reviewer should answer eight questions. First, is the object being scored
the same object named in the objective? A class-mismatch statistic, a
cross-side identity rate, and a detector mAP are not interchangeable
denominators. Second, are the dataset and tree split named beside the number?
This check prevents the RGB pilot and SawitMVC-Depth branch from being merged
into one apparent leaderboard.

Third, is the changed factor isolated well enough for the verb used in the
conclusion? E-020 can support a detector direction because several architecture
factors changed, but it cannot support a backbone attribution. E-032 can test
fusion placement in its stated from-scratch regime, but it cannot support a
universal claim about every pretrained middle-fusion topology. Fourth, does a
negative result pass the implementation gates? A defective registration or
mismatched evaluator must be repaired or bound before a null is interpreted.

Fifth, are seeds, split conditions, and paired intervals visible? A mean from
one seed is a hypothesis generator; a three-seed interval that crosses zero is
inconclusive under the report rule. Sixth, is the control appropriate to the
mechanism? The random-noise arm in E-027 and E-032 tests channel cost and
optimization movement, while the annotation-derived identity graph in E-024 tests the
denominator itself. Removing either control would make the causal wording
stronger than the evidence.

Seventh, can a new reader locate the raw artifact without relying on the
conceptual figure? The required pointer is a script, manifest, JSON file,
metrics document, or checkpoint note. If the pointer is missing, the result is
marked unavailable rather than reconstructed from a nearby run. Eighth, is the
next branch written before the result is summarized? The handoff should state
what uncertainty remains, what was held fixed, and why the next experiment is
the smallest defensible change.

Applying this checklist yields the final two boundaries. RF-DETR-L is the best
observed RGB choice under the SawitMVC split and one evaluator, subject to
development-set exposure. Physical depth is a valid audited data path, but it
has no reliable replicated detection benefit in the tested SawitMVC-Depth
matrix. These boundaries are stronger because they
retain failed shortcuts, withdrawn metrics, and the open G7b gate instead of
compressing them into a single success narrative.
