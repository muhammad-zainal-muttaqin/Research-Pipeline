\begin{abstract}
This report answers one question. A YOLO detector reads three RGB channels.
We added depth as a fourth channel. We measured whether detection improves,
stays the same, or becomes worse.

The answer is that depth did not improve detection. Early fusion was harmful
in two of three seeds. Middle and late fusion moved the score by less than
the noise of the experiment. All twelve paired confidence intervals contain
zero. A random-noise control channel moved the score by the same order of
magnitude as a real depth channel.

We also measured whether the depth implementation itself was correct. It was
not correct at the start. The sensor metadata claimed color alignment, but the
buffer stayed in the depth-camera grid. We corrected this error with full
reprojection. The negative result above comes from the corrected pipeline, not
from the defective one.

Thirty-two numbered experiments (\E{001} to \E{032}) and nine gates (\G{0} to
\G{8}) carry the evidence. This report lists every one of them with its result
in one line.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question}
\label{sec:question}

A YOLO detector reads an image with three channels. These are red, green, and
blue. A depth camera can supply a fourth channel. The fourth channel holds the
distance from the camera to each pixel.

The idea is simple. Two fruit bunches can have the same color and can overlap
in the image. The same two bunches can stand at different distances from the
camera. Depth can separate them when color cannot.

This report measures one thing:

\begin{itemize}
\item Does the fourth channel increase detection accuracy?
\item Does it change nothing?
\item Does it decrease accuracy because it adds noise?
\end{itemize}

The only way to answer this question is to run the experiment and to read the
numbers. This report contains those numbers.

\section{The answer}
\label{sec:answer}

\subsection{Short answer}

The fourth channel did not increase detection accuracy in the tested regime.
Two of three early-fusion seeds became worse. Middle fusion and late fusion
moved the score less than the seed-to-seed variation of the same model.

All twelve paired confidence intervals contain zero. Middle fusion has the
largest mean and a positive sign in all three seeds. This is an indication.
It is not a confirmed improvement.

\subsection{Why the noise arm matters}

The noise arm is the most direct answer to the question. We replaced the depth
channel with random numbers. The noise arm then moved the score by the same
order of magnitude as the three depth arms. No contrast excluded zero.

This measurement does not prove that depth and noise are equivalent. We did not
run an equivalence test. It shows that the depth values demonstrated no usable
information in this regime, because a channel without geometry produced changes
of the same size.

\subsection{What the answer does not say}

The answer applies to the tested regime. That regime is YOLO26n, 640 pixels,
150 epochs, one tree split, three seeds, and training from scratch. We did not
prove that depth and RGB are equivalent. We did not measure a pretrained
middle-fusion model, because no compatible COCO checkpoint exists for the
two-branch topology. We did not measure a larger palm plantation or a second
camera model.

\section{Was the depth implementation correct?}
\label{sec:implementation}

A negative result is only useful when the implementation is correct. We
measured the implementation first. It contained five errors. We corrected all
five before we read the final numbers. The first four errors belong to the depth
pipeline and come from the \E{022} audit. The fifth error is the evaluator
asymmetry, which \E{025} measured as a separate gate.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Errors found in the depth pipeline, and their corrections
(\E{022} and \E{025}).}
\label{tab:defects}
\begin{tabularx}{\columnwidth}{L{0.26\columnwidth}Y}
\toprule
\textbf{Error} & \textbf{Correction and measured effect}\\
\midrule
Wrong registration & The sidecar declared \texttt{alignedTo: color}, but the
buffer stayed in the depth-camera grid. A direct resize missed the target by
29.3 pixels at the median and 61 pixels at the maximum. Full reprojection
increased the mutual-information diagnostic by $+0.0306$ bits, with a 95
percent interval of $[0.0260, 0.0354]$. Mutual information is a registration
diagnostic. It is not proof of geometric alignment, and it is not proof of a
downstream benefit.\\
Shared calibration & Two camera units have different intrinsics. The pipeline
now reads the calibration from the sidecar of each file.\\
Wrong depth range & The default range of 0.3 to 8.0 m did not fit this sensor.
The \E{022} pixel audit found no pixel below 0.3 m, and 10.07 percent of pixels
above 8 m. The corrected train-only range is 0.8 to 15.0 m.\\
Cross-split donor and shared random state & The first favorable result used a
donor image from another split and a shared random-number state. We withdrew
that result.\\
Evaluator asymmetry & The two arms produced 2.44 times different detection
density. This produced an offset of 0.0156, which is large enough to reverse
the sign of the depth-minus-RGB difference. All later work uses one
\texttt{pycocotools} paired evaluation.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The first depth result looked positive. That result came from the defective
pipeline. Its own confidence interval already contained zero, at
$[-0.0215,\allowbreak +0.0632]$. We withdrew it.
Every number in Section~\ref{sec:answer} comes from the corrected pipeline.

Pseudo-depth failed the direct measurement in \E{006}. The contrast of a bunch
box was 0.0089 against 0.0341 for a size-matched random control. The difference
in AUC was 0.009. Pseudo-depth comes from the same RGB image, so its error
correlates with the RGB error. It is not an independent sensor.

Physical depth passed the file audit after the corrections in
Table~\ref{tab:defects}. It then failed the benefit measurement.

\subsection{How the 32 records answer the one question}

Six records give the direct answer. These are \E{022}, \E{027}, \E{029},
\E{030}, \E{031}, and \E{032}. They train the detector with and without the
fourth channel and compare the scores.

The other records remove the alternative explanations for that answer. Without
them, a reader can reject the negative result for a reason that has nothing to
do with depth:

\begin{itemize}
\item \E{001}, \E{002}, \E{015}, and \E{024} make sure that the labels and the
identity graph are not circular.
\item \E{025} makes sure that both arms use one evaluator. Before this record,
the evaluator alone can reverse the sign.
\item \E{031} makes sure that the split is named. The split moves the absolute
score more than the seed does.
\item \E{003} to \E{007} remove pseudo-depth as a cheap substitute for the
sensor.
\item \E{009} to \E{012} explain what the RGB error actually is. B4 is a
contrast problem and B2--B3 is an ordinal maturity problem. Depth can help the
first one. Depth cannot help the second one.
\item \E{013} freezes the channel contract, so that a later run cannot change
the channel order in silence.
\item \E{017} to \E{021} supply a strong RGB control. A weak control makes any
fourth channel look useful.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The state of \G{7b}}

Commit \texttt{7afd274} opened 12 additional runs. These runs cover yolo26m and
yolo26l, three modalities, and the seeds 1337 and 2024. The true state is:

\begin{itemize}
\item 7 of 12 runs finished training, and their curves are archived.
\item We computed no paired contrasts. The paired files exist only for seed 42.
\item We never updated \E{030}, so its one-seed limitation still applies.
\end{itemize}

The capacity turning point between 21.9 and 26.3 million parameters is therefore
a one-seed pattern. It is not a finding. Five runs and one paired evaluation
close this gate.

\section{What this means for the field system}
\label{sec:consequence}

\begin{itemize}
\item Use the RGB path. RF-DETR-L is the best observed control, at test \mAP50
0.6038. Its speed is 8.5 FPS on an L4 GPU, which is too slow for real-time field
use without optimization.
\item Do not add the depth channel to raise accuracy. The measurement does not
support that decision.
\item If depth hardware is already present, keep the capture as an auditable
data branch. Apply the per-file quality gate from Table~\ref{tab:defects}.
\item For the B2--B3 maturity error, use an ordinal loss or a regression head.
\E{012} points there. A fourth channel does not address an ordinal boundary.
\item The one-count-per-bunch inventory target is not validated. No experiment
in this report measures it.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limits of this answer}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
\item The fusion matrix used YOLO26n only, at 640 pixels, for 150 epochs, on one
split, and from scratch.
\item No pretrained checkpoint exists for the two-branch topology, so transfer
learning was not measured.
\item Three seeds and three splits expose variance. They do not estimate a
population distribution.
\item The depth dataset holds 352 trees. The RGB pilot holds 953 trees.
\item The claims apply to the audited Orbbec Y16 files. They do not apply to
every Orbbec camera or every depth encoding.
\item We never trained RF-DETR-L with physical depth.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We added a fourth depth channel to a YOLO detector and measured the effect. In
the tested regime, the channel did not improve detection. Early fusion was
harmful in two of three seeds. Middle and late fusion produced changes that a
random-noise channel also produced. All twelve paired intervals contain zero.

The depth implementation was defective at the start, and we corrected it. The
final answer comes from the corrected pipeline, so the negative result belongs
to the sensor and the method, not to a registration error.

The answer is conditional. Section~\ref{sec:limits} lists the conditions.
One gate, \G{7b}, can still change the capacity part
of the answer. It needs five runs and one paired evaluation.
