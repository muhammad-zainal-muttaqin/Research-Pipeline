\title{Cross-View Identity in Image-Based Fruit Counting: A Systematic Review and Design Space for Class-Wise Inventories}
\author{Muhammad~Zainal~Muttaqin and Fatma~Indriani%
\thanks{Department of Computer Science, Universitas Lambung Mangkurat, Banjarbaru, Indonesia.}}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
Image-based fruit inventories count fruit from photographs, but a camera moving along a row or around a tree sees most fruit several times, so a sum of detections overstates the true number. This review examines how the literature decides which observations belong to the same fruit and how class labels such as maturity are attached to each unique fruit. A systematic search of Scopus identified 971 relevant studies published between 2012 and 2026. The 187 studies that combine several observations of the same fruit were classified into six association mechanisms: no association, statistical correction, appearance matching, temporal tracking, geometric or 3D association, and learned association. Temporal tracking dominates recent work, and geometric association provides the most direct calibration-free counts when camera pose and depth are reliable. On fruit that look alike, appearance features did not improve tracking over motion alone. Few studies report identity errors or counts per class, and comparisons of mechanisms on the same data are rare. In oil palm, published work concentrates on ripeness classification and bunch detection in single images; cross-view association of bunches on a palm has not been evaluated. The review relates each mechanism to the acquisition conditions it requires and proposes the measurements needed to evaluate duplicate-aware, class-wise inventories.
\end{abstract}
