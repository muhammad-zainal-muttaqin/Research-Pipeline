\title[mode=title]{{\normalsize Review Article}\\[3pt] Cross-view identity in image-based fruit counting: A systematic review and a design space for class-wise inventories}
\author[1]{Muhammad Zainal Muttaqin}
\author[1]{Fatma Indriani}
\cormark[1]
\affiliation[1]{organization={Department of Computer Science, Universitas Lambung Mangkurat},
  city={Banjarbaru},
  country={Indonesia}}
\cortext[1]{Corresponding author. \textit{E-mail address:} \texttt{f.indriani@ulm.ac.id} (F. Indriani).}
\begin{abstract}
In image-based fruit inventories, a camera moving along a row or around a tree captures most fruit several times, so summed detections overstate the fruit count. Earlier reviews organize this literature by sensor, detector, or estimation route, not by fruit identity. This systematic mapping review, reported according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA)~2020, examines how studies decide which observations show the same fruit and how class labels such as maturity are attached. A Scopus search on 28 September 2026 returned \nRetrieved records, of which \nIncluded studies from 2012 to 2026 were included. The \nMulti studies that combine observations of one fruit were coded by association mechanism and acquisition design. Each mechanism lost accuracy where its assumption broke: correction factors did not transfer between orchards, trackers lost identities at low frame rates and did not link a row's two sides, and geometric association needed reliable pose and registration. The counting rule changed which tracker ranked first in all four studies that varied both, yet only \nMetIdent of \nMethodMetric method studies measured identity. Per-class counts were less accurate than totals, through confusion between neighbouring maturity classes and missed immature fruit. Oil-palm detectors were evaluated on single images, and no included study associated bunches across views of a palm. As most comparisons favoured the authors' own method or used annotated rather than field counts, only two findings reached high certainty. The review relates each mechanism to the acquisition it requires and proposes minimum measurements for class-wise inventories.
\end{abstract}
