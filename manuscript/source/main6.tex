\title[mode=title]{{\normalsize Review Article}\\[3pt] Cross-view identity in image-based fruit counting: A systematic review and a design space for class-wise inventories}
\author[1]{Muhammad Zainal Muttaqin}
\author[1]{Fatma Indriani}
\cormark[1]
\affiliation[1]{organization={Department of Computer Science, Universitas Lambung Mangkurat},
  city={Banjarbaru},
  country={Indonesia}}
\cortext[1]{Corresponding author. \textit{E-mail address:} \texttt{f.indriani@ulm.ac.id} (F. Indriani).}
\begin{abstract}
Image-based fruit inventories count fruit from photographs, but a camera moving along a row or around a tree captures most fruit several times, so summed detections overstate the true number. Earlier reviews organize this literature by sensor, detector family, or yield-estimation route, not by the identity of a fruit across observations. This systematic mapping review examines how studies decide which observations belong to the same fruit and how class labels such as maturity are attached to it. Reporting follows PRISMA~2020 where its items apply. A Scopus search on 28 September 2026 returned \nRetrieved records, and \nIncluded studies published between 2012 and 2026 remained after full-text assessment. The \nMulti studies that combine several observations of the same fruit were coded by association mechanism (no association, statistical correction, appearance matching, temporal tracking, geometric or 3D association, and learned association) and acquisition design, and synthesized against each mechanism's assumptions. Each mechanism fails where its assumption does not hold. Fitted correction factors did not transfer between orchards or canopy systems and held across seasons in only one study. Motion-based trackers lost identities at low frame rates and did not link the two sides of a row. Generic appearance descriptors improved on plain motion matching but did not consistently improve on other trackers, and the counting rule changed the error more than the tracker. Geometric association removed the duplicates that tracking left, but only while pose and registration were reliable. Net count errors can hide offsetting duplicates and misses, yet only \nMetIdent of \nMethodMetric method studies measure identity. Class errors concentrate at intermediate maturity stages, where a label that changes between frames can turn one fruit into two records. Oil-palm detectors, evaluated on single images, are accurate on harvested bunches, their multi-class scores drop on standing palms, and no study associates bunches across views of a palm. The review relates each mechanism to the acquisition it requires and proposes minimum measurements for class-wise inventories.
\end{abstract}
