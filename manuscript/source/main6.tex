\shorttitle{Cross-view identity in image-based fruit counting}
\shortauthors{M.Z. Muttaqin and F. Indriani}
\title[mode=title]{{\normalsize Review Article}\\[3pt] Cross-view identity in image-based fruit counting: A systematic review and design space for class-wise inventories}
\author[1]{Muhammad Zainal Muttaqin}
\cormark[1]
\author[1]{Fatma Indriani}
\affiliation[1]{organization={Department of Computer Science, Universitas Lambung Mangkurat},
  city={Banjarbaru},
  country={Indonesia}}
\cortext[1]{Corresponding author. \textit{E-mail address:} \texttt{mz.muttaqin1@gmail.com} (M.Z. Muttaqin).}
\begin{abstract}
Image-based fruit inventories count fruit from photographs, but a camera moving along a row or around a tree sees most fruit several times, so a sum of detections overstates the true number. Earlier reviews organize this literature by sensor, detector family, or yield-estimation route, and none takes the identity of a fruit across observations as its organizing variable. This systematic mapping review examines how the literature decides which observations belong to the same fruit and how class labels such as maturity are attached to each unique fruit. Reporting follows PRISMA~2020 where its items apply; a Scopus search on 28 September 2026 returned \nRetrieved records, \nTitles titles were screened, and \nIncluded studies published between 2012 and 2026 were included. The \nMulti studies that combine several observations of the same fruit were coded by association mechanism (no association, statistical correction, appearance matching, temporal tracking, geometric or 3D association, and learned association), acquisition design, and reporting of counts per class, and were summarized with descriptive statistics and a structured narrative synthesis. Temporal tracking dominates recent work, whereas geometric association provides the most direct calibration-free counts when camera pose and depth are reliable. On fruit that look alike, appearance features did not improve tracking over motion alone in the two comparisons made on the same data. Few studies report identity errors or counts per class, and comparisons of mechanisms on the same data are rare. In oil palm, published work concentrates on ripeness classification and bunch detection in single images; cross-view association of bunches on a palm has not been evaluated. The review provides a design space that relates each mechanism to the acquisition conditions it requires and a minimum set of measurements for evaluating duplicate-aware, class-wise inventories.
\end{abstract}
