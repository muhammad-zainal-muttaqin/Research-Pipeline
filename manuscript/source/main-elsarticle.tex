\title{Multi-View and Multimodal Perception for Class-Wise Fruit Inventories: A Design-Space Review}
\author[a]{Muhammad Zainal Muttaqin\corref{cor1}}
\ead{mz.muttaqin1@gmail.com}
\author[a]{Fatma Indriani}
\affiliation[a]{organization={Department of Computer Science, Universitas Lambung Mangkurat},city={Banjarbaru},country={Indonesia}}
\cortext[cor1]{Penulis korespondensi}
\begin{abstract}
Turning many observations of a plant into a single, class-wise inventory of its
fruit is not solved by per-image object detection. A system must separate
crowded instances, attach a class label to the correct instance, and avoid
recording the same fruit twice when it is seen again from another view. This
review maps verified literature on RGB detection, depth estimation, multimodal
fusion, three-dimensional geometry, and cross-view association onto that task.
Its domain is agricultural fruit perception, with oil-palm fresh fruit bunches
as the principal case, but mechanisms are drawn without restriction from
non-agricultural imaging, including tracking, re-identification, multi-view
detection, and structure from motion, which enter as transferable rather than
agricultural evidence. Classification is treated as an instance-dependent class
attribute, of which maturity grading is one case, because a per-class count is
undefined until an instance has been identified. The synthesis identifies a
multi-scale real-time RGB detector as an appropriate baseline, depth as a
conditional geometric cue whose interpretation must be declared, and
quality-aware feature or late fusion as testable alternatives rather than
established agricultural results. It specifies the dataset, ablation, detection,
classification, geometry, count-error, and latency requirements that a field
validation would have to satisfy. No calibrated depth-sensor benchmark for
oil-palm fresh fruit bunches exists in the verified corpus. The authors' own
records against those requirements are reported separately in the companion
evidence-audited results report and are deliberately not folded into this
evidence map.
\end{abstract}
