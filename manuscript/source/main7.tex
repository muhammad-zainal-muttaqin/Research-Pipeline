\shorttitle{Cross-view identity in image-based fruit counting}
\shortauthors{M.Z. Muttaqin and F. Indriani}
\title[mode=title]{{\normalsize Review Article}\\[3pt] Cross-view identity in image-based fruit counting: A systematic review and a design space for class-wise inventories}
\author[1]{Muhammad Zainal Muttaqin}
\author[1]{Fatma Indriani}
\cormark[1]
\affiliation[1]{organization={Department of Computer Science, Universitas Lambung Mangkurat},
  city={Banjarbaru},
  country={Indonesia}}
\cortext[1]{Corresponding author. \textit{E-mail address:} \texttt{f.indriani@ulm.ac.id} (F. Indriani).}
\begin{abstract}
Images of the same plant can show some fruit repeatedly and miss others in every view. Those errors can cancel in a yield estimate, even when the inventory contains the wrong fruit. This systematic map examines how vision systems link observations of individual fruit and assign one class to each record. A Scopus search on 28 September 2026 and targeted reference checking identified \nIncluded reports for inclusion, of which \nMulti combined observations. The synthesis relates six ways of handling repeated observations to the information available at acquisition and to the references used in evaluation. On 16 mango trees, fitted count-to-harvest slopes were 0.27 for a single view, 0.54 for opposed views, and 1.014 with tracking and triangulation. Across video studies, changing the rule for converting tracks to counts could reverse the ranking of trackers. Geometric registration improved agreement with harvest counts when the same apples appeared on opposite sides of a row. Per-class errors persisted when total counts were close to their references because missed fruit and transfers between maturity classes affect the inventory differently. For oil palm, linked annotations identify bunches across photographs, but the retrieved reports contained no method validated for automatically linking detections of on-palm bunches across independently acquired sides. The design space separates the evidence needed for a crop-load estimate from the evidence needed for a class-wise inventory.
\end{abstract}
