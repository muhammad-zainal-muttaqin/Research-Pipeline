\shortauthors{M.Z. Muttaqin and F. Indriani}
\title[mode=title]{{\normalsize Review Article}\\[3pt] Cross-view identity in image-based fruit counting: A systematic review and a design space for class-wise inventories}
\author[1]{Muhammad Zainal Muttaqin}
\author[1]{Fatma Indriani}
\cormark[1]
\affiliation[1]{organization={Department of Computer Science, Universitas Lambung Mangkurat},
  city={Banjarbaru},
  country={Indonesia}}
\cortext[1]{Corresponding author. \textit{E-mail address:} \texttt{f.indriani@ulm.ac.id} (F. Indriani).}
\begin{abstract}
Photographing a plant from several sides can reveal previously hidden fruit and record the same fruit more than once. Misses and duplicates can offset each other, and maturity errors can shift fruit between classes while leaving the total unchanged. This systematic review examines how vision systems use repeated observations to estimate crop load or build inventories of individual fruit by class.
With the same detector across three apple datasets, summing counts from opposite sides gave 104--148\% of the harvest count, and merging the two sides in three dimensions gave 96--98\%. On 30 strawberry videos, changing how tracks were converted into counts reversed the ranking of two trackers by count error. In a tomato video test, mean count error was 6.6\% overall and 17.3\% for unripe fruit.
Continuous video provides motion cues for tracking. Independently captured views require cues that remain comparable across camera positions. Oil-palm annotations link bunches across photographs of the same palm. Automatic matching of bunches across separately photographed sides remains unvalidated in the studies reviewed. A field count and linked, class-labelled observations of the same plants can establish how much of the crop was visible, which fruit were missed or counted twice, and how often class labels were wrong.
\end{abstract}
