\shortauthors{M.Z. Muttaqin and F. Indriani}
\title[mode=title]{{\normalsize Review Article}\\[3pt] Cross-view identity in image-based fruit counting: A systematic review and a design space for class-wise inventories}
\author[1]{Muhammad Zainal Muttaqin}
\author[1]{Fatma Indriani}
\cormark[1]
\affiliation[1]{organization={Department of Computer Science, Universitas Lambung Mangkurat},
  city={Banjarbaru},
  country={Indonesia}}
\cortext[1]{Corresponding author. \textit{E-mail address:} \texttt{f.indriani@ulm.ac.id} (F. Indriani).}
\begin{abstract}
A camera that travels along a crop row or photographs a plant from several sides sees some fruit repeatedly and misses others altogether. Yield estimates can agree with a harvest count when these errors cancel, even if the reconstructed inventory contains the wrong fruit. This systematic mapping review examines how vision systems turn repeated observations into records of individual fruit and assign a class to each record. A Scopus search through 28 September 2026, supplemented by targeted references, yielded \nIncluded included studies, of which \nMulti combined observations. The synthesis distinguishes six association mechanisms and interprets comparisons against the reference counts and acquisition conditions used in each study. In a mango orchard, fitted count-to-harvest slopes were 0.27 for a single view, 0.54 for opposed views, and 1.014 after tracking and triangulation. Across video studies, changing the rule that converts tracks into counts could reverse the ranking of trackers. Geometric merging resolved overlapping observations from opposite sides of rows when registration was sufficiently accurate. Per-class errors persisted even when the total was close to the reference, because missed fruit and transfers between adjacent maturity classes affect the inventory differently. In oil palm, linked annotations identify the same bunch in photographs from several sides, but the reviewed literature provides no validated method for linking detections across those sides. The resulting design space relates each route to the observations it requires and identifies the references needed to distinguish a useful crop-load estimate from a class-wise inventory.
\end{abstract}
