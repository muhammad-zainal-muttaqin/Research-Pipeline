\title{Multi-View and Multimodal Perception for Class-Wise Fruit Inventories: A Design-Space Review}
\author{Muhammad~Zainal~Muttaqin and Fatma~Indriani%
\thanks{Department of Computer Science, Universitas Lambung Mangkurat, Banjarbaru, Indonesia.}}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
Class-wise fruit inventories must convert repeated observations of a plant into
one record for each physical fruit, rather than simply sum detections from
independent images. This design-space review examines the evidence needed for
that conversion in agricultural fruit perception, with oil-palm fresh-fruit
bunches as the principal case and transferable mechanisms drawn from tracking,
re-identification, multi-view geometry, and multimodal perception. Four
findings organize the review. First, a strong per-image detector is necessary
for inventory but cannot by itself establish a persistent identity or prevent
duplicate counts. Second, six identity mechanisms span intra-view counting,
statistical correction, appearance matching, temporal tracking, geometric
association, and learned multi-view association; each succeeds only under
specific assumptions about camera motion, overlap, calibration, and object
appearance. Third, real-time RGB detection is a justified baseline, while
depth is a conditional geometric cue whose value depends on its physical
meaning, registration, and reliability; feature-level and quality-aware fusion
are therefore alternatives to test rather than established field solutions.
Fourth, direct agricultural evidence remains sparse for unique cross-view
inventories, particularly where class assignment, sensor failure, and duplicate
suppression must be evaluated together. The resulting framework separates what
is supported directly for agriculture from what transfers as a mechanism, and
defines the measurements needed to test duplicate-aware, class-wise fruit
inventories under field constraints.
\end{abstract}
