KEY: roy2019vision
CROP AND DATA: Apple (orchard rows, fruit trees viewed from both sides of the row). Test data: three datasets (Dataset1, Dataset3, Dataset4) with 270, 274 and 414 harvested apples; number of trees, videos and images: not stated in the portion read. Reference type: harvest (harvested fruit counts, Table 2, lines 1397-1412).
SAME-DATA COMPARISON: yes
C1 TYPE: MECHANISM
C1 ARMS: (a) Merged count: 3D reconstructions of the front and back sides of the row are merged, overlapping apples are removed by intersection area (inclusion-exclusion), then counted. (b) Sum of single-side counts: each side is counted independently and the two counts are added, with no overlap removal. Same three datasets and same harvest reference.
C1 RESULTS: Harvested-count ratio, as printed in Table 2 (lines 1397-1412). Dataset1 (harvest 270): merged 256 (94.81%); sum of single sides 348 (128.89%). Dataset3 (harvest 274): merged 252 (91.98%); sum 411 (150%). Dataset4 (harvest 414): merged 392 (94.68%); sum 422 (101.93%). Best on every dataset: merged count (closest to harvest). Text (lines 1335-1339) gives the same percentages: merged 94.81%, 91.98%, 94.68%; sum 128.89%, 150%, 101.93%.
C1 WHERE: Section 5.4.3 text (lines 1332-1342); Table 2 (lines 1397-1412); Figure 26 caption (lines 1413-1419).
C1 QUOTE: "It indicates that merging the rows from both sides is essential to obtain accurate yield." (lines 1340-1342)
C1 AUTHORS' EXPLANATION: The authors state that adding single-side counts overcounts significantly (lines 1337-1339), and that the merge computes the intersection area among connected components and adds or subtracts the weighted parts accordingly (lines 1332-1334). Overcounting arises because apples visible from both sides are counted twice. The authors do not state why the Dataset3 merged count (91.98%) is lower than the other two.
C1 CONDITIONS: Monocular camera moved along both sides of fruit tree rows; the reconstruction is 3D (Agisoft-type camera poses, lines 1215-1216 for the same software). Frame rate and lighting for the two-side sequences: not stated in the portion read.
C2 TYPE: MECHANISM
C2 ARMS: (a) Cross-frame merging by homography (pairwise homography between frames, approximating camera motion). (b) Cross-frame tracking using full 3D camera poses (reference). Same manually annotated fruits on the same frames; the authors compare the number of unique apples from each.
C2 RESULTS: Overcounting of homography arm relative to 3D-pose arm: "up to 8%" across datasets (line 1217-1218: "using homography can lead to 8% overcounting"). Per-dataset values are in Figure 22(a), which is not in the text read. The 3D-pose arm is the reference (0%). Best: 3D camera poses. No per-dataset number for this arm is given in the text.
C2 WHERE: Section 5.4.2 text (lines 1201-1219); Figure 22(a) (values not in text).
C2 QUOTE: "We found that across diﬀerent datasets; using homography can lead to 8% overcounting." (lines 1217-1218)
C2 AUTHORS' EXPLANATION: The authors attribute the difference to approximating camera motion with homography rather than full 3D camera motion (lines 1209-1211). They state that the 3D poses are used "to avoid double counting" (lines 1205-1207). Their stated purpose of the comparison is to quantify the overcounting due to the approximation (lines 1209-1211).
C2 CONDITIONS: Same sequences as C1 datasets; camera motion computed with commercial photogrammetry software (lines 1215-1216). Frame rate: not stated in the portion read.
