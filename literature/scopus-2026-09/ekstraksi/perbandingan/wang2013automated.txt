KEY: wang2013automated
CROP AND DATA: apple (Red Delicious red block; Granny Smith green block), Sunrise Orchard, Washington State University, Sept 2011; tall-spindle planting; test data: red block 15 rows x ~48 trees (16 sections of 3 trees per row; 240 sections), green block 14 rows x ~48 trees (224 sections, lines 532-535, 593-594); text gives "about 480 trees" for the red block (abstract line 18, conclusion line 723), which does not match 15 rows x 48 trees (line 532-533); reference type: hand count on tree (professional orchard workers count fruit per flagged 3-tree section; system count per section is matched to flags in images, lines 533-538)
SAME-DATA COMPARISON: yes
C1 TYPE: MECHANISM
C1 ARMS: green Granny Smith block, 224 sections. Arm A: raw computer count (stereo detection, registration of both sides, no calibration). Arm B: raw count multiplied by a calibration factor of 1.4 from linear regression (intercept 0) on 10 random sections of the same block against hand counts (lines 593-599).
C1 RESULTS: row-level mean error, raw count: -29.8% (SD 8.1%) (line 589). Row-level mean error, calibrated (x1.4): 1.8% (SD 11.7%) (lines 600-601). Whole-block error, calibrated: 1.2% (line 604). Best arm: calibrated (Arm B) on both mean error and whole-block error; raw count has the larger systematic undercount. Calibration factor: average 1.4, SD 0.1, over 100 random draws of 10 sections (lines 596-598).
C1 WHERE: Section 5.2, text lines 588-607; Fig. 10 (lines 608, 626-627, regression "y = 1.4x"); Fig. 11 (line 653).
C1 QUOTE: "Using 1.4 as a calibration factor to correct the yield estimation in the green apple block, the average yield estimation error at row level falls to 1.8% with a standard deviation of 11.7%" (lines 599-601)
C1 AUTHORS' EXPLANATION: the undercount is attributed to occlusion by foliage: "The occlusion caused by foliage is thought to be the main reason for the undercount of green apples" (lines 590-591). The authors state the error is consistent enough to calibrate: "the error is relatively consistent, making calibration for the raw counts possible" (lines 592-593).
C1 CONDITIONS: green apples in dense foliage; night acquisition with ring-flash lighting; vehicle speed 0.25 m/s, images taken at 1 Hz (lines 81, 118-124); both sides of each row scanned and registered (lines 126-127, 487-513). Calibration factor is fitted on sections from the same block that is then evaluated, so the calibrated error is an in-sample result (lines 593-596, 599-601); the text does not report a held-out evaluation.
C2 TYPE: MECHANISM
C2 ARMS: red Red Delicious rows 11-15 (untinned, large clusters), same block. Arm A: raw computer count. Arm B: raw count multiplied by a calibration factor of 1.7 found by the same method (lines 605-606).
C2 RESULTS: row-level mean error, raw count: -41.3% (line 550), with SD 3.2% (line 557). Row-level mean error, calibrated (x1.7): 0.4% (SD 5.5%) (lines 606-607). Best arm: calibrated (Arm B). Rows 1-10 (thinned) raw count error: -3.2% for 10 rows together, mean -2.9% with SD 7.1% per row (lines 546-549); this is not a second arm, because rows 1-10 and rows 11-15 differ in thinning, not in method.
C2 WHERE: Section 5.2, text lines 545-559 (raw rows 11-15) and lines 605-607 (calibrated rows 11-15); Fig. 9 (lines 560-587, counts per row, no numbers printed in the extracted text); Fig. 11 (line 653 caption for the green block only).
C2 QUOTE: "We apply the same method to rows 11-15 in the red apple block, and find a calibration factor of 1.7." (lines 605-606)
C2 AUTHORS' EXPLANATION: undercount is attributed to clusters: "some apples are invisible and cannot be detected due to the occlusion caused by other apples nearby" and "some fruit clusters consist of more than two apples, and the current version of software can only split a cluster into two apples" (lines 552-556).
C2 CONDITIONS: unthinned trees with large apple clusters; red apples with good visibility in rows 1-10 (lines 545-553). Same in-sample calibration caveat as C1 (factor from the same rows, lines 605-607).
NOTES: (1) The text says the red block is "about 480 trees" (lines 18, 723), while line 532-533 gives 15 rows of about 48 trees, which is about 720 trees; the text does not reconcile these numbers.
