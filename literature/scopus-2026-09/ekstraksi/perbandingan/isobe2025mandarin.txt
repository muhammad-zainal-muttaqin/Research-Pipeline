CROP AND DATA: Mandarin (Citrus reticulata) trees on a commercial farm in Hiroshima region, Japan, data from two years and several farms (lines 994-996). Videos captured by RGB camera around the perimeter of each tree; frames sampled from video. Test data: trees A to F in Table 8 and Table 9 and the test set in Tables 5-7 (number of test trees not extracted in this pass; not stated in the lines read). Reference: hand count of fruits on the tree (ground truth counts, Table 8 "Actual Counts"); the reference type is not further described in the lines read.

SAME-DATA COMPARISON: yes

C1 TYPE: ACQUISITION (number of views: one image versus two images versus the proposed multi-image method)
C1 ARMS: (a) Single image method, reference [5] (Chen et al., "Counting apples and oranges with deep learning"), re-implemented by the authors on their dataset (lines 681-683); one image per tree. (b) Two-images method, reference [14], re-implemented by the authors on their dataset (lines 681-683); two images per tree. (c) Proposed method: multiple frames from the video, selected by frame number, with a Masked Autoencoder pre-trained temporal Swin Transformer and WeakL1Loss (Table 7 row "proposed"). The arms differ in how many views of the tree are used.
C1 RESULTS (Table 7, lines 884-896; metrics MAE and MAPE in %):
- 1 image [5]: MAE 632.28; MAPE 104.18.
- 2 images [14]: MAE 251.88; MAPE 43.70.
- Proposed: MAE 189.56; MAPE 26.12.
- Best on both metrics: proposed method (lowest MAE and lowest MAPE).
C1 WHERE: Table 7, lines 884-896; re-implementation statement lines 681-683; conclusion lines 1015-1019.
C1 QUOTE: "This significantly outperforms existing methods that rely on a single image or just two images." (lines 1018-1019)
C1 AUTHORS' EXPLANATION: The authors attribute the advantage to multiple images taken from different viewing angles, which let the model count mandarins that are obscured by leaves from specific angles (lines 1015-1017). The text does not give a separate explanation for why the two-image method beats the single-image method.
C1 CONDITIONS: Mandarin trees photographed from the perimeter with enough space between trees (lines 1001-1007). Frame-selection and training settings for the proposed row are as in Table 6 (lines 850-883). Table 7 gives no frame rate, camera model or number of test trees in the lines read.

C2 TYPE: MECHANISM (frame selection by frame number versus by accumulated SSIM difference, used to pick the frames that feed the count model)
C2 ARMS: (a) Frame Number Based: frames selected at fixed frame intervals. (b) Cumulative Sum Based on SSIM: frames selected when the accumulated SSIM difference between adjacent frames reaches a threshold, which the authors designed for non-uniform camera speed (lines 909-912). Same model in both arms; the arms differ only in frame selection.
C2 RESULTS (Table 6, lines 850-883; MAE / MAPE in %):
- Frame Number Based, without MAE pre-training, L1 loss: MAE 208.70; MAPE 28.63.
- Frame Number Based, without MAE pre-training, WeakL1 loss: MAE 223.88; MAPE 30.81.
- Frame Number Based, with MAE pre-training, L1 loss: MAE 194.02; MAPE 27.08.
- Frame Number Based, with MAE pre-training, WeakL1 loss: MAE 189.56; MAPE 26.12.
- Cumulative Sum Based on SSIM, without MAE pre-training, L1 loss: MAE 216.03; MAPE 30.90.
- Cumulative Sum Based on SSIM, without MAE pre-training, WeakL1 loss: MAE 238.97; MAPE 33.19.
- Cumulative Sum Based on SSIM, with MAE pre-training, L1 loss: MAE 195.99; MAPE 27.86.
- Cumulative Sum Based on SSIM, with MAE pre-training, WeakL1 loss: MAE 190.38; MAPE 26.74.
- Best under every loss and pre-training setting on MAE and MAPE: Frame Number Based (lowest MAE and MAPE in all four pairs). The gap is small with pre-training (MAE 189.56 versus 190.38 for WeakL1) and larger without it (MAE 208.70 versus 216.03 for L1).
- Per-tree absolute error (Table 9, lines 1028-1042; column order as printed, frame number first, SSIM second; the mapping is confirmed by lines 917-920): Tree D 198.68 (frame number) versus 60.11 (SSIM); Tree E 86.96 versus 87.05; Tree F 90.69 versus 204.77. Best per tree: SSIM for Tree D, frame number for Tree F, almost identical for Tree E.
C2 WHERE: Table 6, lines 850-883; Table 9, lines 1028-1042; text lines 906-931.
C2 QUOTE: "experimental results indicate that, overall, the frame-number-based sampling approach performed slightly better than the accumulative difference-based approach." (lines 907-909)
C2 AUTHORS' EXPLANATION: The accumulated-difference method was proposed on the assumption that non-constant camera speed would otherwise select many frames during slow movements (lines 909-911). The authors say it may be less effective with constant camera speed and with vertical camera movements (lines 913-916). Tree D, where the SSIM method was better, has intricate curves and slower movement at those points; Tree F, where the frame-number method was better, has a less complex trajectory with generally faster movement (lines 922-927). The authors conclude the SSIM method suits non-uniform camera movement (lines 928-930).
C2 CONDITIONS: Camera trajectories estimated with COLMAP (Fig. 16, line 921-922). Only three trees are compared in Table 9, and the per-tree absolute errors are single values with no repeats or variance stated.
C2 NOTES: Table 9 reports per-tree absolute errors on three trees, while Table 6 reports averages over the test set; the two do not describe the same quantity. The sum of the three Table 9 values is 376.33 for frame number and 351.93 for SSIM, so the per-tree sums do not match the statement that frame number is better overall. The statement in the text refers to Table 6. Column mapping of Table 9 is read from the printed order; the text confirms it through the tree-by-tree winners (lines 917-920).
