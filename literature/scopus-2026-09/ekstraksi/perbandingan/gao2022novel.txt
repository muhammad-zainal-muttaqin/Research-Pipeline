CROP AND DATA: Apple (orchard trees, single-sided row, videos captured by a remote-control vehicle). Test data: 5 random orchard videos for the comparison (lines 765-767). Reference type: manually labeled fruit IDs and real counts measured on each video (lines 550-558).
SAME-DATA COMPARISON: yes
C1 TYPE: TRACKER
C1 ARMS: (a) Proposed method: CSR-DCF single-object tracking of the trunk, with minimum Euclidean distance matching after reference displacement, on YOLOv4-tiny fruit detections. (b) Baseline: Deep SORT (tracking-by-detection with Kalman filter, Hungarian assignment, motion plus appearance metric), using the same YOLOv4-tiny network trained in this research for detection (lines 761-764). The arms differ in the multi-object tracker and its association metric.
C1 RESULTS: Counting accuracy: proposed method 91.60% average over 5 random videos (line 766-767); Deep SORT: not stated as a number, described only as "serious over-counting" (line 768). Best arm: proposed method. Speed: proposed method 2 to 5 fps on CPU; Deep SORT less than 2 fps (lines 780-781). Per-video values are shown only in Fig. 10a and are not stated in the text.
C1 WHERE: Fig. 10a (same videos, lines 765-766); Fig. 10b (tracking failure example, line 779); text lines 761-781; Section 3 overview lines 554-558.
C1 QUOTE: "The proposed method has an average counting accuracy of 91.60% for 5 random videos, while the Deep Sort algorithm has serious over-counting." (lines 766-768)
C1 AUTHORS' EXPLANATION: The authors attribute Deep SORT's failure to the target type and motion: fruit is much smaller than pedestrians or vehicles and has no obvious appearance difference between individuals; the remote-control vehicle produced irregular speed, so the motion-and-appearance metric "basically unable to provide an effective function of fruit counting" and "leads to the failure of fruit tracking" (lines 768-779).
C1 CONDITIONS: Videos captured by a remote-control vehicle moving at varying speed in an orchard row; the authors state that Deep SORT works for pedestrians and vehicles but not for apples (lines 768-779). Frame rate and video count beyond the 5 videos are not stated.
NOTES: The text says the proposed method "can be implemented on CPU at 2 ~ 5 fps" (lines 780-781), which is a speed result on the same platform only as far as the text states; the Deep SORT speed is stated as an upper bound ("less than 2 fps"). The Deep SORT numbers are not printed in the text, so the size of the gap is available only from Fig. 10a, which was not extracted.
