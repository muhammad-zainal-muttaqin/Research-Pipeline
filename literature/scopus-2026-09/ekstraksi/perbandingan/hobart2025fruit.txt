CROP AND DATA: Apple (Gala and Jonaprince), one orchard in Brandenburg, Germany, two seasons (2018 and 2020). Two UAV flights per season at 7.5 m and 10 m altitude, low-cost RGB, photogrammetric point clouds (Metashape). Ten tree rows; rows excluded from analysis: row 10 (both years), row 5 (2020), leaving three rows for count and yield models (lines 377-397). Reference: yield mass from orchard block harvesting (lines 581-582). The reference for the apple count models is not stated in lines 407-411.

The arms differ only in flight altitude of the image acquisition: 7.5 m versus 10 m. Each arm is evaluated against the same harvest-based block counts and yield masses.

C1 QUOTE: "In summary, it can be stated that according to the results presented here, a point cloud generated from 10 m altitude imagery leads to less dispersed results in apple fruit position and better apple mass estimation compared to that generated from the 7.5 m point cloud." (lines 527-529)
C1 AUTHORS' EXPLANATION: At 7.5 m the point cloud has a higher dispersion of apple positions across the rows (lines 373-376, 518-521). Dispersion produces false positives or larger clusters that are split during processing, which explains the extra apples found at 7.5 m (lines 518-521). Table A1 shows that the 7.5 m reconstruction needed higher reprojection error margins than the 10 m reconstruction (lines 522-524). The authors say the count models are "similar quality" at both altitudes (line 516) and that the 7.5 m yield model is weaker (lines 524-526).
C1 CONDITIONS: Single orchard, two seasons, three tree rows per model after exclusions. Last-row and 2020 row-5 effects are described as one-sided and possibly caused by a flight offset (lines 390-397). The 2020 7.5 m flight produced an empty result because the dense cloud contained superimposed clouds (lines 570-573), so this arm has no count or yield result for 2020 in the Conclusions. Ambient light was not investigated (lines 559-561). Fit statistics are R2 of block-wise linear models; no significance test is stated.
