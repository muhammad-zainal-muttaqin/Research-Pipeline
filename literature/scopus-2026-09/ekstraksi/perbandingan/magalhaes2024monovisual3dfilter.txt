C1 QUOTE: "performed worse than square kernels, and the results of weighted and geometric centres are identical, despite the weighted method tending to have a lower error for the same standard deviation." (lines 749-750)
C1 AUTHORS' EXPLANATION: In simulation with noise, the square kernel "rejects some state space positions and can never be recovered", while the Gaussian "has a smooth behaviour" so points "can recover" (lines 756-759). The square kernel also "failed many times to detect the fruits" in the noisy simulation, and the Gaussian was "more failure-prone" for that reason in the simulation (lines 760-761). For the testbed the authors write only that "against expectations, the Gaussian kernels were not very effective and square kernels reached a higher accuracy" (lines 765-767), and do not give a cause; they say the error source (filter or ground-truth baseline) is not identified (lines 769-771).
C1 CONDITIONS: Testbed viewpoints about 0.5 m from the fruits (line 772); YOLO v8 tiny detector on an OAK-1 camera, bounding boxes used as input (lines 255-257); 2% of simulated bounding boxes failed in the noisy simulation (line 667); about 1 min per pose on an Intel Core i7 with 8 GB RAM, about 99 s/pose (square) and 115 s/pose (Gaussian) in simulation (lines 896-897, 911-913). Frame rate and other camera settings: not stated.

C2 AUTHORS' EXPLANATION: The authors say weighted estimation helps because "the positions near the centre of the tomato have a bigger score, using weighted centre estimation procedures allows for a better fit of the estimated centre to the real centre of the tomato, reducing the eﬀect and deviations of sparse clouds" (lines 762-764). For the testbed they say the choice is irrelevant (lines 766-767) and say the two centre estimators are identical (line 749-750). The authors give no separate explanation for the testbed numbers.

NOTES: (1) Table II column order is not labelled in the extracted text. The order used here is the one that matches the header sequence (Square, Square, Gaussian, Gaussian; weighted, geometric, weighted, geometric). The Euclidean row for Gaussian-geometric (0.0204 m) is lower than Gaussian-weighted (0.0222 m), but the MSE, RMSE, MAE and MAPE of that column are the worst of the four; the Euclidean row is inconsistent with the other rows under this order. The column mapping was not verified against the figure. (3) Lines 762-764 say weighted centre estimation gives a better fit, and lines 766-767 say the choice is "always irrelevant". The text does not reconcile these. (4) Lines 708-709 say the Gaussian performed worse than the square "against the expected"; line 668-669 says the Gaussian was more accurate than the square in the noisy simulation. The comparison is reversed between simulation and testbed, which the text does not discuss beyond lines 765-767. (5) The MiDaS comparison (lines 820-832, Figs. 15-16) was considered and excluded. MiDaS is a single-image monocular depth network with a linear calibration, applied to Figs. 8g-8i, and the filter's error on those same images is not printed. The "errors up to 10 cm" (line 830) are residuals of the calibration fit in Fig. 16b (line 880), not localisation errors against ground truth, so no same-data quantitative pair exists for that arm. (6) The 0.5 m viewpoint distance and simulated fruit spheres differ from the testbed; simulation and testbed results are not directly comparable.
