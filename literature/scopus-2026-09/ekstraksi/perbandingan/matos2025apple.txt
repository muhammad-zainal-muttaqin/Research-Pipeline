CROP AND DATA: apple (espaliered trees, orchard videos); test data: 3 datasets, 14 trees in total (Galafab-west 3 trees, Schnico-Red-east 5 trees, Schniga-Schnico-west 6 trees; Table 1, lines 352-364); 233, 356 and 373 ground-truth apples respectively (Table 1). Reference type: ground truth from manual annotation of all fruits in the videos and manual "by hand" tracking (lines 341-346).
SAME-DATA COMPARISON: yes

C1 TYPE: MECHANISM
C1 ARMS: (a) Centroid re-projection, the authors' algorithm: only the 3D centroid of each fruit is re-projected onto each frame (Table 2, lines 399-416; method lines 209-212). (b) Full-point re-projection, the algorithm of [8] as implemented by the authors: all 3D points of each fruit are re-projected and matched with a Hungarian assignment (Table 3, lines 417-434; method lines 201-208, 452-453). Both arms use the same videos, the same YOLOv4 detections and the same ZED 2i stereo pose and depth (lines 201-208, 213-215). The arms differ only in what is re-projected per fruit (centroid vs all points).
C1 RESULTS: Estimated count / APE (%) / MOTA, as printed.
  Galafab-west (GT 233): centroid 267 / 14.59 / 0.32; full-point 193 / 17.17 / 0.49. Best APE: centroid (14.59). Best MOTA: full-point (0.49).
  Schnico-Red-east (GT 356): centroid 286 / 19.66 / 0.14; full-point 248 / 30.34 / 0.25. Best APE: centroid (19.66). Best MOTA: full-point (0.25).
  Schniga-Schnico-west (GT 373): centroid 586 / 57.10 / 0.27; full-point 395 / 5.90 / 0.42. Best APE: full-point (5.90). Best MOTA: full-point (0.42).
  APE was recomputed from the printed counts and the GT counts in Table 1 (Eq. 2, lines 383-390); all six values match the printed APE.
  Overall: full-point re-projection has the higher MOTA on all three datasets; centroid re-projection has the lower APE on the first two datasets only.

C1 QUOTE: "Our algorithm (Table 2) achieves slightly better results in terms of total fruit count on the first two datasets, but produces a much worse count in the third dataset due to over-counting, compared to the algorithm proposed by [8]." (lines 446-448)
C1 AUTHORS' EXPLANATION: Full-point re-projection is more robust: "re-projecting all the points, rather than only the centroids, makes the system more robust to errors generated by the object detector and the depth estimated by stereo." (lines 442-444). The higher MOTA "indicate[s] more stable tracks" (lines 444-445). Reason given: the full point set "reinforces the correct matches frame after frame" with a Hungarian assignment (lines 452-455). Centroid tracking "tends to lose track of a fruit more easily and thus a new tracking 'object' is created for the same fruit, which ends up being counted several times" (lines 456-459), which explains the over-count on Schniga-Schnico-west. The authors attribute that dataset's larger centroid error to "noise in the camera extrinsics estimated in real-time" from visual odometry (lines 449-451).
C1 CONDITIONS: Datasets are the ones of [8] with erratic camera movement, low frame rates and intermittent occlusions (lines 347-351). Stereo camera ZED 2i with ZED SDK pose and depth, not SfM (lines 201-208). Detector YOLOv4 for mature apples (line 366). Frame rate not stated in the passage read.
NOTES: (1) The classification as MECHANISM is a judgment: both arms are association mechanisms of the same pipeline (centroid vs full-point re-projection with Hungarian matching); the paper does not call them trackers. (2) The text gives no MOTA or APE values; the values are from Tables 2 and 3. (3) Table 2 and Table 3 do not state the frame rate or the detector confidence threshold. (4) The authors' own conclusion ("re-projecting all the points ... makes the system more robust", lines 442-445) is not matched by APE, which favours the centroid arm on two of three datasets. (5) The full-point arm is the authors' reimplementation of [8] with ZED SDK in place of COLMAP SfM (lines 201-208, 463-464), so it is not the original [8] pipeline.
