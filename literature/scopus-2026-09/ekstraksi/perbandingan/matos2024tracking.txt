C1 AUTHORS' EXPLANATION: Ours is best because it "never changes the bounding boxes provided as input", so precision is 1.00 (lines 734-737). DeepSORT's low precision is attributed to its changing the input detections, which the authors read as a sign that its learned feature descriptor is unreliable for this image domain (lines 739-743). ByteTrack's low APE on Synthetic-apples-1 is attributed to that dataset having "only horizontal movement", while on the other datasets "the camera moves freely and the frame rate is lower" (lines 751-755). The lower Ours result on Galafab-west is attributed to identities swapped or lost for fruits on the ground that enter and leave the frame (lines 758-764). Ours' high MOTA is read as following "each unique fruit with a a high accuracy" (lines 765-772).

Counts in the # apples column are after the filter of tracks with fewer than 5 detections, applied to all trackers (lines 718-727). MOTA is computed on raw data (lines 725-727). The threshold of 5 "was determined experimentally for the presented datasets and may need to be adjusted for very different scenarios" (lines 788-791).
C2 CONDITIONS: Ground truth detections as input, so the tracking errors and spurious detections counted here come from the tracker, not from a detector (lines 778-782). The threshold 5 was chosen on these datasets (lines 788-791). The filter is applied to the ground truth as well (lines 720-727).
NOTES: The text says the filtered APE is better "in most cases" (line 778). Tables 2-5 show filtered lower in 11 of 16 tracker-dataset pairs, and all four DeepSORT pairs are higher after filtering. The text does not mention the DeepSORT pattern.

C3 AUTHORS' EXPLANATION: ByteTrack's APE falls as recall drops "due to its tendency to overestimate the number of fruits" (lines 851-853).
