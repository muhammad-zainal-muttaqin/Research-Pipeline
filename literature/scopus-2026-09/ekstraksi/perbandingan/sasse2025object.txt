KEY: sasse2025object
CROP AND DATA: grape (Vitis vinifera, table grape) in pergola-trained vineyards, Sao Francisco Valley, Brazil; test data: 1 video of 5 seconds from a separate recording (not in training), 74 clusters ground truth (lines 487-490, 519-522); training/validation data: 445 annotated frames from 4 videos, augmented to 1,069 frames, split 88/8/4 (lines 352-365); reference type: annotated video (ground-truth cluster count by CVAT video annotation, lines 489-490)
SAME-DATA COMPARISON: yes
C1 TYPE: COUNTING-RULE
C1 ARMS: (a) counting by unique tracker IDs (every track ID assigned during the video is counted) vs (b) counting with a predefined counting line (a cluster is counted only when its bounding box crosses the line). Both arms use the same YOLOv8s detections and the same ByteTrack tracks on the same 5-second video; only the counting rule differs (lines 481-485, 519-527).
C1 RESULTS: MAE (counting by unique IDs) = 20 (stated as 27% above the actual value of 74); MAE (counting using a predefined line) = 53 (stated as 71.62% below the actual value). Best arm on MAE: unique IDs (20 vs 53). No other metric is reported per arm for this comparison.
C1 WHERE: Section V RQ2 results, lines 519-527; Fig. 6 (line counting heatmap, lines 515-518); Fig. 7 (both methods shown on the same video, lines 542-544, 553-554); abstract lines 26-28 (final count 27% above actual value).
C1 QUOTE: "On the other hand, the counting line—a technique used in previous works [10] to prevent double counting—results in a severe underestimation, as many grape clusters never cross the line due to the vineyard's perspective." (lines 531-534)
C1 AUTHORS' EXPLANATION: The authors attribute the unique-ID overestimate to the lack of a fusion mechanism: "Relying solely on the number of unique identifiers associated with tracks leads to an overestimation of grape clusters, indicating the need for a fusion mechanism to improve accuracy." (lines 528-530). They attribute the counting-line underestimate to perspective: many clusters never cross the line because of the vineyard's perspective (lines 531-534). They choose unique IDs as "the most practical approach given our specific camera position" (lines 539-541), and state that the line position was arbitrarily defined from the detection heatmap (lines 481-485).
C1 CONDITIONS: handheld smartphone video (iPhone XR, 26 mm lens, 1080p resized to 640x640) moving irregularly along pergola corridors (lines 332-333, 355-358, 366-368, 567-570); people in the scene and dense foliage cause partial occlusion (lines 510-513); single run only (lines 491-494); GPU NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060 (lines 495-496); frame rate not stated.
NOTES: (1) The MAE is computed on one 5-second video with one ground-truth count, so each MAE equals the absolute difference of a single count (20 and 53); the paper reports no variance and no repetition (lines 491-494). (2) The paper states that it uses the same tracking (ByteTrack) for both counting arms, so the comparison isolates the counting rule; the TRACKER and COMPONENT dimensions are not compared. (3) Detection-only results (precision 71%, recall 68.9%, F1 69.9%, mAP@50 75.1%, lines 499-503) are a detector evaluation on 88 validation images and are excluded. (4) Line 487 states a validation set of 88 images; the Dataset section gives an 8% validation split of 1,069 frames (lines 363-365), which is about 85 frames; the paper does not reconcile the two numbers. (5) The text says the counting line was used "in previous works [10]" (line 532), and Section III says Shen et al. [10] also used a counting line (lines 294-296) - consistent.
