NOTES:
1. Table 2 ID switch change: the text states "The number of ID switches reduced by 46.77%" (line 555). From Table 2 values 15 and 8, the reduction is (15-8)/15 = 46.67%. The printed 46.77% does not match.
2. Abstract vs Table 6: the abstract gives the apple total as 310 predicted against 292 actual. Table 6 gives 292 as SORT's prediction and 354 as gt; 310 is Cascade-SORT's prediction. The abstract's "292 actual" conflicts with Table 6.
3. Text vs Table 4 and Table 5 at lines 604-606: the text says "the ID switches of SORT increased less after detector performance degeneration" than Cascade-SORT. Table 4 shows SORT ID switches going from 15 (strong) to 14 (weak), a decrease, while Cascade-SORT goes from 8 to 12, an increase. Table 5 shows SORT going from 26 to 34 (+8) and Cascade-SORT from 20 to 33 (+13). The table values do not support the claim that SORT increased less in either table.
4. Section 4.4 (lines 596-599) states the detector improvement "particularly in reducing ID switches, miss counting, and false positives." Table 4 shows an exception: SORT weak had 14 ID switches against 15 for SORT strong (lines 599-601 acknowledge this).
5. Lines 849-855 describe a DeepSORT arm with pedestrian-trained ReID on the fruit data, reported as "worse than SORT and more ID switches." No table or numbers for that arm are given in the extracted text, so it is not recorded as a comparison.
6. Table 4 and Table 5 column labels ("Counting", "gt", "Prediction") are not separated in the extracted text; the values are reported as printed.
