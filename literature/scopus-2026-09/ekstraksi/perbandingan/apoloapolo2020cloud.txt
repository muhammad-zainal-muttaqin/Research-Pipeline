C1 TYPE: ACQUISITION
C1 ARMS: (a) Field visual count, both sides of the canopy, by a person on the tree (Nfcvt + Nfcvm + Nfcvun, Table 1) vs (b) number of fruits counted on the UAV nadir images of the same 19 trees (Figure 9). Both arms are regressed against the same harvested fruit count. The arms differ in viewpoint and in who counts: ground-level two-sided visual count vs zenithal UAV view.
C1 RESULTS: Arm (a) field visual count vs harvested fruits: R2 = 0.86 (Figure 8 text, line 573). MAE and RMSE for arm (a) are "high" but no values are given in the text. Arm (b) image count vs harvested fruits: R2 = 0.80 (Figure 9 text, line 586). MAE and RMSE for arm (b) are not stated in the results section. Best arm on R2: (a) field visual count (0.86 vs 0.80). The authors describe 0.80 as "a low correlation" (line 587).
C1 WHERE: Figure 8 (lines 783-786), Figure 9 (lines 839-841), Table 1 (lines 612-781), results text lines 571-596
C1 QUOTE: "An R2 value of 0.86 was obtained, which indicates a good correlation for both numerical variables." (lines 573-574) and "An R2 value of 0.80 can be observed, which indicates a low correlation." (lines 585-587)
C1 AUTHORS' EXPLANATION: For arm (a), the authors attribute the weak fit (high MAE and RMSE) to losses during counting with a fruit grading machine, which does not detect small fruit; they suggest manual counting would improve the fit (lines 576-581). For arm (b), the authors say the UAV-detected count "is insufficient for estimating the rest of the fruits present in the canopy tree" because invisible fruit lies under foliage or behind other fruit, and only about 27.31% of fruit is on the top of the tree (lines 553-556, 587-590). They also state the human eye is "the most accurate method" as the premise (lines 582-584), which the image result did not confirm.
C1 CONDITIONS: Nadir UAV images at 15 m altitude (line 234; the Methods also state a flying altitude of 10 m at line 222, see NOTES); 19 trees only; harvest reference counted by a grading machine; the 2018 UAV flight covered only part of the trees because the rest had been harvested (lines 241-243); the two arms were from different dates (field count and harvest in 2018 per line 232-233, pictures from the same flight; not further specified).
