KEY: anderson2021estimation
CROP AND DATA: mango (Mangifera indica), Australian orchards (NT, north/central/south Queensland; Walkamin trial for canopy work). Test data: about 20 orchards per season in 2019-2020 (20 orchards in Table 2 and Figure 6) and 20 orchards in 2020-2021 (Figure 7 and Table 2 list orchards 1-43); 18 manually counted trees per orchard for the CAL method; 3-day-old harvest-season packhouse records as reference. Reference type: packhouse (harvest) fruit counts per orchard; manual on-tree counts for CAL and occlusion factor.
SAME-DATA COMPARISON: yes

C1 TYPE: MECHANISM
C1 ARMS: MV-Raw (multi-view machine vision count, no correction) vs MV-CAL (same multi-view count multiplied by an occlusion factor regressed from the manual count of 18 calibration trees per orchard). Same orchards, same 2019-2020 video. Difference: regression/calibration factor applied or not.
C1 RESULTS: 2019-2020, AVG (9 orchards) APE/PE as printed in Table 2 text: MV-Raw 11.1 (b); MV-CAL 16.9 (ab); CAL 12.6 (ab); FARM 25.7 (a). STD (9 orchards): MV-Raw 11.4; MV-CAL 9.7. Best on the average: MV-Raw (11.1). Best on the SD: MV-CAL (9.7). Statistical letters: MV-Raw and CAL share the 'b'/'ab' group; no pairwise test result for MV-Raw vs MV-CAL is stated beyond the letters.
C1 WHERE: Table 2 (2019-2020 block, text lines 791-800); Section 3.2.1 text lines 913-920; abstract figures (26, 13, 11, 17% and SD 11, 8, 11, 9%).
C1 QUOTE: "The use of an occlusion correction factor calculated from the human count of the calibration trees did not improve the orchard load estimate in comparison to the packhouse count in 16 of 20 orchards in the 2019–2020 season, and MV-CAL estimates demonstrated higher variability than the MV-Raw estimates (Table 2)." (lines 913-916)
C1 AUTHORS' EXPLANATION: The correction factor from the CAL trees "was not representative of the whole orchard" (lines 916-918). The authors then recommend "Use of the multi-view machine vision estimate without correction" (lines 918-919). Conclusions repeat this: the APE "was generally lower 'as-is' than when corrected" because "the trees sampled for estimation of the occlusion factor did not adequately represent the orchard" (lines 1017-1021).
C1 CONDITIONS: Night-time LED imaging at 10 fps, camera about 2 m from canopy (lines 232-233); orchards with open canopies only (lines 919-920). Occlusion factor from 18 trees per orchard (lines 238-240).
NOTES: The 2020-2021 season reverses the direction for the same two arms: C2 below. The text on lines 1017-1019 says the uncorrected APE was lower, which matches Table 2 averages. Two averages are given for the same 2019-2020 data (APE in text, PE/APE in table headers); numbers above are copied from the Table 2 rows as printed (lines 791-800).

C2 TYPE: MECHANISM
C2 ARMS: MV-Raw vs MV-CAL, 2020-2021 season, 6 orchards for which all methods were available (text line 967) and all 20 orchards for the APE sentence. Same data as C1 but a different season and orchard set.
C2 RESULTS: APE across all orchards in 2020-2021 (text): MV-Raw 17%; MV-CAL 23%; CAL 22%; FARM 27%. Lowest: MV-Raw (17%). For the subset of 6 orchards (Table 2, lines 893-907): AVG (6 orchards) MV-Raw 23.5a; MV-CAL 15.5a; CAL 16.8a; FARM 26.2a; STD (6 orchards) MV-Raw 11.6; MV-CAL 7.3. Best on the 6-orchard average: MV-CAL (15.5a). Text lines 971-973 also state the mean percentage error: "PE of 32 and 9% for MV-Raw and MV-CAL methods, respectively".
C2 WHERE: Text lines 965-973; Table 2 2020-2021 block (lines 893-912); Figure 7 caption (lines 962-964, slope 0.62, R2 0.97).
C2 QUOTE: "In this case, the average percentage error of the multi-view count was improved by use of a correction factor (PE of 32 and 9% for MV-Raw and MV-CAL methods, respectively) (Table 2)." (lines 971-973)
C2 AUTHORS' EXPLANATION: The under-estimation in 2020-2021 is attributed to "an extended flowering period such that a single machine vision assessment event ... failed to count later maturing fruit ... rather than to a change in the canopy occlusion factor between seasons" (lines 950-954). The correction helped because "Manual count-based estimates (CAL, MV-CAL) exceeded the machine vision estimate for orchards 1-16 as the manual count was made three weeks after the machine vision estimate" (lines 968-970).
CONDITIONS: Extended flowering period in 2020-2021 (lines 970-971, 949-952). Manual counts made three weeks after the machine vision estimate (lines 968-970).
NOTES: Table 2 2020-2021 block has no header row in the extracted text, so column order (FARM, CAL, MV-Raw, MV-CAL) is taken from the 2019-2020 block (lines 780-800) and the text (lines 965-966). The text says the 6-orchard comparison showed "no significant difference at a 95% confidence level between methods" (lines 966-968). The 2019-2020 and 2020-2021 directions differ: MV-CAL is worse in C1 and better in C2.
