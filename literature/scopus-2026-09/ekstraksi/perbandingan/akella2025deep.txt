NOTES: The paper compares three models (YOLOv11 with Kalman filter and Hungarian algorithm (KF-HA), custom CNN, YOLOv8) on the same test split for detection and ripeness classification metrics (Table I, lines 588-645). These arms differ in detector architecture, not in how observations of the same fruit are linked or counted, so none qualifies under the inclusion rule (detector-only comparison). The counting section (lines 706-743) describes two methods, frame-based summing of detections and line-crossing with Kalman/Hungarian tracking, and states that the line-crossing method "reduces duplicate counts and improves accuracy" (lines 738-741), and the conclusion says tracking "significantly improved counting accuracy" (lines 772-773). Neither statement is backed by a count, a counting-accuracy value or a per-arm number for either counting method; Figs. 4-6 are sample frames only. Because criterion (c) fails, no comparison is recorded. The text also gives no test-set size, so the number of coconuts, videos and images in the test split is not stated. The YOLOv11 arm combines KF-HA with a different detector from the YOLOv8 arm, so even the Table I comparison cannot isolate the tracking component.
