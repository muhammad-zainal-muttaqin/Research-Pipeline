C2 TYPE: COMPONENT
C2 ARMS: Full COLMAP SfM (sequential all-vs-all feature matching and 3D triangulation, lines 509-518) vs 2D feature association (SURF features, brute-force matching, RANSAC homography, lines 540-555). The authors state they "found that extracting 2D features from a frame i and from a small number of subsequent frames ... was enough to map the grape instance correspondences" (lines 537-539). Association mechanism differs; detector and video are the same per the text.

  COLMAP full SfM: "5 h of computation for videos of 500 to 600 full HD frames" (lines 515-517). Hardware: Intel Core i7 3.4 GHz, Nvidia GTX 950m, 16 GB memory (lines 517-518).
  2D feature + RANSAC: "3 frames per second" (line 555), CPU only, same video and hardware (lines 553-555).
  Best on run time: 2D feature + RANSAC arm. No accuracy comparison is reported between these two association methods.
C2 WHERE: Text only, lines 504-518 (COLMAP), lines 519-555 (2D features, Fig. 6 at lines 531-533).
C2 QUOTE: "Using this approach, even using brute force matching, we were able to reach, without particular optimizations and working only on CPU, 3 frames per second on the aforementioned video and hardware." (lines 553-555)
C2 AUTHORS' EXPLANATION: The COLMAP approach "requires considerable computational time and it is viable only for offline elaborations" (Fig. 6 caption, lines 531-532). The 2D approach is motivated because "a full SfM solution ... is not needed, since the kind of videos ... are simple walks without closed loops" (lines 521-535). The text also says the "computational costs increase exponentially with the number of frames" (lines 513-515).
C2 CONDITIONS: Videos are simple walks without closed loops, so each bunch appears in only a few consecutive frames (lines 534-536). Full HD frames (line 516). CPU only, no optimisation (line 554).

NOTES:
(1) Table 4 has no column headers in the extracted text. Labels above are assigned by order (MOTA, MOTP, MT, ML, IDsw, FM, Pr, Re; then TP, FP, FN, Dets, GT Dets, IDs, GT IDs, Yield est. Err). The assignment is confirmed by arithmetic: TP+FN = 721 and TP+FP = Dets in all four rows.
(2) Pr and Re in Table 4 do not match the counts in the same table. Using TP/Dets and TP/GT Dets: SfMTrack WGISD gives 83.1% and 47.7% (printed 91.304 and 52.427); DeepSort pseudo gives 70.9% and 52.7% (printed 85.634 and 63.662). The source of Pr/Re is not stated.
(3) MOTA as printed cannot be reproduced from the table's counts with GT = 721 and the formula at line 430 (MOTA = 1 - (FN+FP+IDsw)/GT). For SfMTrack WGISD this gives 37.3 (printed 46.741); for SfMTrack pseudo, 45.5 (printed 55.756). The GT used for MOTA is not stated.
(4) Text line 887-888 and line 993 say DeepSORT is "stronger" at track continuity. Table 4 shows DeepSORT has more ID switches (16 vs 5; 17 vs 9) and fewer fragmentations (19 vs 29; 18 vs 22). The claim is supported for FM and ML only.
(5) Text line 885-886 says pseudo-labels give "an increase of more than 10%" in MOTA. From Table 4, SfMTrack rises by 9.0 points (46.741 to 55.756) and DeepSort by 10.1 points (40.499 to 50.624).
(6) Caption line 901-902 says the SfM yield error "drops by 29%". Table 4 gives 38% to 9%, which is 29 percentage points (a relative drop of 76%).
(7) Table 4 tracker name "SfMTrack" does not say which SfM variant (COLMAP at lines 504-518 or the 2D-feature variant at lines 519-555) was used. Text line 601-605 describes replication of Santos et al. (2020). Not stated.
(8) Section 3.1 (Tables 2, 3) and Section 3.3-3.4 (Tables 5-7) compare detectors or mask refinement only, with association unchanged. They are excluded.
