KEY: wang2019mango (Wang, Walsh, Koirala 2019, Sensors 19, 2742)

CROP / SETTING / SCALE: Mango (Mangifera indica). Two orchards: Calypso (GE1) and Honey Gold (HG B), Groves Grown orchards. Canopy imaged as a hedge from a moving vehicle at about 5 km/h. About 30 frames per tree. Video frame shift about 20 px per frame; vertical speed assumed zero (Sections 2–3). Tree-level and orchard-level counts (21 trees in Table 2).

ACQUISITION: Video along a row, one side of the canopy. Basler acA2440-75um camera, 10 fps, 2448×2048 px, about 2 m from the canopy, LED panel lighting (Section 2). Compared with a dual-view method (two still images per tree, one per side) using the same rig (Section 2.4, Table 2). Stated: Stein et al. used 5 fps at a similar vehicle speed (Section 4).

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR: MangoYOLO (Koirala et al. 2019), input 1024×1024, confidence threshold 0.24, NMS 0.45 (Section 2). Calypso 2018 images: R² 0.988, RMSE 5.0, bias −4.0 per image. HoneyGold 110-frame video: R² 0.665, RMSE 2.1, bias 0.0 per image; average 31 fruit per frame with 1.4 false positives and 1.4 false negatives per frame (Section 3.1). Detection accuracy beyond these per-image figures: not stated.

ASSOCIATION METHOD: Kalman filter with state (x, y, vx, vy), process noise Q = 1.0, measurement noise R = 0.1, dt = 1 frame (Section 2.3, Eqs. 1–9). Hungarian assignment on Euclidean distance between predicted and detected centroids, gate 60 px (sweep 20–100 px in steps of 5; Section 3.2). Two-pass "improved" variant: multiple-to-one assignments resolved by the smaller cost (Section 2.3). Unobserved-frame limit 15: a track unobserved for more than 15 frames is ended, and a re-appearing fruit is linked to it (Section 3.2). Fruit with fewer than four continuous updates borrow speed from neighbours (Eq. 9). Global speed vx = 20 px/frame, vy = 0 for the first four frames. Counting rule: each track is one fruit; the total is the number of tracks (Section 2.3, Section 3.2). Appearance features: not stated. 3D, SfM, SLAM, or GNSS: not used (Section 4 states this).

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS: Vertical speed of the camera is zero (Section 2). Global speed of 20 px/frame is a fixed starting value for the first four frames (Section 2.3). Four continuous updates give a stable motion model (Section 2.3, lines 329–331). Canopy is managed so that fruit is visible on the external wall; the orchard used was described as "a 'difficult' case, with a high proportion of 'hidden' fruit" (Section 3.5). Harvest tally is used as the reference count for 21 trees (Table 2); whether it is complete is not stated.

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED: (a) same fruit in consecutive frames: addressed by Kalman tracking and Hungarian assignment across frames (Section 2.3). (b) track lost and restarted: addressed by the unobserved-frame limit of 15 and re-linking of re-appearing fruit (Section 3.2). The authors report that setting the limit to 0 gives 357 counts and 15 gives 191 against a human count of 192 (Section 3.2). A fruit out of view for more than 15 frames is still reported as double counted (Section 3.2, lines 457–459). (c) same fruit from both sides or different passes: a size filter excludes boxes narrower than 12 px or shorter than 15 px to reduce double counts from the far side (Section 2.3). Both-side double counting is discussed as a future issue for narrower canopies (Section 4). Not otherwise addressed.

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING: Not stated. No maturity classes are reported.

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL: Human count on the 110 video frames (192 fruit, Table 1). Harvest tally per tree for 21 trees (3286 fruit, Table 2). Per-image R², RMSE, and bias against hand-labelled images (Section 3.1). Per-tree RMSE after bias correction (RMSE-bc) and correction factor (harvest/MV count, Table 2). Identity metrics (MOTA, IDF1, HOTA, ID switches): not stated.

KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS:
- Calypso 2018 images: R² 0.988, RMSE 5.0, bias −4.0 per image (Section 3.1).
- HoneyGold 110-frame video: R² 0.665, RMSE 2.1, bias 0.0 per image (Section 3.1).
- Table 1 (110 frames, human 192): occlusion repeat 3 (1.5%); FN repeat 1 (0.5%); false prediction 15 (7.8%); total repeat 19 (9.9%); missed −14 (−7.3%); estimate 197 (102.6%).
- Table 2 (21 trees, harvest 3286): video tracking 2050 fruit (62.3% in table), RMSE-bc 18.0, correction factor 1.6. Dual-view 1322 fruit (40.2%), RMSE-bc 21.7, correction factor 2.5.
- Section 3.3: repeat 9.7%, miss 7.1%, and a net 2.6% difference (text figures differ from Table 1; see below).

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED:
- Hidden fruit behind a thick, dense canopy pruned to a hedge (Sections 3.5, 4).
- Fruit out of view for more than 15 frames is double counted (Section 3.2).
- False positive predictions: 15 of 197 in Table 1 (7.8%).
- Missed detections: 14 fruit (−7.3%) in Table 1.
- Dual-view estimate is sensitive to the correction factor (lines 107–109, section not checked).
- Registration errors and GNSS costs are noted as a problem for other methods, not tested here (Section 4).

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA: Video tracking vs dual-view imaging on the same 21 trees and harvest tally (Table 2, Section 3.5): correction factor 1.6 vs 2.5, RMSE-bc 18.0 vs 21.7. Tracking vs human count on the 110 frames (Table 1). Calypso and HoneyGold are different orchards and are not compared on the same data. Stein et al. (2016) is cited for frame rate only (Section 4); no numerical comparison on shared data.

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK: The orchard was a difficult case with hidden fruit. The method should be tested on narrower canopies, where double counting from both sides becomes a greater issue (Section 3.5, lines 638–646). A correction factor per tree or per image based on image content (e.g. foliage overlap) is proposed (Section 3.5). The video system does not allow localisation within the orchard or segmentation of trees; GNSS or row locators (e.g. barcodes) are suggested for orchard maps (Section 4). The method could be applied to apples and citrus; not tested (Section 4).
