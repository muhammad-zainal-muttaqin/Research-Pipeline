KEY: vijayakumar2023tree (Vijayakumar V. et al., 2023, "Tree-level citrus yield prediction utilizing ground and aerial machine vision and machine learning", Smart Agricultural Technology 3 (2023) 100077; full text at literature/scopus-2026-09/teks/vijayakumar2023tree.txt, reference list skipped)

CROP / SETTING / SCALE: Citrus, Hamlin oranges, Florida (Conclusion, lines 1780-1781). Experimental block of 48 trees, selected from more than 100 trees (lines 200-201). Harvest count taken one week after imaging (lines 190-191). Single block; number of fruits per tree from 44 to above 350 in the reported examples (Tables 7-8; smallest-tree counts 61, 87, 92, 44 at lines 1569-1570). Number of trees in the block-level claim: 48 (lines 1756-1757).

ACQUISITION: Two sources. (1) UAV: DJI Phantom 4 Pro+ at 122 m, RGB and RedEdge-M multispectral (lines 210-218). Used for spectral values and structural parameters of each tree (lines 1745-1746). (2) Ground: Canon EOS 5D still images of each tree. Front images taken 3.8 m from the tree centre, back images 5.4 m (lines 233-241). "Side" means the front and back of the tree. Frame rate and video: not stated in the read text. Single-image capture, no video.

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR: YOLO v3 (version as named in the paper, "YOLO v3"), transfer learning from a weight file previously trained on immature citrus fruits (lines 242-247, 1552-1553). Detects citrus fruit. Input size: not stated in the read text. Reported detection accuracy: "The fruit detection using YOLO v3 gave a high precision of 0.96, a recall of 0.83, and an average F1 score of 0.88" (Section 4.1, lines 1550-1552). Section 3.1 (lines 333-337) gives the same precision 0.96. Table 2 (lines 390-404): Row-1 P 0.96, R 0.80, F1 0.87; Row-2 P 0.96, R 0.86, F1 0.90.

ASSOCIATION METHOD: None described. No tracker, no cross-view matching, no 3D reconstruction, SfM or SLAM in the read text (lines 1-1812). Per-side detected fruit counts are used as an input variable of the yield regression (Section 4.2, lines 1651-1653). Yield regression: PLSR, RFR, LR and GBR, evaluated with k-fold cross-validation (k = 5) and average MAPE per tree (Tables 4-6). Model-2 and Model-3 use the detected count as a feature, not as a unique-fruit count.

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS: Fruit count from one side of the tree carries enough information for yield prediction (Model-2 chosen, lines 1663-1674). "However, the main objective of this model is not to count all fruits in a tree, rather than to get an estimate of fruit count to be used for the yield prediction models presented in this study." (Section 4.1, lines 1561-1564). Model-3 error is attributed to double counting (lines 1651-1654). Model-1 error is attributed to weather, sunlight intensity and brightness variation during UAV acquisition (lines 1582-1585). Yield estimation from spectral data alone is "unreliable for smaller trees (fruit count less than 100)" (lines 1576-1577).

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED:
(a) same fruit in consecutive frames: not applicable. No frame-to-frame association is described.
(b) track lost and restarted: not applicable. No track is described.
(c) same fruit from both sides of the tree: not removed by any counting step. Model-2 uses the count from one side only; Model-3 uses counts from both sides. The authors state that Model-3 is worse "because of the double counting of fruits when using the fruit count of images from both sides of a tree" (lines 1651-1653), and that "using the fruit count from one side (Model-2) is a better alternative to using fruit count from images taken from both sides of the tree" (lines 1659-1661). Whether Table 1 "2-side count" was checked against manual counts: not re-verified in this pass (Table 1 not re-read here).

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING: None. Single class (citrus fruit). Maturity not used as a class. The authors propose immature-fruit training and prediction 4-6 months before harvest as future work (lines 1793-1795).

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL: Count reference: harvest count per tree, taken one week after imaging (lines 190-191). Yield error: MAPE (average over k = 5 folds, per tree), APE per tree (Tables 7-8), RMSE (Tables 7-8, text 1642-1644). Block level: "all three models predicted yield with more than 99% accuracy" (lines 1756-1757); the accuracy measure is not defined in the read text. Detection metrics: precision, recall, F1 (Section 3.1, 4.1). Identity metrics (MOTA, IDF1, HOTA, ID switches): not stated.

KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS:
- Detection (Section 4.1, lines 1550-1552): P 0.96, R 0.83, F1 0.88.
- Detection, Table 2: Row-1 P 0.96, R 0.80, F1 0.87; Row-2 P 0.96, R 0.86, F1 0.90.
- Model-1 (UAV only), average MAPE (Table 4): LR 35.59 (lowest); PLSR 35.84; GBR 41.12; RFR 41.47.
- Model-2 (UAV + one-side count), average MAPE (Table 5): PLSR 23.45 (lowest); GBR 31.72 (highest, line 1524).
- Model-3 (UAV + both-side count), average MAPE (Table 6): PLSR 25.72 (lowest); LR 30.31; GBR 33.37; RFR 37.78 (highest).
- Improvement Model-1 to Model-2 stated as 34.11% (lines 1407-1419, read in an earlier pass; 35.59 to 23.45).
- Paired t-tests (Section 3.2, lines 1543-1547): Model-1 vs Model-2 p = 0.0013; Model-1 vs Model-3 p = 0.0010; Model-2 vs Model-3 p = 0.29.
- Smaller trees (fewer than 100 fruits, Table 7): Model-2 RMSE 54.64; Model-1 102.77; Model-3 79.07 (lines 1642-1644). Model-2 reduced APE by at least 40% vs Model-1 in 3 of 4 small-tree cases (lines 1640-1641).
- Larger trees (more than 350 fruits, Table 8): Model-2 better than Model-1 in 5 of 7 cases (lines 1645-1646); Model-1 better than Model-2 in 2 of 7 (line 1580-1581); Model-3 lower APE than Model-1 in 6 of 7 and than Model-2 in 3 of 7 (lines 1657-1659).

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED:
- Occlusion of fruit by leaves and branches (lines 1556-1558).
- Camera specifications, time of image, and shadows affect image quality and detection (lines 1559-1561).
- Outliers with error above 100% in trees with fewer than 100 fruits (fruit counts 61, 87, 92, 44) (lines 1567-1570). Rootstock variety suggested as a cause of tree-size variability (lines 1572-1573).
- Weather, sunlight intensity, and brightness variation during UAV acquisition (lines 1582-1585).
- Double counting from images of both sides (lines 1651-1654).
- High variability of GBR and RFR under Model-3 (lines 1537-1540).
- Ground image capture may be hard where rows are close and a farm vehicle cannot drive along the canopy (lines 1751-1755).

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA: Model-1, Model-2 and Model-3 are compared on the same trees with the same four algorithms and the same k = 5 folds (Tables 4-6). Paired t-tests across models (lines 1543-1547). Tree-size subsets: Table 7 (fewer than 100 fruits) and Table 8 (more than 350 fruits) compare the three models on the same trees. Block-level comparison (48 trees) described only in one sentence (lines 1756-1757).

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK: Need for a large-scale experiment (lines 1757-1759). Models should be tested "in different locations and during different dates before harvest" (lines 1762-1764). Improve detection with "more advanced robust networks" (lines 1791-1793). Detection training with immature fruit to predict 4-6 months before harvest (lines 1793-1795). Evaluate estimation performance and cost against manual methods (lines 1798-1801). Detection improvement with more images of mature fruit from several farms and times of day (lines 1554-1556).

INCONSISTENCIES INSIDE THE PAPER:
- Model-2 "gave the overall lowest error in yield prediction" (lines 1533-1535), but Model-2 vs Model-3 is "no significant difference" (p = 0.29, lines 1546-1547), and the Model Comparison says "the difference in MAPE was around 2%, the difference was not statistically significant" (lines 1667-1669). The choice of Model-2 rests on input-data cost, not on a significant error gap (lines 1671-1674).
- Conclusion (lines 1782-1785): "Model-2 also estimated the yield better for trees with the smallest and largest fruit load (harvest count less than 100 and more than 350 fruits, respectively) as compared to Model-1 and Model-3." Table 8 values as printed: Model-2 APE is lower than Model-3 for 4 of 7 large trees (442, 362, 387, 409), and in Table 7 Model-2 APE (32.69) is higher than Model-3 (21.51) for the 61-fruit tree. Section 4.2 (lines 1657-1659) gives Model-3 lower APE than Model-2 in 3 of 7 large-tree cases. These counts are computed from the printed table values in this pass.
- Conclusion (lines 1780-1781): "the developed Model-2 could be used to predict the fruit count in orange orchards in Florida." Model-2 is a yield model; its fruit count is an input (Sections 2-3). The paper does not report a fruit-count error for the final model in the read text.
- Block-level "more than 99% accuracy" (lines 1756-1757) is not tied to a defined measure, while the same paragraph calls for a large-scale experiment to evaluate performance (lines 1757-1759).
- Model-1 explanation (lines 1582-1585, weather and sunlight) and the "double counting" explanation for Model-3 (lines 1651-1654) are offered as causes; the read text reports no test that isolates either cause.
