KEY: gongal2016apple
(Gongal, Silwal, Amatya, Karkee, Zhang, Lewis, "Apple crop-load estimation with over-the-row machine vision system", Computers and Electronics in Agriculture 120 (2016) 26-35. Page numbers are from running headers; section and table numbers are exact.)

CROP / SETTING / SCALE: Apple, 'Jazz' cultivar, tall spindle orchard in Prosser, Washington, imaged 4-1 weeks before harvest in 2013. Scale is the tree canopy and the row; 20 trees were imaged (Section 2.1, lines 208-213 of the text file). Crop load is counted as total apples per tree.

ACQUISITION: Over-the-row (OTR) platform, 2.13 m x 2.74 m x 3.67 m, mounted on a tractor three-point hitch and driven along the row inside a tunnel structure with tarpaulin and LED lights (Section 2). Colour camera: Prosilica GigE 1290c, 1280x960 px, FOV 43.6 x 33.4 degrees. Time-of-flight (ToF) camera: PMD CamCube 3.0, 200x200 px, FOV 40 x 40 degrees. Images were taken from opposite sides of the canopy at five camera heights ("To cover entire canopies, images were acquired from five different camera heights (Fig. 4)."). Totals: 424 images, 212 colour and 212 3D (Section 2.1). Frame rate, travel speed and subject distance are not stated. Discrete paired views, not continuous video.

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR: Apple identification in colour images (Section 2.2), implemented in MATLAB R2012a:
- Histogram equalisation (HSI), Wiener filter 5x5, grey image R-(G+B), Otsu threshold, objects under 100 px removed, Canny edge detection.
- Iterative Circular Hough Transform (CHT), radius 30 to 65 px, from the stated apple size: "image size of apples with 6–8 cm physical diameter varied approximately from 35 to 65 pixels."
- Colour filter, then blob analysis (area > 90 px, compactness > 90), and clustering with radius 65 px.
- Input size: the full colour frame (1280x960) is implied; no other input size is stated.
- Accuracy as reported: identification accuracy 79.8% (abstract), 78.9% (Conclusions); MAPE on identifying all apples 21.2% (Section 3.1; Table 2 all-tree MAPE 21.17). Table 3: 1,697 correctly identified apples, 843 false negatives (33.2%), 254 false positives (13.0%).

ASSOCIATION METHOD: Each apple in the colour image is located in 3D through co-registration of the colour and ToF cameras (Bouguet camera calibration toolbox, checkerboard, 25 images; intrinsic parameters in Table 1; rigid transform with R near identity and T = (3.176, -60.50, 18.11), Eq. 2). Apples from the two sides are mapped into one common 3D coordinate system (Section 2.4). Duplicates are detected by distance: an apple from one side is a duplicate if it lies within a buffer of 0.075 m of an apple from the other side. The text does not state an explicit counting rule beyond removing the apples flagged as duplicates. No tracking, no re-identification and no SfM or SLAM step is used; the matching is a 3D distance test between two single-view sets.
Parameters of the 3D registration (Z offset of the two sides and X/Y translation) were set by trial and error on five apples visible from both sides in each tree (Section 2.4, lines 557-558 of the text file).

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS:
- Stated: the buffer distance of 0.075 m (three-fourths of the average apple diameter) is sufficient to match the same apple in both sides, given that duplicates are "displaced slightly" and "some closely positioned apples were overlapped in the registered 3D space" (Section 2.4).
- Stated: "The lower MAPE in apple counting with dual side imaging compared to single side imaging was mainly due to substantial improvement in apple visibility." (Section 3.3).
- Implied: both sides are at the same camera height, so the Z offset is constant (Section 2.4, lines 505-508). The text does not test a different height on the two sides.
- Implied: the ground truth from two people's manual counts is valid (Section 2.1, lines 213-220; Section 2.4 lines 611-614, average of the two counts).
- Implied: the orchard is flat (the authors note the study was in a "relatively flat orchard").

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED:
(a) Same fruit in consecutive frames: not applicable; discrete images, no video, and the paper does not discuss it.
(b) Track lost and restarted: not applicable; no tracking.
(c) Same fruit from both sides: addressed directly. Apples from opposite sides are mapped to a common 3D frame; apples within 0.075 m are duplicates. The paper reports two error types for this step: false positives (non-duplicates called duplicates, which the authors attribute to displacement of apples in 3D positions) and false negatives (duplicates missed). Table 5 gives the confusion matrix. The 3D transform assumes the cameras on the two sides are at the same height and on the same plane, so only a Z offset is applied (Section 2.4, lines 505-508). Duplicates within one side are not discussed.

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING: None. One class, "apple". Maturity is handled only in the sense that the detector was "optimized for red apple identification"; greenish immature apples were missed (33.2% false negatives, Section 3.1). The paper excludes trees with greenish apples in one subset analysis (12% MAPE). No class is attached to a unique apple. The authors state greenish apples are not addressed in the conclusions (lines 1099-1101).

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL:
- Tree level: ground truth is the manual count of each tree's total apples, by two people, averaged (Section 2.1; Section 2.4 lines 611-614). Harvest or packhouse counts are not used.
- Duplicate level: manual count of duplicate apples in the images (Section 2.1, lines 213-220).
- Metrics: MAPE, Eq. 4 (Section 2.4 lines 617-630), applied to total counts per tree (Table 2, Table 4, Table 6). Producer's and consumer's accuracy for identification and for duplicate identification (Section 2.4 lines 601-606; Table 3 and Table 5). No MOTA, IDF1, HOTA or ID-switch metrics.

KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS:
- Identification accuracy 79.8% (Abstract); 78.9% (Conclusions).
- Identification MAPE 21.2% for all apples (Section 3.1, Table 2; Table 2 all-tree MAPE 21.17).
- Identification with greenish apples from 3-4 weeks before harvest excluded: MAPE 12%, false positives 13.9%, false negatives 25% (Section 3.1).
- Table 3: 1,697 identified apples correct; 843 false negatives (33.2%); 254 false positives (13.0%).
- Duplicate identification error 21.1% (Abstract); Table 4 MAPE 21.1 (Section 3.2 gives "21%").
- Table 5: duplicates correct 319, duplicates missed 90, non-duplicates called duplicates 21, non-duplicates correct 1,896. Producer's accuracy (duplicate) 78.00%; consumer's accuracy (duplicate) 93.8%. Printed non-duplicate values: 95.47% and 98.9% (see INCONSISTENCIES).
- Duplicate identification accuracy 79% (Conclusions).
- Overall MAPE on total apple counting: 18% with dual-side imaging (Table 6); 42% from front side A and 41% from back side B alone (Section 3.3, Fig. 13).
- Overall accuracy of crop-load estimation: 82% dual-side vs 58% single-side (Abstract, Conclusions).
- Apple visibility: "only around 60%" from one side (Section 3.3).

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED:
- Occlusion by leaves, stems and other apples: about 40% of apples invisible from one side (Section 3.3).
- Green (immature) apples missed because the detector was optimised for red apples: 33.2% false negatives (Section 3.1).
- False positives from branches and leaves with colour similar to apples (Section 3.1).
- Displacement of apples in 3D positions, which causes the consumer's-accuracy false positives in duplicate identification (Section 3.2, lines 878-890).
- Overlapping, closely positioned apples in 3D (Section 2.4): duplicates displaced slightly or overlapping in the registered space.
- Identification error was "the major factor for underestimation of apple counts" (Section 3.3).
- Net count effect: false positives in identification and false negatives in duplicate identification "canceled their effects slightly" (Section 3.3).

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA:
- Dual-side vs single-side on the same 20 trees: MAPE 18% vs 42% (front, A) and 41% (back, B) (Section 3.3, Table 6, Fig. 13).
- No external baseline. The comparison is within the authors' own system.

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK:
- Training, pruning, pollination and thinning "may be investigated to improve visibility, and reduced occlusion and clustering of fruits" (Section 3.3).
- "further research may be carried out to detect and reconstruct partially visible apples in modern fruiting wall orchards" (Section 3.3).
- Real-time operation and sloped orchards: "Further studies may also be needed to address engineering challenges of the platform for real time operation in sloped orchards as this study was carried out in a relatively flat orchard." (Section 3.3).
- Crop load was estimated only "when fruit have developed desired color and size"; early-season estimation is not tested (Section 3.3).
- Conclusions: greenish (immature) apples and apple sizing and real-time sensing "will have to be added" for practical use (Conclusions).

INCONSISTENCIES INSIDE THE PAPER:
1. Identification accuracy. Abstract: "an identification accuracy of 79.8%". Conclusions: "with an accuracy of 78.9%". Section 3.1: "mean absolute percentage error in identifying all apples was found to be 21.2%", which implies about 78.8% accuracy.
2. Duplicate identification. Abstract: "The error on identifying duplicate apples was found to be 21.1%", which implies 78.9% accuracy. Conclusions: "Accuracy on identification of those duplicate apples was 79%." Section 3.2 (per the notes): producer's accuracy for duplicates 78.0%, matching Table 5 (319/409 = 78.0%).
3. Table 5 non-duplicate values. The printed "95.47%" sits beside the non-duplicate row and "98.9%" under the non-duplicate column. Arithmetic from Table 5 counts: non-duplicate producer = 1,896/1,917 = 98.9%; non-duplicate consumer = 1,896/1,986 = 95.47%. The printed positions are reversed relative to the arithmetic. Reported here as printed.
4. The Abstract's 82% overall accuracy and the Section 3.3 MAPE of 18% are consistent (100 - 18 = 82).
