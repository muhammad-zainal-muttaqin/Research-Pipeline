KEY: linker2015estimation
(Linker, Kelman and Cohen, "Estimation of apple orchard yield using night time imaging", Precision agriculture '15, pp. 533-539; Technion, Israel. Page numbers below are the running-header numbers in the text file and are approximate; section and figure names are exact.)

CROP / SETTING / SCALE: Apple, Golden Delicious, 42 trees at the Matityahu Research Station, northern Israel, imaged on 17 July 2014. Scale is the tree and the row ("at least at the row scale", Conclusions). Yield ground truth is the total harvested per tree at end of season ("The trees were picked individually at the end of the season in order to obtain ground truth yield data", Materials and methods, Data collection).

ACQUISITION: Night-time imaging with artificial light. Three identical Canon PowerShot G7 cameras (3,648x2,736 px) on a vertical pole at 110, 180 and 240 cm above ground; two extra poles 60 cm from the camera pole carry LED lights at 130 and 250 cm. The assembly is on a trailer driven in the middle of the tree rows. Each tree is photographed from two sides, giving six images per tree and 252 images in total. Frame rate, subject distance and exposure settings are not stated. Discrete views, not video.

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR:
- Approach #1 (from Linker et al. 2012 and Cohen et al. 2014): colour-and-texture pixel classifier, blob detection and region growing, contour split into arcs, arcs grouped and compared with a circle model. Classifier trained on pixels sampled from "apple" and "non-apple" regions; about half of the apples in the 12 calibration images were marked manually and the rest masked out. Only radius parameters were tuned: minimum 30 px, expected 60 px, maximum 150 px.
- Approach #2 (from Linker and Kelman 2015): finds local intensity maxima (specular highlights), forms nested light patches at decreasing thresholds and tests their geometry for circularity. No colour information is used. Parameters were the set from the 2013 dataset of Linker and Kelman (2015), "used without any change".
- Input size of the detectors is not stated beyond the full camera frame.
- Detection accuracy: reported only as per-image totals against visual count (Table 1, below). No per-image precision, recall or F1 is reported.

ASSOCIATION METHOD: None. Detections in the six images of each tree are summed to a "tree count" ("the number of objects detected in the six images of a tree were summed up to produce a 'tree count'", Data analysis). There is no tracking, no matching across views, no 3D or SfM step, and no statistical correction for overlap between views. The image-level counts are converted to apple counts by a linear calibration fitted on 12 images (Figure 2 left and Figure 4 left; coefficients are not given in the text). Tree counts are converted to yield by a second calibration fitted on 14 randomly chosen trees; the form of that relationship is called "relationship" in the text, and its coefficients are not given.

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS:
- Approach #1 assumes "the circular contour on the apple is at least partially visible" (Data analysis).
- Approach #2 assumes "that the visible region of the apple is sufficiently large to enable analysis of the light distribution on its surface" (Data analysis).
- Implied: a fixed per-tree relationship between summed detections and yield, and a fixed per-image relationship between detections and visual count, both transfer from calibration images and trees to the remaining images and trees. The validation design (Tables 2 and 3) tests this transfer on the same orchard, season and camera set-up only.
- Implied: visual counts of images are a valid reference for the apple count in the image.

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED:
(a) Same fruit in consecutive frames: not applicable; the paper uses discrete images, not video, and does not discuss it.
(b) Track lost and restarted: not applicable; no tracking.
(c) Same fruit from both sides or from different camera heights: not addressed. Each tree is imaged from two sides by three cameras at three heights, and the six image counts are summed directly. The text does not say whether one apple can appear in more than one of the six images, and no de-duplication step is described. Reviewer note (inference, not stated in the paper): any such overlap would inflate the tree count, and the tree-level calibration to yield would absorb any constant bias of that kind.

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING: None. One class, "apple". The paper images Golden Delicious trees and does not discuss maturity. The introduction cites earlier night-time work on "green mature apples" (Cohen et al. 2014; Linker and Kelman 2015), but this paper does not attach a class to any detection.

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL:
- Image level: visual count of apples in each image (Table 1, "Number of apples according to visual count").
- Tree level: harvested yield of each tree at end of season (ground truth, Data collection). Yield is in apples, as the text uses "number of apples" for yield (Tables 2 and 3 show "Actual yield" in counts, e.g. "All trees (yield: 21,047)").
- Metrics: total ratio (estimated/visual, shown as "Actual/estimated ratio" in Table 1; see inconsistencies), per-tree yield error in percent (Tables 2 and 3), and total yield error in percent. No identity metrics (MOTA, IDF1, HOTA, ID switches) and no per-tree detection metrics.

KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS:
- Table 1, visual count total over 252 images: 18,242 (bottom 4,218; middle 6,551; top 7,473).
- Table 1, Approach #1 estimated total: 17,191 (bottom 3,871; middle 6,887; top 6,334); ratio 0.94 (overall), 0.92 (bottom), 1.07 (middle), 0.85 (top).
- Table 1, Approach #2 estimated total: 18,663 (bottom 4,362; middle 7,209; top 7,092); ratio 1.02 (overall), 1.03 (bottom), 1.10 (middle), 0.95 (top).
- Text (Results, Approach #2 paragraph): "Overall both approaches gave total estimates which were within 6% of the visual count."
- Actual yield of all 42 trees: 21,047 apples (Tables 2 and 3 headers).
- Table 2 (Approach #1, ten random calibration subsets of 14 trees), error on validation trees: from -10.49% (A10) to +9.37% (A7). Error on all trees: from -7.34% (A10) to +5.73% (A7).
- Table 3 (Approach #2, same subset design), error on validation trees: from -4.69% (B7) to +12.51% (B10). Error on all trees: from -3.10% (B7) to +8.33% (B10).
- Authors' summary in text: "the error exceeds 10% only once for each approach" (Yield estimation). Tables confirm: A10 (-10.49%) for Approach #1 and B10 (+12.51%) for Approach #2, both on validation trees.
- Single-tree error: "sometimes large (up to ~300 apples in extreme cases)" (Yield estimation). This figure does not appear in any table and cannot be checked against the tables.
- Figure 5 and 6 left frames: detections per tree correspond "to only about one third of the actual tree yield" (Yield estimation).

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED:
- Approach #1, top camera: "The worst results were obtained for the images of the top camera analyzed using Approach #1." Cause given: "the poor quality of the illumination in the upper part of the tree in some of the images, which resulted in many apples being too dark to be recognized by the color classifier" (Results, Table 1 paragraph).
- Approach #1 classifier is dataset-specific: colour depends on "the apple variety and their developmental stage but also on the illumination" (Results, Calibration).
- Single-tree yield error can be large (see above).
- Approach #2 does not depend on colour, so the authors expect it to be more robust to illumination and camera type (Results, Approach #2).

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA:
- Approach #1 vs Approach #2 on the same 252 images and the same 42 trees (Table 1, Tables 2 and 3). The two are close at the image-total level (0.94 vs 1.02 ratio) and at the yield level (all-tree errors within about 7% and 8% respectively, with Approach #2 having the smaller range on all trees, -3.10% to +8.33%, than Approach #1, -7.34% to +5.73%; validation-only ranges are -10.49% to +9.37% and -4.69% to +12.51%).
- The paper compares its two approaches with each other only. No external baseline is given.
- Cited earlier studies (Linker et al. 2012; Cohen et al. 2014; Linker and Kelman 2015) are described but not re-run on other data, and no numbers from them are compared.

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK:
- "Although these results need to be confirmed using a larger dataset, one can envision a self-calibrating system which would require the farmer to provide fruit counts for 15-20 trees." (Conclusions)
- "The key issue is the calibration of the algorithm for identifying the apples in the images." (Conclusions)
- Approach #1's classifier "requires careful labeling of apple regions in the calibration images, which is time consuming." (Conclusions)
- The overall yield estimate depends on calibration trees; the authors tested ten random subsets of 14 trees and report that the choice of calibration trees does not strongly affect the overall result (Yield estimation).
- Single-orchard, single-season, single-variety study; the authors call for "a larger dataset".

INCONSISTENCIES INSIDE THE PAPER:
1. Table 1 column label versus values. The row label is "Actual/estimated ratio", but the values are estimated/visual. Check: Approach #1, all images, 17,191 / 18,242 = 0.94 (the table shows 0.94); the actual/estimated ratio would be 1.06. The same holds for every cell checked (for example, Approach #2 all images, 18,663 / 18,242 = 1.02, the table value).
2. Table 1, Approach #1, middle images: estimated 6,887 and visual 6,551 give 6,887 / 6,551 = 1.05, but the table shows 1.07.
3. Table 1, Approach #1, all images: the column sums are 3,871 + 6,887 + 6,334 = 17,092, but the table total is 17,191 (difference 99). Approach #2 and the visual counts sum correctly (18,663 and 18,242).
4. Figure reference. Text (Results, Approach #2): "Figure 3 shows the detection results for the same image as in Figure 2". Figure 3 caption: "Detection results of Approach #2 for the image shown in Figure 1." Figure 1 is the Approach #1 example image, so the text and the caption disagree on which image is shown.
5. Calibration sample. Approach #1 is described as using "about half of the apples" marked in the 12 calibration images, with the rest masked. The text does not give the number of apples marked. Not an inconsistency, but the count is missing.
