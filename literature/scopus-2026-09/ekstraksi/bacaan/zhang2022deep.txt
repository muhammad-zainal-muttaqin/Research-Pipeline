KEY: zhang2022deep (Zhang et al., Horticulture Research 2022, 9: uhac003, "Deep-learning-based in-field citrus fruit detection and tracking")

CROP / SETTING / SCALE: Citrus (oranges). Two orchards in Meishan, Sichuan, China, containing 22 trees. Six video sequences were recorded. Dataset of 1465 samples split 7:3 into 1025 train and 440 test images; a separate standard set of 182 images was used for one test row. Number of fruit per sequence is given in Table 4 (ground-truth counts for the sequences are in the table; per-sequence totals not re-verified here).

ACQUISITION: Video along a path. Camera: DJI Osmo Action, 60 fps, 1920x1080, FOV 145 degrees. Platform: self-designed field rover moving at 2 m/s along tree rows. Camera viewing direction is perpendicular to the tree rows. Only one camera direction (one side of the row) is used. Sequences include poor lighting (sequences 2-4) and overexposure with camera shake (sequences 5-6) (Figure 12 caption, txt lines 1232-1234; OrangeSort results, txt lines 1277-1289).

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR: OrangeYolo, a modified YOLOv3 with a DarkNet53 backbone, a receptive-field scale matching strategy (detection branches matched to the effective receptive field), and a dual-attention multiscale fusion module (channel and spatial attention). Detects oranges (single class). Input size: not verified (not recorded in working notes). Detection accuracy (Table 2, test set of 440 images): AP 0.938, F1 0.897, FPS 83.3. On the 182-image standard set: AP 0.957, F1 0.916, FPS 83.3 (txt lines 1201-1212). Baselines in Table 2: YOLOv3 AP 0.905 / F1 0.873 / FPS 83.3; YOLOv4 AP 0.911 / F1 0.854 / FPS 71.4; YOLOv5 AP 0.917 / F1 0.883 / FPS 58.9.

ASSOCIATION METHOD: OrangeSort, a tracker based on SORT-style Kalman filtering and Hungarian matching, extended with motion displacement estimation. Unmatched detections create new IDs. Unmatched (lost) tracks are predicted using the average displacement of matched tracks (Eq. 6). A scale factor decays by alpha per frame (Eq. 7); a track is deleted once its scale falls below a threshold. Counting rule: unique tracking ID counted inside a defined counting region (OrangeSort*). The counting region is 3/5 of frame width, with 1/5 invalid margins at the start and end of the frame; Table 1 maps occlusion states (initial, intermediate, leaving) A-G. No 3D, SfM or appearance-based re-identification is reported in the sections read. Comparison trackers: Sort (Bewley et al. 2016) and DeepSort (Wojke et al. 2017).

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS: "In a field fruit video sequence, all the objects are stationary, and the trajectory of each fruit is approximately the same as the distance the camera moves during a video sequence." (OrangeSort section, p. 8). Fruit are stationary and their image displacement approximately equals camera displacement. Oranges grow in clusters, so spatial features are used (Method, p. 6). Occluded or undetected targets are written off after several consecutive frames (OrangeSort, p. 8).

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED:
(a) Same fruit in consecutive frames: addressed by Kalman prediction, Hungarian matching and the displacement model within OrangeSort.
(b) Track lost and restarted: addressed by displacement-based prediction for unmatched tracks and the scale-decay deletion rule. The text states OrangeSort "significantly lower number of double-counting instances" than Sort and DeepSort (txt lines 1264-1266).
(c) Same fruit from both sides or different passes: not addressed. Only one camera direction is used; no cross-side or cross-pass matching is described in the sections read.

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING: none. Single class (orange). No maturity classes are reported.

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL: Manual counts from the video sequences (video-based manual counting, txt lines 1302-1313). Per-sequence count error defined as MAE(m=1) with counts of correct, missed, false and double-counted fruit (Table 4). Standard deviation across sequences (Table 5). Detection metrics: AP, F1, FPS. Not stated: MOTA, IDF1, HOTA, or a formal ID-switch count (the text mentions an "ID switch problem" qualitatively, txt lines 1271-1273).

KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS:
- OrangeYolo test (440 images, Table 2): AP 0.938, F1 0.897, FPS 83.3.
- OrangeYolo on standard 182-image set (Table 2): AP 0.957, F1 0.916.
- Ablation (Table 3): AP 0.905 baseline; receptive-field matching raised F1 by 1.4% and AP by 2.3% (txt lines 1216-1219); dual-attention fusion raised F1 to 0.897 and AP to 0.938 (txt lines 1220-1236).
- Table 4, OrangeSort per-sequence count errors (text, txt lines 1267-1269): 0.1455, 0.0938, 0.3714, 0.1265, 0.1522, 0.3185. Mean of these six values computed here as 0.2013 (not stated by authors).
- Table 4, OrangeSort* per-sequence count errors (txt lines 1274-1276): 0.0061, 0.1094, 0.2429, 0.071, 0.0326, 0.0242. Mean computed here as 0.081; authors state MAE 0.081 (txt line 1308).
- Sequence 2 detail (txt lines 1290-1293): OrangeSort counted 70 (40 correct, 5 double, 25 error); OrangeSort* counted 57 (40 correct, 4 repeat, 13 error).
- Table 5 (SD, from table, not re-verified in text): Sort 0.4741, DeepSort 1.3975, OrangeSort 0.2555, OrangeSort* 0.08.
- Abstract MAE values for Sort (0.45) and DeepSort (1.212): the mean of Table 4 per-sequence errors for these trackers was computed here as 0.4503 and 1.2122, which is consistent with the abstract values.

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED:
- Poor lighting: sequence 3 is the worst, where "it is almost impossible to distinguish the categories of fruits and leaves" (txt lines 1281-1284).
- Overexposure, bumps and camera shake in sequences 5-6, with a few fruit not tracked accurately (txt lines 1285-1289).
- Global-region counting (OrangeSort without counting region) produces about twice as many error counts as OrangeSort* in sequence 2 (txt lines 1296-1299).
- Dense fruit growth and heavy overlap in orchards (Introduction).
- Occlusion conditions catalogued in Table 1 (A-G).

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA: OrangeYolo vs YOLOv3, YOLOv4, YOLOv5 on the same test set (Table 2): OrangeYolo has the highest AP and F1; FPS equals YOLOv3 (83.3). OrangeSort* vs Sort, DeepSort and OrangeSort (no counting region) on the six sequences (Tables 4-5).

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK: "Future work can be aimed at using 3D technology to locate fruit spatial coordinates to enable more accurate counting, and line tail turns will be explored further in subsequent research work." (Conclusion, txt lines 1336-1339). Also: a lightweight network for edge devices, and linking counts to unseen fruit for yield estimation (txt lines 1340-1344).

INCONSISTENCIES INSIDE THE PAPER:
- Standard deviation of OrangeSort*: the text says "a standard deviation of 0.8" (txt line 1309) while Table 5 gives 0.08 and the Conclusion gives "SD = 0.08" (txt line 1333).
- Abstract/Results dataset for AP 0.957: the text says it is obtained "on standard fruit images" from the 182-image set (txt lines 1204-1207). The abstract and the summary-level description in the Conclusion refer to the citrus dataset generally, with 0.938 as the field test result (Conclusion, txt lines 1326-1327). The 0.957 figure is therefore not the 440-image test result.
