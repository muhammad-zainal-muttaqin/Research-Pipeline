KEY: genemola2023video (Gené-Mola et al., 2023, video-based fruit counting and yield estimation in an apple orchard; citation details in the text file's author list, txt lines 1-30)

CROP / SETTING / SCALE: Apple (tall spindle). One experimental orchard, Story Inored, Mollerussa, Catalonia. Four rows of 105 m, 420 trees, planting 3.6 m x 1 m. Evaluation over 84 stretches of 5 m. Scans on 06/09/2021 (four weeks before harvest, fruit bicoloured) and 28/09/2021 (one week before harvest, fruit fully red). Harvest on 05/10/2021. Ground truth: each stretch harvested and counted on a sorting machine (Maf Roda Agrobotic). Some ground truth is missing (see FAILURE CONDITIONS).

ACQUISITION: Video along a path. Kinect Azure DK RGB-D camera plus Ardusimple simpleRTK2B GNSS, mounted on a vertical mast at the rear of an electric all-terrain vehicle. Sensor 2.25 m from the row axis. Vehicle speed 0.556 m/s. Frame rate 30 fps. Each tree scanned from both sides (east and west). Scanned fruit positions are geo-referenced with GNSS.

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR: YOLOv5x, COCO pretrained, fine-tuned (SGD, learning rate 0.01, batch size 4) on 1087 annotated frames from stretches 5-6 of row 2 (annotated in Supervisely). Trained 273 epochs (validation loss minimum). Detects apples (single class). Input size: not stated (the pipeline section, txt lines 237-269, gives none). Detection accuracy: F1 = 0.834 and mAP = 0.871 (Results A, txt lines 332-335). The paper states these are "in line with" reported F1 range 0.730-0.970 (ref. 17, Koirala et al. 2019).

ASSOCIATION METHOD: Three trackers compared: SORT (Kalman filter and Hungarian matching, ref. 7), DeepSORT (adds a cosine-metric-learning Re-ID network, refs. 9 and 13), and ByteTrack (ref. 8). SORT and DeepSORT assign IDs to detections with score above 0.7 (Pipeline, txt lines 263-265). Counting: per stretch, east and west counts summed, x_ew,i,j = x_e,i,j + x_w,i,j. Yield calibrated with a linear model f = b0 + b1·x. Calibration validated by leave-one-row-out cross-validation using sum of squared residuals S_k (Methods, txt lines 284-296). Counting rule as stated: sum of per-side tracked counts per stretch; unique tracking ID (within each side) is what the trackers produce; explicit cross-side de-duplication is not described in the sections read beyond the sum.

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS: The authors state that appearance features do not help because apples look alike: "The authors attribute this finding to the inherent similarity in appearance among apples, which may limit the effectiveness of the Re-ID network" (txt lines 352-354). Counting closer to harvest is more reliable (Results B, txt lines 416-418; Conclusion, txt lines 503-506). The authors recommend ByteTrack as the tracker for fruit tracking applications (txt lines 364-371). The stretch-level linear calibration is assumed to transfer across rows (leave-one-row-out check, Methods, txt lines 284-296).

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED: (a) same fruit in consecutive frames: addressed by the tracker (ByteTrack, SORT, DeepSORT compared); the paper reports tracking metrics MOTA, IDF1 and HOTA, which penalise identity switches, but no explicit double-count count is reported. (b) track lost and restarted: addressed only implicitly through the tracker's association; no separate analysis reported. (c) same fruit from both sides or different passes: addressed by summing east and west counts per stretch (x_ew = x_e + x_w). The text does not describe a method to detect the same fruit seen from both sides; the sum is the counting rule. Whether this sum double-counts fruit visible from both sides is not discussed in the sections read.

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING: none. Single class (apple). No maturity classes are reported.

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL: Harvest-level ground truth per stretch: fruit harvested and counted on a sorting machine (Maf Roda Agrobotic) (Methods, txt lines 213-219). Count error metrics: MAE, MBE, MAPE, RMSE and R2 (txt lines 318-329; Table II, txt lines 451-470). Tracking metrics: MOTA, IDF1, HOTA (txt lines 307-317; Table I, txt lines 372-393). Detection metrics: precision, recall, F1, mAP (txt lines 297-306). Per-image processing time reported in Table I. Tracking metrics are computed on the annotated frames (1087 frames, stretches 5-6 of row 2, Methods, txt lines 232-236); the text does not state whether the tracking evaluation used the same frames as the detector test set.

KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS:
- YOLOv5x detector (Results A, txt lines 332-335): F1 = 0.834, mAP = 0.871 at 273 epochs.
- Table I, tracking (txt lines 372-393):
  - SORT: MOTA 0.6401, IDF1 0.8091, HOTA 0.6804, time 15.3 ms/img.
  - DeepSORT: MOTA 0.5735, IDF1 0.7646, HOTA 0.6824, time 128.0 ms/img.
  - ByteTrack: MOTA 0.6817, IDF1 0.8369, HOTA 0.6894, time 15.4 ms/img.
- Table II, yield with ByteTrack (txt lines 452-470):
  - 06/09/2021 (4 weeks before harvest): MAE 38.43, MBE -0.20, MAPE 8.45 %, RMSE 49.04, R2 0.66.
  - 28/09/2021 (1 week before harvest): MAE 34.98, MBE 0.15, MAPE 7.47 %, RMSE 45.64, R2 0.70.
- Results B (txt lines 401-410): MAPE 8.45 % (±0.8 % SE) and MAPE 7.47 % (±0.6 % SE) at the two dates.
- Abstract figures reported in the study summary for ByteTrack: MOTA 0.682, IDF1 0.837, HOTA 0.689, per-image time about 15 ms; yield MAPE 7.47 %, R2 0.70 at one week before harvest (abstract is not in the sections read in full; figures match Table I and Table II).

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED:
- Missing ground truth: "Due to an error during ground truth data acquisition, the harvested fruit production of stretches 4, 5 and 6 of row 2, as well as stretch 4 of row 4, are missing." (txt lines 411-415). Those stretches are excluded from the qualitative map only; the quantitative numbers in Table II are not stated to exclude them.
- Appearance similarity among apples limits Re-ID (DeepSORT), txt lines 349-354.
- Earlier scans (4 weeks pre-harvest) give larger errors than later scans (1 week pre-harvest), txt lines 401-410.
- Scanning conditions (lighting, vehicle speed, data acquisition speed, camera orientation and field of view) are named as factors not yet studied (txt lines 511-514).

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA: SORT, DeepSORT and ByteTrack on the same annotated video frames (Table I). ByteTrack is best on MOTA, IDF1 and HOTA; DeepSORT is lowest on MOTA and IDF1; HOTA is close across all three (0.6804, 0.6824, 0.6894). The authors contrast their result with Villacrés et al. (2023, ref. 18), who reported DeepSORT better than SORT (txt lines 354-357). Two dates compared for yield (Table II). Detection compared only to the cited range of other works (ref. 17).

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK: Future research to refine tracking methods and to study the influence of scanning conditions such as lighting, vehicle and data acquisition speeds, and camera orientation and field of view (txt lines 508-514). Extending the method to other fruit varieties with different appearances (txt lines 514-517).

INCONSISTENCIES INSIDE THE PAPER:
- R2 at 06/09/2021: Results B says "The R2 value of 0.66" (txt line 404) and Table II gives 0.66 (txt line 464); the Conclusion says "The R2 values of 0.65 and 0.70" (txt lines 494-495). The 0.65 in the Conclusion does not match the Results or Table II.
- Appearance features: Results A says the Re-ID similarity "may limit the effectiveness of the Re-ID network" (txt lines 352-354), while the Conclusion says the appearance similarity "limits the effectiveness of feature-based approaches" (Conclusion, txt lines 443-447). The Conclusion states the limit as fact; the Results hedge it.
- Otherwise the abstract-level tracking values (MOTA 0.682, IDF1 0.837, HOTA 0.689) match Table I (0.6817, 0.8369, 0.6894) after rounding.
