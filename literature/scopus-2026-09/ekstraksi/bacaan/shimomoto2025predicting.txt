KEY: shimomoto2025predicting
Shimomoto, K. et al. 2025. "Predicting yield of sweet pepper from ripeness-stage fruit counts with a double-camera monitoring system" (title as given in the text file header; verify before citing). Horticulturae 11, 718. DOI 10.3390/horticulturae11070718. Source text: literature/scopus-2026-09/teks/shimomoto2025predicting.txt. Line numbers below are text-file line numbers; page numbers are the journal pages shown in the text.

CROP / SETTING / SCALE:
Sweet pepper (paprika), cultivar 'Nagano', in a commercial high-tech greenhouse (Ai-Sai Farm Kokonoe, Japan; Conclusions, lines 1152-1153). Greenhouse area 3.0 ha; monitored cultivation area 0.6 ha with 144 planting rows (lines 141-145, 315-317). Monitoring used six rows at Sites 1-3 (lines 1071-1073), less than 5% of the 144 rows. Monitoring period: weekly measurements from 25 Nov 2022 to 10 Feb 2023, about three months, winter season (lines 326-328; 1036). Counting unit: fruit count per ripeness stage per row; yield in kg per week for the whole 0.6 ha.

ACQUISITION:
- Device: trolley-mounted scanning device pushed by a person riding it (lines 1143-1145); two USB cameras (ELP-USBFHD01M-L21-JP), 60 fps, zenith angle 60 degrees; trolley speed about 0.24 m/s (lines 164-197; 1084-1085).
- Views: double-camera capture of each row side, from outside the canopy; a single camera per side is used for the trend estimate (Discussion, lines 979-981; double-camera in title of source header).
- LED illuminance 540 lx at 30 cm; device depth 0.25 m; light exposure about one second per plant (lines 1084-1086).
- Frame-level processing: square 512x512 crops (lines 220-224, 344-345).
- Number of sides sampled per row and per-frame distance beyond the zenith angle: not stated in the parts read.

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR:
- Detector: Mask R-CNN with ResNet101 backbone, COCO pre-trained weights; single class (fruit) (lines 230-255). 1042 training images and 190 validation images; 500 epochs (lines 230-255).
- Input: 512x512 square crops (lines 220-224).
- Detection accuracy as reported: average precision (AP) at IoU 0.5 of 0.71-0.77 across weeks 1-12 (Table 2, lines 456-482; text line 437). Discussion gives "an average precision (AP) of about 70%" (Section 4, line 932-933).
- Precision and recall were reported as plots (Figure 6); numeric values in the parts read: not stated.
- Ripeness classification: hue-based, applied after detection, three bins 1-40%, 41-80%, 81-100%; green fruit (0%) excluded from the final count (lines 331-333; 353-354; 949-952).

ASSOCIATION METHOD:
- No tracker, no 3D reconstruction, no SfM or SLAM stated. Fruits are counted per image frame and summed per row and week.
- Double-camera views are used, but how a fruit seen by both cameras is matched across views is not stated in the parts read. The Discussion says identifying the same fruit across camera views "adds difficulty and significantly increases the processing time" (lines 970-972), and the authors use a single camera for the final system.
- Counting rule: count of detected fruit per ripeness bin per row per week (Figure 7-8).
- Counting approach for temporal correction: none. The authors propose tracking as future work (lines 1012-1017).

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS:
- Stated: the visible surface captured by the camera is the basis for classification (lines 991-992).
- Stated: "the system consistently underestimated actual fruit counts" and this "was likely due to occlusion, as only the surface of the fruit facing the camera is visible" (lines 963-966).
- Stated: stability of AP reflects "consistent appearance and condition of the crops" (lines 957-959).
- Stated: misclassification of 41-80% fruit into other stages is "likely" but the 1-40% and 41-80% misclassification is attributed to occlusion, "not due to errors in the hue-based classification algorithm itself" (lines 1002-1005).
- Stated: the illuminance from the device has a negligible physiological effect (lines 1082-1089).
- Implied: fruit size ratio is stable over the period, based on fruits above 160 g being over 80% of weekly yield (lines 1094-1096). Fruit size was not measured.

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED:
(a) Same fruit in consecutive frames: not addressed. The authors say tracking individual fruits over time with a monocular camera "may be effective" in future work (lines 1012-1013).
(b) Track lost and restarted: not applicable; no tracking.
(c) Same fruit from both sides or different passes: the double-camera design is intended to reduce occlusion (lines 968-973); how duplicate counts across the two cameras are removed is not stated in the parts read. The authors state that multi-view identity matching adds difficulty and processing time (lines 970-972).

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING:
- Classes: three ripeness stages by hue, 1-40%, 41-80%, 81-100% (lines 331-333). Green fruit (0%) detected but not counted (lines 353-354; 951-952).
- Class per fruit: assigned per detection from its hue; the authors state classification "based on hue" (line 949). Classification is per frame detection; there is no multi-frame vote.
- Frames disagree: not applicable (no tracking).
- Ripeness classifier accuracy: not stated as a number in the parts read. The authors state misclassification between 41-80% and 81-100% is likely (lines 991-1004).

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL:
- Reference: manual counts by the grower (lines 941-943 "those manually counted by the grower"), and manual annotation of images for detection training and evaluation (Mask R-CNN training labels; method section).
- Count error: linear regression of automatic count on manual count per ripeness stage, slopes 0.80 (1-40%), 0.19 (41-80%), 0.87 (81-100%), with Pearson r 0.96, 0.63, 0.92 (lines 486-489; 963-964).
- Yield reference: total yield in kg per week for the 0.6 ha area, from harvest records (Figure 8, Figure 10).
- Metrics: AP at IoU 0.5 (detection); Pearson r; slope; WAPE (weighted absolute percentage error) with leave-one-out cross-validation for yield prediction (Eqs. 2-3, lines 371-397). Identity metrics (MOTA, IDF1, HOTA, ID switches): not applicable; not used.

KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS:
- AP at IoU 0.5: 0.71-0.77 across weeks 1-12 (Table 2, lines 456-482); about 70% (Section 4).
- Manual vs automatic counts: r = 0.96, slope 0.80 (1-40%); r = 0.63, slope 0.19 (41-80%); r = 0.92, slope 0.87 (81-100%) (lines 486-489; 963-964).
- Next-week total yield vs average Nf (simple regression): r = 0.88 (1-40%), r = 0.94 (41-80%), not significant for 81-100% (r = 0.48) (lines 696-700; 1074-1075; 1041-1044).
- Multiple linear regression, WAPE (Table 3, lines 903-928):
  - ('1-40%', '41-80%'): WAPE 21.35
  - ('1-40%', '41-80%', '81-100%'): WAPE 24.06
  - ('41-80%'): WAPE 25.70
  - ('41-80%', '81-100%'): WAPE 28.70
  - ('1-40%', '81-100%'): WAPE 29.24
  - ('1-40%'): WAPE 38.53
  - ('81-100%'): WAPE 63.18
- Yield range: total yield exceeded 7000 kg in week 4 and was lowest in week 10 (lines 1018-1019).
- Fruits over 160 g were more than 80% of weekly yield, except weeks 5-7 (lines 1094-1096).

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED:
- Leaf occlusion; green fruit visually similar to leaves gives false detections and low precision (lines 932-953).
- Only the surface facing the camera is visible; this is given as the cause of underestimation (lines 963-966).
- Misclassification between ripeness stages, mainly 41-80% fruit labelled 81-100% when partly hidden (lines 1004-1010).
- Operational constraints in weeks 6-7 (holiday harvesting once a week) and solar radiation effects produced a spike in 81-100% counts in week 7 (lines 1027-1035).
- Only six rows monitored; representativeness of 0.6 ha not established (lines 1071-1081).
- Residuals above 4000 observed symmetrically for one combination (lines 899-902).

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA:
- Naito et al. [7] tomato AP approximately 0.8 under high occlusion; the authors state their result "was clearly lower" (lines 933-936). Different crop and data; no same-data comparison.
- Viveros Escamilla et al. [14] misclassification pattern for sweet pepper (lines 1008-1010), reported qualitatively.
- No numeric comparison on the same data.

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK:
- Newer instance segmentation models may be explored (lines 954-955).
- Model trained on colored fruit only might improve AP (lines 952-954).
- Multi-camera or tracking to resolve ripeness misclassification; cost and processing time noted (lines 968-1017).
- Assess yield variability across the full cultivation area (lines 1079-1081).
- Fruit size not measured; to be verified (lines 1090-1093).
- Trolley device requires a rider; mobile platform or suspended unit suggested (lines 1143-1145).

INCONSISTENCIES INSIDE THE PAPER:
- The Discussion says the counting performance was "sufficiently high" for colored fruit (line 950) and that the system "has a sufficient performance to serve as a substitute for manual counting" (lines 943-945). The 41-80% regression slope is 0.19 with r = 0.63 (lines 963-964; 486-489), and the text calls this "extremely low" (line 983). The authors nevertheless state that the system "consistently underestimated" counts and that the 41-80% undercount is not the cause of the poor fit (lines 990-993).
- The Conclusions call the system "useful for short-term yield prediction" (lines 1166-1168) while the best WAPE is 21.35% and the authors say it "does not represent a high prediction accuracy" (lines 1127-1128).
- Abstract and Conclusions give the best model as the two-stage combination; Table 3 agrees (lines 1097-1099; 910-911).
- Abstract AP and Discussion AP are both "about 70%" (line 932); Table 2 gives 0.71-0.77 (lines 456-482). These agree in range but use different rounding.
