ACQUISITION: (video along a path / discrete views / 3D scan / revisits / single images; camera, platform, frame rate, distance, sides of row)
- Discrete views: two images per tree, one from each side of the row (dual view), taken from a moving vehicle (sections 2.1 and 2.4; "images from two sides of one tree", Figure 8 caption, line 665).
- Camera and platform (section 2.1, lines 232-234, read before compaction): Basler acA2440 camera, 720 W LED lighting, vehicle speed 5 km/h.
- Dual-view background (introduction, lines 78-85, read before compaction): the paper cites a dual-view mango study [3] with an occlusion factor range of "1.05 to 2.43".
- Multiview acquisition is described in the introduction (lines 88-91) as one that "relies on a method to track and register individual fruit across frames." The authors did not implement a multiview method here; the multiview result is cited from [6] (lines 92-97).
- Frame rate and distance from camera to canopy: not stated in the sections read in this pass.

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR: (detector or segmenter and its version, input size, what it detects, detection accuracy as reported)
- MangoYOLO: a modified YOLOv3 with 33 layers (section 2.3.1 (i), lines 336-344, read before compaction). It detects and counts individual fruit as bounding boxes per image. Detection accuracy (AP or mAP) of MangoYOLO: not stated in the sections read in this pass.
- Xception_count: a CNN trained on MangoYOLO fruit counts per image, input 1024 x 1024 (section 2.3.1 (ii), lines 371-380, read before compaction). Trained to predict fruit count directly on images.
- Xception_classification: k-means clustering (n = 3) on global-average-pooled (GAP) features, with the cluster quality checked by the Silhouette score (section 2.3.2, lines 415-427, Table 3, read before compaction). Image categories a, b, c.
- Canopy-and-fruit reconstruction: each tree's two sides are combined into one image (section 2.4.2, lines 507-522, read before compaction). Partly occluded fruit are separated from fully visible fruit by ellipse fitting with eccentricity threshold 0.75 (section 2.4.2, lines 496-499, read before compaction).
- Count sources per tree used as features: fully visible, partly occluded, and total MangoYOLO counts (Table 4, read before compaction).

ASSOCIATION METHOD: (tracker, motion model, appearance features, 3D/SfM/SLAM, learned matching, statistical correction or calibration; counting rule e.g. unique ID or counting line; "none" if single images)
- None. The paper does not track or match individual fruit across frames or between the two sides of a tree. Dual-view counts are summed per tree from per-image counts.
- Statistical correction: the MangoYOLO_yield method multiplies the dual-view MangoYOLO count by an occlusion factor (harvest count divided by machine-vision count) estimated from a calibration set of sample trees. The 2017 ABC sample set provides the factor for 2017 (Table 11 caption, lines 934-936; section 3.5, lines 928-931).
- Direct yield models: Random_forest_yield (sklearn RandomForestRegressor with default settings, section 2.5.3, lines 598-599, read before compaction), MLP_yield (12-6-1, section 2.5.2, lines 564-570, read before compaction), Deep_yield (MangoYOLO, MLP, Regression, Xception_siamese, Xception_classification; section 2.5.4, lines 601-604), and Xception_yield (section 2.5.5, lines 647-655).

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS:
- Stated: the number of hidden fruit can be inferred from visible canopy features. The authors hypothesised that "the number of partly occluded fruit normalised to the number of visible fruit would be correlated to the ratio of fully occluded fruit normalised to the number of visible fruit" (section 3.3, lines 790-792).
- Stated: direct models trained on fruit number per tree avoid the need for a manual occlusion factor each season (conclusions, lines 1087-1089).
- Stated: a model trained on one season will generalise to another season if the canopy features that relate to hidden fruit are consistent. The authors attribute the failure to "variability in tree architecture and foliage density between seasons and between orchards" (conclusions, lines 1106-1109).
- Implied: the Random_forest_yield model is trained on per-tree counts from the same orchard and season, so its training-set result (R2 0.98) is not a test of generalisation (section 3.5, lines 1024-1029).
- Implied, from the Table 13 discussion: packhouse count of the whole orchard is treated as the reference for yield (section 3.5, lines 1030-1037).

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED: (a) same fruit in consecutive frames, (b) track lost and restarted, (c) same fruit from both sides or different passes — which, and how
- (a) Not applicable: single images per side, no consecutive frames.
- (b) Not applicable: no tracking.
- (c) Same fruit from both sides: the introduction states that "In sparse canopies, more fruits are visible, but a given fruit may be seen twice in images from both sides of the tree row, leading to a double count" (lines 78-81, read before compaction). The paper does not measure or correct this double count in its own dual-view data. Its dual-view counts are not deduplicated.
- The paper reports that in a multiview study [6], "the number of hidden fruits was balanced by the number of over counted fruits in the multiview method" (lines 92-97, read before compaction). This is cited from [6], not measured here.

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING: (classes; how a class is attached to a unique fruit; what happens when frames disagree; "none" if no classes)
- Image categories a, b, c from Xception_classification (k-means on GAP features, section 2.3.2). These are canopy-appearance categories per image, not maturity classes. The paper does not state a maturity or ripeness class per fruit.
- Each image gets one category. When the two sides of a tree disagree, the paper's Deep_yield model takes one category vector per side (section 2.5.4, lines 612-616). No rule for resolving a per-tree category is given.
- Categories are not attached to unique fruit. Not stated.

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL: (hand count on tree / harvest / packhouse / annotated images; count error, identity metrics MOTA/IDF1/HOTA/ID switches, detection metrics)
- Per tree: harvest count of sample trees (Table 11, Table 12). Harvest count is the sum of hidden (fully occluded), partly occluded and fully exposed fruit (Table 9 caption, lines 811-814).
- Per image: human count on images (Table 6, lines 713-715) and MangoYOLO count (Table 5 and Table 6).
- Per orchard block: packhouse count (Table 13).
- Metrics: slope, intercept, R2, RMSE (Tables 5, 6, 9, 11, 12); percentage error against packhouse count for orchard blocks (Table 13).
- No identity metrics (MOTA, IDF1, HOTA, ID switches) are used. No detection metrics (AP, mAP) for MangoYOLO are reported in the sections read in this pass.

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED: (bullets)
- Partly occluded fruit are not a usable proxy for hidden fruit: no visual canopy feature was found to correlate with hidden fruit count (conclusions, lines 1092-1096: "However, no such attribute cor-relates were identified.").
- Season change: the direct yield models trained on 2017 fail on 2018. Table 12 RMSE rises to 97.4 to 129.1 for the direct models, against 72.7 for MangoYOLO_yield (ABCx 2017) (Table 12).
- Canopy variability: "variability in tree architecture and foliage density between seasons and between orchards" (conclusions, lines 1106-1109).
- Dual-view under-count: the MangoYOLO count of two images per tree gives a slope of 0.44 (Table 11) and 0.32 (Table 12), "indicating that a large proportion of fruits were not visible in dual view imaging" (section 3.5, lines 926-928).
- Xception_count underestimates relative to MangoYOLO: "the Xception_count underestimated fruit load relative to the MangoYOLO model" (section 3.1, lines 680-681).
- Classification: confusion between categories a and b (Table 7 and Table 8; the authors say "a greater level of confusion exists between categories (a) and (b)", lines 785-787).
- Human workload: "initial workload is increased in this method in that many trees must be human counted for training of the models" (conclusions, lines 1090-1091).

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA:
- Yes, on the same 2017 and 2018 orchard A sample trees: MangoYOLO_yield versus four direct models (Table 11, Table 12; section 3.5, lines 1022-1029).
- Xception_count versus MangoYOLO on orchard A (Table 5 and Table 6).
- Whole-orchard packhouse comparison, 2017 (Table 13).
- Random_forest_yield is best on the 2017 training set (R2 0.98). In 2018, MangoYOLO_yield with the 2018 occlusion factor (ABC 2018 row, RMSE 69.0) is best, followed by Random_forest_yield (lines 1025-1028).
- Stein et al. [6] and the earlier canopy-volume study [1] are cited for the same orchard A (section 3.4, lines 912-917), not re-analysed here.

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK:
- "The direct yield estimation models were therefore not robust in pre-diction of a population of another season." (section 3.5, lines 1027-1029).
- "This result indicates that training on one season of data was insufficient for the development of a robust model." (conclusions, lines 1104-1106).
- Recommendation: "training of the yield estimation models across several seasons and orchards is recommended" (conclusions, lines 1109-1110).
- "While a robust method for estimate of total fruit load per tree from canopy images has not been demonstrated, several methods have been presented that should serve to extend the 'toolkit' used in machine learning based yield estimation methods." (conclusions, lines 1110-1113).

INCONSISTENCIES INSIDE THE PAPER:
- Reference-method R2: the abstract gives "a R2 of 0.68 for the reference method" (line 23). Table 11 gives R2 0.69 for MangoYOLO_yield (ABCx 2017) on the same training set. The paper does not say which is correct.
- Table 13 MangoYOLO_yield row: the listed counts do not match the bracketed percentage errors. Orchard A: "58074 (2.6)" against packhouse "97382". Computed from those two numbers, (58074 - 97382) / 97382 is about -40%, not 2.6%. Orchard B: "16189 (17.1)" against "26273" is about -38%. ABC: "91592 (13.6)" against "164492" is about -44%. The MLP, Random_forest, Deep and Xception rows are consistent with the same formula (for example MLP orchard A "93879 (-3.6)"). Computed by hand from the table values in this session, not verified against the source data. Not resolved in the paper.
- Section 3.5 text says the Random_forest_yield model "provided the best estimate for both orchard A and the combined ABC orchards, with a 2.5 and 2.3% error" (lines 1032-1033). Table 13 agrees (2.5 and 2.3).
- Abstract "1.6% for the MLP model" matches Table 13 (ABC 167052 (1.6)).
