KEY: hong2025fruitgaussian
Title: FruitGaussian: 3D Gaussian Splatting for Automated Fruit Counting in Natural Orchard. Hong, Qin, Shi, Wu (USTC). Proc. 44th Chinese Control Conference, 28-30 July 2025, Chongqing.

CROP / SETTING / SCALE: Persimmon (cultivar 'Taishuu'), Feidong County, Hefei, Anhui, photographed in September (harvest season). Yellow-green fruit overlaps foliage (Fig. 2). Scale: one orchard scene reconstructed per dataset; scene size and tree count not stated.

ACQUISITION: Discrete multi-view images, not video. Nikon D750, 24 mm lens, f/8. 400 RGB images at 4000x6000 px, taken from 12 azimuthal positions within a 1 m radius, each with 3 vertical tiers spaced 0.5 m apart (Sec. 2.1). Camera poses and sparse points from COLMAP (SfM). No frame rate, platform, distance to tree, or sides of row stated beyond the radius and tiers.

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR: No per-image fruit detector drives the count. Fruit is segmented in 3D on the Gaussian point cloud. Grounding DINO [11] and SAM 2 [12] are named as "Grounded SAM2" in the read text; their role in the counting path is not stated. Depth Anything V2 [13] gives monocular depth, scale-aligned to SfM sparse depth by least squares (Eq. 6-7). Detection or segmentation accuracy of these 2D models: not stated. Fruit/sky/others semantic classes are predicted per Gaussian (Sec. 2.2.2).

ASSOCIATION METHOD: Multi-view 3D reconstruction, not cross-view identity linking. 3D Gaussian Splatting [14] with a 16-dimensional semantic embedding per Gaussian, a linear classification head (Eq. 5), depth regularization against Depth Anything V2 depth (Fig. 4), and "dynamic illumination compensation and adaptive point cloud density modulation" (Sec. 3, named; no ablation seen). Counting rule: Gaussian centroids form a point cloud; fruit instances are formed by semantic-guided RGB thresholding, then DBSCAN [19] (Sec. 2.3). Sec. 3 links counting accuracy to DBSCAN clustering. The explicit counting rule (number of clusters) is not written out as a formula in the read text. Matching of the same fruit across views: not described in the read text. Whether one cluster can correspond to one fruit seen from several views is not discussed.

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS: Static scene (the text says the static-scene assumption fails under wind). Fruit point clouds are "highly cohesive" enough for DBSCAN (Sec. 3). Sufficient SfM coverage of the orchard is needed; sparse reconstruction "insufficient for direct dense depth estimation in few-shot settings" (Sec. 2.2.2). Depth Anything V2 depth is assumed scale-alignable to SfM points.

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED: (a) same fruit in consecutive frames: not applicable (no video). (b) track lost and restarted: not applicable; the paper criticises Kalman tracking [4] for drift under occlusion (Intro) but does not use tracking. (c) same fruit from different views or passes: not stated as a topic. The paper does not discuss double counting across the 12 x 3 views and reports no double-count rate.

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING: Classes are sky, fruit, and others (Sec. 2.2.2). No maturity or ripeness classes. Class is assigned per Gaussian, so each fruit cluster carries the fruit label by construction; what happens when views disagree is not applicable in the 3D model and not discussed.

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL: Ground truth is a hand count on the orchard: "We determined the actual total number of fruits by manually counting them." (Sec. 2.1). The unit (per tree, per scene, per view) is not stated. Metric: "counting accuracy" (96% reported); its formula is not given. No MOTA, IDF1, HOTA, or ID-switch metrics. Rendering metrics: PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS (Table 1).

KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS:
- Counting accuracy: 96% (Abstract; Sec. 3; Conclusion). Gap to NeRF baselines stated as 16 % (Abstract), 16 percentage points (Sec. 3), 16% higher (Conclusion).
- Fig. 8 counting bars: numbers 24 (Ours), 20 (FruitNeRF), 22 (FruitNeRF-big), 25 (GroundTruth) as read from the figure text. Which bar each number belongs to is inferred from the label order; the text does not map them explicitly. Count reference: unit not stated.
- Table 1 (rendering): FruitNeRF PSNR 14.57, SSIM 0.44, LPIPS 0.71; FruitNeRF-big 14.40, 0.43, 0.72; Base 3DGS 16.21, 0.49, 0.54; Gaussian Grouping 16.15, 0.50, 0.58; Ours 16.56, 0.54, 0.49.
- LPIPS: "a 30.99% relative improvement over FruitNeRF variants in perceptual similarity" (Sec. 3).
- Speed: point cloud export in "approximately a few seconds", described as two-order-of-magnitude acceleration over FruitNeRF's 7-minute duration (Sec. 3). Training: 30k iterations on one RTX 4090D 24 GB; FruitNeRF-big 100k iterations, 3 h (Sec. 3).
- Split: 9:1 train-test split by images, "across all compared methods" (Sec. 3); no tree-level split stated.

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED:
- Dynamic vegetation motion (wind) breaks the static-scene assumption (Conclusion / limitations).
- "Structural homogeneity and texture repetition in large-scale orchards challenge SfM with unordered images (only 30% success rate in our field tests)." (limitations, lines 401-520 of the text file; section number not recorded).
- Sparse SfM depth: 0.04% valid pixels on average for 19 images (Sec. 2.2.2, citing [18]).

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA: Base 3DGS, Gaussian Grouping, FruitNeRF, FruitNeRF-big on the same persimmon scenes (Table 1; Fig. 6; Fig. 8). Counting comparison is only against FruitNeRF and FruitNeRF-big (Fig. 8). No comparison to video trackers, SfM-only counting, or any other fruit counter.

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK: Static-scene assumption; structural homogeneity challenges SfM; "handling dynamic vegetation motion and texture repetition in large-scale orchards" (Conclusion). Future: temporally-aware Gaussians [20]; hybrid 3DGS and SLAM [21, 22]; inverse rendering for peduncle junctions [23]. Quote: "Future work will explore spatiotemporal Gaussian representations and SLAM-3DGS fusion to enhance robustness." (Conclusion).

INCONSISTENCIES INSIDE THE PAPER:
