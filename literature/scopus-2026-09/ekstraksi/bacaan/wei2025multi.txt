KEY: wei2025multi (Wei, Kevric, Zach, Stelldinger, Rose, "Multi-Object Tracking for Apple Counting in Orchards Using Stereo Vision", 2025 IEEE MetroAgriFor, pp. 196-201)

CROP / SETTING / SCALE: Apple (Malus), orchard trees in the experimental fields of Esteburg, Altes Land near Hamburg, Germany. Four cultivars for detector training (Elstar, Wellant, Rockit, Pia 41; line 165). Tracking evaluation on Wellant and Elstar. Count validation on 30 Elstar trees, 2024 season (lines 170-172, 182-183, 519-522). Single site, single season stated.

ACQUISITION: Stereo pair of Basler ace 2 Pro cameras (a2A5328-15ucPRO, 24.4 MP, up to 15 fps capable) mounted on a tractor three-point hitch (lines 132-146). Tractor speed 1 m/s (line 173-174). Image resolution 4608 x 5328 at 5 fps (lines 174-175). Camera-to-tree distance about 1.5 m, maximum canopy height 3 m (lines 144-146). Video along a tree row; both sides of the row are imaged (front and rear viewpoints, lines 98-100; "both sides of each tree", line 525). RTK-GNSS (Septentrio simpleRTK3B Heading, mosaic-H, two multiband antennas) for georeferencing (lines 147-150). Jetson AGX Orin on board; training on RTX 6000 Ada workstation (lines 150-156). Stereo calibration in the field with PuzzleBoard (lines 176-178). A LiDAR is part of the framework but used for validation only (lines 138-140).

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR: YOLOv8x, pretrained on COCO and fine-tuned on the self-collected set (lines 268-271). Input size: not stated. Annotations: 200 images with bounding boxes for detection; 131 consecutive frames of Wellant and Elstar labelled with trajectories for tracking (lines 178-181). Augmentation with Albumentations; hyperparameters by random search in Ray Tune (lines 272-279). Detection accuracy as reported: mAP0.5:0.95 = 0.56 under "k-fold cross-validation, with the dataset split into 80% for training and 20% for validation" (lines 392-395); F1 = 0.78 (line 398). Detection is also used to find trees only indirectly; tree positions are not detected (lines 311-319).

ASSOCIATION METHOD:
- Tracking: ByteTrack and OC-SORT, both Kalman-filter based with Hungarian assignment (lines 289-297). OC-SORT adds a motion-consistency (velocity) cost term (lines 297-303). Optical flow from DVSO is added as a velocity measurement in the Kalman filter with noise set from certainty values (lines 303-307). Tracker run with and without optical flow on two datasets (Table I).
- 2D-to-3D: optical flow from a customised PWC-Net within self-supervised deep visual stereo odometry (DVSO) gives flow, dense depth and camera odometry (lines 208-246). Depth from disparity: z = -f·b/d (Eq. 1, lines 220-233). Camera pose: Unscented Kalman Filter fusing GNSS and visual odometry, giving global position and rotation (lines 252-266). Apple centres back-projected with K^-1 and the mean bounding-box depth (Eq. 2), then transformed to the global frame (Eq. 3).
- Fruit-to-tree: each apple assigned to the tree with the smallest Euclidean distance (Eq. 4). Tree positions recorded once per tree with a GNSS pole (lines 313-319).
- Counting rule: "Potential duplicate counts are mitigated using unique IDs generated by the object tracker" (lines 371-373). Validation additionally matched counts from both sides of each tree by GPS with a distance threshold of 70-85 mm (lines 525-531). The paper does not state explicitly which of these two rules produced the final 555 count.

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS:
- Stereo rig is well rectified and the baseline is fixed, so the relative transform is constant (lines 217-219).
- Tree positions are constant over years and need registration only once (lines 316-317).
- Apple diameter at pre-harvest is 70-85 mm, used as the duplicate distance threshold (lines 527-529).
- Linear motion assumption is a limitation that optical flow mitigates (lines 119-122).
- Velocity initial estimates are pre-set by hyperparameter tuning; without that, tracks degrade (lines 484-490).
- Overestimation is "assumed" to come from identity switches and from green Pia 41 apples being detected as leaves (lines 546-553).
- Harvest machine count (Pluk-O-Trak) is treated as ground truth, to avoid hand-count bias (lines 519-524).

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED:
(a) Same fruit in consecutive frames: addressed by tracker IDs (lines 371-373); tracking accuracy measured as HOTA, MOTA, IDF1 (Table I).
(b) Track lost and restarted: not addressed explicitly. Identity switches are named as a probable cause of overcounting (lines 546-550); the paper gives no re-identification mechanism.
(c) Same fruit from both sides or different passes: addressed by 3D projection to a global frame (lines 582-586) and by GPS matching of the two-side counts with a distance threshold (lines 525-531). The text also says many apples were visible from one side only, so duplicate impact was smaller (lines 543-545). Per-tree split of duplicates removed by each rule is not stated.

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING: None reported. Detector output is apple instances; four cultivars are in training data (line 165); cultivar-specific class handling is not described. Green Pia 41 apples were a source of false positives (lines 551-553). Maturity is not classified.

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL:
- Reference: machine count by the Pluk-O-Trak harvest platform, 535 fruits across 30 Elstar trees (lines 519-523, 536). This is a harvest-stage reference, not a hand count on tree and not packhouse data.
- Count metrics: total error (net) of 20 apples; RMSE of 6.24 % "per tree" (basis of the percentage not stated); R2 = 0.96 (lines 537-542).
- Identity metrics: HOTA, MOTA, IDF1 and "DeTA" (DeTA is not expanded in the text) on the 131 labelled frames (Table I).
- Detection metric: mAP0.5:0.95 and F1.

KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS:
- Abstract: OC-SORT with optical flow, HOTA = 0.59, MOTA = 0.52, IDF1 = 0.67; detector mAP0.5:0.95 = 0.56; count error 20 apples, RMSE 6.24 %, R2 = 0.96 (abstract, p. 196).
- Table I, OC-SORT with optical flow: Wellant HOTA 0.64, MOTA 0.64, IDF1 0.70, DeTA 0.65; Elstar HOTA 0.59, MOTA 0.53, IDF1 0.69, DeTA 0.51 (Table I, p. 200).
- Table I, OC-SORT without optical flow: Wellant HOTA 0.61, MOTA 0.68, IDF1 0.74, DeTA 0.64; Elstar HOTA 0.59, MOTA 0.52, IDF1 0.67, DeTA 0.52.
- Table I, ByteTrack with optical flow: Wellant HOTA 0.63, MOTA 0.63, IDF1 0.70, DeTA 0.63; Elstar HOTA 0.58, MOTA 0.52, IDF1 0.67, DeTA 0.50.
- Table I, ByteTrack without optical flow: Wellant HOTA 0.59, MOTA 0.62, IDF1 0.66, DeTA 0.63; Elstar HOTA 0.58, MOTA 0.51, IDF1 0.67, DeTA 0.50.
- Count validation (30 Elstar trees): 535 machine-counted fruits; 619 estimated before threshold, 555 after threshold; net difference 20 (lines 536-540).
- Dataset difference: Elstar has denser canopy and severe occlusion (lines 407-410).

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED:
- Low acquisition rate (5 fps) makes Kalman initialisation random and degrades tracks (lines 478-483).
- Identity switches (IDF1 0.69 on Elstar) assumed to cause overcounting (lines 546-550).
- Green Pia 41 apples resemble small leaves and cause false positives (lines 551-553).
- Dense canopies: detection of trees struggles even when trunks are visible (lines 311-313); apples occluded by more than 90 % were annotated (line 396).
- Elstar harder for detection than Wellant, shown by lower DeTA (lines 491-493).
- Manual tree-level registration is required, and tree detection in winter is an untested alternative (lines 316-319).

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA: ByteTrack vs OC-SORT, each with and without optical flow, on the same Wellant and Elstar tracking data (Table I). Text states the no-flow tracker "still achieved similar results" because initial velocities were pre-set by tuning (lines 483-486). No comparison of the 3D association against a 2D counting baseline is reported. Detector mAP is said to be "consistent with results reported in [4], [16], [17]" (lines 400-401). Preliminary detector comparison (YOLOv8, RT-DETR, Faster R-CNN, YOLOv5) is cited to a source (see INCONSISTENCIES, item 4).

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK:
- "additional data must be collected and annotated to thoroughly evaluate the generalizability of the framework" (lines 588-590).
- LiDAR-labelled 3D fruit data would allow 3D tracking that "potentially" removes the reliance on 2D trackers (lines 591-594).
- Full LiDAR integration is impractical due to cost (lines 138-140).
- Validation limited to Elstar, the only cultivar harvested with the Pluk-O-Trak in 2024 (lines 519-522).

INCONSISTENCIES INSIDE THE PAPER:
1. Abstract vs Table I: the abstract gives OC-SORT with optical flow as HOTA 0.59, MOTA 0.52, IDF1 0.67 (abstract, p. 196). Table I gives OC-SORT with optical flow on Elstar as HOTA 0.59, MOTA 0.53, IDF1 0.69 (p. 200). The abstract MOTA and IDF1 match OC-SORT without optical flow on Elstar (0.52, 0.67). The text on p. 200 quotes IDF1 0.69 (line 550), which matches Table I, not the abstract.
2. Text vs Table I: "OC-SORT achieved the highest HOTA score of 0.64 across all datasets" (lines 411-412). The 0.64 value is Wellant only; Elstar with optical flow is 0.59.
3. Conclusion vs Table I: "OC-SORT with optical flow outperformed the other methods across all benchmarking metrics" (lines 577-578). On Wellant, OC-SORT with optical flow ties ByteTrack with optical flow on IDF1 (0.70 vs 0.70), and OC-SORT without optical flow has higher MOTA (0.68 vs 0.64) and IDF1 (0.74 vs 0.70) on Wellant.
4. Citation: reference [6] is Cao et al., OC-SORT (ref list, p. 201). The text cites [6] for a preliminary study comparing Faster R-CNN, YOLOv5, YOLOv8 and RT-DETR on apples (lines 79-82), for single-image counting errors (lines 86-89), and for Kalman and Hungarian methods (lines 93-96). The first use matches OC-SORT in the reference list (lines 115-116); the others do not match reference [6] as written.
5. Method: detector validation is described as "k-fold cross-validation" (line 394) but the same sentence gives one 80/20 train/validation split.
6. Naming: the stereo odometry method is "DVSO" (line 212) and "DSVO" (line 304).
