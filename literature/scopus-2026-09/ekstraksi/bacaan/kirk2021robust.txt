KEY: kirk2021robust (strawberry counting with a Thorvald robot; tracking and counting multiple classes in clusters). Author list and year not re-checked against the title page in this pass. Extracted text: lines 1-526 read; references from line 526 skipped.

CROP / SETTING / SCALE: Strawberries in table-top rows; robot platform Thorvald (Univ. of Lincoln, Riseholme, as stated in the paper). Sequences of 500 frames each. Test sequence Amesti-1 (12219 boxes, 233 tracklets). Other sequences: Amesti-2 (4895 boxes, 172 tracklets), Katrina-1 (15850 boxes, 299 tracklets), Katrina-2 (14507 boxes, 326 tracklets). Split: train on Katrina-1, Katrina-2, Amesti-2; test on Amesti-1 (Sec. 3.3).

ACQUISITION: Video from a moving robot along a fruit row. Camera: Intel RealSense D435i, resolution 1920x1080, frame rate 7 Hz. Robot speed printed in the extracted text as 0.1 and 0.2 with unit "ms" (extraction artefact; the unit is not confirmed from the source layout). Recorded in September. Robot odometry gives position along the row; no ego-motion information is assumed beyond that (Sec. 3.1). Side of row and number of passes: not stated in the body read.

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR: Detector name, version and input size: not stated in the body read. Fig. 2 shows input as bounding-box detections plus images. Faster R-CNN, RetinaNet and YOLOv3 appear only in related work as background; the paper does not state that any of them was used here. Detection accuracy: not reported as a detector metric in the body read; the tracking results are the reported measures.

ASSOCIATION METHOD: Tracking-by-detection with a 10-dimensional Kalman filter state (u, v, gamma, h, r and their velocities) under a constant-velocity model. Camera not calibrated; no ego-motion estimate; odometry row position r is used as an input (Sec. 3.1, lines 146-152). Association gate: squared Mahalanobis distance thresholded at chi-square 11.07 for 10 degrees of freedom (Eq. 1-2). Appearance: minimum cosine distance between the new detection embedding and a gallery of the last 100 observations of each track (Eq. 3). Class cost: cosine distance over a gallery of label probabilities (Eq. 4). Combined cost m = lambda d(1) + sum (1 - lambda) d(p), gated by the product of indicators (Eq. 5-6). Assignment with the Hungarian algorithm in a DeepSORT-style matching cascade. Embedding: RNN re-ID feature stem (Fig. 3) producing a 128-dimensional embedding, with two heads (re-ID and maturity/label); 825,152 trainable parameters; 412 microseconds per bounding box on a GTX 3090 at batch size 128 (Sec. 3.2). Counting rule: the total number of unique associated track IDs gives the count (Sec. 3).

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS:
- No ego-motion information is available; odometry gives only the robot position along the fruit row (Sec. 3.1, lines 146-148).
- Constant-velocity motion model for the Kalman filter (Sec. 3.1).
- Class appearance is stable enough for a label-probability cost; the paper notes that class appearance can change under occlusion (lines 187-191).

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED: (a) same fruit in consecutive frames: addressed by the Kalman filter with squared Mahalanobis gating and appearance matching. (b) track lost and restarted: addressed by the appearance gallery of the last 100 observations, used to re-identify a track after it is lost. (c) same fruit from both sides or different passes: not stated; the paper does not describe a multi-pass or two-sided rule.

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING: Three classes annotated: Ripe (more than 85% red coverage), Flower (white petals with no calyx shown), Unripe (small to large immature green calyx visible) (Sec. 3.3). Classes are attached through a label-probability gallery and the class cost (Eq. 4-6); the combined cost fuses identity and class. When frames disagree: the paper describes the class cost gating and the occlusion-induced appearance change (lines 187-191), but a voting or resolution rule for disagreeing frames is not stated in the body read.

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL: Count reference: hand-labelled tracks on Amesti-1 (233 tracklets); annotation by manual labelling with expert knowledge (Sec. 3.3). Metric: L1 error of the per-frame count summed over 500 frames of Amesti-1 (Eq. 9). The abstract reports MAPE, but the MAPE formula is not given in the body read. Identity metrics (MOTA, IDF1, HOTA, ID switches) and detection metrics (AP, mAP): not stated in the body read.

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED:
- Occlusion: the paper's stated motivation for tracking and the cause of track loss.
- Mahalanobis gating fails under small angular or Cartesian camera movement (lines 175-178).
- Class appearance changes under occlusion, so class labels can flip (lines 187-191).
- Pad-16 has the highest agnostic L1 (86) among the tested variants in Table 1.

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA: Baseline detect-to-track multi-object tracking (Table 1) on Amesti-1. Other variants (Square, Sub-Net, Pad-8, Pad-16, Pad-32, Pad-64, Combined) are input-handling variants of the proposed system. Related-work counting numbers are quoted as background only: mango SfM mean error 27.8 [11]; oranges and apples per-frame L2 13.7 and 10.5 [4]; sequence counting L1 203 oranges and 322 apples [12]. These are not on the same data.

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK (Conclusion, lines 512-516): more evaluation; more tracking cues; different feature extractors; class embeddings; extension to full 3D through depth data or SfM.
