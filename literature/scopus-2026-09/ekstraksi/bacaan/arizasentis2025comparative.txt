KEY: arizasentis2025comparative (Ariza-Sentís, Baja, Vélez, van Essen, Valente, 2025, Journal of Agriculture and Food Research 19, 101736; "Comparative analysis of single-view and multiple-view data collection strategies for detecting partially-occluded grape bunches: Field trials")

CROP / SETTING / SCALE: Grape (white variety; bunches under leaf canopy), commercial vineyard in Spain-region context (Bodegas Terras Gauda acknowledged), no leaf removal. Video of one row side; 5 videos per trial. 5,958 frames manually annotated (PNG). Flights 19-20 Sept 2023. Scale: 3 m above ground; number of vines/bunches in total not stated.

ACQUISITION: UAV video along the canopy side, DJI Phantom 4 RTK with integrated camera (sensor resolution not stated in text). Flight altitude 3 m AGL; speed not stable, 0.1-1 m/s; sunny, wind < 0.5 m/s. Two methods: (1) single-view, UAV flown along side of canopy with no enhancement; (2) multiple-view, three perspectives per plant (frontal, left, right) with a customised route planner (Fig. 2, adapted from ref. 25). Sample: 5 videos per method, same plants where stated (Fig. 5). Camera and frame rate not stated.

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR: PointTrack (Spatial Embeddings for instance segmentation plus a tracking component), trained on MOTS-style masks; transfer learning from prior work; Adam, lr 5e-5, finetune lr 5e-6; SE batch 20, 1200 epochs; tracking batch 32, 100 epochs. Input crops 256x512 (initial) and 512x1024 (finetune), vertically oriented. Hyperparameters tuned "up and down until no further improvement". Detection accuracy reported as MOTSA/sMOTSA/MODSA/MODSP (Table 1), not as mAP or AP. Ratio of tracked to GT detections: 74% (multi) vs 23% (single) in abstract.

ASSOCIATION METHOD: PointTrack tracking component (learned, MOT-based); IDs persist across frames. Counting is per video: predicted bunch count vs annotated count (relative difference, Fig. 4; numbers not given in text). Ground-truth ID continuity annotated by hand (ref. 43 procedure). No 3D reconstruction, SfM, or cross-view matching described: the three views are used as training/inference video segments, and the phenotype measurements use the best of the three views per bunch (Section 3.4). How a bunch seen in multiple views is linked across views is not described.

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS: Occlusion is the main cause of missed detections; a fruit "completely visible from at least one of the three views" can be measured (Section 3.4). Continuity of ID across frames is needed for accurate metrics (Section 4.1). Training on a combined single+multi dataset helps generalisation to multi-view (Section 3.1). Trials assume the same video number is comparable across methods (Section 3.1).

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED: (a) same fruit in consecutive frames: addressed by tracking and ID persistence; ID switch counted when a mask changes ID vs previous frame (Eq. 3-4). (b) track lost and restarted: named as a problem: "motion blur from UAV movement, causing the camera to lose track of the fruit, and impacting the continuity of ID numbers through frames" (Section 4, bunches 5, 6, 7). Discontinuous tracks were tested and gave lower metrics (Section 4.1). (c) same fruit from different views/passes: not addressed as a counting rule. The count is compared with GT per video; the paper does not state how views are merged to avoid counting one bunch three times. Not stated.

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING: none. Single class (grape bunch). Phenotypic traits OIV 202 (length) and OIV 203 (width) are measured per bunch on 10 bunches (not classes).

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL: Count reference: annotated frames (GT masks in video), not hand count on tree or harvest. Metrics: MOTSP/MODSP (Eq. 1-2, text and table naming inconsistent), MOTSA and sMOTSA (Eq. 3-4; ID switches counted), MODSA (Table 1). IoU threshold 0.5 for TP. Count error: relative difference per video (Fig. 4, figure only). Phenotype: R2 and RMSE of bunch length/width in pixels against GT (10 bunches, Fig. 6). No MOTA, IDF1, HOTA.

KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS:
- Tracked/GT detection ratio: 74% multi-view vs 23% single-view (Abstract; Conclusion, Section 5).
- Discussion ratio ranges: single-view 13% to 23%; multi-view 27% to 74% (Section 4, p. 5).
- Table 1, single-view sMOTSA: video 1 -1.18 (trial 1 and 2), video 2 -0.08 (trial 1 and 2), trial 3 video 1 -7.51, video 2 -0.9.
- Table 1, multi-view sMOTSA: trial 1 videos 3-5: -16.21, -4.14, -2.62; trial 2 videos 3-5: -5.74, -2.06, -2.2; trial 3 videos 3-5: -7.08, -1.35, 0.69.
- Table 1, multi-view MOTSA trial 3 videos 3-5: -4.7, 0.3, 3.44. MODSA trial 3 videos 3-5: -4.14, 0.68, 3.84.
- Phenotype (Section 3.4, Section 4): R2 = 0.53, RMSE = 19.13 (multi-view) vs R2 = 0.39, RMSE = 28.05 (single-view); units pixels, n = 10 bunches.
- MODSP (Table 1, multi-view trial 3 video 5): 70.8.

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED:
- Occlusion by leaves; fully hidden fruit not detectable from any view (Introduction).
- Motion blur from UAV movement breaks ID continuity, esp. bunches 5, 6, 7 (Section 4).
- False positives from colour similarity and varying illumination/shadows (Section 4). Table 1 negatives "due to the occurrence of false positives" (Section 3.1).
- White bunches are visually homogeneous with the green canopy, making annotation hard (Section 4.1).
- Undercounting in most videos (Fig. 4 caption: "The values are negative due to undercounting").

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA: Single-view vs multi-view on the same vineyard, same UAV and altitude (Section 2, 3). Three trials differ by training data: trial 1 multi-view only, trial 2 single-view only, trial 3 combined. Within each trial, single-view and multi-view videos are evaluated with the same algorithm. Comparison with prior work only qualitative (ref. 32, Ariza-Sentís et al.; similar false-positive issues).

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK: Single vineyard, one variety (white); future work on other latitudes, red varieties, other fruit species (Section 4.1, Conclusions). Motion blur for multi-view tracking. Dataset publicly available (ref. 29). Future work to validate broader applicability.

INCONSISTENCIES INSIDE THE PAPER:
- Single/multi ratio: Abstract "74 % compared to 23 %" vs Section 4 "13 % to 23 %" (single) and "27 % to 74 %" (multi). 74% and 23% are the top/one value, not the general range; the Abstract and Conclusion present them as the headline comparison.
- Best trial: Section 3.1 "for the multiple-view dataset, Trial 3 exhibited the highest metrics" vs Section 4 "with the highest metrics observed in the second trial".
- Trial 3 claim: Section 3.1 "Trial 3 exhibited the highest metrics (positive values for all sMOTSA, MOTSA and MODSA)" vs Table 1 trial 3 video 4 sMOTSA = -1.35 and video 3 = -7.08.
- Abstract range: "multiple viewing trials metrics ranging from −1.35 to 3.84 for MOTSA ... and sMOTSA" vs Table 1: multi-view sMOTSA range is -16.21 to 0.69; MOTSA range is -12.92 to 3.44; 3.84 appears only in MODSA (trial 3 video 5). The abstract range mixes metrics.
- Figure reference for correlation: Section 3.4 cites Fig. 6 for the R2/RMSE correlation; Section 4 cites "Fig. 5 further supported ... increased correlation ... R2 of 0.39 ... 0.53". Fig. 5 is the plant image comparison.
- Metric names: text line "MODSP (Multiple Object Detection and Segmentation Precision)" vs Eq. 2 labelled "MODSA" and Table 1 header "MODSP" column vs values labelled MODSA.
- Single-view best: Section 3.1 says "Trial 2 achieved the highest ... equal to −0.08" for single-view; Table 1 shows video 2 is -0.08 in trial 1 too.
