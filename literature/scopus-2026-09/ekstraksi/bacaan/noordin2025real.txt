CROP / SETTING / SCALE: Oil palm fresh fruit bunches (FFB), Malaysia (UMPSA). Handheld edge device on a Raspberry Pi 4 Model B, field use on trees and on the ground. Scale not stated (no count of fruits per tree or per plot).

ACQUISITION: Discrete still images, not video. Raspberry Pi Camera Module V2 (8 MP) on CSI. The operator presses a button to log each classification. Two scenarios: FFB on the tree (pre-harvest, camera aimed at the canopy) and FFB on the ground (camera pointed downward, post-harvest). Camera frame rate, distance in metres and number of sides of a row: not stated. Field test: "two groups, each scanning FFB both on the ground and on the trees" (Section III.B); number of images or fruits in that test not stated.

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR: Ultralytics-style YOLOv8n ("pre-trained YOLOv8n models that are fine-tuned on a custom dataset", Section II.D). Ultralytics package version not stated. Input resized from 3024x4032 to 640x640. Augmentation: horizontal flip, 90-degree rotations, random crop (0 to 20 % zoom), grayscale for 15 % of images (Table 3). The detector outputs a bounding box and a ripeness class for each fruit. Quantization and "hardware acceleration" are named in the abstract, but no method or setting for them is described in Section II. Dataset: 4,194 annotated images, split 3,681 train / 348 validation / 165 test (88 / 8 / 4 %; Table 2). Annotation tool: Roboflow. Reported accuracy: average precision (AP) 0.898, mAP 0.952, average recall (AR) 0.945 (Section III.A). The split on which these metrics were computed is not stated; Figure 3 is described as covering "both the training and validation datasets". No per-class AP is given. Detection accuracy on the on-site test is not quantified (Section III.B is qualitative). Why YOLOv8n was chosen: lightweight variant for edge deployment, "to address this challenge" of GPU requirements (Section I). No frames-per-second figure is given, although the paper calls the system "real-time".

ASSOCIATION METHOD: None. Each image is classified on its own, and each button press logs one record (class, GPS coordinates when in GPS mode, temperature, humidity, pressure) to a JSON file. No tracker, no motion model, no appearance matching, no 3D reconstruction, no counting rule, no unique-ID scheme.

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS: Each logged detection is treated as one fruit event ("ensures that the location of each fruit detection event is accurately recorded", Section II.B). The authors assume that the operator logs each distinct fruit once; the paper gives no procedure for this. Dashboard use assumes that the logged events show "the spatial distribution of scanned fruits across the plantation" (Section III.D).

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED: (a) same fruit in consecutive frames: not addressed (the system processes single frames and does not track). (b) track lost and restarted: not applicable. (c) same fruit from both sides or different passes: not addressed.

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING: Three classes: kurang masak (unripe / under-ripe), masak (ripe), terlalu masak (overripe) (Section II.D). Results text uses "ripe, under ripe, and over ripe" (Section III.A). Class is assigned per image or per detection by the detector and logged once per button press. No handling of frames that disagree. The authors' own notion of a "clear" class boundary is not defined; no class definition by colour or time after anthesis is given.

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL: No count reference (no hand count on the tree, no harvest weight, no packhouse count). Metrics are detection metrics on the image dataset only (AP, mAP, AR, and precision/recall at Section III.C). No identity metric (MOTA, IDF1, HOTA, ID switches). No count error.

KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS:
- Dataset 4,194 images; 3,681 / 348 / 165 train / val / test (Abstract; Section II.D; Table 2).
- AP 0.898; mAP 0.952; AR 0.945 "for the ripe, under ripe and over ripe classes" (Abstract; Section III.A).
- Section III.C: "The proposed YOLOv8n model achieved a precision of 0.952 and a recall of 0.945" (same number 0.952 used as mAP in III.A).
- Table 4 (PC-based simulation, different data): Herman et al. [8] DenseNet P 0.87 R 0.86; Purba et al. [35] YOLOv8 P 0.98 R 0.83; Harmiansyah et al. [36] YOLOv5 P 0.87 R 0.95; Japar et al. [33] YOLOv4 P 0.97 R 0.81; Mamat et al. [37] YOLOv5 P 0.98 R 0.98; proposed YOLOv8n P 0.952 R 0.945.
- Human accuracy for ripeness "typically ranges between 85-90 %" (cited as [21]; Section III.A).
- Cited comparison of DenseNet vs AlexNet: 8.5 % accuracy gain (Introduction, citing [8]).

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED:
- Tall trees: detection "up to an eye-level height of approximately six feet"; "the accuracy decreased for taller trees" (Section III.B).
- Cause given by authors: "difficulty in capturing clear images of the fruits, which are often obscured by foliage or situated at angles that are not ideal for the camera" (Section III.B).
- Lighting: poor lighting and shadows named as challenges (Section IV), but no test result is given for them.
- Higher fruit clusters in complex environments (Section III.F).
- Hardware: Raspberry Pi processing limits and heat under tropical conditions (Section III.F).
- Occlusion and clustering: stated as a risk for the EfficientDet-Lite2 work [11] (Introduction), not tested here.
- Small or frond-covered bunches as a separate class of failure: not discussed; the only occlusion mention is the foliage cause above.
- Confusion between neighbouring ripeness classes: not reported (no confusion matrix; per-class metrics absent).

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA: None. Table 4 compares against published numbers from other studies with different datasets, classes, and methods, and is labelled "PC-based simulation" for all rows. The authors state the comparison is "competitive", not like-for-like.

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK: Higher camera resolution; more training data for lighting conditions, shadows and multiple fruit types; telescopic cameras or a "calibration method that accounts for varying tree heights" (Section III.F); cooling or more robust edge hardware; disease monitoring (Section IV).

INCONSISTENCIES INSIDE THE PAPER:
- The value 0.952 is called "mAP" in the Abstract ("a mean average precision (mAP) of 0.952") and in Section III.A ("The model achieves a mean average precision (mAP) of 0.952"), but called "precision" in Section III.C ("The proposed YOLOv8n model achieved a precision of 0.952 and a recall of 0.945") and in Table 4 (Precision 0.952).
- Abstract: "average precision of 0.898"; Section III.A: "The model demonstrates an average precision (AP) of 0.898"; Section III.A also gives AR 0.945 for the classes. Precision 0.898 is never used in the comparison.
- Section III.A says recall (AR) 0.945 is "for the 'masak' (ripe), 'kurang masak' (under ripe), and 'terlalu masak' (overripe) classes"; Table 4 gives recall 0.945 for the proposed method under the same label. Consistent, but the same number serves as AR and as recall.
- Section II.A describes "camera mode" and "GPS mode"; the Abstract says "live data feeds" with "real-time" performance, but no latency or FPS value is reported anywhere.
