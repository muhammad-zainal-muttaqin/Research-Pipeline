CROP / SETTING / SCALE: Oil palm fresh fruit bunches (FFB) in piles at the grading area of a palm oil mill, Central Kalimantan (Section II, dataset collection). Post-harvest sorting, not on the tree. Dataset: 4,214 files converted from video, 7,256 images and 14,757 objects (Section III.A, Table 2). Average 2 objects per image because each image shows a pile. Scale in terms of fruits per tree or per plot: not stated.

ACQUISITION: Smartphone video (1280x720 .mp4 in the dataset description; field app on Samsung Galaxy S20+, camera up to 4032 x 3024 pixels, Section III.E). Videos of 10 to 15 s, 56 multi-category and 45 single-category videos (Section II). Frames extracted from video with a recording ratio of 10 (Section II). Camera-to-pile distance, number of sides, and source video frame rate: not stated in the sections read. Single-frame detection on the phone; no revisits or multi-view capture.

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR: YOLOv4 (Darknet framework, COCO-pretrained weights, CSPDarknet53 backbone in YOLOv4 and YOLOv4-CSP), plus YOLOv4-Tiny. Variants tested: YOLOv4-320, -416, -512, -608, YOLOv4-CSP-640, YOLOv4-Tiny. The model is trained on images of FFB piles, with one bounding box per object and a ripeness class for each (six classes, see CLASS). Quantization: YOLOv4-416 converted to TensorFlow Lite and quantized to 16-bit (fp16) and 8-bit (int8); YOLOv4-Tiny also quantized to fp16 (Sections III.E and IV). Reason for quantization given: "due to the computational constraints of this mobile device model". Detection accuracy as reported (Section III.C): best test mAP@50 99.98% (YOLOv4-320), 99.97% (YOLOv4-512), 99.93% (YOLOv4-416). Per-class mAP and F1 are in Table 7 (not in the text extracted for this note); text states empty and unripe classes above 97% mAP and F1 0.99, ripe/abnormal/overripe 40-60% mAP and F1 0.30-0.50, underripe below 20% mAP for YOLOv4-Tiny, combined-category video 20-40% mAP and F1 0.15-0.30 (Section III.E). The paper's own mobile results (Table 7 text) give best average mAP 65.53% and lowest 53.31%, both attributed to YOLOv4tiny-q-fp16 (see INCONSISTENCIES). Augmentation (Section III.B, Table 3): rotation 45 and 90 degrees, translation ratio 0.5, brightness -40% to 60%, Gaussian blur variance 7-9% (augmentation parameters from Section II; Table 3 numbers not in the text read).

ASSOCIATION METHOD: None. Each frame is detected independently. The "MultiBoxTracker" class in the Android app only draws boxes for display (Section III.D describes a "tracking overlay" for display). The app can save detection results to a file. No tracker, motion model, appearance matching, 3D reconstruction, counting line, or unique-ID scheme.

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS: Each video frame is a sample of the pile for classification; the paper does not state how repeated frames of the same pile are linked or deduplicated. Testing on 5 frames per video is described as the evaluation unit (Section III.E). The authors state the model "can detect and categorize in real-time" on smartphone video input (Conclusion), but no per-frame latency figure is interpreted beyond Figure 11 comparisons (see RESULTS). The authors say the model is "effective in detecting post-harvest palm oil FFB" and that pre-harvest use is limited by "the diverse datasets required" (Conclusion).

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED: (a) same fruit or pile in consecutive frames: not addressed. (b) track lost and restarted: not applicable, no tracking. (c) same pile from both sides or different passes: not addressed.

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING: Six classes: empty bunches, unripe, underripe, ripe, overripe, abnormal (Section III.A; Table 2 category list). Class is assigned per detected object in each frame. No rule for attaching a class to a unique fruit across frames; no handling of frames that disagree. Each video was labeled with one category (single-category videos, 10 per class, Section III.E) or as a combined multi-category video (10 videos). Confusion between neighbouring ripeness classes is reported in text: "Models are often confused in classifying ripe and overripe FFB due to the similarity in color and shape" and underripe is "often classified as unripe" (Section III.E). Class definitions by colour, time after anthesis, or count of fruits: not stated.

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL: No count reference (no hand count, no harvest weight, no packhouse count). Metrics are detection and classification metrics on annotated images or video frames: mAP@50, precision, recall, F1, average IoU, average confidence score, latency (ms or s, see RESULTS). No identity metric (MOTA, IDF1, HOTA, ID switches). No count error.

KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS:
- Dataset: 4,214 files; 7,256 images; 14,757 objects; ~2 objects per image (Section III.A, Table 2).
- Split 7:2:1 train/val/test (Section II, as recorded in earlier notes).
- Augmentation improved all Table 3 metrics for YOLOv4-320 (mAP@0.50, precision, recall, F1, average IoU), stated in text (Section III.B).
- Validation: best mAP@50 for YOLOv4-320, then YOLOv4-416 and YOLOv4-512 (Section III.C, Table 5).
- Test: highest mAP@50 99.98% (YOLOv4-320), 99.97% (YOLOv4-512), 99.93% (YOLOv4-416) (Section III.C).
- FPS (as written in the text): YOLOv4-320 "59.7 seconds" (largest), YOLOv4-608 "33.1 seconds" (smallest) (Section III.C).
- F1: 0.99 for YOLOv4-320, -512, -608 (Section III.C); largest F1 0.64 for YOLOv4-q-fp16 on the mobile test, lowest 0.52 for YOLOv4tiny-q-fp16 (Section III.E).
- Average IoU by class (mobile test, Table 6): underripe 92.4% (highest), abnormal 74.65% (lowest). Highest IoU model YOLOv4-q-fp16, lowest YOLOv4-tiny-q-fp16 (Section III.E).
- Average confidence: all models above 75%; YOLOv4-tiny 90%, YOLOv4-q-fp16 87%, lowest YOLOv4-q-fp8 (Section III.E, Figure 10).
- YOLOv4-q-int8 has "only a 1.1% decrease in mAP compared to YOLOv4-q-fp16" (Section III.E).
- Mobile test: 10 videos per class plus 10 combined videos, inference recorded on 5 frames per video, 350 frames per model (Section III.E).
- Prior study [45] (Junior 2023), as cited: single-class mAP 97.64% (YOLOv4-CSPDarknet53); multi-class in one frame 70.21% (YOLOv4-Tiny) (Section III.C).
- Abstract: "the YOLOv4 model with 16 quantization performs 12% better than YOLOv4 Tiny" on mAP (as recorded in earlier notes; exact abstract wording not re-read in this pass).

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED:
- Mixed piles: IoU lower for the combined class because objects vary in size and ripeness; abnormal class also lower IoU because of varied FFB sizes (Section III.E).
- Colour gradation: underripe and ripe have "more complex characteristics in terms of color gradations", model less consistent on them (Section III.E).
- Ripe and overripe confused because of similar colour and shape (Section III.E).
- Underripe classified as unripe, mAP below 20% for YOLOv4-Tiny (Section III.E).
- Combined multi-category video: mAP 20-40%, F1 0.15-0.30 (Section III.E).
- YOLOv4-tiny: detection mistakes on ripe and unripe (Figure 12B, 12C); quantized model error on ripe combined with overripe (Figure 13C) (Section III.E).
- Lower mAP for the combined category attributed to "difficulty differentiating between the many types of FFB" (Section III.E).
- Small bunches, occlusion, fronds, and tree-top FFB: not discussed. Pre-harvest use is limited by "the diverse datasets required" (Conclusion).

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA: Within this paper, YOLOv4 variants and YOLOv4-tiny are compared on the same dataset (Tables 4-7). Comparison with [45] (Junior 2023) is made by reporting its published mAP numbers on "a slightly different dataset" (Section III.C). No external same-data comparison.

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK: "One of the proposed improvements is to increase the training data on multi-category datasets." (Conclusion). Future use "more suitable embedded devices" (Conclusion). "Implementation is currently restricted to mobile devices as the model parameters need to be reduced" (Conclusion).

INCONSISTENCIES INSIDE THE PAPER:
- Image counts: "The dataset utilized in this study has 4,214 files altogether" (Section III.A) versus "there were 7,256 total images utilized in this study" (Section III.A, Table 2 text).
- Selected model: Section III.C says the YOLOv4-416 and YOLOv4-320 models are selected for mobile apps, but the highest test mAP@50 is YOLOv4-320 (99.98%). Section III.C also says YOLOv4-320 has the largest FPS, while the Conclusion says the best results are "the YOLOv4-416 model with mAP, IoU, FPS, and BFlops criteria".
- FPS unit: "YOLOv4-320 model had the largest FPS value (59.7 seconds)"; FPS is a rate, not seconds.
- Same model named for highest and lowest mAP: "highest value being 65.53% in the YOLOv4tiny-q-fp16 model and the lowest value in the YOLOv4tiny-q-fp16 model with a value of 53.31%" (Section III.E).
- Best model contradiction: F1 is highest for YOLOv4-q-fp16 (0.64) and lowest for YOLOv4tiny-q-fp16 (0.52), yet the text says "the model that can classify the best ripeness level when implemented on smartphones is YOLOv4tiny-q-fp16" (Section III.E).
- Conclusion says the quantized YOLOv4 "is more accurate when used on mobile devices", while Section III.E says YOLOv4-q-fp16 has "a higher level of detection precision" but "higher latency" (consistent in direction, but the Conclusion phrase "selected ... quantization" is garbled: "The results of the quantification of the selected Its performance").
- Confidence statement: all models "greater than 75%" (Section III.E) versus "Models were evaluated using a confidence score ... the YOLOv4-tiny model received the highest score" (confidence ordering not stated numerically for all models).
- The abstract wording "16 quantization" is not defined in Section II (quantization bit width is given only in Section III.E as fp16 and int8).
