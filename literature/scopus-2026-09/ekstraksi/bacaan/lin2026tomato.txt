KEY: lin2026tomato
Lin et al. 2026, "Tomato Maturity Classiﬁcation and Fruit Counting Based on RGB and Multispectral Images", Appl. Sci. 2026, 16, 3227 (doi 10.3390/app16073227). Extension of an ICIT 2024 paper (per paper).

CROP / SETTING / SCALE:
Greenhouse tomato. Three datasets: 19th_tomato, lin_tomato, 19th2_tomato (Farm Nineteen and Lin Family Farm). Sampling weekly for the first two datasets and twice weekly for 19th2_tomato (lines 418–421). Scale: number of trees, plants or fruits in total not stated in the notes for this entry. Counting unit is tomatoes (fruit count), not yield.

ACQUISITION:
- Multispectral camera: Parrot Sequoia+ (lines 398–410). Shooting distance about 40 cm from the trellis (lines 398–410).
- Integrated sunshine sensor recorded incident solar radiation for radiometric calibration of multispectral images (Discussion, lines 1062–1065).
- Sides of row, frame rate, RGB camera model and motion pattern: not stated in the notes for this entry.
- Sampling: weekly (datasets 1 and 2), twice weekly (19th2_tomato).

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR:
- Detector: YOLOv8 with an OSNet module (lines 280–291). One class, "tomato" (lines 802–803).
- Training: learning rate 0.01, SGD, momentum 0.937, weight decay 0.001, 200 epochs, batch size 8 (lines 489–492).
- Preprocessing: Otsu threshold on the V channel in YUV colour space to segment the fruit (lines 319–329). Elliptical approximation inside each bounding box to compute features (Discussion, lines 1074–1080).
- Detection accuracy as a standalone figure: not stated in the notes for this entry.
- Inputs: RGB and multispectral images; vegetation indices NDVI, GNDVI, GRRI (Table 4 header).

ASSOCIATION METHOD:
- Tracker: StrongSORT with AFLink (lines 302–313). Track IDs are used for counting (lines 307–309).
- Counting rule: per-track unique ID, then maturity assigned per track (majority vote over frames, see below).
- No 3D reconstruction, SfM or SLAM mentioned in the notes for this entry.

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS:
- Temporal redundancy across frames can be exploited by associating observations over time (Discussion, lines 1041–1044).
- Maturity is stable enough within a tomato over the sampling window that a majority vote over frames gives a correct tomato-level label (lines 584–586).
- Illumination variation is reduced by radiometric calibration, Otsu preprocessing, indices, and multi-frame voting (Discussion, lines 1062–1073).
- The number of tomatoes is the output; yield in mass or volume is not estimated (Limitations, lines 1100–1102).

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED:
(a) Same fruit in consecutive frames: handled by StrongSORT tracking; track IDs are the unit of counting (lines 302–313).
(b) Track lost and restarted: AFLink is cited for linking trajectories (lines 302–313). The authors name tracking inaccuracy as a cause of count error (lines 1045–1050). Explicit handling of a restarted track: not stated.
(c) Same fruit from both sides or different passes: not stated. The camera is a single trellis-facing view.

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING:
- Three maturity classes: mature (−1), almost mature (−2), immature (−3). Assigned retrospectively by the authors (lines 463–483).
- Classifiers: SVM (RBF kernel, C 10–40), KNN (K 2–30), ANN (300 epochs, batch 40). 10-fold cross-validation (lines 494–517).
- Class per unique fruit: majority consensus among frame-level classifications of the same tracked tomato (lines 576–584).
- Disagreement between frames: resolved by majority vote; the text says this reduced transient errors (lines 1040–1042). Tie handling: not stated.
- Table 3 (lines 590–643): accuracies range from 38% to 81%, computed as correctly identified boxes over all boxes across frames.

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL:
- Reference: ground-truth tomato counts per maturity class (Table 6, lines 823–1009). Whether the reference is on-tree hand count, harvest or packhouse: not stated.
- Metrics: classification accuracy, count difference and RMSE per maturity class. Identity metrics (MOTA, IDF1, HOTA, ID switches): not stated. Detection metrics (mAP, AP): not stated in the notes for this entry.

KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS:
- Table 3, lines 590–643: classification accuracy between 38% and 81% across algorithms.
- Averages, lines 656–661: RGB plus indices 66.8%; indices only 64.9%; RGB only 63.8%.
- Table 5, lines 765–785: accuracy 66.7% (19th_tomato), 79.8% (lin_tomato), 72.2% (19th2_tomato).
- Table 6, lines 823–1009: counting by maturity class. lin_tomato (row "4th" as labelled in Table 6) difference −22, RMSE 9.20. lin_tomato (row "6th") difference −10, RMSE 4.40. 19th_tomato (row "7th") difference −2, RMSE 2.45.

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED:
- Heavy occlusion or dense foliage: predicted count deviated more from ground truth (lines 1046–1048).
- lin_tomato dataset had large discrepancies (lines 1048–1049).
- Irregular fruit motion, partial occlusion by leaves, similar appearance of neighbouring tomatoes (lines 1049–1052).
- Bounding boxes including leaves, stems or neighbouring fruit (lines 1076–1078).
- Limited greenhouse environments; lighting, shadows and occlusion affect robustness (lines 1097–1100).
- Heavily occluded tomatoes lower detection and classification accuracy (lines 1103–1105).

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA:
- Compared with earlier RGB-only maturity methods, the authors state the addition of multispectral indices (lines 1087–1091). No numeric comparison on the same data is given in the notes for this entry.
- Authors say their approach "achieved comparable detection and classification performance" to previous methods (lines 1081–1083).

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK:
- Limited number of greenhouse environments (lines 1097–1100).
- Estimates tomato count, not yield by mass or volume (lines 1100–1102).
- Accuracy drops under heavy occlusion (lines 1103–1105).
- Suggested future work: improved segmentation or depth information to reduce counting errors (lines 1052–1054); optimise sampling schedules based on crop growth dynamics (lines 1058–1061).

INCONSISTENCIES INSIDE THE PAPER:
- Maximum accuracy: abstract (line 32) and Conclusions (lines 1112–1114) state "a maximum maturity classification accuracy of 81%". Table 5 (line 785) and the text (line 760) give 72.2% for 19th2_tomato, the highest per-dataset figure in Table 5 is 79.8% (lin_tomato). The 81% is the Table 3 maximum across algorithms, not a dataset result.
- Table 4 header (lines 688/694) reads "N + G + G = NDVI + GNDVI + GRRI"; the second "G" should presumably read "GR" (typo).
