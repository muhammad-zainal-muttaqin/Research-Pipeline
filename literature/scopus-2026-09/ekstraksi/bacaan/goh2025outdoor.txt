KEY: goh2025outdoor (Scientific Data 12:687, 2025, doi 10.1038/s41597-025-04953-6). Title: "Outdoor RGB and Point Cloud Depth Dataset for Palm Oil Fresh Fruit Bunch Ripeness Classification and Localization".

CROP / SETTING / SCALE: Oil palm fresh fruit bunches (FFB), outdoor plantation, Johor, Malaysia (four locations, including Batu Pahat and Yong Peng). Dataset of 400 scenes: 400 RGB images, 400 depth maps, 400 point clouds (Abstract). One tree-side view per scene; no multi-view or per-tree grouping described.

ACQUISITION: Static camera. RGB camera (50 MP Sony IMX766V, 8688 x 5792 px, f/1.75, 24 mm equivalent) on a tripod, 2 m from the trunk, viewing angle 15-60 degrees (Methods). Depth sensor Intel RealSense D455f, 1280 x 720, 90 fps, working range 0.6-6.0 m (Methods). Temporal sync 8.3 ms +/- 0.1 ms, spatial registration RMSE under 2 cm, mounting angular tolerance +/-0.1 degree (Table 3, from the Methods section read earlier; not re-checked in this pass). Capture between 0700 and 1900 hours (Technical Validation). No video, no revisits, no multiple passes of the same tree. Each scene is one capture.

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR: No detector or segmenter is trained or evaluated in the paper. It is a dataset paper. Annotation is by polygon (CVAT or LabelMe, JSON); bounding boxes for YOLO are derived from the polygons (Methods). 3D clusters are formed by DBSCAN on the point cloud, then merged or split by experts (Methods). Accuracy of the annotation is reported only as agreement (see KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS).

ASSOCIATION METHOD: None across frames or views. Each 3D cluster gets an ID (idj) that is unique within its scene only (Methods). Registration of depth to RGB is a rigid transform T in SE(3), estimated from fiducial markers seen in both views, with intrinsic calibration from checkerboards (Methods, Technical Validation section on calibration). The paper states the transform is for "cross-modal data augmentation, sensor fusion, or multi-view geometry tasks" (use cases, not tested here). No tracker, no SfM, no SLAM, no learned matching.

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS: Static tripod view of one tree at 2 m, so the depth and RGB fields are registered once per scene. Ripe/unripe is a binary label to the MPOB standard. Expert consensus is treated as ground truth. Natural daylight variation is treated as a variable that may affect classification (Technical Validation). Manual ripeness grading error is stated as 15-25 percent (Introduction, from the summary of the earlier read).

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED: (a) same fruit in consecutive frames: not addressed, no frame sequence exists. (b) track lost and restarted: not addressed, no tracking. (c) same fruit from both sides or different passes: not addressed. The IDs in the depth clusters are scene-local, so the same bunch seen in two scenes would receive two IDs. The paper does not state this.

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING: Binary: ripe or unripe, to the MPOB standard (Abstract). Each FFB in a scene is classified as one object; no class-change rule across views exists because there is one view per scene. Annotator agreement is reported per FFB (Table 6 rows).

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL: No count reference. No hand count, harvest count, or packhouse count is reported. No count error, no MOTA, IDF1, HOTA, or ID switch. The data are reported as classification agreement and depth measurement tolerance. Depth tolerance is checked against a tape measure (Table 6 in the depth validation section, see INCONSISTENCIES).

KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS:
- 400 RGB images, 400 depth maps, 400 point clouds (Abstract).
- RGB-depth registration mean error about 1.8 cm at 3 m (Abstract; Technical Validation: "ensuring a mean registration error of approximately 1.8 cm at a 3 m distance").
- Expert agreement 92.5 percent (Abstract: "Expert validation resulted in 92.5% inter-rater agreement.").
- Image quality pass rates (Table 5): exposure 94.5, focus 92.3, colour balance 96.7, completeness 91.8, file integrity 100.0 (percent).
- Annotation agreement (Table 6 rows): initial 87.5 percent (three-expert assessment, 400 FFBs); after group discussion 100 percent (400 FFBs); random recheck by single expert 95.2 percent (40 FFBs).
- Session distribution (Table 7 rows): morning 35 percent, midday 40 percent, afternoon 25 percent of data; clear weather 60 percent, cloudy 40 percent.
- Depth tolerance against tape measure (Table 6 in the depth caption, read in chunk 3): +/-10 cm at 1.5-2.0 m, +/-15 cm at 2.0-3.0 m, +/-20 cm at 3.0-4.0 m.

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED:
- Natural lighting and weather variation "primarily affect ripeness classification results" (Technical Validation). No quantified effect is shown.
- Initial expert agreement was 87.5 percent before group discussion (Table 6).
- Depth error grows with range: tolerance widens from +/-10 cm to +/-20 cm over 1.5-4.0 m (Table 6 depth rows).
- No failure modes for occlusion, leaves, or bunch overlap are reported.

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA: None. The paper compares no detector or classifier. It names prior datasets as "87% artificial lighting, 13% natural" (Introduction, from the summary of the earlier read); no quantitative comparison is run on this data.

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK: Not stated as a section. The Technical Validation claims "enhanced robustness and generalizability compared to existing datasets limited to controlled conditions" without a test. Future work on the same repository is not described beyond the codebase.
