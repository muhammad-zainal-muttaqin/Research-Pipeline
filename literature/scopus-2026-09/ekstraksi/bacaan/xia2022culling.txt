KEY: xia2022culling (Xia et al., Agronomy 2022, 12, 440; fruit culling of duplicate fruits in sequence images for yield estimation; apples and oranges). Author list and year as printed in the body; not re-checked against the title page.

CROP / SETTING / SCALE: Apple orchard and orange orchard video (orchard rows, handheld camera moving along rows). 120 apple and 120 orange image sequences, split equally into train and test. Yield-estimation evaluation: 20 apple sequences (910 fruits) and 20 orange sequences (844 fruits) from the test data (Sec. 3.4). Fruit-matching evaluation: 20 adjacent-pair groups per fruit, 828 apple fruits and 793 orange fruits (Sec. 3.3). Sequences from a single side of the tree row; other-row fruit may appear in frames (Discussion).

ACQUISITION: Video of fruit trees from an iPhone 8 at 1080 x 1920, handheld, moved along the rows. Single-side view only. Frame rate, distance and row-side details: not stated in the body read. Ground truth from a self-developed label tool (boxes) and pair tool (duplicates); two data engineers computed the ground truth in sequence images with the pair tool (Sec. 2.1).

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR: CenterNet (anchor-free detector; keypoint heatmap with Gaussian peaks, local offset and object-size branches), input 512 x 512, Adam optimizer, learning rate 1e-4, 1000 epochs, batch size 16 (Table 2). Detects fruit bounding boxes (min-x, min-y, max-x, max-y). Detection accuracy (Table 3): apple AP at IoU 0.5 = 0.927, orange AP at IoU 0.5 = 0.951, mAP = 0.939 at IoU 0.5; mAP = 0.898 at IoU 0.6; mAP = 0.790 at IoU 0.7. ACFR-Apple AP at IoU 0.5 = 0.924 (Table 3, as read in the body). Hardware: TITAN Xp 16 GB, i7-7700 CPU, 32 GB RAM, Ubuntu 16.04, CUDA 10.0, PyTorch 1.0.

ASSOCIATION METHOD: Fruit matching model that decides whether two fruit patches in adjacent frames are the same fruit. Patches are 94 x 94 greyscale crops from the detections. Architecture based on DeepCompare: a convolutional matching layer followed by a Kuhn-Munkres (Hungarian) decision layer that pairs fruits one-to-one. Cross-entropy loss (Eq. 4). Training on 3066 apple and 6028 orange patch pairs; 300 epochs, learning rate 0.0005, batch size 16, greyscale input. Counting rule: count(S) = sum over sequence images of count(s_i) minus sum over adjacent pairs of count(s_i intersect s_{i+1}) (Eq. 1), i.e. duplicates between neighbouring images are subtracted. No motion model, no Kalman filter, no 3D/SfM, no calibration reported. Pairing is brute force over all patches (Discussion).

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS:
- Fruit in adjacent images can be matched on patch appearance alone (learned similarity).
- Single-side view is sufficient for counting, but the authors state that fruit may not be detected from one side because of tree morphology, branches and leaves (Discussion).
- Fruit in other tree rows appears smaller in the image and can be excluded by annotation and detector training (Discussion).
- Greyscale patches are assumed adequate for the DeepCompare input; the authors state colour could add information (Discussion).
- The sequence images are close enough in time that the same fruit appears in adjacent frames (Sec. 2.2.3 implied; the body does not give a frame-rate-based condition).

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED: (a) same fruit in consecutive frames: addressed by the learned patch matching and Kuhn-Munkres one-to-one pairing between adjacent images, then subtraction of matched duplicates (Eq. 1). (b) track lost and restarted: not stated; no tracker and no re-identification after loss is described, and the method only links adjacent images. (c) same fruit from both sides or different passes: not addressed. The authors state that the pipeline works from a single-side view and that a both-sides method using multiple cameras is future work (Discussion).

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING: none. Two fruit types (apple and orange) are evaluated separately; no ripeness or maturity class is described.

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL: Ground truth is per-fruit boxes and duplicate pairs annotated by two data engineers in sequence images with the label tool and pair tool. Detection: AP at IoU 0.5, 0.6, 0.7 and mAP (Table 3). Matching: accuracy, precision, recall and F1 for DeepCompare and the proposed model (Table 4). Yield: regression against ground-truth fruit count per sequence, R-squared and RMSE (Sec. 3.4, Fig. 14). Identity metrics (MOTA, IDF1, HOTA, ID switches): not stated. Count reference is annotated fruit in image sequences, not hand counts on the tree, at harvest or at packhouse.

KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS:
- Detection (Table 3, Sec. 3.2): mAP 0.939 / 0.898 / 0.790 at IoU 0.5 / 0.6 / 0.7; apple AP 0.927 and orange AP 0.951 at IoU 0.5.
- Fruit matching, apple (Table 4): DeepCompare accuracy 0.937, precision 0.503, recall 0.592, F1 0.544; proposed accuracy 0.975, precision 0.793, recall 0.840, F1 0.816.
- Fruit matching, orange (Table 4): DeepCompare accuracy 0.966, precision 0.701, recall 0.776, F1 0.737; proposed accuracy 0.985, precision 0.853, recall 0.875, F1 0.864.
- Conclusions (Sec. 5): F1 0.816 (apple) and 0.864 (orange) for the proposed model against DeepCompare.
- Yield (Sec. 3.4, Fig. 14): R-squared apple 0.9737, orange 0.9562; RMSE apple 10.0920, orange 4.2544.
- Computation: average 5.33 min per image sequence (Sec. 3.4).

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED:
- Partial occlusion of fruit by branches and leaves, varying perspectives and light conditions cause detection misses or low confidence (Discussion).
- Training samples do not cover all interference cases; the model may misjudge unlearned fruit features (Discussion).
- Single-side view misses fruit hidden by tree morphology, branches and leaves (Discussion).
- Fruit from other tree rows in poorly managed orchards can be counted (Discussion).
- Pairing is brute force and does not meet real-time speed (Discussion).

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA: Proposed fruit matching model vs DeepCompare on the same 828 apple and 793 orange fruit groups (Table 4, Fig. 13). The paper states the proposed model is "significantly better" than the reference model (Discussion); no statistical test is reported.

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK:
- Single-side view; use multiple cameras on both sides and combine local and global feature similarity (Discussion).
- Colour information in patches not used (greyscale input); future work to add colour (Discussion).
- Real-time speed: pairing compares all patches in a brute-force way; to be studied (Discussion).
- Time comparison between automatic and manual counting planned (Discussion).
- Other-row fruit: avoid labelling it and train the detector to ignore it, or use dense planting (Discussion).
- Larger training samples and transfer learning for detection robustness (Discussion).

INCONSISTENCIES INSIDE THE PAPER:
- Sec. 3.4 and Sec. 5 give yield R-squared as "R2 apple = 0.9737" and "R2 orange = 0.9562"; Sec. 3.4 sentence "the ground truth for both apple and orange are 0.9737 and 0.9562" wording is garbled and reads as if these were ground-truth values. The meaning follows from the regression context.
- Fruit counts differ between evaluations: 828 apple and 793 orange fruits in the matching evaluation (Sec. 3.3) vs 910 and 844 fruits in the yield evaluation (Sec. 3.4). Different subsets are plausible but the body does not state the reason.
- Sec. 2.2.3 says the DeepCompare-based model "is prone to allocate one fruit with many different ones as duplicate fruit because of the maximum assignment strategy", while Discussion credits Kuhn-Munkres with one-to-one pairing. Consistent in direction; the body does not report a separate baseline for the assignment alone.
