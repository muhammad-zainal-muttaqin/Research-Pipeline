KEY: stein2016image. Stein, Bargoti, Underwood (2016), "Image Based Mango Fruit Detection, Localisation and Yield Estimation Using Multiple View Geometry", Sensors 16, 1915. Source text: literature/scopus-2026-09/teks/stein2016image.txt (read lines 1-1199 in 300-line chunks; reference list lines 1300-1425 skipped).

CROP / SETTING / SCALE: Mango (Mangifera indica) orchard at Simpson Farms, Bundaberg, Queensland. Target block of 522 trees scanned from both sides; 71,609 mangoes counted (abstract; Section 3.6, line 976). Single-view, dual-view and multi-view methods compared on 16 target trees (Section 4.1, line 1094). Orchard block has 10 rows (Figure 14c).

ACQUISITION: Unmanned ground vehicle ("Shrimp") driven past the trees at 1.4 m/s (Section 2, first chunk). Prosilica GT3300C colour camera, 3296 x 2472 px, 5 Hz, with strobes; Kowa LM8CX lens; Excelitas MVS-5002 strobes (refs 26-28). Velodyne HDL64E LiDAR mounted sideways (ref 29). Novatel SPAN GPS/INS at 50 Hz (ref 30). About 25 viewpoints per side per tree (Section 2, first chunk). Images taken from left and right sides of the row (Figure 18, "left and central perspective"; Figure 20, "Left/Central/Right Perspective"). Trajectory about 5 km (Section 2, first chunk).

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR: Faster R-CNN with VGG16 backbone (refs 25, 32); no version number stated. Training set of 1500 crops of 500 x 500 px drawn from about 15,000 images; split 1000/250/250 (Section 2, first chunk). Reported test F1 = 0.881 at IoU > 0.2 (Section 2, first chunk). Tiled predictor run on full-resolution images (Section 2, first chunk). Detector detects individual mango fruit in each image. Hand-labelled images were used as a reference for detector sensitivity (Section 4.1, line 1101).

ASSOCIATION METHOD: Geometric. Each fruit detection is projected as an epipolar line into the other frames, using camera pose from GPS/INS and time-offset-corrected navigation (Section 2.4, first chunk). The epipolar ray is clipped to a constant minimum and maximum range of 2.5 m and 8 m from the camera (Section 2.4, first chunk; also line 1233-1234). Cost = perpendicular distance from the detection centroid to the epipolar line, with one tunable pixel threshold; threshold 30 px chosen (R^2 = 0.90, slope 1.014; instability below about 15 px) (Section 3.2, first chunk). Matching solved with the Hungarian algorithm (refs 23, 37, 38). Each fruit receives a unique ID that persists across frames; a track is established only after a fruit is observed in at least two frames (line 1204-1205). Unmatched single detections are rejected as false positives (Section 2.4, first chunk). Triangulation: midpoint of shortest segment between observation rays, and the mean intersection of all rays for a fruit (Section 3.5, first chunk; line 1230-1231). Time offset between navigation and camera estimated by minimising the mean association pixel error: 0.111 s, SD 0.0044 s across 32 graphs (Section 3.2, first chunk). Counting rule: count of unique track IDs per tree, row and block (Section 2.4 / Section 3, first chunk). Association is per-tree via LiDAR-derived image masks (Section 2, first chunk; Section 4.3, line 1153-1154).

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS: Fruit is stationary between frames, so epipolar geometry applies (implied; Section 2.4). Camera pose from GPS/INS and time offset are accurate enough for epipolar lines (Section 4.4.1, line 1179-1183). Fruit lies between 2.5 m and 8 m from the camera (line 1233-1234). Frames are not missing from the sequence (implied by track association, Section 4.4.1, line 1190-1196). A constant range limit is adequate; the authors say per-fruit range could be refined after a match (line 1234-1237). LiDAR mask removes points below 20 cm above ground so fallen fruit are excluded (line 1240-1241). The stated stability of the threshold plateau is taken as evidence that nearly all fruit were counted (Section 4.2, line 1136-1137). Multi-view approach assumed to be stable across orchard blocks, varieties and pruning regimes; authors state this is untested (line 1124-1127).

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED: (a) same fruit in consecutive frames: addressed. Epipolar association links detections in sequential frame pairs (Section 2.4), and the text says "The epipolar geometry permits matching from one frame to any other" (line 1213-1214). (b) track lost and restarted: NOT addressed. A track is broken when a fruit is not matched, even if visible again later (line 1205-1206). Figure 20 (line 1222-1223): a fruit "is counted as a new mango (37 in c) when reappearing." The authors list this as a remaining problem. (c) same fruit from both sides or different passes: multi-view approach links detections across left, central and right perspectives of one side of one pass (Figure 18; Section 4.2). Dual-view output is "the sum of two single views" (line 1117), i.e. no cross-side de-duplication is described for the dual method. Whether the multi-view method was applied across the two sides of a row is not stated.

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING: not stated. The text describes a single class ("mango"); no maturity or colour classes appear in the text read.

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL: Field count: manual counts per tree from Central Queensland University (acknowledgements, line 1288) and per-tree hand counts for the target trees (Table 1; 18 trees in Table 1, 16 used in analysis, per Section 4.1 line 1094). Harvest weights mentioned in acknowledgements (line 1288). Metrics: linear regression of vision count vs field count (R^2 and slope, Figures 10, 11); repeatability regression of repeated counts (Figure 12); triangulation error as nearest-neighbour distance between triangulated fruit and hand-labelled fruit (Section 3.5, line 943-944). No MOTA, IDF1, HOTA or ID-switch metrics reported. Detection metrics: F1 at IoU > 0.2 (Section 2, first chunk).

KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS:
- Single-view vs field count, 16 target trees: R^2 = 0.81, slope 0.2680 (27%) (Section 3.3, Figure 10a; first chunk).
- Dual-view vs field count: R^2 = 0.94, slope 0.5376 (Figure 10b; line 1107-1108 gives 0.54). Dual-view counted "approximately half" of fruit (line 1103).
- Multi-view vs field count: R^2 = 0.90, slope 1.0136 (Figure 10c; line 975 gives 1.0136, line 1109 gives 1.014). Stated error "1.36%" (line 1110, 1270).
- Hand-labelled dual-view: R^2 = 0.95, 64% of fruit observed (line 1101; Section 3.3, first chunk).
- 522-tree comparison: single east vs west R^2 = 0.59; multi east vs west R^2 = 0.63; dual vs multi R^2 = 0.84, slope 0.48; about 20 outliers out of 522 (Section 3.3-3.4, chunk 600-899).
- Repeatability (Figure 12): single-view y = 1.018x, R^2 = 0.95619 (Figure 12a); multi-view y = 0.96525x, R^2 = 0.83847 (Figure 12b). Text gives slope 1.02, R^2 = 0.96 (single) and slope 0.97, R^2 = 0.84 (multi) (Section 3.4, line 873-899, chunk 600-899).
- Row totals, repeated single-view counts: 1505 and 1532, error 1.8%; multi-view: 3026 and 2981, error 1.5% (Section 3.4, chunk 600-899).
- Triangulation: mean nearest-neighbour distance 0.11 m, 95th percentile 0.36 m, for four hand-labelled trees (line 943-944).
- Orchard total: 71,609 mangoes; Row 6 = 10,112 and Row 7 = 6,371 (line 976-978; Figure 14c, line 1015-1017).
- Time offset 0.111 s; mean association error 11.4 px to 4.2 px (Section 3.2, first chunk).

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED:
- Missing image frames (logging error): epipolar lines become longer and ambiguous; Trees 12 and 60 repeats had about half the images missing (line 1192-1196).
- Platform oscillation after a bump: lateral pixel error from rotation rate times time-sync error; Tree 1 (line 1178-1186).
- Track fragmentation from one missed detection or alternating occlusion (line 1204-1214).
- Fruit bunching: nearby fruit exchange IDs when both epipolar lines are equally close; the total count is unaffected, but triangulation is affected (line 1224-1233).
- Tall grass (above 20 cm) retained in LiDAR mask; fallen fruit detected there is counted for the tree (line 1240-1243; Figure 22).
- Occlusion by trunk and foliage (Figure 18, Figure 20).
- Single-view repeatability outliers from missed frames and oscillation (Figure 12b; Section 4.4.1).

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA: Single, dual and multi-view methods on the same 16 target trees against field counts (Figure 10; line 1093-1111). Dual-view calibration vs hand-labelled detections (line 1101-1103). Single vs dual vs multi repeatability on one side of row (Figure 12; line 1112-1116). Tracking vs hand labels for four trees (line 938-943). Stereo-based 3D association cited as prior work with the note that GPS alignment errors from opposing sides were too large compared to fruit clustering (line 1216-1218, citing ref 4, Wang et al. 2013, apple).

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK:
- Extension to other varieties and pruning regimes needs testing (line 1267-1268).
- Calibration stability for other blocks, orchards, varieties and pruning regimes is "not known" (line 1124-1125); multi-view stability "remains as future work to prove" (line 1126-1127).
- Multi-view has additional failure modes because it is algorithmically more complex (line 1118-1119).
- Future work: use triangulated depth during association to limit epipolar range (line 1232-1238); morphological operations on the LiDAR mask (line 1244-1246); count fallen fruit as a separate ground layer (line 1246-1253).
- Missing-frame and synchronisation errors reported as already corrected (update to vendor API; NTP and PTP time sync, line 1196-1202).
