CROP / SETTING / SCALE:
- Orchard fruit monitoring over a season. Title: "Spatio-Temporal Metric-Semantic Mapping for Persistent Orchard Monitoring: Method and Dataset" (Lei, Prabhu, Liu, Cladera, Mortazavi, Ehsani, Chaudhari, Kumar; arXiv 2409.19786v3, 6 May 2025).
- Abstract: "1,790 apples across 60 trees" for the counting evaluation.
- Section IV (dataset, l.478-509): 284 trees in total: 120 apple, 63 pear, 50 peach, 34 cherry, 17 pistachio. Data span April to August 2024, collected every two or three weeks (l.499-501).
- Sites: apple and pear at Lititz, Pennsylvania; apple, peach and cherry at Harford, Maryland; pistachio at Merced, California (l.502-509).
- Time sessions: "We use time sessions to refer to different times from June to August when the data were collected." (l.194-196)
- Scale: per-tree fruit counts (3D counting); per-fruit matching across sessions (4D association).

ACQUISITION: (video along a path / discrete views / 3D scan / revisits / single images; camera, platform, frame rate, distance, sides of row)
- Video/scan along a row: the sensor stack is scanned along both sides of a row of trees (l.479-482). Quadrotor mount is mentioned (l.497-498); the paper does not state a frame rate or the working distance to the trees.
- Sensors (l.487-492): Ouster OS0-128 LiDAR; FLIR Chameleon global-shutter RGB camera; Intel RealSense D435i RGB-D; VectorNav VN-100 IMU; UBlox ZED-F9P GPS.
- Revisits: the same orchard is revisited every two or three weeks (l.499-501), giving the time sessions used for 4D association.
- Point clouds from the LiDAR are accumulated over 1.5 s before projection (l.228-230).
- Ground truth for 3D counting: images from the video stream, chosen where each tree is fully visible and centred (l.527-529).

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR: (detector or segmenter and its version, input size, what it detects, detection accuracy as reported)
- Segmenter: "A fine-tuned YOLOv8 [20] is used for instance segmentation to obtain the instance mask of each fruit." (l.208-209). The input size is not stated in the text read.
- Pose: Faster-LIO [31] estimates the sensor pose (l.209-211).
- LiDAR and RGB are time-synchronised and calibrated (l.205-224).
- Fruit position: the centroid of the LiDAR points projected inside the mask (l.230-232).
- Detection accuracy as a standalone detector is not reported. Accuracy is reported only at the counting and association level (see KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS).

ASSOCIATION METHOD: (tracker, motion model, appearance features, 3D/SfM/SLAM, learned matching, statistical correction or calibration; counting rule e.g. unique ID or counting line; "none" if single images)
- Within one session, frame-to-frame tracking uses the Hungarian assignment. Cost is 1 - mask IoU after the 3D cloud of each fruit is projected into the next frame (Eq. 1-2, l.233-255). A dummy block with cost C_u = 1 - u, where u is a minimal IoU threshold (value not stated in the text read), allows unmatched fruits (l.256-266).
- Counting rule: a fruit is counted only if observed in more than three consecutive frames (l.267-272).
- Reprojection error minimisation (Eq. 4, l.273-336): least squares over fruit positions X_j and camera poses R_i, t_i, with a binary visibility term a_ij and a box constraint |X_j - X_j,0| <= d (the value of d is not stated in the text read). Noise sources are listed at l.274-278.
- Re-identification of tracks (l.337-352): for each track and nearby tracks within a distance threshold, the 3D clouds are projected into the same image plane. A pair is merged if (1) mask IoU is above a predefined threshold and (2) the frame sets are disjoint, Track_i and Track_j with no shared frame. Each fruit receives a unique index (l.351-352).
- Across sessions (Section III-B, l.353-399), a two-stage 4D association is used:
  - Stage 1: for each image in session A, the most similar image in session B is chosen. Hungarian matching uses Cost = C_visual + C_position (l.357-363).
  - Reference fruits are chosen by thresholding entropy H(M(f_i)) (l.387-391).
  - Stage 2 adds C_topology. Matched reference fruits get cost 0 (l.395-397).
- Position cost: after ICP alignment of successive sessions (l.405-410), the Euclidean distance between fruit positions.
- Visual cost: sub-image crop per fruit, DINOv2 [33] features, C_visual = ||Dino(f_i) - Dino(f_j)||_2 (Eq. 5, l.411-437).
- Topology cost (Eq. 6, l.438-468): vectors from a fruit to reference fruits in image coordinates. The cost counts vector pairs whose angle exceeds a 10 degree threshold. It replaces the histogram descriptor used in [27] and [34] (l.438-451, 466-468).
- Final match: majority vote over M(f_i) (l.395-398).
- Algorithm 1 (l.414-431) gives the two-stage steps. Only the summary is recorded here; step details are not verified in this file.
- No learned matching is used: "our approach leverages pre-trained models and heuristically derived features, eliminating the need for neural network training." (l.184-187). The only learned component is the pre-trained DINOv2 backbone.

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS:
- Fruit positions are fixed within a session, so reprojection error can be minimised over them (Eq. 4 context, l.273-336). The text does not state this in one sentence; treat as implied.
- Fruits can be aligned between sessions by ICP alignment of the two point clouds (l.405-410). The authors call this a "precise relative transformation".
- Visual similarity of a fruit crop across sessions is measured with DINOv2 features (l.411-437).
- Viewpoint similarity is checked with DINOv2 latent similarity (l.469-477).
- Reference fruits with low entropy are assumed to be reliably matched (l.387-391).
- Positions and visual costs are normalised; the topology cost is not normalised and acts as a penalty (l.469-477).
- Missing detections, duplicates, and new fruits are treated as exceptions; the robustness test removes fruits to simulate them (l.699-706).

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED: (a) same fruit in consecutive frames, (b) track lost and restarted, (c) same fruit from both sides or different passes - which, and how
- (a) Same fruit in consecutive frames: addressed by the Hungarian frame-to-frame tracking with mask IoU cost (l.233-266).
- (b) Track lost and restarted: addressed by re-identification with mask IoU threshold and disjoint frame sets (l.337-352). The paper states: "Because of canopy occlusions and missing detections from the instance segmentation model, some fruits may be initially tracked, lost, and then re-tracked again in later frames, resulting in double counts of the same fruit." (l.337-341)
- (c) Same fruit from both sides or different passes: the paper says the data cover both sides of a row (l.479-482), and that double counting may occur when the same tree appears in multiple frames (l.524-525). The text does not describe a specific method for matching fruit seen from opposite sides of the row.
- Across time sessions: addressed by the 4D association with C_visual, C_position, C_topology and majority vote (l.353-398). One-to-many matches are attributed to missing detections, duplicates from failed re-tracking, and positional change (l.373-379).

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING: (classes; how a class is attached to a unique fruit; what happens when frames disagree; "none" if no classes)
- none. The paper tracks fruit instances and counts them. Maturity or class labels are not discussed in the text read.

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL: (hand count on tree / harvest / packhouse / annotated images; count error, identity metrics MOTA/IDF1/HOTA/ID switches, detection metrics)
- 3D counting reference: counted by hand from video-stream images using digital markers (l.519-525). The paper says the reference "may still" double count: "However, double-counting may still occur when the same tree appears in multiple frames." (l.524-525). Counts are not stated to be per session.
- Counting metric: count error (absolute error and percent), per-batch mean and standard deviation of absolute error (Table I, l.563-578; text at l.609-611).
- 4D association reference: manual matching of fruits from five trees across three session pairs: June 12 to July 4; July 4 to July 19; July 19 to August 13 (l.613-614, 641-644). Fruits with ambiguous matches (less than 5 percent) were excluded (per notes; see INCONSISTENCIES for the verification status).
- 4D metrics: precision and F1. Recall is not used as the main metric. Reason given: matching range is 0.3 m, and "falsely matching two fruits would lead to errors." (l.645-666).
- Identity metrics (MOTA, IDF1, HOTA, ID switches): not reported.
- Size estimation: mean absolute size error against Vernier caliper measurements of 19 apples attached to a tree in a physical simulation (l.633-635, 708-723).

KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS: (bullets, exact numbers as reported, with table/section)
- Abstract: "our method achieves a 96.9% fruit counting accuracy for 1,790 apples across 60 trees, a mean fruit size estimation error of 1.1 cm, and a 23.7% improvement in 4D data association precision over baselines."
- Table I (l.563-578): total estimated 1846 vs ground truth 1790; absolute error 56 (3.1%).
- Table I per-batch: mean absolute error 6.0 (8.0%); standard deviation 5.0 (6.4%). The batches are 22 groups of 2 to 3 trees (l.597-599).
- Size: mean absolute error 1.10 cm, standard deviation 0.45 cm (l.708-723; also conclusion l.737-743).
- 4D association, average over the 4 time sessions: precision 93.5%, F1 92.1% (l.682-686).
- Improvement over the second baseline: precision by 23.7% and F1 by 11.9% (l.683-686).
- Ablation (l.687-697): position and visual costs alone are comparable to the baselines. Adding the topology cost reduces false positives. Of the reduced FP, 43.2% are correctly rejected, 28.4% are corrected to TP, and 28.4% are incorrectly rejected (l.692-696).
- Robustness (l.699-706): the method outperforms the baselines "even when up to 50% of the fruits are removed" (l.704-705).

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED: (bullets)
- Overcounting from double counting of noisy fruit point clouds (l.587-596).
- Counting fallen fruit on the ground, which the ground truth excludes (l.587-596).
- Small and partially occluded fruits have noisy background point clouds (l.591-596).
- Size error from leaves and stems in the segmentation mask, which inflate the 3D fruit cloud: "The errors induced in the size measurements are mainly due to the presence of background points such as leaves and stems in the segmentation mask of the fruits, which in turn inflates the extracted 3D fruit point clouds, leading to slightly larger size estimates compared to the ground truth." (l.719-722)
- Topology cost can reject fruits that should match: "However, the topology cost could mistakenly reject fruits that should be matched." (l.692-693)
- One-to-many matches from missing detections, failed re-tracking duplicates, and positional change (l.373-379).
- Trees very close together make fruit assignment to one tree or the other hard, so trees are grouped in batches (l.601-606).

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA:
- B1: 3D position only, Hungarian matching (l.667-675).
- B2: re-implementation of Riccardi et al. [27] with a histogram descriptor and Hungarian matching; costs normalised and fruit radius excluded (l.667-675).
- Hyperparameters tuned with Optuna on a subset of fruits (l.680-682). Baselines are not stated to have been tuned in the same way; treat as not stated.
- Result against B2 only: precision +23.7%, F1 +11.9% (l.683-686).
