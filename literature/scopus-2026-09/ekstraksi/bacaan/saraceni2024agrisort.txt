KEY: saraceni2024agrisort (Saraceni, Motoi, Nardi, Ciarfuglia; AgriSORT; arXiv:2309.13393v2, 28 Sep 2023; IEEE submission version)

CROP / SETTING / SCALE: Table grapes (Tendone trellis system) in a vineyard in southern Lazio, Italy; plants more than three years old, about 3 m apart. Multi-object tracking (MOT) of grape bunches for robotics. Five-sequence-scale dataset: 4 sequences (CloseUp1 300 frames, CloseUp2 300 frames, Overview1 100 frames, Overview2 100 frames), HD 1280x720; 23, 22, 20, 31 tracks; 2583, 3581, 1040, 721 boxes (Table I, Sec. III-A).

ACQUISITION: Video along vineyard rows, handheld Intel RealSense D435i (on a tripod for stabilization in Fig. 3) "simulating the motion of the robot". Recorded at 1280x720, 30 FPS; some sequences downsampled to 10 FPS. Pointed at grape bunches; close-up and overview shots; sudden direction changes, U-shaped paths. Side of row (left/right) and number of passes: not stated. Robot platform (CANOPIES, wrist-mounted D435i, Fig. 1) is described but the test data were not recorded on it. Ground truth: manual annotation in CVAT in MOT format.

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR: YOLOv5s (chosen for speed/accuracy trade-off). Trained on the table-grape dataset of [9, 8]: 242 annotated images and 1469 images labelled by a pseudo-label generation strategy. Input size: not stated. Hardware: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3070 Ti Laptop GPU. Detection accuracy: not reported; the same detector is shared by all trackers compared (Sec. IV-B). Detector output is the input to the tracker only; no detection-only metric (AP/mAP) is given.

ASSOCIATION METHOD: Tracking-by-detection, no appearance features. Each detection gets a Kalman filter with state [xc, yc, w, h] (Eq. 1-2). Camera motion estimated per frame by Shi-Tomasi features + Lucas-Kanade sparse optical flow, fitted to a 2x3 affine transform (Eq. 3). Prediction moves only the box centre by the affine transform and keeps w, h fixed (Eq. 4). Association by IoU between detections and predicted boxes, solved with the Hungarian algorithm (Sec. III-C). Track IDs are kept by the tracker; no counting line or counting rule is described. Alternatives tested in Table III: ORB matching and homography. Q and R noise: diagonal, sigma_q = 0.05, sigma_r = 0.00625, scaled by dt = 0.033 (30 FPS) or 0.1 (10 FPS) (Eq. 5).

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS:
- Objects of interest are static; the only motion source is the moving camera (Sec. III-B; Conclusion).
- Linear motion of boxes (the usual KF velocity terms) does not hold on rough terrain with vibration; velocity and size derivatives are therefore left out of the state (Sec. III-B).
- High frame rate relative to camera speed, so affine transforms approximate camera motion; perspective error is "limited" because robot motion is mostly parallel to the rows (Sec. III-B).
- Appearance features are not reliable because "all crops are visually similar and static" and no re-ID training data exist (Sec. I).
- Method is "general and easily extendable to any crop type for which a working detector is available" (Sec. I). Not tested beyond table grapes.

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED:
(a) same fruit in consecutive frames: addressed by the IoU/KF association; the paper says tracking is needed "to ensure objects are only counted once" (Introduction, citing [16][10]), but no counting experiment is reported.
(b) track lost and restarted: addressed only indirectly through MT/ML and ID-switch counts (Table II). The paper states that losing tracks makes "the same grape bunch gets counted more than once" (Sec. IV-D). No re-identification after loss.
(c) same fruit from both sides or different passes: not stated.

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING: None. Single class (grape bunch). The authors list "extending the tracker to mixed classes of objects" as future work (Conclusion).

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL: No count reference (no hand count on tree, harvest, or packhouse count). Ground truth is manual box and track annotation in CVAT (MOT format). Metrics computed with TrackEval: MOTA, IDF1, HOTA, FP, FN, IDs (ID switches), MT (mostly tracked), ML (mostly lost) (Sec. IV-C). Detection metrics: not reported.

KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS (AgriSORT row of Table II; columns MOTA / IDF1 / HOTA / FP / FN / IDs / MT / ML):
- CloseUp1: 65.93 / 72.00 / 48.71 / 459 / 872 / 11 / 11 / 4. Best MOTA, IDF1, HOTA, FN, IDs, MT among the five trackers (BoT-SORT, ByteTrack, OC-SORT, SORT, StrongSORT). FP 459 is the highest of the group (BoT-SORT 197, ByteTrack 51, OC-SORT 37, SORT 31, StrongSORT 21).
- CloseUp2: 66.13 / 73.00 / 56.08 / 655 / 1146 / 10 / 12 / 1. FP 655 is the highest (next: ByteTrack 371, StrongSORT 371).
- Overview1: 62.21 / 73.72 / 52.74 / 255 / 284 / 12 / 13 / 0. ByteTrack and StrongSORT also have ML 0 or close (StrongSORT ML 0); SORT has the highest MOTA among baselines (62.69) and HOTA 51.20.
- Overview2: 45.08 / 56.88 / 41.33 / 298 / 316 / 14 / 16 / 4. SORT has higher MOTA (49.515), IDF1 (61.96) and HOTA (44.431) than AgriSORT here (Table II, Sec. IV-D says SORT "remains competitive").
- Camera-motion variants (Table III, MOTA / HOTA / FPS): LK+Aff (the default) CloseUp1 65.93 / 48.71 / 25.93; CloseUp2 66.13 / 56.08 / 66.09; Overview1 62.21 / 52.74 / 55.99; Overview2 45.08 / 41.33 / 55.43. ORB+Aff is slower by about 34 FPS on average (except CloseUp1, where the gap is 5 FPS) (Sec. IV-D). No single variant dominates.
- Detection timing: about 10 ms per frame, same for all trackers, excluded from FPS.

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED:
- Frequent occlusion by overlapping crops, dense foliage and poles (Sec. III-A).
- Motion blur from fast motion; potholes or obstructions cause extreme movement (Sec. III-A, Fig. 4).
- Rapid illumination change, with light directed into the camera "causing unusable frames" (Sec. III-A).
- Camera vibration on steep terrain (Sec. III-A).
- SORT loses almost all IDs in CloseUp1 (fast non-linear motion, illumination change) (Fig. 5 caption).
- Homography is better in CloseUp sequences by HOTA, because of toughest motion including scale change (Sec. IV-D; mixed evidence, see below).
- Affine transform is "overly restrictive" for real robot motion (Sec. IV-D) (a limitation of the linear-motion case, not a measured failure).
- AgriSORT has the highest FP count in both CloseUp sequences (459 and 655); the paper does not discuss this.

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA: Yes, on the same four sequences and same detector: BoT-SORT, ByteTrack, OC-SORT, SORT, StrongSORT (Table II). Appearance-based trackers (BoT-SORT, StrongSORT) use pretrained default re-ID models, not fine-tuned (Sec. IV-B). LettuceTrack [18] is discussed but not run. Camera-motion variants compared in Table III.

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK: Improve camera-motion estimation with learning-based methods; extend the tracker to mixed classes including moving objects such as people or tractors (Sec. V). The paper says the affine model is "overly restrictive" (Sec. IV-D). No explicit limitations section.

INCONSISTENCIES INSIDE THE PAPER:
1. Text vs Table II on ID switches: Sec. IV-D says "OCSORT provides the best results in terms of 'IDswitch'", but in CloseUp1 OC-SORT has IDs 16 and AgriSORT has IDs 11 (Table II). In CloseUp2 OC-SORT has 9 and AgriSORT 10 (OC-SORT lower). So the claim holds for CloseUp2 only.
2. Text vs Table III on homography: Sec. IV-D says "homography works better in the 'CloseUp' sequences". In CloseUp1, LK+hom has MOTA 64.85 and IDF1 71.53, both lower than LK+Aff (65.93 and 72.00); only HOTA is higher (51.90 vs 48.71). In CloseUp2, LK+hom has MOTA 64.59 (lower than 66.13), IDF1 74.99 (higher), HOTA 57.24 (higher).
3. Method vs formula (Sec. III-C): Aij is defined as IoU, but then as binary ("Aij = 1 if the ith observation perfectly overlaps ... Aij = 0 otherwise"). The Hungarian assignment is said to "minimize the overall IOU", which is opposite to the usual overlap-maximizing assignment. The definition does not match the stated objective.
4. Table II, CloseUp2: ByteTrack and StrongSORT rows have identical FP (371), FN (1703), IDs (18), MT (8) and ML (3), but different MOTA, IDF1 and HOTA. Possible copy error; not checked against source data.
5. Table II vs Sec. IV-D on ML: text says AgriSORT "consistently outperforms all its competitors in terms of MT and ML tracks". In Overview1, AgriSORT ML 0 ties with StrongSORT (ML 0). Not a contradiction, a tie.
