KEY: anderson2021estimation

CROP / SETTING / SCALE: Mango (Mangifera indica) in commercial orchards in Australia (Northern Territory, north, central and south Queensland; regions as listed in Table A1). Main data: 20 orchards in 2019–2020 and 20 orchards from one farm in 2020–2021 (Sec. 3.2.1, 3.2.2). Tree spacing commonly 3 m (Sec. 3.3). Scale of output: orchard fruit load (count per orchard), with per-tree estimates from cumulative counts at tree-spacing intervals (Sec. 3.3). Example orchard in use case: 3 ha, 1000 trees (Sec. 3.3).

ACQUISITION: Continuous imagery of the tree row from a vehicle travelling along the row, two 5 MP Basler RGB cameras facing each row side, 10 fps (Sec. 2, as summarised in the text; the methods section was read in part only). Vehicle speed stated as 5–7 km/h (Sec. 2). Imaging at one assessment event per orchard; in 2020–2021 the imaging event was timed at stone hardening of fruit (Sec. 3.2.2). Per-tree estimate built from cumulative counts taken at the tree-spacing interval (Sec. 3.3). Imaging for a 3 ha, 1000-tree orchard takes about 21 min drive time (Sec. 3.3).

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR: MangoYOLO, described as a modified YOLOv3 (Sec. 2). Input size, training data size and detection accuracy (AP or precision/recall) not captured in the parts read in this pass. Orchard-level accuracy is reported as APE against packhouse counts (see KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS).

ASSOCIATION METHOD: Kalman-filter tracking of fruit across frames (Sec. 2). Counting rule: a fruit is counted when it has been absent for seven subsequent frames (Sec. 2). Size filter of 12 × 12 px applied to reject far-side duplicates (Sec. 2). Occlusion correction (method MV-CAL): a per-orchard factor for the proportion of occluded fruit, calculated from human counts of 18 calibration trees (Sec. 2, Sec. 3.2.1). No 3D reconstruction or SfM stated in the parts read. Random forest used only as an analysis of variance-explained for the MV/packhouse ratio (Appendix B.1.2), not as a counting step.

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS:
- Trees must have open canopies that give a view of fruit from interrow positions (Sec. 3.2.1, Sec. 4).
- The occlusion factor from the calibration trees represents the whole orchard. The authors state this was not supported (Sec. 3.2.1, Sec. 4).
- A single imaging event captures the crop when fruit set and maturity are uniform. The authors ascribe late-season under-counts to a prolonged flowering period, not to a change in occlusion (Sec. 3.2.2).
- The packhouse record is the reference value. The authors note several sources of error in it and recommend probing its accuracy before use (Sec. 4).
- Human counts of fruit per tree are accurate enough to be used as an input. The authors state errors in them propagate to orchard estimates (Sec. 3.2.1).

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED:
(a) Same fruit in consecutive frames: handled by Kalman tracking. A fruit is counted once when its track ends, i.e., after seven subsequent frames without detection (Sec. 2).
(b) Track lost and restarted: not stated in the parts read. The seven-frame absence rule is the only stated mechanism.
(c) Same fruit from both sides or different passes: cameras face both row sides; the 12 × 12 px size filter is stated to reject far-side duplicates (Sec. 2). Appendix B.1.2 states the risk of double counting from the two sides of the tree in young, small trees. Different passes are not discussed in the parts read.

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING: None stated. Fruit are counted without maturity classes. Fruit that reach stone hardening later in a prolonged flowering period are missed by a single imaging event (Sec. 3.2.2); no class attachment rule is given.

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL:
- Reference: packhouse fruit count per orchard (Sec. 3.2.1, Sec. 3.2.2). Manual reference methods: FARM (manual count on a single transect across each orchard) and CAL (manual count on calibration trees, stratified random sampling in three NDVI classes) (Sec. 3.2.1).
- Metric: APE (absolute percentage error) and PE (percentage error) per orchard and across orchards (Table 2, Sec. 3.2.2). Linear regression with R2 and slope against packhouse count (Figures 6 and 7). Significance test at 95% confidence for six orchards with all methods (Sec. 3.2.2); test name not captured in the parts read.
- No identity metrics (MOTA, IDF1, HOTA, ID switches) and no detection AP reported in the parts read.

KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS:
- 2019–2020 season, 20 orchards, Table 2 averages across all orchards (APE): FARM 26.7, CAL 17.9, MV-Raw 10.5, MV-CAL 16.4 (summary as read; Table 2 column headers were read earlier in the pass).
- 2019–2020 season, nine-orchard averages in Table 2: 25.7, 12.6, 11.1, 16.9 (as read earlier in the pass).
- 2019–2020 season, AVG (6 orchards) row in Table 2 (lines 903–907 of the extracted text): 26.2, 16.8, 23.5, 15.5; STD row 8.2, 8.8, 11.6, 7.3. Column assignment follows the Table 2 header, not re-verified here.
- Occlusion correction did not improve the estimate versus the packhouse count in 16 of 20 orchards in 2019–2020; MV-CAL had higher variability than MV-Raw (Sec. 3.2.1; Table 2).
- Figure 6: raw MV estimates vs packhouse, 2019–2020, n = 20, R2 = 0.98, slope = 0.74, p < 0.001 (Fig. 6, Sec. 3.2.1).
- Random forest on the MV/packhouse ratio, 15 orchards, 500 trees: R2cv = 0.39, RMSECV = 0.09; variable importance: tree age 10%, SD of TCA 8%, mean TCA 6%, row spacing 4%, tree spacing 3% (Sec. 3.2.1; Appendix B.1.2; Figure A1).
- 2020–2021 season, one farm, 20 orchards: APE across all orchards MV-Raw 17%, MV-CAL 23%, CAL 22%, FARM 27% (Sec. 3.2.2). For six orchards with all methods, no significant difference at 95% confidence between methods (Sec. 3.2.2).
- 2020–2021, orchards 1–16: PE of 32% (MV-Raw) and 9% (MV-CAL) (Sec. 3.2.2).
- Figure 7: 2020–2021 MV count vs packhouse, 20 orchards, R2 = 0.97, slope = 0.62, p < 0.001 (Sec. 3.2.2). A second farm of the same cultivar gave slope 0.77, R2 = 0.97 ("data not shown") (Sec. 3.2.2).
- Night-time repeatability: 12,902 vs 13,008 fruit, a difference of 106 fruit (0.1%) (Sec. 3.1; as read earlier in the pass).
- Daytime counts were 37–102% of the 21:00 count for orchard 28 (Sec. 3.1; as read earlier in the pass).
- Conclusions: APE of 3.4%, 5.0% and 8.2% for hedge, single leader and conventional systems respectively, across three cultivars and three planting densities (Sec. 4).
- Drive time about 21 min for a 3 ha, 1000-tree orchard versus about 3 h for manual counting of 18 trees (Sec. 3.3).

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED:
- Occlusion factor from calibration trees does not represent the orchard; corrected estimates less accurate than raw in most orchards (Sec. 3.2.1, Sec. 4).
- Extended flowering: fruit reaching stone hardening after the single imaging event are not counted, causing under-estimates (Sec. 3.2.2).
- Human count per tree errors propagate to orchard estimates; example: orchard 36, FARM APE 34%, and on-tree vs harvest count of 18 trees differing by 34% ("data not shown") (Sec. 3.2.1).
- Random forest explains only 39% of the variance in the MV/packhouse ratio; tree age and SD of TCA most important (Appendix B.1.2).
- Double counting from both sides of young, small trees, and occlusion in old, large trees with dense canopies (Appendix B.1.2).
- Packhouse record errors: several sources documented; not itemised in the parts read (Sec. 4).
- FARM under-performed CAL, attributed to a pseudo-systematic single-transect sampling strategy (Sec. 3.2.1).

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA: FARM, CAL, MV-Raw and MV-CAL compared against packhouse counts for the same orchards in 2019–2020 (Table 2) and 2020–2021 (Sec. 3.2.2). Six orchards with all methods available were compared for significance (Sec. 3.2.2). Manual count-based estimates (CAL, MV-CAL) were made three weeks after the MV event in 2020–2021 for orchards 1–16 (Sec. 3.2.2).

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK:
- Method restricted to orchards with open canopies (Sec. 3.2.1, Sec. 4).
- Need for better tools to estimate an occlusion factor for a given orchard (Sec. 3.2.1, Appendix B.1.2).
- Repeat estimates needed during seasons with extended flowering; down-sampling to imaging every third interrow recommended as a compromise (Sec. 4).
- Use cases such as the agronomic use of per-tree distributions "remains to be explored" (Sec. 3.3).
- Packhouse accuracy to be probed before use as reference (Sec. 4).
