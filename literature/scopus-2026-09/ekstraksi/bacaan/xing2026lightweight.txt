CROP / SETTING / SCALE: Cherry tomato (truss tomatoes) in a greenhouse, Qingdao (Kaisheng Haofeng (Laixi) Smart Agriculture Industrial Park), Nov 2024 to Mar 2025. Camera-to-plant distance 400-600 mm (lines 176-195). Counting is per video segment of plant rows; the unit of the Fig. 14 regression is not stated in the text read.

ACQUISITION: Intel RealSense D455 mounted on a mobile greenhouse inspection robot. Video 1280x720 at 30 FPS. Two-sided rows: "Each row consists of two lines of tomato plants growing in opposite directions" (lines 176-195). Lighting classes: normal about 65%, low about 25%, strong about 10%, all sunny. Frame-rate study at 10, 20 and 30 FPS (Table 5). Training images: part of a video used directly as validation; the rest keyframe-extracted at 60 FPS intervals, 1792 images annotated with Makesense (lines 196-200). Single camera; "multi-view" means image subregions over time, not multiple cameras (lines 1480-1482).

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR: Proposed detector is a lightweight cascaded network built on YOLOv8n-style baseline ("Model0"). Components: GhostConv, L-MFA, G-FConv (stacked twice), SPPF, CG block, LADH head (the text also writes "LDAH", see inconsistencies), SimAM after the highest-level head feature map (lines 246-379). Input 640x640 (line 536). Training: batch 64, LR 0.00672, momentum 0.86304, weight decay 0.00241, early stop after 50 epochs without mAP gain (lines 538-550). Classes: "rt" (ripe) and "ut" (unripe) (lines 201-203). Dataset: 17,940 "ut" and 33,433 "rt" instances (line 204). Split train/val/test 7:2:1 (lines 211-212).
Detection accuracy as reported: mAP50 90.8 (Table 1, Table 2), precision 88.3, recall 86.2, mAP50-95 59.8 for the proposed model (Table 1). Speed: 31.3 FPS on Jetson Orin Nano with TensorRT (conclusion, lines 1524-1527).

ASSOCIATION METHOD: Deep OC-SORT as the baseline tracker, with three modules: CMC (affine camera-motion compensation of predicted states, lines 438-441), DA (ReID appearance features, lines 434-438), AW (adaptive weighting of Mahalanobis and cosine distance, lines 442-446); Hungarian assignment (lines 431-432). The MVC method (lines 449-510) runs the same tracker separately inside each image subregion: "The MVC method partitions each frame into distinct regions" (lines 459-461). Within each subregion the cost matrix is Mahalanobis plus cosine, solved by Hungarian (lines 485-489). Detection confidence threshold 0.5 and IoU threshold 0.3; "If the IoU is equal to or below 0.3, they are treated as different targets and no association is performed." (lines 489-500). Cross-region step: the text names a "cross-view compensation module" that uses context across subregions to refine counting decisions (lines 463-468), and in Sec. 5 "re-detected and counted in adjacent regions, or counting errors can be reduced through boundary-based logical constraints" (lines 1234-1237). The matching rule that links a fruit seen in one subregion to the same fruit in another is NOT stated in the text read. No 3D, SfM or SLAM is used. The MVC is described as working "directly on 2D video streams from a single camera, using spatial partitioning and confidence-weighted fusion" (lines 1478-1483); the fusion rule is not given.
Counting rule: the text does not give an explicit counting rule (unique ID or counting line) for the baseline; it is described as "ID-based counting" (lines 1224-1226). For MVC the counting rule is not stated in formula form.
SVC (single-view compensation) is the comparison variant; its definition is given only by name in the Fig. 15 caption (lines 1465-1466).

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS:
- Tracking stability is only needed locally: "the stringent requirement for long-term tracking stability is relaxed to a weaker constraint on local tracking stability" (lines 476-479).
- Occluded targets are visible in another view: "targets occluded in one view can be effectively detected in other complementary views" (lines 481-484).
- Short-distance tracking keeps IDs stable (lines 497-500), no numbers given.
- Inter-frame displacement drives ID switches: higher FPS lowers them (lines 1053-1056).
- Camera motion is a source of error; no quantitative motion model is given.

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED:

(b) Track lost and restarted: the authors say "When a target is temporarily lost within a specific region due to occlusion or ID switching, it can be re-detected and counted in adjacent regions" (lines 1234-1237). - Camera motion shifts tomato positions between frames (lines 1508-1511).

- Authors' statement: "the primary bottleneck lies in association, not detection" (lines 994-995).

- ID switches and position shifts under camera motion "still require further refinement" (lines 1508-1511).
