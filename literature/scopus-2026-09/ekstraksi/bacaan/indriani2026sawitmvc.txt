Title: SawitMVC: A multi-view oil palm fruit bunch dataset for detection and counting. Data in Brief 67 (2026) 112990. Data article. Received 26 May 2026, revised 16 June 2026, accepted 16 June 2026.

CROP / SETTING / SCALE: Oil palm fresh fruit bunches (TBS), commercial plantations in Kabupaten Tanah Laut, Kalimantan, Indonesia. Two plantations: DAMIMAS 854 of 953 trees, LONSUM 99 trees (Sec 4.1, Abstract). 953 trees, 3,992 images, 18,540 YOLO bounding boxes, 9,823 unique bunches. Four Black Bunch Census (BBC) maturity classes B1 to B4, described as months to harvest: B1 (<=1 month), B2 (~2), B3 (~3), B4 (~4) (Abstract).

ACQUISITION: Discrete views, fixed positions, not video. 908 trees (95.3%) photographed from four views at 90-degree intervals; 45 trees (4.7%) from eight views at 45-degree intervals (Sec 3.2). Photographed 2-3 m from the trunk; "exact adherence was not verified" (Sec 4.2). Consumer smartphones, ten models listed (Sec 4.2). Images 960 x 1280 px portrait JPEG. Ambient outdoor light, automatic exposure. Collected February 2026 (Sec 4.1). Frame rate, platform and camera rig: not stated. A custom app, PohonKu, handled registration and view sequencing (Sec 4.2).

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR: Per-view bounding-box detection of fruit bunches. Ground truth: 18,540 boxes over 3,926 annotated images; 66 images have zero boxes (Sec 3.3). Annotation by seven trained annotators under supervision of an expert agronomist; one reviewer checked all annotations in full and applied corrections before export (Sec 4.3). Baseline detector: YOLO26m fine-tuned on the 716-tree training split, 60 epochs, batch 32, imgsz 640, patience 60, seed 42 (Sec 4.7). Test-split detection (Table 3, AP50 / precision / recall): overall 0.531 / 0.508 / 0.571; B1 0.739 / 0.602 / 0.776; B2 0.433 / 0.482 / 0.441; B3 0.599 / 0.515 / 0.674; B4 0.354 / 0.432 / 0.393. Only AP50 is reported; no mAP50-95.

ASSOCIATION METHOD: Cross-view identity is assigned by manual annotation, not by tracking, reconstruction or learned matching. Annotators link a bunch between side-by-side adjacent views using a custom web tool; each confirmed pair is stored in _confirmedLinks with sideA, bboxIdA, sideB, bboxIdB (Sec 3.4, Sec 4.4). A unique bunch is a connected component (transitive closure) of the link graph (Sec 4.4). The class_mismatch flag is set automatically when a component contains different classes (Sec 4.4). SfM, stereo or other reconstruction: not used for the labels; the paper contrasts this with apple datasets that "recover bunch identity from structure-from-motion or stereo reconstruction rather than expert annotation" (Sec 2).
Counting on per-view detections, evaluated on the 141-tree test split (Sec 4.7):
- Global correction: the naive class-wise sum divided by k = 1.8905, then rounded. k = 14,041 train boxes / 7,427 train unique bunches.
- SVR counter: four independent RBF support vector regressors, one per class, on a 13-dimensional per-tree vector (naive per-class sum; maximum and mean per-class count across sides; number of sides photographed). Scikit-learn defaults C=1.0, epsilon=0.1, gamma="scale". No hyperparameter search or validation-split selection. Features not standardised. Predictions clipped at zero and rounded.

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS:
- Dividing the naive sum by a single ratio k rescales for the average cross-view duplication. Stated: "Dividing by k rescales the inflated multi-view sum by the average degree of cross-view duplication, which makes it a natural heuristic deduplication reference." (Sec 4.7)
- The text states k "equals the mean number of views in which a bunch appears" (Sec 4.7). k is computed as boxes per unique bunch. These match only if a bunch never has two boxes in one view; this is not stated.
- Annotators can recognise the same bunch in adjacent views. Not measured beyond the single reviewer pass.
- Bunch class is the same across linked views. Implied by the class_mismatch flag; how often it was set is not reported in the text read.
- Test trees represent the population. Split stratified by variety and dominant maturity class (Sec 4.5).
- Maturity labels are the reference. The paper states that B2/B3 boundary is ambiguous "even under expert supervision" (Sec 4.x, Limitations); agreement was not measured.

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED:
(a) Same fruit in consecutive frames: not applicable. The dataset has discrete views and no video; no frame-to-frame method is described.
(b) Track lost and restarted: not applicable. No tracker is described.
(c) Same fruit from different sides: addressed. Bunches visible in adjacent views are linked by manual annotation; linked boxes form connected components, and each component is one unique bunch (Sec 4.4). Each unique bunch appears in 1 to 6 views; 74.6% in two or more; mean appearance count 1.89 (Sec 3.5). Example tree: 17 detections, 9 duplicates linked, 8 unique bunches (Sec 3.4). The two correction strategies estimate deduplication from per-view counts, not from the links (Sec 4.7).

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING: Four classes, class_id 0=B1, 1=B2, 2=B3, 3=B4 (Sec 3.3). Visual cues given by the authors: B1 red-orange at optimal harvest; B2 dark purple-to-black with full structural density; B3 tightly packed, elongated black fruitlets still expanding volumetrically; B4 smallest, recently out of the hidden pre-visible stage (Sec 3.3). Each unique bunch carries one class in the bunches array of the per-tree JSON (Sec 3.4). When linked views differ, class_mismatch is set; how such components are resolved is not stated. Unique bunches per class across the dataset: B1 954 (9.7%), B2 1,791 (18.2%), B3 5,067 (51.6%), B4 2,011 (20.5%) (Sec 3.5). Per-class counts are the operational output (Sec 2, Background).

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL: Count reference is the per-tree unique bunch count per class, from the annotated links, independent of detection (Table 1 footnote). Count metrics (Table 4 footnotes): Class ±1 accuracy (fraction of trees within ±1 of GT, macro-averaged over B1-B4); Tree ±1 accuracy (all four classes within ±1 at once); macro MAE; mean bias, macro-averaged. Detection metrics: AP50, precision, recall (Table 3). No MOTA, IDF1, HOTA or ID-switch metrics reported. No hand count on tree, harvest or packhouse comparison reported.

KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS:
- Split (Table 2): train 716 trees, 3,000 images, 14,041 boxes, 7,427 unique bunches; validation 96 trees, 404 images, 1,887 boxes, 992 unique; test 141 trees, 588 images, 2,612 boxes, 1,404 unique (B1 120, B2 256, B3 745, B4 283). Totals 18,540 boxes and 9,823 unique bunches; the split sums match.
- Per-tree unique bunches: 0 to 22, mean 10.31, median 10 (Sec 3.5).
- Per-image annotations: 0 to 10, mean 4.64, median 5 (Sec 3.3).
- Table 3 (YOLO26m, test): AP50 overall 0.531; B1 0.739; B2 0.433; B3 0.599; B4 0.354.
- Table 4 (test, 141 trees), Class ±1 / Tree ±1 / Macro MAE / Mean bias:
  - GT + naive sum: 50.00% / 6.38% / 2.142 / +2.142
  - GT + global correction (k=1.89): 95.57% / 86.52% / 0.356 / +0.009
  - GT + SVR: 96.81% / 88.65% / 0.303 / -0.048
  - YOLO26m + global correction: 72.34% / 30.50% / 1.119 / +0.381
  - YOLO26m + SVR: 75.35% / 33.33% / 1.027 / +0.158

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED:
- Detection error is the main source of loss end to end. Class ±1 falls from 96.81% (GT + SVR) to 75.35% (YOLO26m + SVR); Tree ±1 from 88.65% to 33.33%. Quote in QUOTES 6.
- Naive summation overcounts: Class ±1 50.00%, Tree ±1 6.38%, bias +2.142 on GT (Table 4).
- Lowest AP50 for B4 (0.354) and B2 (0.433) (Table 3).
- Two plantations, one collection period (February 2026), 89.6% DAMIMAS. Generalisation to other varieties "not established" (Limitations).
- Uncontrolled outdoor light, ten phone models, no depth information (Limitations).
- B2/B3 boundary ambiguous "even under expert supervision"; no inter-annotator agreement measured.
- Views taken at 2-3 m; adherence not verified (Sec 4.2).

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA:
- Global correction vs SVR on the same test split, from Table 4. Under GT detections SVR is higher on Class ±1 (96.81 vs 95.57) and Tree ±1 (88.65 vs 86.52), lower on MAE (0.303 vs 0.356), and its bias is larger in magnitude (-0.048 vs +0.009). Under YOLO26m detections SVR is higher on Class ±1 (75.35 vs 72.34) and Tree ±1 (33.33 vs 30.50), lower on MAE (1.027 vs 1.119), and smaller in bias (+0.158 vs +0.381). The text says the SVR gain is "most clearly on Tree ±1 and MAE" (Sec 4.7).
- Naive sum vs corrected counts on GT (Table 4).
- Dataset comparison (Table 1) is framed as capability, not performance: "a direct numerical comparison of model performance across them would not be meaningful" (Sec 2).
- Baselines are not tuned; the YOLO26m result is a reference point (Sec 4.7).

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK:
- Two commercial plantations in Kalimantan, one collection period; no seasonal change or wider management differences captured (Limitations).
- Generalisation to other varieties not established; DAMIMAS dominates (Limitations).
- No controlled lighting and no depth recorded. Future extension: "Future extensions could incorporate depth or reconstructed 3D geometry" (Limitations).
- B2/B3 boundary ambiguity; no formal inter-annotator agreement.
- No per-tree metadata such as tree age or canopy height (Limitations).
- Counters are "deliberately untuned reference[s]"; more method development is left to future work the dataset is intended to enable (Sec 4.7).

INCONSISTENCIES INSIDE THE PAPER:

- k = 1.8905 (Sec 4.7, from the train split) appears as "k=1.89" in Table 4. Consistent with rounding.
