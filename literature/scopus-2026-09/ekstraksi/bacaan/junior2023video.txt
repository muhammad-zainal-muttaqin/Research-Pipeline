CROP / SETTING / SCALE
- Crop: oil palm fresh fruit bunches (FFB, tandan buah segar), grading site in Central Kalimantan (Sec 2.1).
- Setting: outdoor, various positions and lighting (Q1).
- Classes: six maturity classes, ripe, over_ripe, under_ripe, unripe, empty, abnormal (Sec 2.1).
- Scale: 47 training videos and 10 test videos (Q2); 929 training frames from 49 videos (Q5). The two counts conflict (see INCONSISTENCIES).

ACQUISITION
- Smartphone video, outdoor (Q1). MP4, 1280x720, 30 fps (Sec 2.1). 360 degree rotation per bunch (Sec 2.1).
- Frames extracted with VLC at 1 frame per second: "using the VLC media player application using a ratio of 30 which means it takes 1 image frame every 1 s to avoid redundancy [27]." (Q4, Sec 2.2.1, pp.3-4).
- Split 7:3 for train and validation (Sec 2.1, 2.2.1). 4645 images after augmentation (Sec 2.2.1, Q5 context).
- Test data: 6 single-class videos, one per class (Q14, Sec 3.2); 4 multi-class videos (Sec 2.2.1). Ground truth: 3238 annotations for single-class, 1288 for multi-class (Sec 3.2).
- Data source: "the oil palm mill for supporting the preparation of dataset." (Q39). No plantation, location detail or tree count given.

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR
- Detectors: YOLOv4-CSPDarknet53, YOLOv4-Tiny, YOLOv4-Tiny 3L (Darknet framework) (Sec 2.2.2.1).
- Comparison models (TensorFlow Object Detection API, pretrained on MS COCO): SSD-MobileNetV2 FPN (input 320x320) and EfficientDet-D0 (input 512x512) (Q7, Sec 2.2.2.2).
- YOLO input 416x416 (Q8, Sec 2.2.2.3). Hardware: Nvidia Tesla P100, CUDA 11.2 (Q10, Sec 2.2.2.3).
- Observation unit: each frame is detected and scored on its own. No cross-frame or cross-video step is described.

ASSOCIATION METHOD
- not stated. The paper has no tracking, matching, or re-identification step. Tracking is named as future work: "Object detection against sequential images of oil palm fresh fruit bunches can be developed back into the next research such as object tracking." (Q3, Sec 2.1, p.3).

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED
- not stated. No counting method is reported. Counting is named as future work: "to automate in calculating each class that has been detected by the camera that can be used in real time." (Q31, p.19).
- The paper only mentions reducing redundant frames by sampling at 1 frame per second (Q4). It does not say this addresses the same bunch appearing in several frames.

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING
- Training images each contain one class only: "the training data used only has 1 class in each image." (Q16, Sec 3.2, p.14).
- Results: single-class videos get mAP50 up to 90.56% (Tiny 3L, Table 7). Multi-class videos get mAP50 up to 70.21% (Tiny 3L, Table 8).
- Authors attribute the multi-class drop to training data: "The results of experiments conducted showed that training using a dataset with only 1 category resulted in a model that was not good at testing data that had combination category." (p.16-17).
- Remedy proposed: "to improve detection accuracy in videos that have combination of class category, it is recommended to add multi-category data for training." (Q33, p.19).
- Maturity is the only target. Classes are not merged or ranked by the model.

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL
- Metric level: per-frame detection. Metrics are mAP50, F1, IoU, precision, recall (Sec 2.2.2.5, Eq 11-14).
- Ground truth: bounding boxes per frame (Q14, Sec 3.2: "3238 ground truth annotations").
- No object-level count reference, count error, or per-bunch count is reported.
- IoU definition conflict: see INCONSISTENCIES.

KEY QUANTITATIVE RESULTS
Single-class video (Table 7, p.14; test set):
- YOLOv4-CSPDarknet53 (Model_5): mAP50 97.64%, IoU 73.36%, F1 0.93, 19 s, 41.5 FPS.
- YOLOv4-Tiny (Model_12): mAP50 83.57%, IoU 56.90%, F1 0.78, 4 s, 105.03 FPS.
- YOLOv4-Tiny 3L (Model_16): mAP50 90.56%, IoU 58.35%, F1 0.79, 5 s, 104.95 FPS.

Multi-class video (Table 8, p.16):
- CSPDarknet53 (Model_5): mAP50 48.54%, IoU 33.13%, F1 0.43, 13 s, 44.8 FPS.
- Tiny (Model_12): mAP50 59.17%, IoU 42.83%, F1 0.59, 2 s, 118.6 FPS.
- Tiny 3L (Model_16): mAP50 70.21%, IoU 45.50%, F1 0.63, 4 s, 108.2 FPS.

Augmentation (Table 5, Sec 3.1, test set):
- No augmentation: mAP50 91.82%, F1 0.83, IoU 64.72%.
- Photometric: mAP50 92.87%, F1 0.82, IoU 63.17%.
- Geometric: mAP50 93.93%, F1 0.87, IoU 67.73%.
- Combination: mAP50 94.98%, F1 0.87, IoU 67.38%.

Frozen layers (Table 9, Sec 3.3, test set, single-class): 0 frozen 94.98 / 67.38; 10 frozen 94.68 / 69.69; 23 frozen 98.53 / 74.31; 54 frozen 96.83 / 73.42 (mAP50 / IoU, %).

Comparison, single-class video (Table 10, p.20): EfficientDet-D0 mAP50 99.30%, IoU 87.10%, 52 s; SSD-MobileNetV2 FPN mAP50 98.51%, IoU 87.12%, 30 s.

Comparison, multi-class video (Table 11, p.21): EfficientDet-D0 mAP50 54.51%, IoU 85.11%, 36 s; SSD-MobileNetV2 FPN mAP50 70.04%, IoU 84.47%, 20 s.

Table 11 values have not been re-checked against the text (see INCONSISTENCIES).

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED
- Multi-class video: "the entire YOLOv4 model has not been able to detect more than 1 class well in a video frame due to the training data used only has 1 class in each image." (Q16, p.14).
- Outdoor conditions: "Detecting oil palm maturity in real-time is challenging due to obstacles such as piled objects, dynamic lighting, objects with different angles of view, and shadows that can cause discoloration of the oil palm fruit to get darker." (p.15).
- Anchor boxes: "The IoU value on EfficientDet-D0 and SSD-MobileNetV2 FPN is much higher than YOLOv4 because the methods for generating anchor boxes differ." (p.16). Not tested.
- Data collection: "Collecting video data takes longer than collecting non-sequential images due to several problems such as unpredictable weather and limited time for ideal lighting, such as between 11:00 a.m. and 01:00 p.m." (p.17).
- Training: the model is "stuck at the local minimum, this is because the number of steps in the training is still not enough." (Q27, p.19). No loss curve or evidence is given for this claim.

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA
- Same single-class and multi-class test videos for YOLOv4 variants, EfficientDet-D0 and SSD-MobileNetV2 FPN (Tables 10-11). The paper does not state that the TensorFlow models used the same split, augmentation, or training budget as the Darknet models. Not verified.
- Single-class: EfficientDet-D0 and SSD have higher IoU (87.10, 87.12) than the best YOLO (74.31). Authors say: "all YOLOv4 architectures perform very well to detect single category class videos." (Q28, p.19).
- Multi-class: SSD mAP50 70.04 is close to Tiny 3L 70.21, but EfficientDet mAP50 is 54.51. Both TensorFlow models have much higher IoU (85.11, 84.47) than YOLO (at most 46.16).
- Authors' recommendation: "YOLOv4-Tiny 3L architecture is the most balanced among other architectures because it has fast training time, fast detection rate and light computing level but has relatively high accuracy but with a concern YOLOv4-Tiny 3L is more suitable for relatively small datasets." (Q34, p.19).

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK
- Single-class training data: "This could be because the dataset used for training is a single category with only one type of class in the video data for each category." (Q22, p.16).
- Tracking and per-class counting: Q3 and Q31 (see ASSOCIATION METHOD and DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES).
- Localization: "We suggest adding geometric augmentation with a variety of more varied tilt angles to increase accuracy in object localization." (Q32, p.19).
- Dataset size: Tiny 3L "is more suitable for relatively small datasets. We believe that the use of large datasets will be more suitable against YOLOv4-CSPDarknet53." (Q34, p.19).
- Novelty claim: "We succeeded in detecting the type of maturity level of oil palm fresh fruit bunches using a dataset in the form of video or sequential image which was applied to the object detection model as a novelty, where previous studies only used non- sequential images." (Q30, p.19). The paper does not show a comparison with prior non-sequential studies.

INCONSISTENCIES INSIDE THE PAPER
- Video count: Sec 2.1 gives 47 training videos and 10 test videos (57 total) (Q2). Sec 2.2.1 gives 929 frames "extracted from 49 videos" (Q5).
- IoU definition: Sec 2.2.2.5 says the evaluation IoU is "the CIoU loss value described in section 2.2.2.3." (Q9). Eq 3 defines IoU as the overlap ratio. These are different quantities.
- Validation IoU: Sec 3.2 says Model_5 is "2.03% higher than Model_1" (Q13). Table 6 gives 84.12% and 82.41%, a difference of 1.71 points (about 2.07% relative). The stated 2.03% matches neither.
- Relative vs absolute percentages in Sec 3.1: "1.5% better" and "1.15% better" are relative differences from Table 5, but the text does not say so. Test IoU "4.56% better" is an absolute difference.
- Table 5 F1: photometric test F1 0.82 is lower than no augmentation 0.83. The text says augmentation "can improve the mAP50 and IoU Score of models." (Q12, Sec 3.1, p.13). F1 is not mentioned.
- Model labels: the text says Tiny 3L is on "Model_14" (p.14), but Table 8 gives Tiny 3L as Model_16.
- Tiny 3L multi-class mAP50: text gives 72.15% (p.19, Q27), Table 8 gives 70.21%.
- Depth: Sec 3.2 says CSPDarknet53 is better "because YOLOv4-CSPDarknet53 has more layer depth than YOLOv4-Tiny." (p.13). p.14 says "Layer depth and the number of parameters are not determinants of model performance." (Q20).
- Speed: Table 7 gives CSPDarknet53 19 s and Tiny 3L 5 s (gap 14 s). Table 8 gives 13 s and 4 s (gap 9 s). The text says "The detection speed is only 2 s different" (p.14) without naming the pair.
- Table 7 and Table 10 disagree on detection time: Tiny (Model_12) 4 s in Table 7 and 5 s in Table 10; Tiny 3L (Model_16) 5 s and 4 s.
- Table 10 "Parameters (Million)" column gives 53 for DarkNet 53 and "<10" for the Tiny models, and 3.9 and 15 for the TensorFlow models. The unit does not match the column name.
- Table 10 combine tuning gives test IoU 59.5%, below Model_1 (67.38%). The text says the combined tuning "increases the IoU by 3.76%" (Q29, p.19). The 3.76 base is not identified in the tables.
- Table 8 Tiny IoU 42.83% and Table 11 Model_12 IoU 42.84%. Table 8 Tiny 3L time 4 s and Table 11 Model_16 time 3 s.
- Figure 15 caption contains the typo "abnormla" (Q35).
- Time-to-train: the text says freezing layers "can reduce the required training time of approximately 2-3 h" (Q18, p.15). No training times per model are shown.
