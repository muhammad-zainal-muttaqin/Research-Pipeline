KEY: genemola2020fruit

CROP / SETTING / SCALE: Fuji apple (Malus domestica Borkh. cv. Fuji) in a commercial orchard (Universitat de Lleida, Catalonia, Spain, per affiliation). 11 trees (T01-T11), Table 1. Ground truth 1444 fruits (GTfield) and 1353 labelled fruits (GTlabels), Table 1. Canopy geometric characterization (height, width, cross-section area, leaf area) is also reported.

ACQUISITION: Mobile terrestrial laser scanner (MTLS) on an air-assisted sprayer, pulled by a tractor. Sec 2.1: "The MTLS system was placed on an air-assisted sprayer and was pulled by a tractor at 0.125 m/s forward speed along a linear trajectory parallel to the row of trees." Sensor: Velodyne VLP-16 3D LiDAR (16 beams, +-15 deg horizontal FoV), replacing a 2D LiDAR in Escola et al. (2017) (Discussion). Two sensor heights: H1 and H2 (numeric heights not verified in the lines read; not stated in this record). Sides: E and W. Conditions: forced air flow (af) versus no air (n). Single linear pass per side; scans from east and west sides were merged into one point cloud (Sec 2.2). Frame rate, camera and exact distance to the row: not stated in the sections read.

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR: Not a camera detector. Pipeline on 3D point clouds: pre-processing by IR reflectance threshold (Sec 2.2: "Pre-processing was based on the fact that apples have a higher IR reflectance than background."); DBSCAN clustering with eps = 0.03 m; linear SVM (C = 0.35) predicts number of fruits per cluster; K-means for clusters with K > 1; a second SVM as false-positive filter; feature selection (FS). Detection accuracy reported as DR, R, P, FDR, MDR, F1, each on GTlabels (L) and GTfield (F) bases where stated. Ground truth: bounding-box annotation of apples in 3D point clouds (TP = overlap > 50%).

ASSOCIATION METHOD: No tracker or temporal association across frames. Cross-view handling is by merging point clouds from different scans before clustering. Sec 2.2: "Merging different scans in a single point cloud allows for reducing the number of multi-detections, e.g., points from an apple appearing in two different scans (such as from east and west sides) were merged in a unique point cloud, and in consequence, the apple was detected only once." Multi-view = combining sides E and W, or sensor heights H1 and H2 (H(1+2)). Counting is done per cluster by the SVM/K-means step, not by a unique ID. Matching of identities across scans by SfM, SLAM or learned matching: not stated.

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS: Apples have higher IR reflectance than background (Sec 2.2). Fruits closer than eps = 0.03 m are grouped in one cluster (Sec 2.2). Ground truth overlap threshold 50% defines TP (Sec 2.4). In yield prediction, correlation between detections and actual fruit count matters more than the percentage detected (Sec 2.4, citing Linker, 2017). The detector is trained, and test uses an 11-fold cross-validation, one tree per fold (Sec 2.4). Implied: fruits not visible in the point clouds are excluded from the count (see Sec 2.1 below).

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED:
(a) Same fruit in consecutive frames: not applicable (no frame sequence; point cloud per scan).
(b) Track lost and restarted: not applicable; not stated.
(c) Same fruit from both sides or different passes: addressed by merging E and W scans into one point cloud before clustering (Sec 2.2 quote above). Also, multi-detections counted as false positives when clusters split wrongly (Sec 2.2: "...or from clusters that were wrongly split, detecting apples more than once (multi-detections)."). In TP scoring, "In case of multi-detections, only one true positive was counted." (Sec 2.4).
Effect reported: Sec 3.2 and Abstract (merging sides and heights reduces occlusion, not explicitly double counts). Sec 2.2 claims merging reduces multi-detections, but no quantified double-count rate is stated.

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING: none. Single class (apple). Ripeness or maturity classes: not stated.

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL: Two references. GTfield: fruits counted on the tree in the field (1444 total). GTlabels: fruits labelled in the 3D point clouds (1353 total). Sec 2.1: "Thus, 6.3% of apples were discarded since they were not visible in the 3D point clouds." Metrics: DR (detection rate), R (recall), P (precision), FDR, MDR, F1, each with L and F bases where stated; yield prediction by linear regression; per-tree percentage error; RMSE over 11 trees; R2. No MOTA, IDF1, HOTA or ID switch metrics. Harvest or packhouse count: not stated.

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED:
- Single-side scanning gives the lowest detection (Sec 3.2).
- Forced air flow lowered detection under H1,af,(E+W): point cloud "blurred", some apples dis-occluded and others occluded (Sec 3.2).
- Sensor at H2 (upper position) fails on fruits at the bottom of trees (Sec 3.2).
- Combining all trials H(1+2),(n+af),(E+W) raised DR but lowered recall; cloud more blurred, harder to split groups (Sec 3.2).
- False positives: trunks or leaves with reflectance above R > 60%, and wrongly split clusters (Sec 2.2).
- 6.3% of apples not visible in point clouds (Sec 2.1).
- Yield error: Tree11 E side error 49.3% in Table 4 (predicted 237.3 vs GT 159); not discussed in text read.

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA: Single-side vs both-side vs height vs air-flow conditions within this orchard (Tables 3-5). Gongal et al. (2015) cited for the GTfield basis. No comparison to other methods on the same data is reported in the sections read.

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK: Conclusions: "Future works should focus in the analysis of fruit occlusions in different training systems and extending the present study to other fruit varieties." Discussion: distribution of fruits across the tree centre attributed to canopy porosity "however further analysis is needed."
