CROP / SETTING / SCALE: Calamansi (Citrofortunella microcarpa) fruit on a commercial farm, Calangi Farm, Aurora, Naujan, Oriental Mindoro, Philippines. Farm is 5 ha with more than 3000 trees; data taken from 300 trees (Sec. II.A). Shooting Jan-Feb 2024, 8-11 AM and 2-4 PM. Tree height 3-6 m (Sec. II.A). Paper is an ICCAE 2025 conference paper (17th ICCAE). Title says "Fruit Yield Prediction", but the measured outputs are tracking metrics on video; no harvest or yield measurement is reported.

ACQUISITION: Video along a path around the tree. Camera: DJI Air 25 drone with a 20 MP camera, video 5472x3078 at 25 fps, daylight (Sec. II.A). Distance: "The drone maintained a distance of 1 to 1.5 meters from the target, with the camera positioned vertically upward or below while either hovering or gradually navigating around the tree." Minimum altitude "of 4 to 8 meters over the tallest tree". Video clips stored on SD card; frames converted to pictures via Roboflow. Sampling or frame-skip rate: not stated. Number of videos per tree or number of passes: not stated. Sides of the row: not stated; the flight is described as a path around one tree.

OBSERVATION / DETECTOR: YOLOv8m, input size 640 (Table I). Trained on a customized dataset annotated in Roboflow, with two classes: flowers and fruits (Sec. II.B). Training: 100 epochs, batch 32, imgsz 640, SGD, initial LR 0.01, patience 20 (Table I). Detector accuracy, Table II (labeled "EVALUATION RESULTS OF YOLOV8M"): YOLOv8m precision 0.839, recall 0.794, F1 0.815, mAP@0.5 0.875; Roboflow 3.0 precision 0.774, recall 0.668, F1 0.717, mAP@0.5 0.724. YOLOv8n and YOLOv8s were compared in text only ("consistently exhibits enhanced performance across many metrics"); their numbers are not reported. Dataset size, train/val/test split, per-class AP, and mAP@0.5-0.95: not stated.

ASSOCIATION METHOD: Tracking-by-detection. YOLOv8m outputs a bounding box and class ID per frame. "The tracker assigns a unique ID to each object that it detects and tracks across frames" (Sec. II.C). Three trackers compared (Sec. II.B): SORT (Kalman Filter and Hungarian Algorithm; motion model); DeepSORT (SORT plus appearance data from a re-identification network, Re-ID); ByteTrack (uses both high- and low-confidence detections). Tracker hyperparameters and the Re-ID network: not stated. No 3D, SfM, SLAM, calibration, or statistical correction. Counting rule (Sec. II.C): "It checks if the object's ID has already been counted for that class. If the ID is new (not yet counted), the count of the object will be added. Each unique object is counted only once for each class across all frames." Counting is per class.

ASSUMPTIONS STATED OR IMPLIED BY AUTHORS:
- Tracker IDs stay stable enough that one fruit keeps one ID across a video (implied by the counting rule, Sec. II.C).
- Multi-view acquisition gives views of fruits occluded from one viewpoint: "obtaining data from many views, positing that calamansi fruits obscured from one viewpoint will be observable from another [20]" (Introduction). The authors accept the cost: "this method may result in an overestimated fruit count due to double counting from various image angles" (Introduction).
- Yield estimates need tracking so that objects are "counted only once in every frame [24]" (Introduction).
- Training data represent the target fruit and flowers (implied by Discussion: the model detects only the trained classes).
- Not stated: frame rate used for tracking, maximum time a fruit may be out of view, or how a fruit visible from both sides of a tree is handled.

DOUBLE-COUNT SOURCES ADDRESSED:
(a) Same fruit in consecutive frames: addressed by the tracker ID plus the once-per-class counting rule (Sec. II.C). No per-frame double-count rate is reported; the only evidence is aggregate MOTA, HOTA and IDF1 (Table III).
(b) Track lost and restarted: not stated. No ID-switch count, fragmentation count, or track-restart analysis. DeepSORT's Re-ID is the only re-linking mechanism described (Sec. II.B); the paper does not test whether it helps on this data.
(c) Same fruit from both sides or different passes: not stated as addressed. The paper names multi-angle double counting as a risk (Introduction) and proposes multi-view sensing, but the counting rule (Sec. II.C) works only per tracker ID within one video. No cross-video or cross-pass association is described.

CLASS / MATURITY HANDLING: Two classes, flowers and fruits (Sec. II.B). The class comes from the detector output for each frame. Counting is per class: "Each unique object is counted only once for each class across all frames" (Sec. II.C). Maturity or ripeness classes: none. What happens when frames disagree on the class of one ID: not stated.

COUNT REFERENCE AND METRIC LEVEL: No hand count, harvest count, packhouse count, or count error is reported. Tracking metrics: MOTA, IDF1, HOTA (Sec. II.D; Table III). Detection metrics: precision, recall, F1, mAP@0.5 (Table II). ID-switch counts: not reported. How the tracking ground truth was annotated (who, which frames, how many frames): not stated.

FAILURE CONDITIONS / ERROR SOURCES REPORTED:
- Occlusion: "Counting fruits poses a challenge, especially because of fruit occlusions [19]." (Introduction)
- Appearance variation: the Introduction notes that fruit can look different under varying lighting and storage conditions (wording not quoted; exact sentence not re-checked against the text).
- Double counting from multiple image angles, which "may result in an overestimated fruit count" (Introduction).
- Detector limited to the trained classes: "The model could only detect calamansi fruits and flowers because the data used to train the model were customized." (Discussion)
- SORT performed worst. The authors attribute this to missing appearance information: "these findings suggest that the appearance characteristics of the SORT algorithm do not significantly assist fruit tracking." (Conclusion). This is an interpretation; no test isolates appearance features.
- No failure-case analysis or error breakdown reported.

COMPARISONS ON THE SAME DATA:
- Detectors: YOLOv8m vs Roboflow 3.0 (Table II), on the paper's dataset. YOLOv8n and YOLOv8s: text only, no numbers.
- Trackers: SORT, DeepSORT, ByteTrack (Table III). Whether all three ran on the same videos is implied by the Results section; the tracking dataset is not described further.
- No comparison with other fruit-counting, multi-view, or 3D methods.

AUTHORS' STATED LIMITATIONS / FUTURE WORK:
- Detection limited to flowers and fruits because the training data are customized (Discussion).
- Future database for detected objects, with indexing, partitioning, sharding, and caching, citing [25] (Discussion). Not implemented or tested.
- No other limitations stated. Not stated: tracking evaluation sample size, generalization to other orchards or cultivars, validation against harvest or hand counts.
