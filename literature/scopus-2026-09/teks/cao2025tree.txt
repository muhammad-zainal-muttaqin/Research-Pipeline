In orchard environments, mango detection faces multiple 
challenges, including variations in lighting (Tang et al., 2023; 
Kang et al., 2020), complex backgrounds (Liu et al., 2022), 
and the similarity in color between mangoes and leaves (Zhang 
et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2020; Xu et al., 2020), among others 
(Fig. 1), these factors significantly increase the difficulty of 
detection. Single-modal RGB images struggle to meet 
detection requirements in complex orchard scenes, while the 
application of depth and thermal images offers new 
possibilities for fruit detection. For instance, depth images can 
enhance recognition accuracy by extracting features of fruits 
and leaves (Liu et al., 2018), and thermal images can enable 
fruit detection under insufficient lighting conditions (Hespeler 
et al., 2021). However, depth images are prone to information 
loss under poor lighting or extreme weather conditions, and 
thermal images often fail to distinguish between similar targets 
Mango, hailed as the “King of Tropical Fruits”, holds a 
significant position in the global fruit trade (Hussain et al., 
2024; Gupta et al., 2022). Fruit identification is an essential 
component of contemporary agricultural systems. It serves as 
the foundation for yield estimation and automated harvesting, 
among other orchard management activities. It also plays a 
crucial role in the development of innovative harvest 
technology.

Abstract: Accurate mango detection holds an important role in for harvest robotics. Addressing the issue 
of low detection efficiency of mangoes on trees in complex environments, this study proposes a mango 
detection method based on multi-modal image fusion. This method utilizes the YOLOv8 object detection 
algorithm to perform a pairwise fusion of RGB, depth, and thermal images, as well as a fusion of all three 
types of images, and compares the results with those from single-image detection. This approach fully 
leverages the advantages of different image modalities: RGB images provide rich color and texture 
information, depth images offer spatial location information of the target objects, and thermal images 
effectively overcome the impact of lighting variations and occlusions. Specifically, RGB-depth-thermal 
fusion method effectively compensates for the shortcomings of single-image or pairwise fusion under 
complex lighting, occlusions, and background interference, significantly enhancing the performance of 
mango detection. Experimental results show that the fusion of RGB, depth, and thermal images achieves 
an average precision (AP) value of 97.2%, significantly improving the accuracy and robustness of mango 
detection compared to single-image and pairwise fusion methods. This study provides new insights and 
methods for fruit target detection in complex environments, offering practical application value.

2. MATERIALS AND METHODS 
The proposed method focuses on addressing challenges such 
as lighting variations and complex backgrounds in detecting 
on-tree mango images. It encompasses five critical steps: data 
acquisition, image registration, fusion, dataset construction, 
and model evaluation, aiming to optimize detection 
performance. 
2.1 Image acquisition 
In a commercial orchard in Tiandong County, Baise City, 
Guangxi, China, we conducted data collection for “Guiqi” 
mangoes using an D435i RGB-D camera (Intel Corporation) 
and an Optris PI infrared thermal imager (Optris Corporation) 
from August 6-10, 2024, in the afternoon. RGB and depth 
images were captured simultaneously by the same camera to 
ensure data consistency, with resolutions of 1280×720 and 
640×360, respectively. The infrared thermal imager provided 
thermal images with a resolution of 382×288, visually 
displaying the temperature distribution on the surface of the 
mangoes. To enhance collection efficiency and minimize 
external interference, we utilized a handheld platform 
equipped with both devices to simultaneously capture image 
samples of these three modalities (Fig. 2).  Ultimately a total 
of 353 sets of images were acquired.

2.2 Image registration 
The registration process aims to ensure spatial consistency 
across images. However, in practice, various factors such as 
differences in camera fields of view, focal lengths, frame rates, 
and camera movement, often lead to inconsistencies in the 
spatial alignment of images. Due to the differing fields of view 
and hardware characteristics of the RGB-D camera and the 
infrared thermal imager, the images captured by these devices 
frequently exhibit geometric misalignment. Therefore, it is 
necessary to register the RGB and depth images obtained from 
the RGB-D camera with the thermal images acquired from the 
infrared thermal imager. 
Initially, considering the broader field of view of the RGB-D 
camera, we crop the RGB and depth images to match the field 
of view of the thermal images. Since the depth images are 
proportionally aligned with the RGB images in terms of pixels, 
we developed a tool that allows for the cropping of the RGB 
image, simultaneously adjusting the depth image at the 
corresponding pixel points. By selecting two corresponding 
points on the diagonal of the thermal image and clicking on the 
RGB image, we obtain RGB and depth images that align with 
the field of view of the thermal image. Subsequently, we 
proceed with image registration, including between RGB and 
depth images, RGB and thermal images, depth and thermal 
images, and among all three. Given the significant differences 
in image resolution and the complex background of the 
orchard, we designed a manual registration tool that supports 
affine transformation through the selection of control points. 
We employ a feature point registration method, which involves 
identifying as many corresponding feature points as possible 
in the two images, storing their coordinates, and calculating 
the transformation matrix. This matrix is then used to perform 
an affine transformation on the image to be registered, 
preserving the straightness and parallelism of the image. 
Specifically, the operation steps for fusing three modalities 
involve first annotating the image data of any two modalities, 
followed by annotating the remaining modality with the fused 
image of the first two. In this study, the RGB and thermal 
images were first annotated, and then the resulting fused image 
was used for further annotation with the depth image. Fig. 3 
presents the feature point annotation interfaces for RGB and

2.3 Image fusion strategy 
In deep learning, multimodal fusion strategies combine data 
from different modalities to enhance model performance and 
generalization 
capabilities, 
thereby 
obtaining 
more 
comprehensive and richer information. In this study, we adopt 
a semi-automatic approach to fuse different modal data at the 
initial stage of the model, generating fused images. We have 
designed a semi-automatic fusion tool that does not require the 
images to have the exact resolution, only similar fields of view. 
During operation, two images to be fused are first selected, 
followed by manual annotation of their corresponding feature 
points. Specifically, the RGB and thermal images are 
annotated first, then their fused image is annotated with the 
depth image. Finally, the images are fused using a 
transformation matrix to perform an affine transformation, 
resulting in RGB and depth, RGB and thermal, depth and 
thermal, and three-modal fusion images (Fig. 3).

2.4 Dataset generation 
In a Python environment, we utilized the LabelImg tool to 
manually annotate rectangular bounding boxes around mango 
fruits in RGB images, depth images, thermal images, and their 
four types of fused images, with the category labeled as 
“Mango”. After annotation, the data was saved in PASCAL 
VOC format as XML files, which include the location, size, 
and category information of the mangoes, suitable for training 
machine learning models. Additionally, we applied various 
data augmentation techniques such as translation, rotation, 
mirroring, brightness adjustment, and adding salt-and-pepper 
noise to increase the diversity and complexity of the dataset, 
ultimately resulting in 2118 sets of image data across seven 
categories. 
To adapt the dataset for training with YOLO series networks, 
we converted the XML format annotation files into the TXT 
format required by YOLO, containing object categories and 
bounding box coordinates. Subsequently, the dataset was 
divided into a training set (80%), a validation set, and a test set 
(10% each), partitioning the validation and test sets from the 
remaining non-training data. 
2.5 Detection model 
YOLO is an efficient real-time object detection algorithm that 
occupies a niche in numerous real-time application scenarios 
due to its end-to-end training approach and exceptional 
performance in both speed and accuracy (Jiang et al., 2022). 
YOLOv8, as a newer member of this series, demonstrates top-
tier performance in object detection, image classification, and 
instance segmentation. It integrates the advantages of 
predecessor models such as YOLOv5, YOLOv6, and YOLOX. 
It introduces several innovations: (1) In the Backbone, 
YOLOv8 adopts the Cross Stage Partial (CSP) module concept, 
replacing the C3 module in YOLOv5 with the C2f module. 
This achieves lightweight performance while, in combination 
with the SPPF module, fine-tuning is conducted for models of 
different scales, significantly enhancing performance. (2) The 
Head section employs a popular decoupled head structure, 
separating classification and detection tasks and shifting 
towards an Anchor-Free mechanism. (3) In terms of loss 
functions, YOLOv8 introduces Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) 
Loss for handling classification tasks while simultaneously 
using Distribute Focal Loss (DFL) and Complete-IoU (CIoU) 
Loss to optimize regression tasks. Additionally, it adopts the 
Task-Aligned Assigner strategy for positive and negative 
sample assignments, abandoning traditional methods such as 
IoU or one-sided ratio assignment.

3.2 Analyzing the approaches in the mango detection task 
Firstly, the performance of the baseline methods was evaluated 
as single-modal projects. Among the three baselines, RGB 
images achieved the highest performance on YOLOv8, with 
an AP value of 94.8%. This indicates richer information about 
mangoes in RGB images extracted by the detection model. On 
the other hand, the model’s performance with depth images 
was the lowest, with an AP value of 93.8%. Noise in depth 
images can be caused by dense shadows and reflections, 
making it easy for the detection model to misidentify target 
objects. In addition, the uneven distribution of pixel brightness 
in the target area affects overall performance. 
Secondly, the fusion methods’ performance results were all 
higher than the single-modal baselines. From the analysis of 
these values, it can be concluded that fusion methods 
significantly outperform non-fusion methods in detecting 
mangoes. When RGB, depth, and thermal data are combined, 
they complement each other and enhance the results. This is 
because fusion preserves all modal information, allowing the 
detection model to learn more comprehensive features. For 
dual-modal projects with pairwise fusion, the fusion of RGB 
images and thermal images achieved the highest performance 
on YOLOv8. Still, the performance of the final three-modal 
fusion was the best, with an AP value of 97.2%. 
This multimodal method helps to enhance detection rates and 
contributes to enhanced decision-making in agricultural 
operations. The increased reliability that is produced via this 
strategy is beneficial.  In fact, by utilizing different datasets, 
we can we can develop more robust models that adapt to 
various environmental conditions, ultimately contributing to 
more efficient and sustainable agricultural management. 
4. CONCLUSIONS 
Within the scope of this investigation, fusion techniques were 
used to examine mango identification tasks by using 
multimodal data, which included RGB, depth, and thermal 
information. The experimental results demonstrate that multi-
modal data fusion exhibits significant advantages in 
agricultural fruit detection tasks. By integrating the RGB 
images and depth information obtained by the RGB-D camera, 
its detection performance is significantly better than that of the 
single-modal method. When higher detection accuracy is
