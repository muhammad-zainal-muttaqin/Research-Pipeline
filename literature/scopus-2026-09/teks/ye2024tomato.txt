Abstract—In order to address the challenges of inaccurate 
crop phenotypic parameters and unclear crop growth status in 
crop yield prediction, this study focuses on tomato fruits in 
greenhouse production environments. It delves into the 
three-dimensional reconstruction of multi-view images of 
tomato fruits. This paper introduces an enhanced YOLOv8 
tomato fruit recognition model to address the challenge of 
overlapping fruits, leaves, and stems in complex background 
environments. 
By 
incorporating 
the 
CBAM 
attention 
mechanism into the model backbone, the recognition 
mAP@0.5 index improves to 99.3%, a 1.9% increase from the 
original model. Three-dimensional reconstruction technology 
is currently a research focus in the field of crop yield 
prediction. The three-dimensional structure of a crop is a 
reflection of its growth status. By establishing an accurate 
three-dimensional crop model, researchers can analyze crop 
organs to obtain phenotypic parameters. The MiDaS algorithm 
estimates depth, and the Wonder3D algorithm achieves 
three-dimensional reconstruction of single-view images. These 
methods offer an effective approach for reconstructing tomato 
fruits in three dimensions from multiple viewpoints, with 
potential applications in tomato yield prediction and the 
development of harvesting robots. 
Keywords—multi-view image, Tomato, three-dimensional 
reconstruction, MiDaS 
I. INTRODUCTION 
The tomato originates from the Andes Mountains of 
South America and holds significant value as a fruit and 
vegetable crop worldwide[1]. Its cultivation area is steadily 
increasing, making it the top crop in global vegetable 
production with the highest consumption rate among 
vegetables[2]. In China, tomatoes play a crucial role as an 
economic crop, leading to income growth for farmers and 
enhancing agricultural efficiency[3]. The tomato detection 
results of traditional algorithms are often influenced by 
factors such as lighting and occlusion, leading to a low 
recognition rate for tomatoes. Additionally, these algorithms 
struggle to strike a balance between accuracy and real-time 
performance, which limits their practical utility. In recent 
years, the advancement of graphics processing unit (GPU) 
computing capabilities has facilitated the widespread 
adoption of deep learning in various domains, particularly 
revolutionizing the realm of smart agriculture[4]. Currently, 
target 
detection 
algorithms 
utilizing 
deep 
learning 
techniques are typically categorized into one-stage and 
two-stage approaches. In one-stage object detection, a 
single convolutional neural network (CNN) is employed to 
directly process the entire image for object detection and 
category prediction, resulting in faster processing compared 
to the two-stage method. Examples of one-stage methods 
include Single Shot MultiBox Detector (SSD) [5] and You 
Only Look Once (YOLO) [6]. On the other hand, the 
two-stage object detection method involves generating 
candidate regions first and then classifying these regions 
using a CNN. Representative methods in this category 
include Region-based Convolutional Neural Network 
(R-CNN) [7], Fast R-CNN[8], Faster R-CNN[9], and Mask 
Region-based Convolutional Neural Network (Mask-RCNN) 
[10]. Existing models face challenges in achieving rapid 
detection while maintaining high accuracy. Both domestic 
and foreign scholars have conducted thorough research on 
methods for identifying tomato fruit and have made 
significant progress. Wang et al. [11] identified mature fruits 
with varying growth stages by extracting characteristics and 
shape parameters of interference objects. Chen et al. [12] 
isolated the region of interest for mature tomatoes and 
extracted edges within that area. Iwasaki et al. [13] detected 
red areas using image information and then conducted 
detection by tracking contours. Fang Zheng et al. [14] 
utilized the hue component in the HSI model to employ the 
maximum variance method and the Hough transform 
method for image segmentation in order to detect ripe fruit 
under varying degrees of occlusion. Conventional image 
segmentation techniques are susceptible to interference 
from external factors such as light, wind, and noise [15]. As 
GPU technology and big data mining continue to advance 
[16], deep learning-based target detection methods have 
emerged as the predominant approach in fruit recognition 
[17], supplanting traditional detection methods.

II. YOLOV8 ALGORITHM 
YOLOv8 is an upgraded version of the YOLO series, 
building upon its predecessor [18,19]. Its architecture is 
composed of several key components, including input, 
backbone, neck, and prediction. The prediction layer is 
further divided into 4 parts, incorporating essential elements 
like pooling module, path aggregation network, and 
detection head. The backbone network extracts features 
from the input image, while the neck bridges the gap 
between the backbone and the head, providing additional 
feature maps [20]. The prediction layer utilizes multiple 
convolutional layers to forecast the location, size, and 
category of objects. The network structure is visually 
depicted in Fig. 1. 
 
Fig. 1. Improved YOLOv8 network structure 
A. Integrating 
Attention 
Mechanisms 
for 
Improving 
Backbone Networks 
Tomato fruits are typically photographed while still 
attached to the tomato plants, rather than being harvested 
and then photographed. These plants are known for their tall 
stature, lush branches, and abundant leaves, which often 
obstruct the view of the fruits. This obstruction poses a 
challenge 
for 
the 
subsequent 
three-dimensional 
reconstruction of the tomato fruits. To address this issue, 
especially considering the large number of fruits in some 
images and the occlusion caused by leaves and stems, the 
CBAM (Convolutional Block Attention Module) was 
integrated into the C2f module within the YOLOv8 model 
backbone 
network. 
The 
CBAM 
is 
a 
lightweight 
convolutional attention module that incorporates both 
channel and spatial attention mechanisms[21]. By focusing 
more on the target object through attention operations in 
both spatial and channel dimensions, the algorithm aims to 
enhance accuracy and robustness in fruit recognition. The 
network structure can be visualized in Fig. 2.

III. 
OVERVIEW OF DEPTH ESTIMATION 
Depth estimation plays a crucial role in the realm of 
computer vision as it involves generating a depth map from 
RGB images to represent the spatial distance between image 
pixels and the camera. This technology finds widespread 
applications in various fields such as industrial machinery, 
autonomous driving, and 3D modeling. Depth estimation 
techniques are typically categorized into supervised learning 
and unsupervised learning approaches, each with distinct 
characteristics and practical uses. Monocular depth 
estimation, which involves inferring depth information from 
a single image, encompasses both traditional machine 
learning and deep learning methods, with the latter 
generally exhibiting superior accuracy. On the other hand, 
stereo depth estimation utilizes image sequences or 
binocular images to create three-dimensional stereoscopic 
visuals, offering higher accuracy but necessitating higher 
data quality, thus limiting its application scope. 
A. MiDaS Model 
1) 
Model introduction 
The accuracy of monocular depth estimation relies 
heavily on diverse and comprehensive training datasets. To 
address the challenge of obtaining precise depth information 
across various scales, Ranftl et al. [22] introduced a 
scale-shift invariant supervision mechanism using a large 
dataset in 2020. Additionally, they implemented a more 
efficient optimization strategy to enhance the model, paving 
the way for advancements in the field of depth estimation. 
MiDaS is a model trained using multi-objective 
optimization across 12 diverse datasets, such as RedWeb, 
DIML, Movies, MegaDepth, WSVD, TartanAir, HRWSI, 
ApolloScape, BlendedMVS, IRS, KITTI, and NYU Depth 
V2. For model training, Ranftl et al. chose five 
representative 
and 
varied 
datasets: 
DIML.Indoor, 
MegaDepth, ReDWeb, WSVD, and 3D movies. In Fig. 6, an 
overview of different MiDaS models is provided through 
bubble size, visually representing the scale differences 
among them. The size of each bubble corresponds to the 
number of model parameters.

B. 3D Reconstruction of Tomato Fruits 
Traditional methods for reconstructing 3D point cloud 
fruit focus on achieving a balance between accuracy and 
robustness to noise and missing points. Transforming 
single-view images into three-dimensional geometric 
structures is a fundamental task in computer graphics and 
three-dimensional computing. This task holds significant 
importance in various fields including virtual reality, video 
games, three-dimensional content generation, and precise 
robot operations. However, there are limited reconstructions 
for crops due to the complex growth environment where 
reconstruction objects may be obstructed by leaves, stems, 
and weeds. The challenge of 3D reconstruction from 
single-view images is particularly arduous as it lacks direct 
solutions and necessitates the ability to infer both visible 
and 
invisible 
3D 
shapes. 
This 
study 
adopts 
a 
three-dimensional reconstruction method proposed by Long 
et al. [23], which focuses on generating high-fidelity texture 
meshes from single-view images. The method incorporates 
multi-view 
cross-domain 
attention 
mechanisms 
and 
geometry-aware 
normal 
to 
achieve 
high-quality 
three-dimensional reconstruction. The fusion algorithm 
demonstrates excellent reconstruction results on animal 
objects, showcasing robust generalization performance and 
high efficiency. The network structure diagram can be seen 
in Fig. 8. A single-view image is used to generate a 
consistent multi-view normal map and color image by 
incorporating the image, text embedding from the CLIP 
model, multi-view camera parameters, and domain switcher 
as conditions. A normal fusion algorithm from the 2D 
representation is then applied to robustly reconstruct 
high-quality 3D geometries and generate high-fidelity 
textured meshes.

IV. 
CONCLUSION 
This 
study 
presents 
an 
in-depth 
experimental 
investigation on the three-dimensional reconstruction of 
single-view tomato fruit images, enhancing the YOLOv8 
model through the incorporation of the CBAM attention 
mechanism. Experimental findings demonstrate that the 
performance of the YOLOv8-CBAM model surpasses that 
of the original model, leading to improved image feature 
extraction capabilities. The MiDaS algorithm is utilized to 
determine the depth of tomato fruits, and its experimental 
results are satisfactory for the subsequent three-dimensional 
reconstruction process. Subsequently, the Wonder3D 
algorithm was employed for three-dimensional modeling of 
tomato 
fruit, 
successfully 
generating 
a 
complete 
three-dimensional image from a single-view perspective. 
These findings offer valuable insights for future research in 
three-dimensional reconstruction and serve as a reference 
for multi-view image reconstruction. The application of the 
above methods has important guiding significance in tomato 
yield prediction and development of picking robots. 
Although this article has achieved certain results in the 
three-dimensional reconstruction of tomato fruits, there are 
still many aspects that require further exploration and 
research on existing problems. This article mainly realizes 
the three-dimensional reconstruction of tomato fruits in the 
mature stage in the actual production environment. The 
reconstruction objects less. Subsequent research will focus 
on tomato fruits from the fruiting period to the ripening 
period, conducting three-dimensional reconstruction to 
obtain a model for data support in predicting tomato fruit 
yield. To address the issue of model size, future work will 
focus on creating a lightweight and improved model that 
can be deployed on a tomato picking robot to enhance fruit 
picking efficiency.
