In order to achieve accurate detection of litchi fruits in their natural environment, a ripe
litchi recognition method with a Red-Green-Blue Depth (RGB-D) camera, which can be used
to estimate fruit yields, is proposed in this paper. First, both colour and depth images of
litchis are collected using an image capturing system with an RGB-D camera. Then,
redundant image information outside the effective picking range of the manipulator is
excluded using depth image segmentation. Finally, the random forest binary classiﬁcation
model is trained employing colour and texture features to recognise litchi fruits. Simul-
taneously, an approach based on multi-scale detection and a non-maximum suppression
Algorithm is proposed to further improve the fruit detection precision. Visual recognition
experiments using the proposed method to recognize red and green litchis are designed,
and the method achieves a recognition accuracy of 89.92% for green litchis and 94.50% for
red litchis.

1.
Introduction
In modern agricultural production, while the workforce is
declining, fruit-picking robots can replace part of the tradi-
tional labour force to perform tedious and repetitive tasks
(Bergerman et al., 2013; Bechar & Vigneault, 2016, 2017).
However, most of the existing fruit recognition and localiza-
tion methods are applied in speciﬁc scenarios, and many of
them are not robust enough when facing uneven illumination
and high colour similarity between target fruits and the
background. Therefore, designing a robust fruit recognition
Algorithm for complex scenes is critical for picking robots.
For some fruits with colours obviously different from the
background, most scholars have utilised colour feature-based
segmentation algorithms to recognise fruit targets (Fu et al.,
2011, 2019; Liu et al., 2016; Zhao et al., 2016). In addition,
some scholars mapped original images into different colour
spaces, such as HIS, Lab, or YCrCb, to extract of more diverse
colour features (Areﬁet al., 2011; Yin et al., 2009). However,
these recognition algorithms are not robust enough in com-
plex scenes, especially when the target’s colour is similar to
the background or the lighting conditions change (Wang et al.,
2016). Therefore, recognition methods based on texture or
geometric shape features to effectively recognise fruits have

combining colour features and texture or shape features is
beneﬁcial to solve the fruit misjudgement problem. Recently,
with the development of deep learning, some fruit detection
methods (Bargoti & Underwood, 2017; Fu et al., 2020; Inkyu
et al., 2016; Liang et al., 2020; Apolo-Apolo et al., 2020) have
employed deep neural networks (such as the Faster R-CNN

recognition performance. It is undeniable that deep neural
networks have achieved superior recognition performance in
fruit detection; however, they are usually implemented with
relatively higher computational complexity and larger storage
requirements than non-deep learning approaches.
Litchis are a characteristic fruit of South China, and its
annual yield is approximately 1.45 million tons (Chen &
Huang, 2014). It is more difﬁcult to recognise string fruits
such as litchis and grapes than single fruits such as apples
and pears. First, the litchi growth environment is too
complex, making it difﬁcult to separate litchi fruits from the
image background. Second, the surface of the litchi fruit is
uneven, so the recognition results could be easily affected by
light changes. Finally, as the colours between green litchi
and leaves are similar, it is trickier to accurately recognise
green litchis. Therefore, few studies on the recognition of
green litchi fruits are available. Wang et al. (2017) used four

He et al. (2017) employed improved LDA (Linear Discriminant

build a strong classiﬁer of green litchis based on the Ada-

not high, and the calculation process was complex due to
excessive hyper parameters. In another study, Xiong et al.
(2018a,b) used the Otsu method to segment red litchi fruits,
but the recognition environment was monotone and strict,
and the method was not suitable for litchi recognition in
their natural environment. Guo et al. (2019) presented a
detection method based on monocular machine vision to
detect overlapping conditions of growing litchi fruits in or-
chard environments with changing illumination, but more
effective descriptors with higher precision rates should be
considered for litchi detection.
In recent years, with the development of depth sensors,
increasingly more researchers have applied 3D spatial infor-
mation to computer vision tasks to optimize fruit detection
algorithms and further fruit positioning (Andersen et al.,
2012). To detect individual apples, Nguyen et al. (2016) ob-
tained colour and depth images of apples through an RGB-D
camera and then used a clustering Algorithm based on the
Euclidean distance to classify point cloud data. However, this
method was only effective for red fruits and required complex
pre-processing of depth data and colour data. When the
colour of fruit is similar to the background, it is often impos-
sible to effectively segment fruit based on the colour feature
only. To solve this problem, Barnea et al. (2016) proposed a
method using three-dimensional sliding windows and the
SVM algorithm to extract the shape feature of fruit by
combining the image plane and three-dimensional space for
fruit recognition. Instead of directly utilising the point cloud
data to perform fruit segmentation experiments, Tao and
Zhou (2017) ﬁrst removed redundant information and the
background by setting a depth threshold; then used Colour-
FPFH, an improved three-dimensional descriptor, as the
feature; and used the genetic algorithm to optimise the SVM
model. Similarly, Fu et al. (2020) used depth features to ﬁlter
out undesirable background objects, and their experiments
indicated that this method could potentially increase the
precision and efﬁciency. In summary, the above studies
illustrate that the depth information of an image is conducive
to the separation of the fruit target and the background.
Simultaneously, depth images also provide depth information
for picking robots so as to realise the picking process after fruit
recognition. Although the Kinect depth sensor does not work

2.
Materials and methods
2.1.
RGB-D imaging system
The RGB-D imaging system designed in this study is shown in
Fig. 1. It is comprised of a Kinect V2 and a computer with an
8 GB RAM, the Windows 10 OS and the OpenCV 3.4.1 software
program with C/Cþþ to implement the recognition Algorithm.
The Kinect V2 is made by Microsoft Inc. and composed of an
infrared (IR) light source, an IR camera and an RGB camera. The
infrared and depth images can be obtained by the IR camera
with a resolution of 512  424 while the RGB images can be
obtained by the RGB camera with a resolution of 1920  1080.
In this study, the RGB-D imaging system was used to collect
images of red litchis (Nuomici) and green litchis (Feizixiao) in a
natural light environment. Litchi image collection was per-
formed in Donglin Fruit Garden, Zengcheng district, Guangz-
hou city, Guangdong Province, China from 10:00 am to 15:00 pm
on May 31, 2018 and from 10:00 am to 15:00 pm on August 3,
2018. The scenes of litchi image collection are shown in Fig. 2.
In total, colour, infrared and depth images of Nuomici and
Feizixiao litchis were randomly collected in 220 groups,
among which 110 groups were Nuomici and 110 groups were
Feizixiao images. Among these images, 200 groups of litchi
images were selected, including 100 groups of Nuomici and
100 groups of Feizixiao images, with some examples shown in
Fig. 3. Figure 3a and c are Feizixiao images, and Figure 3b and
d are Nuomici images. As shown in Fig. 3, litchi fruits,
branches and leaves on trees are littery and complex in the
natural environment. The colour of Feizixiao in the natural
environment is green and similar to that of the leaves.
Particularly, it is more similar to the leaves in backlight than in
normal light, so it is hard to recognise Feizixiao litchi fruits
automatically with texture and colour features only. In order
to realise the automatic detection of multiple litchi fruits, the
texture, colour and depth information of litchi fruits should be
combined.
2.2.
Dataset
In order to train the random forest classiﬁer and evaluate the
performance of the Algorithm, ground truth labelling was
performed for the collected RGB-D images. In this study, the
image blocks of positive and negative samples, namely, litchi
fruit samples and background samples, were used as the input
for feature extraction. To assess the litchi fruit recognition
accuracy, the positions of litchi fruits in images needs to be
marked. Therefore, the ground truth labelling was divided
into two components. The ﬁrst component was image block
cropping, which aims to obtain positive and negative samples,
respectively, used in the training set, as shown in Figures 4a
and b. The second part was labelling the litchi fruits in the
images after depth segmentation, as shown in Figures 4c and
d.
In this study, a total of 100 groups of green litchi images
and red litchi images were collected and pre-processed via
image registration and depth segmentation. First, 100 groups
of images were selected randomly with 50 groups for each
experiment, and then 2000 image blocks were cropped from
the RGB images directly with the sizes being the same as the
size of the sliding window. The cropped positive sample
blocks all contain a whole litchi fruit, which covered the whole
block as far as possible. The cropped negative sample blocks
consist of 60% green litchi leaves, 20% litchi branches, 10%
land and 10% other background, such as the black image areas
beyond the effective distance after depth segmentation. In
this study, 400 positive samples and 600 negative samples
were cropped in each experiment.
In order to make the whole images covered by ground truth
labelling, 80 groups of the remaining litchi images after depth
segmentation were selected randomly with 40 groups for each
experiment. The litchi fruits in the image were labelled, and
then the unmarked pixels were labelled as background, as
shown in Fig. 5. Figure 5a is the RGB-D litchi image after image
registration, Fig. 5b is the litchi image after depth segmenta-
tion, and Fig. 5c is the image after ground truth labelling.
Similarly, litchi fruit covers the whole image block as far as
possible.
It is worth noting that the ground truth labelling used in
the controlled experiment to explore the effectiveness of
depth segmentation, which will be described in Section 2.5, is
different from that of other experiments. In this controlled
experiment, ground truth labelling was directly conducted
using the RGB-D images in the test set without depth seg-
mentation, as shown in Fig. 6. Figure 6a is a litchi RGB-D
image, and Fig. 6b is the corresponding result of the ground
truth labelling.

To classify litchi fruits and background, the input features
of the random forest (Breiman, 2001) are the R and G compo-
nents of the RGB colour space, all components of the HSV
colour space, the a and b components of the Lab colour space,
the Y and Cr components of the YCrCb colour space, the HOG
descriptor and the improved LBP descriptor.
Owing to the different sizes of litchis in an image, the
detection Algorithm would not be robust when bounding
boxes with the same size are used. The lengths of image
feature vectors will be different, and the prediction accuracy
will be decreased when bounding boxes with different sizes
are used. In order to obtain a general feature extraction
interface and reduce the difference in the sizes between the
bounding box and the corresponding fruit, a multiscale
detection method based on bilinear interpolation (Hager &
Belhumeur, 1998) was adopted in this study. Litchi fruit was
recognised using a single bounding box from multiple images
of different sizes. After the whole image was marked, 10 litchi
images were selected randomly, including 5 red and 5 green
litchi images, and 10 litchi fruits were randomly chosen from
these images to calculate the areas of their bounding boxes.
Then, the area ratio of the maximum bounding box to the
minimum bounding box was calculated, and the zooming
scales were set as 0.8, 1.0 and 1.2. Meanwhile, the average area
of bounding boxes was determined as the area of the sliding
window. Since most of the litchis are regularly circular, the
sliding window was designed as a square with a size of 16  16
pixels for calculation convenience.
The results of multi-scale detection often have multiple
bounding boxes for the same litchi target. To select an optimal
bounding box for a litchi target, the idea of Non-maximum
Suppression (NMS) Algorithm (Felzenszwalb et al., 2010) was
used in this study. We set the suppression threshold as the
intersection area between two prediction windows greater
than 50% of the smaller prediction window, and the number of
votes of the decision tree in the random forest model was used
as the sorting basis to select the optimal window. The algo-
rithm is described as follows.
Step 1. The bounding boxes of the RF prediction are sorted
by number of votes from large to small and stored in list L1.
Step 2. Obtain the ﬁrst element with the most votes from L1
and calculate its intersection area with the rest of the
elements in turn. If the intersection area is less than 50% of
the area of the smaller bounding box, the element with the
fewest votes will be removed from list L1.
Step 3. The ﬁrst element of list L1 described in step 2 is
removed from L1 and stored in list L2.
Step 4. Repeat steps 2 and 3 until list L1 is empty.
2.5.
Experimental design
In this study, 1000 red litchi and green litchi image blocks with
the same size of sliding window (16  16) were used as the
training sets of the red and green litchi experimental groups,
respectively. That is, there are 400 negative sample image
blocks and 600 positive sample image blocks in each experi-
mental group. Then, the image-registered RGB-D images cor-
responding to the 100 groups of litchi images labelled by the
whole image described in Section 2.2 were used as the test set
to evaluate the performance of the proposed Algorithm. For
the experiment, the shooting distance was empirically deter-
mined as 50e150 cm. Because litchi fruit is string fruit and a
single litchi has a small volume, a short shooting distance
leads to fewer fruits in an image, and a long shooting distance
leads to more fruit region areas in an image, making detection
difﬁcult.
First, using the training set of green litchi fruits, the colour
and texture feature vectors of each sample image were
computed to train the random forest classiﬁer. The training
procedure was stopped when the accuracy reached 95% or
more so as to obtain the random forest classiﬁer C g for green
litchi fruit detection in the experimental group. The same
method was used to train the random forest classiﬁer C r for
red litchi fruit recognition.
In order to verify the adaptability of the proposed Algo-
rithm to different litchi varieties, comparative experiments,
namely, the detection of green litchi under natural light and
the detection of red litchi under natural light, were designed.
The trained random forest classiﬁer was used for each
experimental group. Depth segmentation was conducted
using the test set to remove invalid backgrounds. Then, the
image was scaled at multiple scales and processed by a sliding
window of the same size. The feature of each image block was
extracted, and each image block was predicted by the trained

3.2.1.
Analysis of the feature selection
The results of the controlled experiment with feature sets as
variables are shown in Table 3. Owing to the large colour dif-

features were selected, achieving decent recognition results.
However, both of them were lower than the recall and accu-
racy, 0.9517 and 0.9450, respectively, with a combination of
colour features and texture features. For the green litchi
dataset, when only colour features were selected, the recall
and accuracy of litchi recognition were 0.5387 and 0.5740,

slightly

than
random
judgements,
namely, 50% for binary classiﬁcation. Because there are some
negative samples in the green litchi dataset with large colour
differences from the target samples, such as the black invalid
background excluded after depth segmentation and the trunk,
the colour features can be used to pre-process the green litchi
images. Since the textures of different varieties of litchis are
similar, the recall and accuracy of litchi recognition for
different varieties were very close when the texture features
were selected only, namely, 0.6480 and 0.6751, respectively,
for the red litchi dataset and 0.6517 and 0.6756, respectively,
for the green litchi dataset. In summary, combining texture
features and colour features can recognise litchi fruits more
accurately. In particular, the combination of texture and
colour features can greatly improve the recognition accuracy
of green litchis.
3.2.2.
Analysis of the classiﬁcation algorithms
The results of the controlled experiments with different
classiﬁcation algorithms are shown in Table 4. In the process
of collecting litchi image samples, there are often fewer
available target samples than background samples, leading to
the problem of unbalanced samples. The random forest is an
integrated learning Algorithm that is stochastic and insensi-
tive to multicollinearity and has the advantages of robustness
and high accuracy for recognition of unbalanced samples.
Therefore, in most cases, the recall, precision and accuracy of
the random forest classiﬁcation method, which were 0.9517,
0.9001 and 0.9450, respectively, using the red litchi dataset and
0.8996, 0.8335 and 0.8992, respectively, using the green litchi
dataset in our experiment, were higher than those of the SVM
and the Adaboost algorithms. Nevertheless, these machine
learning classiﬁcation methods can achieve relatively decent
litchi recognition results. Therefore, the classiﬁcation algo-
rithm used in our proposed method can be replaced.
3.2.3.
Analysis of the effectiveness depth segmentation
The results of the controlled experiment with and without
depth segmentation are shown in Table 5, which shows that
the recall rate of litchi recognition without depth segmenta-
tion is slightly higher. However, this does not mean that the
result of litchi recognition without depth segmentation is
better. When ground truth labelling was conducted on the
images after depth segmentation, as in other experiments, the
recalls of red litchis and green litchis were 0.9517 and 0.8996,
respectively. Both of them were higher than those without
depth segmentation, namely, 0.8772 for the green litchi
dataset and 0.9475 for the red litchi dataset. The images
without depth segmentation were directly labelled to obtain
the ground truths, and some pixel regions belonging to litchi
fruits that were inaccessible to the picking robot were
excluded directly during the depth segmentation. Namely,
this kind of pixel is a false negative in the experiment with
depth segmentation, so the recall will be lower; however, this
part of the judgement errors has no inﬂuence on the picking
robot. Therefore, this study believes that it is more accurate to
measure the results using the FPR in the controlled experi-
ment. Table 5 shows that the false positive rates of the
experiment with depth segmentation, 0.0873 with the green
litchi dataset and 0.0444 with the red litchi dataset, were lower
than those of the experiment without depth segmentation,
0.1579 with the green litchi dataset and 0.0734 with the red
litchi dataset. This shows that depth segmentation can
effectively reduce the false positive rate of litchi recognition.
Moreover, the FPR will be reduced by nearly 50% in our
experiment with depth segmentation for green litchi detec-
tion. The reason is that depth segmentation removes some
invalid backgrounds that are quite different from the positive
samples, such as blue sky and white clouds, and it reduces
types of negative samples that should be learned by the binary
classiﬁer. That is, in the training process of binary classiﬁers,
the classiﬁers can pay more attention to distinguishing posi-
tive samples from indistinguishable negative samples, such
as green litchi leaves.

4.
Conclusions
This study proposed a method of ripe litchi recognition for two
varieties of litchis using RGB-D images. Colour images and depth
images were ﬁrst captured, and litchi fruits were subsequently
recognised and located after image registration and depth seg-
mentation. The conclusions are summarised as follows.
(1) The proposed Algorithm is effective not only for the
recognition of red litchis such as Nuomici, but also for
the recognition of green litchis such as Feizixiao in a
natural environment. The recall, precision and accu-
racy are 89%, 94%and 92%, respectively, for green litchi
recognition. This method can be effective for green
litchi recognition and provides technical support and
references for the recognition of other green fruits
such as green pears, apples, grapes and citrus fruits.
(2) Depth segmentation can effectively reduce the false
positive rate of litchi recognition.
In addition, taking advantage of the registration of RGB and
depth images, we can establish the three-dimensional co-
ordinates of the fruits, so to further achieve the spatial loca-
tion of the fruits.
