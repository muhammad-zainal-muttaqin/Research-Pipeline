Integration of Hybrid GA-PSO-SA with Self-adaptive Dynamic Attention for 
Multi-objective CNN-based Oil Palm Fruit Ripeness Classification 
 
Usman Usman1*          Fatchul Arifin2          Rustam Asnawi3  
 
1Department of Engineering Science, Faculty of Engineering, Yogyakarta State University, Yogyakarta, Indonesia

Abstract: Determining exactly when oil palm fruit is ripe is crucial for maximizing harvest efficiency, but current 
methods achieve only 65–75% accuracy, resulting in 15–20% annual losses.  This research introduces a hybrid GA-
PSO-SA metaheuristic optimization framework featuring self-adaptive dynamic attention for multi-objective CNN-
based classification.  The best CNN+GA+PSO setup used 10,000 photos from five Indonesian plantation sites, with 
four ripeness classifications (Unripe, Underripe, Ripe, Overripe). It achieved 95.73% accuracy and 95.84% F1-score, 
indicating a better balance between bias and variance with only a 0.16% precision-recall gap.  The suggested technique 
achieves a mean test accuracy of 96.12% ± 0.39% (max 96.86%) across 10 independent runs, with a narrow 95% 
confidence range [95.85%, 96.39%]. This is much better than baseline methods like MobileNetV2 (95.53%), ResNet50 
(95.27%), and ViT-tiny (96.06%).  The ablation study shows that dual-metaheuristic optimization is 18.23% better 
than single methods. However, adding more architectural complexity (SA, CBAM, SADA) worsens performance due 
to the curse of dimensionality.  Leave-Site-Out cross-validation achieved 94.66% ± 0.54% accuracy across previously 
unobserved locations, thereby validating spatial generalization.  The Pareto-optimal solution has a latency of 2.61 ms 
and a model size of 43.6 MB, which is 4.1 times quicker than ViT-tiny. This enables the deployment of precision 
agriculture apps on edge devices in real time. 
Keywords: Hybrid GA-PSO-SA, Multi-objective CNN, Oil palm fruit, Dynamic attention, Pareto-optimal. 
 
 
1. Introduction 
Figuring out the right time to harvest is a major 
challenge in the industry. Mistakes in judging fruit 
ripeness cause losses of 15-20 percent, costing 
billions of rupiah each year [1]. The current method 
depends on harvesters visually checking the fruit, 
which is only 65-75% accurate and varies a lot 
depending on each worker’s experience. As a result, 
the quality is often inconsistent [2]. 
CNNs have been successful in agricultural image 
classification and reported satisfactory accuracies of 
85–92% for multiple fruit commodities [3]. 
Nevertheless, the use of CNNs on oil palm fruits 
involves complex issues that are not well addressed. 
The VGG-16 model achieved an accuracy of 83.4%; 
however, under strong lighting conditions and 
occlusion, it was unable to generalize properly. 
ResNet50 outperformed with 87.1% accuracy, but its 
inference time (2.3 seconds) is not fast enough for 
real-time field applications [4]. The MobileNetV2 
approach is more efficient (0.4 seconds), but its 
accuracy drops dramatically to 79.2% on datasets 
with high variation [5]. 
Three fundamental gaps were identified from the 
literature review: First, conventional CNNs have 
difficulty extracting discriminative features in 
conditions of uneven lighting, extreme viewing 
angles, and partial occlusion by leaves or fronds-
conditions that are very common in plantations. 
Existing models tend to overfit on training datasets 
and fail to generalize to field conditions [6]. Second, 
no research has systematically optimized CNN

Most studies use default hyperparameters or 
inefficient and suboptimal manual trial-and-error 
tuning [7]. Third, the balance among accuracy, 
inference speed, and memory consumption has not 
been optimally addressed jointly [8]. 
Accurate models are slow and resource-hungry, 
but if efficiency comes at the expense of accuracy, it 
is unacceptable in field applications. Attention 
mechanisms have been found to enable CNNs to 
concentrate on informative regions, which achieves a 
3-7% increase in fruit classification accuracy [3]. 
However, the static attention mechanism, which uses 
a single fixed weight for each input, is not very 
flexible in responding to changes in inputs, including 
those from oil palm fruits. It is possible that dynamic 
attention, which can fine-tune the focus strategy for 
each sample, could address these challenges; 
however, it has not been well studied in this setting. 
Optimal 
hyperparameter 
selection 
through 
metaheuristic algorithms, such as Genetic Algorithm 
(GA), Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), and 
Simulated Annealing (SA), has been proven to 
enhance the performance of CNNs in numerous 
applications [9]. 
GA excels in global exploration but converges 
slowly; PSO is fast but easily gets stuck in local 
optima; SA is effective at escaping local optima but 
is sensitive to control parameters. Hybrid algorithms 
that adaptively combine the strengths of GA, PSO, 
and SA can produce more optimal solutions than 
single methods; however, they have not been applied 
to CNN optimization for oil palm fruit ripeness [10]. 
This research develops an integrated framework 
that addresses this gap through three main 
contributions: (1) A CNN architecture with a self-
adaptive 
dynamic 
attention 
mechanism 
that 
automatically adjusts the attention strategy based on 
the complexity characteristics of each input image-
providing more intensive attention to difficult 
samples and efficient attention to easy samples [11]; 
(2) A hybrid GA-PSO-SA algorithm for simultaneous 
optimization of multiple CNN hyperparameters 
(learning rate, batch size, dropout rate, number of 
layers, filter size) with an adaptive switching 
mechanism that leverages the strengths of each 
algorithm in different optimization phases [12]; (3) A 
true multi-objective optimization framework that 
explicitly optimizes three conflicting objectives: 
maximizing classification accuracy, minimizing 
inference latency, and minimizing model size 
through a Pareto-optimal solution set, resulting in an 
ensemble of solutions with different trade-offs that 
can be selected according to deployment needs (high 
accuracy for quality control vs.high efficiency for 
edge devices) [9]. 
The integration of hybrid GA-PSO-SA with self-
adaptive dynamic attention in this multi-objective 
optimization formulation is expected to produce 
solutions that are not only accurate but also practical 
for real-time implementation in the field, supporting 
increased operational efficiency of oil palm 
plantations and optimization of national palm oil 
production quality [13].

2. Related work 
The 
last 
decade 
has 
witnessed 
rapid 
developments in automatic classification using deep 
learning; however, numerous challenges remain in 
simultaneously optimizing multiple objectives and 
incorporating adaptive attention mechanisms. This 
chapter reviews the literature, highlights the 
limitations of traditional approaches, and lays the 
groundwork for this research. 
Some studies have investigated the application 
of 
single 
metaheuristic 
algorithms 
to 
CNN 
optimization. The GA-based method has achieved 
better results in multiple domains, such as building 
heating load estimation and pattern classification [14]. 
However, GA has some inherent drawbacks in terms 
of speed of convergence and its propensity to get 
trapped by local optima in complex, multi-modal 
optimization landscapes. Moreover, GA needs large 
populations with sufficient iterations to obtain good 
quality solutions, which leads to an excessive amount 
of computational time [14] . 
As an optimization algorithm, PSO has been 
successfully 
applied 
to 
neural 
network 
hyperparameter optimization and has demonstrated

their proposed model had better positioning accuracy

optimization formulation [19]. A notable difference

While attention mechanisms have been shown to 
be effective when embedded on top of a CNN for 
image and text classification, [20] found that a CNN 
with

performance than a traditional CNN in SER. 
Nevertheless, the paper employed static attention

Convolutional Neural Network), which features 
dynamically adjustable attention and residual 
connections, thereby accelerating the convergence 
process. Nonetheless, dynamic attention was not 
integrated with the hybrid metaheuristic optimizer, 
and the resulting problem was not transformed into a 
multi-objective optimization one [21]. The main 
difference from this work is that AC-CNN does not 
utilize the GA-PSO-SA hybrid optimization method 
for concurrent hyperparameter tuning. 
Integrating multi-objective optimization with 
classification remains one of the under-researched 
topics. [22] investigated multi-task learning for lung 
nodule categorization by employing the weighted 
fusion of multiple models. However, this approach 
did not explicitly optimize multiple objectives within 
a single model, nor did it utilize metaheuristic 
optimization [22]. 
Presented 
a three-objective-partner-banking 
sector selection system with GA and a multilayer 
perceptron. However, the research didn’t hybridize 
multiple metaheuristic algorithms and did not apply 
them to image classification [23]. 
There is a lack of existing research on fruit 
ripeness classification using deep learning. [24] 
presented a stacking fusion model that focused on 
strawberry 
quality 
grading 
by 
incorporating 
YOLOv5 and machine learning classifiers; however, 
it did not include an attention mechanism or hybrid 
metaheuristic optimization. Research on PSO for 
optimizing neural networks in fruit ripeness 
classification has also been reported , but it does not 
utilize a hybrid GA-PSO-SA approach and lacks a 
dynamic attention mechanism [25]. 
Contribution of this work This research 
contributes to the best of our knowledge in the 
literature by combining: 1) Genetic Algorithm – 
Particle Swarm Optimization-Simulated Annealing 
hybrid to take advantage of using parallel and 
distributed computing with increased convergence 
speed; better solution quality; (2) a dynamic attention 
mechanism that adapts its selective focusing based on 
input, outperforming static attentive approaches 2; 
and (3) an explicit multi-objective optimization 
problem: 
accuracy 
maximization, 
latency 
minimization, and size minimization.The combined 
use of these feature sets for the problem of classifying 
oil palm fruit ripeness is a novel contribution that has 
not been studied to date. 
3. Methodology 
3.1 Dataset 
In this study, the substrate used was oil palm fruit 
at four different ripening stages, Unripe, Underripe, 
Ripe, and Overripe. The dataset consists of 
approximately 10.000 images of oil palm fruit. These 
images were collected carefully from diverse and 
robust resources. A subset was obtained from public 
domain elements available in the Roboflow 
repository 
(https://universe.roboflow.com/zah/). 
However, a large part of the dataset was expanded by

To enhance the diversity and practicality of the

scenes. These included natural and artificial lighting 
conditions, as well as both plain and complex

Before developing the models, we preprocessed 
the merged dataset. All original images were resized 
and standardized to have the same size. This enabled 
compatibility with deep learning architecture. These 
steps were necessary to enable batch processing and 
model training. The entire population was then 
stratified to maintain the same proportion of all 
maturity classes in the subsets. After shuffling, we 
kept approximately 8.000 images (80%) for training. 
Approximately 1.000 images (10%) were used for 
testing, with an additional 1.000 for validation. This 
ensured a strict and fair test of our model's ability.

In Table 5. Site-level separation across splits 
confirms complete spatial independence with no 
overlap. Sites 1-2 exclusively have 8,000 training 
samples (4,001 + 3,999), Site 3 holds all 1,000 
validation samples, while Sites 4-5 have 1,000 test 
samples (500 + 500). This stringent partition 
eliminates leakage, preventing the model from 
memorising specific site features and allowing it to 
learn generalizable fragments of ripeness patterns. 
The source-aware strategy guarantees that validation 
and test performance reflect the model's ability to 
classify fruits from completely new and diverse 
geographies with differing environmental conditions, 
camera perspectives, and lighting, while accounting 
for 
real-world 
deployment 
challenges 
across 
heterogeneous Indonesian plantation environments. 
In Table 6. All three splits maintain a balanced 
class distribution, with each class representing 
approximately 25% within their respective splits. 
Train comprises 88.9% of the dataset (8,000 images), 
while validation and test each represent 11.1% (1,000 
images each), which aligns with an 80-10-10 
distribution strategy. Within the training set, class 
distribution ranges from 24.4% (Unripe) to 25.6% 
(Underripe), demonstrating near-perfect balance. 
Validation and test sets achieve perfect 25.0% 
distribution across all four classes (250 images per 
class). This balanced data splitting helps avoid class 
bias in the training data, treating all ripeness 
categories equally during evaluation to prevent any 
one class from dominating predictions. The uniform 
distribution across splits indicates that stratification 
was properly implemented.

3.2 Proposed model 
Fig. 2 shows, the proposed work combines a 
ResNet-based convolutional base (ResNet18/34/50) 
with a self-adaptive dynamic attention mechanism, 
based on a 9-dimensional hyperparameter search 
space that includes architectural and training settings. 
Data

regularization methods, including weight decay (1e-
5 to 5e-3) and a dropout probability of 0.2 to 0.3. 
When enabled, the attention module implements

16, and 32), query-key-value spatial attention (kernel

comprises 
three

5. Conclusion 
In developing a framework that integrates a 
hybrid GA-PSO SA metaheuristic optimization with 
self-adaptive dynamic attention mechanisms within a 
multi-tier optimization, this work has begun to bridge 
the gap in the automatic classification of oil-palm 
fruit ripeness stages by proposing methods that have 
not been previously documented. A relative 
CNN+GA+PSO balance was achieved at 95.73 
percent for accuracy, 95.84 percent for F1-score, and 
0.16 percent for the precision-recall gap, validated 
across 10 distinct runs with a mean test accuracy of 
96.12 percent ± 0.39 percent. Furthermore, the 
ablation study demonstrated that complementing dual 
search methods hyperparameter optimization was far 
superior in impact than simply engineering the 
architecture of the network, where GA-PSO 
produced 18.23 percent more improvement than the 
single algorithms within the pseudosingle approach 
and was more stable in convergence relative to the 
other complex topologies suffering the curse of 
dimensionality. 
The 
multi-objective 
Pareto 
optimization 
framework has successfully balanced trade-offs 
among accuracy, inference latency, and model size, 
yielding deployment-ready solutions despite highly 
variable operational constraints. Pareto_2, the best-
balanced model, achieves an accuracy of 88.61%, a 
latency of 4.93ms, and a size of 43.6 MB, resulting in 
4.1× faster inference than ViT-tiny and surpassing the 
accuracy of MobileNetV2 (95.53%) and ResNet50 
(95.27%). 
The 
Leave-Site-Out 
5-fold 
cross-
validation attains an uncommonly high accuracy of 
94.66% ± 0.54% with unseen geographical sites, in 
combination with test-validation gaps (0.76-0.94%) 
substantially lower than typical literature gaps (3-5%), 
confirming remarkable spatial generalization, and is 
robust to a wide range of environmental changes and 
intra-class variance, camera hardware differences,
