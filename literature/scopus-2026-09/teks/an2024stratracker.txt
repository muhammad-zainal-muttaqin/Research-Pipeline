A B S T R A C T
Accurately counting fruit in orchards is a critical step for effective digital farming management. However, the
variability in fruit size, overlapping shadows, and light interference present significant challenges to applying
computer vision during the strawberry growth phase. To address these challenges, we propose StraTracker, a
multi-object tracking (MOT) algorithm specifically designed to identify and count strawberries at various growth
stages. StraTracker transforms the counting task into a frame-by-frame tracking problem, integrating both mo-
tion and appearance features. The algorithm is composed of three key components: a strawberry detector based
on YOLOv8n, a feature association module, and a dual-area counting (DC) module. First, the strawberry detector
accurately recognizes five growth stages, achieving an average accuracy of 91.93 % at 38.3 FPS. Next, the feature
association module, incorporating the Feature Slicing Attention (FSA) and Adaptive Kalman Filtering (AKF)
modules, mitigates issues such as light interference, impractical tracking frames, and ID switching (IDs). As a
result, StraTracker achieves a Multi-Object Tracking Accuracy (MOTA) of 83.28 % and a Higher-Order Tracking
Accuracy (HOTA) of 77.26 %, with only 259 IDs, outperforming existing baseline models. Finally, the DC module
categorizes fruit counts based on the unique IDs assigned during tracking. The algorithm’s coefficient of
determination (R2 = 0.91) and GEH of 2.33 indicate a strong correlation between predicted and actual counts. In
conclusion, StraTracker offers a promising solution for farmers to optimize planting strategies and develop more
precise harvesting plans.
1. Introduction
Strawberries are a crop of great economic and nutritional impor-
tance, with global cultivation expanding from 310,000 ha in 1999 to
more than 900,000 ha by 2023.(H¨akkinen et al., 2000; Gudowska et al.,
2024). However, the absence of timely monitoring frequently results in
challenges such as incomplete flower thinning, untimely picking, and
improper
fertilization,
significantly
diminishing
fruit
production
(Samtani et al., 2019). Therefore, real-time solutions for monitoring
fruit growth stages and counting information are urgently needed. Some
existing monitoring solutions rely on manual observation or specialized
instruments(H¨ani et al., 2020; Culman et al., 2023), but these are
expensive and require damage to the fruit(Scott et al., 2021). In contrast,
deep learning and computer vision-based methods provide a new solu-
tion for digital farming management with the benefits of low cost, high
efficiency, and real-time performance(Attri et al., 2023).
Deep learning addresses the challenge of target recognition and
detection by modeling human visual perception(Sanaeifar et al., 2023).
The orchard has unique challenges compared to general detection sce-
narios, including complex backgrounds, diverse fruit growth patterns,
and light interference(Lu et al., 2023). These factors increase the
complexity of fruit counting, which leads to most of the studies
improving the accuracy of recognition and counting by improving the
models of target detection(Vasconez et al., 2020). For example, Song
et al. (2014) employed a color classifier to count chili fruits in images,
achieving a correlation of 74 % with manual counting. Mirhaji et al.
(2021) introduced the utilization of the YOLO model for detecting and
counting ripe oranges in an orchard, achieving a detection accuracy
mAP of 90.8 %. Parvathi et al. (2021) utilized Fast-RCNN and ResNet-50
algorithmic models to assess the ripeness of coconuts, classifying them
into two categories: ripe or unripe. Bhattarai et al. (2022) employed
weakly supervised methods to detect apple flowers and fruits, achieving
root mean square errors of 2.9 and 4.3, respectively. However, tradi-
tional target detection encounters significant limitations in monitoring
strawberry growth: it can only effectively analyze static single-frame
images, rendering it inadequate for the comprehensive task of

counting and overseeing the entire orchard. To overcome this challenge,
we employ Multi-Object Tracking (MOT) to facilitate holistic orchard
monitoring through cross-frame correlation.
MOT algorithms leverage information from preceding and succeed-
ing frames to predict the target’s state, thus facilitating cross-frame
target association(Y. Zhang et al., 2024). The most canonical and
widely-adopted MOT algorithms include the SORT(Bewley et al., 2016),
DeepSORT(Pujara et al., 2022), and BoT-SORT(Aharon et al., 2022)
models. Both SORT and DeepSORT algorithms utilize KF to forecast
target trajectories and employ an IOU model for data similarity
matching. DeepSORT further incorporates a ReID module to re-extract
target appearance features. BoT-SORT, recognized as a state-of-the-art
(SOTA) MOT algorithm, augments the feature association module by
integrating motion and appearance information, implementing a camera
motion compensation module, and optimizing the model structure with
a low-score quadratic correlation function.
In the field of agriculture, researchers track and count fruits using
UAVs(Zheng et al., 2023), Robots(Mirbod et al., 2023), or Monitoring
platforms(W. L. Zhang et al., 2022). For example, He et al. (2022)
monitored and counted camellia flowers using YOLOv3, Kalman
filtering (KF), and multiple feature matching through video captured by
a cell phone, achieving a MOTA of 66.1 %. Gao et al. (2022) used KF and
Euclidean distance to count ripe apples in robotic applications,
achieving 69.14 % MOTA with their algorithm. Lin et al. (2022) inte-
grated the improved YOLOv5s and Deepsort algorithms to count peanuts
on UAVs. This approach assigns ID to all targets using the MOT algo-
rithm, facilitating counting by tracking these ID. However, algorithmic
errors can cause ID switching(IDs), which exaggerates the number of
fruits by resulting in mismatched counts. Wu et al. (2023) developed an
automated fruit counting system for apples on mobile platforms by
combining abnormal fruit detection, trunk tracking, and ID assignment,
achieving a MOTA of 89.9 %. Bolouri et al. (2024) combined Mask-
RCNN with SORT for the split counting of cotton in four states at the
edge device. Although existing studies have effectively integrated target
detection and tracking algorithms, they are limited to fruit targets
ripening during the same time period, such as camellia, peanuts and
apples. These studies lack the capability to comprehensively monitor the
entire growth cycle of strawberries. Specifically, strawberries continue
to produce new flowers and fruits throughout their growth cycle,
resulting in the simultaneous appearance of fruits at different growth
stages (Yang et al., 2010; J. Zhang et al., 2023). Hence, the MOT method
applied to strawberry fruits during the growing stages requires further
improvement and modification. Additionally, the SOTA BoT-SORT al-
gorithm still faces challenges related to tracking frame irrelevance, light
interference, and IDs.
To address the above challenges, we propose a dynamic counting
model for strawberries named StraTracker based on the BoT-SORT al-
gorithm. The structural model structure is illustrated in Fig. 1. To the
best of our knowledge, this is the inaugural study of the MOT algorithm
for monitoring and counting fruits during the strawberry growth cycle.
The StraTracker model monitors and visualizes the distribution of fruit
quantities in the orchard, which facilitates timely management and
harvesting by growers. The main contributions of this paper can be
summarized as follows:
Collected and curated a StraMOT dataset tailored for the MOT al-
gorithm, undergoing data calibration and preprocessing.
Designed the Feature Slice Attention (FSA) module to refine small
target features and enhance the real-time performance of the
tracking model.
Introduced the Adaptive Kalman Filter (AKF) module to optimise the
ability of model adaptive tuning under real-time scene changes.
Implemented a Dual-area Counting method(DC) at the output to
quantify the number of fruits based on their unique identifiers (ID).
This work is structured as follows: Section 2 describes the method-
ology for data collection and preprocessing. Section 3 outlines the
methodology, highlighting improvements to the detector, feature asso-
ciation, and counting modules. Section 4 details the experiments con-
ducted, providing an in-depth analysis of the detector, feature
association, and counting experiments. Section 5 discusses the limita-
tions of the StraTracker model and proposes directions for future
research. Finally, Section 6 concludes the study.

2.2. Data calibration
The accuracy of data calibration directly influences model training.
Based on the practical experience of senior farmers and relevant in-
dustry standards, we classify the growth of strawberry fruits into five
stages: flowering (FLOWING), greening (GROWING_G), white ripening
(GROWING_W), coloring (NEARLY_M), and ripening (MATURE), Spe-
cific calibrations and corresponding labels are detailed in Table 1. Ac-
cording to industry standards for strawberries in both China (NY/T
444–2001) and American (USA Strawberry Grade Standard), straw-
berries are considered mature and suitable for picking, sale, and con-
sumption when their overall color exceeds 70 %. The flowering period is
primarily determined through the detection of strawberry flowers.
These flowers typically form cymes featuring white petals that are nearly
round or obovate-elliptic. During the white ripening and green fruit
stage, strawberries undergo an expansion period where the overall
growth transitions from flowers to green fruits. Subsequently, the fruits
gradually shift in color, evolving from green to white, and the texture
transforms from hardness to a gradual softening.

coordinates, and labels present in each image in preparation for
subsequent training of the model. The captured videos were annotated

the accurate bounding box(B-box), aiming to minimize the inclusion of
background pixels in the actual box. The captured data was stored at a
resolution of 1920x1080, with a video frame rate of 60 fps(frames per
second) in MP4 format. The specific steps of data labeling are outlined
below:

2.3. Dataset augmentation
We investigated three hybrid data enhancement methods to augment
the original dataset, including RandAugment, motion blur, and AI-for-
background, to increase the original dataset to 20,792 valid data, as
shown in Fig. 4. The data enhancement is performed as follows:
• RandAugment: randomized augmentation of data by mixing pretzels,
amplitude incremental cropping and rotation.
• Motion blur: Combine data randomly mixed linear motion blur,
Gaussian blur, mean blur, and median blur in the horizontal direc-
tion for enhancement.
• AI changes background: batch the data through AI to remove the
background and then randomly generate different backgrounds.
3. Methodology
In this section, we detail the components of the StraTracker model, as
shown in algorithm 1. The framework consists of three main parts: the
strawberry detector, the feature association module, and the Dual-area
Counting (DC) module. In the detection module, the YOLOv8n algo-
rithm was developed to detect strawberries at different growth stages.
The feature association module incorporates the Feature Slice Attention
(FSA) module, the Adaptive Kalman Filter (AKF) module, and deep fruit
feature fusion. The counting module employs the DC method based on
the tracked target frames and ID. The workflow of the model is as fol-
lows: firstly, the consecutive frames are inputted into YOLOv8 to detect
the strawberries and obtain the confidence, position, and category in-
formation of the fruits. Secondly, the results are inputted into the feature
correlation module to be processed, such as FSA, AKF module, Hun-
garian Algorithm, etc. Thirdly, based on the tracking results, the DC
Module is utilized to realize the counting of the fruits in different
growing states by judging the moving direction, ID, and category of the
fruits.

3.2. Feature association
We refer to the BoT-SORT algorithm (Aharon et al., 2022) to design
the association module of StraTracker. Unlike existing MOT algorithms,
StraTracker focuses more on fine-grained underlying features due to the
small size of strawberry fruits and the presence of light shading.
Therefore, we designed the FSA and AKF modules to be incorporated
into the association module while retaining the twice feature fusion and
deep feature extraction modules.

3.2.1. Feature slicing attention module (FSA)
During the development of the original BoT-SORT algorithm, vari-
ations in fruit size led to challenges, resulting in leakage and false
detection problems. Additionally, the existing Reid feature extraction
module in the MOT algorithm employed a global search for feature
extraction, resulting in prolonged processing times and falling short of
real-time tracking requirements. To address these issues, we introduced
the FSA module, inspired by the Vision Transformer(Vaswani et al.,
2017). The FSA module integrates the target slicing architecture in front
of the feature extraction network, enabling the model to rapidly and
meticulously extract strawberry appearance features.

3.2.2. Adaptive Kalman filtering module(AKF)
To predict and estimate the motion trajectory of the target on the 2D
plane, MOT algorithms universally employ the KF(Mandic et al., 2015)
with constant velocity as the prediction module. While the KF can pre-
dict the next trajectory of the target accurately during the motion state,
it has limitations. The original KF input state matrix is modeled with a
constant velocity, causing a mismatch in the tracking frame during
platform motion, as depicted in Fig. 8.
The initial state matrix is determined by estimating the aspect ratio
of the strawberry target to select the tracking box. During the develop-
ment of the correlation module, we discovered that the accuracy of the
bounding box directly affects tracking and counting precision. Even a
small deviation of only 1 cm can lead to two major issues: First, closely
positioned target bounding boxes may overlap and interfere with each
other, resulting in tracking failures and significant IDs problems. Sec-
ond, there is an increased likelihood of misidentification, causing other
objects to be recognized as strawberries, which affects counting
accuracy. Therefore, we redefined the state vector of the KF in Eq. (2)
and Eq. (3) to directly estimate the width and height of the tracking
frame.

3.3. Count module
To accurately count strawberries during the growing period, we in-
tegrated a cross-frame counting module into StraTracker and explored
four counting methods through comparison. The specific counting
methods are as follows:
1. ID-based counting(IC): An ID will be assigned to each target during
the monitoring process, and strawberry fruits with different growth
stages will be counted based on ID, respectively(X. Li et al., 2022), as
illustrated in Fig. 9(a). This method heavily depends on the perfor-
mance of the tracking and detection algorithm, and the detection
results often exceed the true value.
2. Line-based counting(LC): A counting line is incorporated into the
algorithm to indicate the center coordinates of the tracking box and
estimate when the trajectory of the target center intersects with the
counting line(S. Li et al., 2020), as shown in Fig. 9(b). This method is
prone to a significant number of missed detections due to the limited
range of counting lines.
3. Area range-based counting(AC): Integrate a region range into the
algorithm to detect the target’s tracking box as a signal and count the
strawberry tracking box when it is detected within the region range,
as shown in Fig. 9(c). This method has the potential to reduce the
tracking range and enhance the counting accuracy of the model.
However, it is limited in its ability to categorize strawberry targets,
only providing a total count.
4. Based on dual-area counting(DC): Introduce two judgment regions
into the algorithm to monitor the tracking box of the fruit, as shown
in Fig. 9(d). When the tracking box passes through the first region,
confidence judgment is performed, and its corresponding ID is
stored. Upon reaching the second region, the growth stages is
assessed, and the ID is reevaluated for classification in the count.
Refer to Algorithm 2 for the pseudo-code of our DC method (line
1–10 in Algorithm 2).
We conducted several experiments on these four fruit counting
methods, as described in Section 4.4. The results indicate that the DC
method is the most efficient and capable of monitoring the overall
growth of fruit in the orchard.

4. Experiments
Several sets of experiments were conducted to validate the efficacy
and utility of StraTracker. (1) detector experiments: Experiments were
carried out on different lightweight deep learning detectors, involving a
trade-off analysis between real-time processing and detection accuracy.
The goal was to identify the most suitable detector for integration into
StraTracker. (2) feature association experiments: We compared the
proposed StraTracker with a variety of popular MOT algorithms and
conducted ablation experiments and counting tests. (3) field dynamic
experiments: dynamic evaluation of strawberries was conducted in the
orchard based on the experimental monitoring platform, and real-time
strawberry growth distribution and occupancy maps were generated.
4.1. Experimental environment
In this study, the StraTracker model underwent training on a work-
station equipped with an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU, coupled with an Intel
(R) Xeon(R) Gold 6234 @3.30 GHz processor and 192 GB of RAM. The
programming language employed was Python 3.8, and the deep learning
framework utilized was PyTorch 1.7.1 GPU version. Subsequent tests
were conducted using Intel’s NUC minicomputer, featuring an NVIDIA
GeForce RTX 2060 GPU, 32 GB of RAM, and an Intel(R) Core(TM) i7-
1165G7 @2.80 GHz processor.
4.2. Detector experiments
Detectors are essential for accurately identifying the growth stages
and ripeness of strawberry fruits. To determine the most effective model,
we evaluated several lightweight detection algorithms, including Fast-
RCNN, RetinaNet, NanoDet, YOLOv5s, and YOLOv8n, using the Stra-
MOT dataset. The experimental results, detailed in Table 3 and Fig. 10,
revealed distinct performance differences among these models. Fast-
RCNN, due to its high parameter count and two-phase structure,
exhibited poor accuracy and real-time performance, achieving a mAP of
only 73.26 %. In contrast, YOLOv5s demonstrated higher real-time FPS
but slightly lower detection accuracy, particularly in the colouring
phase, where it achieved an accuracy of only 88.86 %. NanoDet, while
being a lightweight model, also showed lower accuracy and unsatis-
factory real-time performance for detecting strawberry growth stages.
Among the tested models, YOLOv8n emerged as the best-performing
lightweight detector, with a mAP of 93.74 % for ripening fruits, an F1
score of 90.46 %, and a minimal parameter count of just 3.2 M. This
made YOLOv8n the most suitable model for monitoring the growth and
ripeness of strawberries.
Notably, key parameters, including the real-time loss function, mAP,
F1 score, and FPS, were visualized throughout the training process of the
five detection algorithms, as depicted in Fig. 10. This visualization
provides an intuitive depiction of each detector’s strengths and weak-
nesses. In particular, the similarity between the training and validation
loss curves suggests that the model is not overfitting. Overfitting, which
occurs when a model performs well on training data but poorly on un-
seen data, was effectively mitigated by optimizing the dataset and
applying a regularization strategy to control model complexity. More-
over, YOLOv8n demonstrates a tendency toward stabilization in its loss
function after 40 epochs, indicating faster convergence, while also
achieving an impressive mAP of 91.93 %, signifying superior detection
accuracy. Additionally, on the StraMOT dataset, YOLOv8n attains a real-
time monitoring frame rate of 38.3 fps, demonstrating effective real-
time detection capabilities. Thus, we have chosen YOLOv8n as the de-
tector for the StraTracker model.

4.4.2. Experiments with different conditions
During the data collection process, we discovered that varying
lighting conditions and webcam angles significantly impact the accuracy
of the model’s counting. Consequently, we evaluated the model’s per-
formance under actual orchard conditions and tested its counting
function using the StraMOT dataset. The results are detailed in Table 7
and Fig. 14.
First,
we
primarily
considered
sunlighting,
backlighting,
and

(a-c). Under backlighting conditions, exposure and background dark-
ening occur, impacting the feature association module and counting
function. This lead to association failures or missed counts, resulting in
an accuracy and GEH of only 86.04 % and 2.87, respectively. In contrast,
under both smooth and shaded lighting conditions, the counting results
were more accurate, with accuracy rates exceeding 93 %.
Moreover, We evaluated the monitoring effect on fruits at angles of
0◦, 30◦, and 45◦, as depicted in Fig. 14(c-f). The highest counting ac-
curacy was achieved when the camera was positioned directly at 0◦,
with an accuracy of 93.97 % and a GEH of 0.96. As the angle increased,
significant occlusion of fruits and leaves occurred, providing a wider
field of view but also introducing redundant information. This increased
monitoring complexity, leading to misses and misdetections. Specif-
ically, at 45◦, the model’s counts exceeded the true value due to the
detection of fruits in adjacent rows, resulting in redundant counts.
In summary, enhancing the model’s counting accuracy under
different lighting conditions, particularly backlighting, is crucial and
provides a direction for future research. Preliminary experiments veri-
fied that the monitoring model was most effective at a 0◦angle under
sunlight conditions. Therefore, for optimal results, the camera angle
should be controlled between 0◦and 30◦.
4.5. Field dynamics experiment
Real-time visualization of the distribution of fruits in the orchard can
assist growers in performing timely tasks such as flower thinning,
pollination, fertilization, or picking. Therefore, we conducted testing of
the StraTracker model in the orchard using the monitoring platform
from December 2022 to January 2023. We selected four high ridges in
the orchard for validation, with the total length of the ridges being
approximately 100 m, as illustrated in Fig. 15 for the path planning
diagram.
The StraTracker model enables us to accurately obtain the distribu-
tion and occupancy of strawberries in the orchard. As depicted in
Fig. 16, we visualized the distribution of the five growth stages of
strawberries in each ridge through stacked plots and the overall orchard
distribution through percentage plots. In the distribution map, a, b, c,
and d represent the four high rows in the orchard corresponding to the
planning trajectory in Fig. 15. These are visualized in the form of per-
centages, with different colors representing different growth stages.
In Fig. 16(a) and (d), the white ripening stage in the overall orchard
reached a maximum of 33.84 %, while the ripening stage accounted for
only 6.79 %. The analysis indicates that the strawberries in the orchard
at this time are predominantly in the early stage of planting and
development. It is crucial to implement early treatments, such as flower
thinning and pollination, for them in a timely manner. By January 2, the
proportion of white ripening and coloring stage fruits reached 24.41 %
and 27.43 %, respectively. This suggests that the fruits in the orchard are
in the process of expansion in anticipation of maturity. To support their
development, additional measures such as spraying, fertilizing, water-
ing, and other protective measures are needed. As of January 10, the
ripening stage reached 30.64 %, indicating an overall ripening of fruits
in the orchard. Timely picking is now necessary. The StraTracker model
not only captures the growth of strawberries in the orchard but also
predicts the date of fruit ripening, enabling the scheduling of subsequent
management tasks in a timely manner.

6. Conclusions
Real-time, accurate, and efficient identification and counting of
strawberry growth are crucial for the orchard management industry. In
this paper, we propose a novel strawberry tracking algorithm (StraT-
racker), specifically designed for identifying and counting strawberry
growth stages. Firstly, we developed a large-scale strawberry tracking
dataset (StraMOT), for training and testing the proposed model. Sec-
ondly, we selected the lightweight YOLOv8n detector to reduce infer-
ence time and improve the speed of fruit growth stage detection.
Furthermore, we introduced the AFK and FSA modules in feature asso-
ciation to enhance small target tracking and optimize adaptability.
Finally, we designed the DC module, a fruit counting method that tracks
real-time monitored fruits based on detected growth stages and IDs
using a dual-area approach. Experimental results demonstrate that
StraTracker achieves MOTA, MOTP, and accuracy scores of 83.28 %,
81.35 %, and 90.43 %, respectively, with only 259 IDs. These outcomes
surpass existing baseline models while meeting real-time processing
requirements, with a speed of 38.3 frames per second. Moreover, real-
