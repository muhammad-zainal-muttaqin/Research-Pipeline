At present, the vegetable yield estimation in China is
performed by manual sampling and visual observation
of vegetable counts. This is not only time-consuming
and labor-intensive, but it also has low precision. In
this study, we capture video surveillance images of the
tomatoes during plant maturation, and use neural net-
works to identify pictures, extract growing features,
identify the number of vegetables hanging from the
plants, and establish an estimation model for tomato
yield. We then take a sample of the vegetables to be
measured. Strains are image-analyzed and processed
to predict yield per plant and yield per unit area to
obtain an accurate prediction of tomato yield.
Keywords:
tomato, yield estimation, object detection,
regression analysis
1. Introduction
Accurate, labor-saving, and real-time estimation of
fruit yield allows producers to implement scientiﬁc man-
agement and marketing. Distributors and processing com-
panies formulate accurate and scientiﬁc harvesting and
processing plans, and administrative and business authori-
ties understand the status of yield and the income of farm-
ers, as do scientiﬁc research and extension personnel. Sci-
entiﬁc research and data collection form an important ba-
sis for technical requirements. At present, tomato yield
estimations still use traditional sampling and visual count-
ing of the number of vegetables per plant, which places a
high requirement on the experience of the surveying per-
sonnel, great labor intensity, and involves lower precision,
and a lesser likelihood of horizontal year-on-year compar-
isons. In order to establish a scientiﬁc, efﬁcient, labor-
saving, and accurate vegetable yield estimation technol-
ogy, this study developed the “Tomato Vegetable Yield
Estimation” program based on deep learning object de-
tection and regression ﬁtting algorithms. The program is
characterized by high precision and simple operation, and
is quick and easy, cost-saving, and technically accurate.
It can provide tomato yields to citrus industry authorities,
companies (associations), vegetable farmers, etc., by ac-
curately predicting the output of tomato vegetables before
harvesting. Estimation provides an important reference
for tomato vegetable fertilization management.
Object detection is one of the most important research
methods that computers use to perform a variety of be-
haviors such as object recognition, detection, and track-
ing, and it has also become a hot topic in recent years. So
far, object detection based on deep learning has achieved
many excellent research results. For example, Fast R-
CNN (Region-based Convolutional Network method) [1]
and Faster R-CNN [2] are results from the research in re-
cent years. Not only does the detected intersection over
union (IOU) values reach an advanced level, but they also
show better performance than traditional object detection
technologies, which is a great improvement. In 2017, the
Google Mask R-CNN made a huge leap forward from the
original R-CNN series model. It not only replaced the
original region of interest (RoI) pooling layer with RoI
Align, but also added a side-by-side FCN layer and called
it Mask R-CNN [3].
Regression analysis is a predictive modeling technique
that studies the relationship between dependent variables
(targets) and independent variables (predictors).
This
technique is commonly used for predictive analysis, time
series models, and ﬁnding causal relationships between
variables.
Regression analysis is an important tool for modeling
and analyzing data. Here, we use a curve/line to ﬁt the
data points so that the distance from the curve or line to
the data points is the shortest.
In the proposed scheme, we ﬁrst collected 20,000 im-
ages of tomato plants. First, the R-CNN deep learning
model was used to detect the number of tomatoes in the
collected images.
Then, the regression algorithm was
used to ﬁt the relationship between the number of toma-
toes detected and the actual yield of each tomato. In the
actual yield environment, the vegetable’s output can be
estimated directly from the number of tomatoes detected
in the captured image.

In recent years, there have been a number of research
results based on machine learning.
China’s per capita
food yield was estimated to be of great signiﬁcance
for accurately understanding future food security status
and adopting effective measures to achieve food secu-
rity in [6]. On the basis of comparing various estima-
tion methods, BP neural networks and a logistic model
were used to predict China’s grain yield and population
from 2016 to 2030, and based on this, China’s per capita
food yield was estimated. The empirical data from 1995
to 2014 show that China would not be able to achieve food
security by its own food yield from 2016 to 2030. It would
be a basic approach for China to achieve food security by
increasing agricultural yield and increasing food imports
through advances in agricultural technology. In order to
improve the accuracy of food yield estimation, based on
the data characteristics of China’s grain yield, an esti-
mation model combining a gray-scale prediction model
and extreme learning machines is used in [7] to make a
short-term and accurate prediction of China’s grain out-
put. Firstly, output data that is close to the year to be pre-
dicted is selected from the original grain sequence to form
a sub-sequence, a gray-scale prediction model is then es-
tablished for the sequence, and the model prediction error
is calculated. Then, the original sequence is normalized,
the sequence learning machine prediction model is built,
and the prediction error of the model is calculated to re-
ﬂect the long-term variation of the sequence. Finally, ac-
cording to the prediction errors of the two models, the
weight coefﬁcients of the model are obtained, and then
the ﬁnal grain yield is obtained from the combination of
the prediction values of the two models. However, the
solutions proposed above all have the problems of com-
plicated operation and low accuracy.
In recent years, deep learning object detection tech-
niques and regression ﬁtting algorithms have seen con-
siderable development [8]. In order to accurately detect
salient objects in an image, a new object detection algo-
rithm based on visual saliency maps and pseudo-property
is proposed. The algorithm ﬁrst extracts a large number of
rectangular windows with a high plausibility measure in
the image, estimates the possible position of the object,
converts the window-like physical measure to a pixel-
level physical measure, and then compares the original
saliency map with the pixel-level maps merged to gener-
ate weighted saliency maps. The original saliency maps
and weighted saliency maps are binaries. The maximum
search window area and seed window area are obtained by
using convex hull detection. Finally, the edge probability
density is estimated. The saliency object detection model
in [9] based on the human visual attention mechanism is
an effective method for actively perceiving important in-
formation in the image, and is of great signiﬁcance for
exploring large-scale perceptual information organization
in early visual cognitive processes. However, due to the
low signal-to-noise ratio and low contrast characteristics
of nighttime images, the existing visually signiﬁcant ob-
ject detection model is susceptible to noise interference,
weak texture blur, and other factors in the night scene.
In view of this, this paper proposes a night object saliency
detection method based on regional covariance and global
search. A structure-aware deep saliency detection algo-
rithm was proposed in [10]. The algorithm is based on a
deep neural network with multi-stream structure in four
parts: a feature extraction network, an object skeleton
detection sub-network, a saliency object detection sub-
network, and a cross-task connection component. First, in
the training and testing phase of the saliency object sub-
network, the structure information of the object is learned
through the object skeleton detection sub-network, and
the cross-task connection component is used to make the
saliency object detection sub-network automatically en-
code the information of the skeletal sub-network learning
object so as to perceive the overall structure of the ob-
ject and overcome the problem of incomplete detection
of the object area. Second, in order to further improve
the accuracy of the proposed method, a fully connected
conditional random ﬁeld is used to optimize the detection
results. Experimental results on three common datasets
show that the proposed algorithm is superior to the exist-
ing deep learning algorithm in terms of detection accuracy
and operating efﬁciency.
3. Tomato Yield Estimation Model Based on
Object Detection
The model we use is mainly divided into two parts. The
ﬁrst part is used to detect the tomato objects and the num-
ber of images captured by the camera. The second part
is to establish a regression model based on the number of
tomatoes on a single sample plant, and the actual yield of
an individual tomato.

3.1. Object Detection Model for Tomato Plants
The object detection model we use is an R-CNN model
based on deep learning, namely the Region-based Convo-
lutional Neural Network [11]. Its involves three steps:
(1) With a selective search algorithm that can generate
approximately 2,000 region proposals, the R-CNN
can scan the input image to obtain the possible tar-
gets.
(2) Run a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) on each
regional proposal.
(3) Enter the output of each CNN:
a) A Support Vector Machine (SVM) to classify the
above areas.
b) A linear regression to shrink the bounding box
around the target, provided that such a goal exists.

Figure 1 shows the steps of the R-CNN. In this sce-
nario, we added an object counter behind the R-CNN
model to count the number of tomatoes in the current im-
age according to images of the sample plants from differ-
ent angles. The number of tomato objects in the picture
was calculated.
This part was mainly divided into two steps: data col-
lection and model training. Finally, we saved the trained
model for subsequent ofﬂine detection.
Data Image Acquisition
Image acquisition conditions: The image acquisition
should be as clear as possible, without cloud or wind, in
order to ensure the clarity of the picture and the authen-
ticity of the color; as far as possible, the shooting time
should be between 10:00–14:00, which is a relatively sta-
ble maintenance angle. To ensure that the shooting direc-
tion and angle of view are the same for each shooting ses-
sion, the distance, angle, resolution, shooting mode, and
exposure time of the camera, and the camera used should
 
Fig. 2. Tomato vegetable image acquisition example.
be the same. The camera should focus on the target veg-
etable trees. The resolution of the image must be more
than 8 million pixels, and the maximum resolution should
be set when shooting. The horizontal distance from the
lens to the vegetable tree is 3 m, and it is perpendicular to
the center of the plant.
Sample data collection: Taking into account the dif-
ferences in tree age, growth status, size, and annual sta-
tus of different plants for different vegetables, this model
establishes an open modeling system to collect a certain
amount of the speciﬁcity of each vegetable before pre-
dicting yield. The plant image information and vegetable
quantity information are modeled, and then the model is
used to predict the output using the sample plant image
information of the target vegetable. The number of mod-
eling samples is not as high as it could be, but it should
satisfy the required accuracy. For the sake of representa-
tiveness, it is advisable to reduce the number of modeling
samples as much as possible. If the conditions and status
of the vegetable are the same, the corresponding estab-
lished model can be used to predict the output, so that the
yield estimation is fast and scientiﬁc.
In order to increase the accuracy of yield prediction,
the selected modeling samples and yield prediction sam-
ple plants should be representative of the vegetable and
randomly chosen. When predicting the output of vegeta-
bles, according to the size of the vegetable to be measured,
the number of modeled samples is generally required to
be 10–30 strains, and the actual number of vegetables and
the vegetable weight should be counted; the number of
sample plants required for yield prediction is generally
not less than 30 strains. In order to model the training
demand, we should predict yield based on the number of
tomatoes detected, and the weight of a single vegetable
and the planting density are required in order to calculate
the yield per plant, and the total yield of the whole plant.
A total of 20,000 growth images of 5,000 trees of
tomato vegetables in a local area were selected, and image
information was collected for each of the vegetable trees
from four orientations, front, rear, left, and right, at the
same height, as shown in Fig. 2.
We divided all 200 million images into training sets,
