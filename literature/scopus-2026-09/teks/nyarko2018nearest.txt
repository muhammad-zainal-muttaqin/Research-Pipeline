Automatic fruit picking is a challenging problem in robotics with a wide application ﬁeld. A prerequisite 
for realization of a robotic fruit picker is its ability to detect fruits in tree tops. An expert system, which 
would be able to compete with human perception, must be capable of recognizing fruits among leaves 
and branches under uncontrolled conditions, where fruits are occluded and shaded. In this paper, a novel 
approach for fruit recognition in RGB-D images based on detection and classiﬁcation of convex surfaces is 
proposed. The input RGB-D image is ﬁrst segmented into convex surfaces by a region growing procedure. 
Each convex surface is then described by an appropriate descriptor and classiﬁed with the aid of the 
associated descriptor. A novel descriptor of approximately convex surfaces is proposed, which we named 
Convex Template Instance (CTI) descriptor. It is based on approximating surfaces by convex polyhedrons 
with quantized face orientations, where every polyhedron face corresponds to one descriptor component. 
Computation of the proposed descriptor is simple and can be performed very eﬃciently. The proposed 
CTI descriptor is compared to the SHOT descriptor, a standard descriptor for 3D point clouds. Two variants 
of the both CTI and SHOT descriptor are evaluated, a variant which uses color and a variant which does 
not. A k-nearest neighbor classiﬁer is used to classify detected surfaces into two classes: fruit and other . 
The main advantage of the proposed expert system in comparison to other fruit recognition solutions 
is its computational eﬃciency, which is of great importance for its target application – an automatic 
fruit picker. The proposed approach is evaluated using a challenging dataset containing RGB-D images of 
four fruit sorts acquired under uncontrolled conditions, which has been made publicly available to the 
scientiﬁc community, allowing benchmarking of novel fruit recognition methods.

1. Introduction 
From its very beginning, the main purpose of robotics was to 
free humans from hard and tedious labor. Since their creation 
to this day, robots have gradually entered almost all industrial 
branches, while their wide application in households, health care 
and agriculture is expected in the close future. However, in con- 
trast to industrial environments, which are structured and adapted 
for robots, in the sense that most of the tasks can be solved by 
preprogrammed actions, agricultural processes require adaptation 
of robot actions to unpredictable situations, which can be achieved 
only by using intelligent perception. Fruit picking is an example of 
such a process, where neither the shape of fruit nor their position 
in space can be predicted in advance. Hence, in order to solve this 
task, robots need the ability to detect fruit, measure their shape

and size and determine their accurate position. For that purpose, 
an intelligent robotic fruit picker must be equipped by an expert

is a fruit or something else, e.g. a leaf or a branch. In order for 
this expert system to be practically applicable, its ability to per- 
ceive the environment must be competitive to that of a human 
fruit picker, i.e. it must make correct decisions in uncontrolled con- 
ditions, where fruits are occluded and shaded. 
Today, expert and intelligent systems are used in industry, en- 
gineering, ﬁnance, marketing, information management, informa- 
tion retrieval, telecommunications, medicine, chemistry, robotics 
and many other application areas. In this paper, an expert system 
for recognizing fruits in tree tops aimed for automatic fruit pick- 
ing is considered. A novel fruit recognition approach based on de- 
tection of convex surfaces is investigated. The approach is based 
on the observation that most fruits have an approximately convex 
shape. This property can be used to distinguish fruit from leaves

system, trained using RGB-D images of different fruit sorts, can be 
used for recognition of various fruit sorts. This is clearly an advan- 
tage, since such a system, once trained, could be applied to auto- 
mated fruit picking of various fruit sorts, without the need to train

In the research presented in this paper, an RGB-D camera is 
used to obtain 3D geometry of a scene. The main advantage of this 
type of sensor over a standard RGB camera is that it allows accu- 
rate measurement of fruit size, shape and position. In Cupec, Filko, 
Vidovi ´c, Nyarko, and Hocenski (2014) , segmentation of depth im- 
ages obtained by an RGB-D camera into approximately convex sur- 
faces for the purpose of fruit recognition is investigated. However, 
it has only been shown that fruit mostly appear in depth images 
as separate approximately convex surfaces, while classiﬁcation of 
convex surfaces into fruit, leaves and branches has not been inves- 
tigated. In this paper, we use the same method to segment RGB-D 
images of fruit trees into approximately convex surfaces and then 
apply two descriptors: SHOT ( Tombari, Salti, & Di Stefano, 2010 ) 
and a novel Convex Template Instance (CTI) descriptor to classify the 
obtained convex surfaces into two classes: fruit and other . 
The proposed CTI descriptor is a natural extension to the 
applied segmentation approach. It is based on approximating the 
obtained segments by convex polyhedrons with standardized face 
orientations. The performance of the CTI descriptor is compared to 
SHOT – a standard method for describing 3D surfaces. In compar- 
ison to SHOT, the proposed descriptor is signiﬁcantly simpler than 
the compared SHOT descriptor with respect to both its size and 
the required computation time. The computational eﬃciency is the 
main advantage of the proposed CTI descriptor, which is demon- 
strated by the experimental analysis presented in this paper. 
Another advantage of the proposed approach is that it does not 
require a user deﬁned radius of the neighborhood used to compute 
the descriptor, like, for example, SHOT. Instead, the descriptor is 
computed from the convex surface obtained by an appropriate 
segmentation method. Hence, the shape of the object itself deter- 
mines the size of the neighborhood used to compute the descrip- 
tor. Furthermore, the proposed descriptor is robust to point density 
variation, which is important because objects of the same size can 
be acquired from different distances and therefore represented by 
different amounts of points. 
The contributions of this paper can be summarized as follows: 
• This is the ﬁrst investigation of using convexity as a cue for 
fruit recognition in RGB-D images. 
• We propose a novel descriptor of convex surfaces, which can be 
computed very eﬃciently. 
• The third contribution of this paper is a challenging dataset, 
which has been made available to the research community. 
The dataset includes images of four sorts of fruit acquired in 
a highly unstructured environment and in uncontrolled lighting 
conditions and includes a semi automatically annotated ground 
truth data. 
The paper is structured as follows. In Section 2 , a comprehen- 
sive survey of related research is given. The investigated method- 
ology is described in Section 3 . In Section 4 , an analysis of fruit 
recognition experiments with the considered approaches is pre- 
sented. Finally, the paper is concluded in Section 5 . 
2. Related research 
Fruit and vegetable detection, recognition and/or localization 
have been the subject of many scientiﬁc papers. Results of the 
mentioned methods are used for different applications, like au- 
tomatic harvesting, yield estimation, phenotyping, breeding, etc. 
There are many different approaches to solving the aforementioned 
problems and some of them are listed below. In this review, the 
papers are divided into two groups: (i) 2D image-based methods 
and (ii) 3D image-based methods. 
Apart from original research papers, there are several review 
papers of computer vision methods and algorithms for automatic 
fruit and vegetables recognition in the literature ( Jiménez et al., 
1999, 20 0 0; Kapach, Barnea, Mairon, Edan, & Ben-Shahar, 2012; 
Gongal et al., 2015 ). 
2.1. 2D image-based methods 
In one of the ﬁrst papers in this ﬁeld ( Harrell et al., 1989 ), 
the authors proposed a method for fruit size and location detec- 
tion based on image color information and obtained a detection 
accuracy of up to 75% under favorable conditions. The method 
proposed in Bulanon et al. (2002) represents another color-based 
approach which relies on red color difference and ﬁnding the 
maximum between-class variance in the histogram for automatic 
recognition of Fuji apples. Using an optimal threshold, the method 
achieved a success rate of over 88%, while for images with back 
lighting the error rate was over 18%. In Lu et al. ( 2014 ), a method 
for citrus fruit detection based on fusion of color map and lu- 
minance map is proposed. The recognition accuracy under differ- 
ent lightening conditions was up to 86.81% with a false detection 
rate of 2.25%. The forward feature selection algorithm (FFSA) for 
color feature selection is used in Li et al. ( 2014 ). The obtained fea- 
tures are processed with different classiﬁers to separate blueberry 
from the background and it is shown that the k -nearest neigh- 
bor ( k -NN) classiﬁer has the best recognition rate. The authors in 
Peng et al. (2014) proposed a method for mature citrus recogni- 
tion based on YCbCr color model, improved fuzzy C-means algo- 
rithm and optimized Circular Hough transform. An algorithm for 
citrus fruit and obstacles recognition based on multi-class support 
vector machine (SVM) is proposed in Qiang, Jianrong, Bin, Lie, and 
Yajing (2014) , where a recognition rate of 92.4% was obtained. 
Besides color features, fruit and vegetable recognition based on 
shape features is another commonly used approach. In Murillo- 
Bracamontes et al. (2012) , the authors exploited shape proper- 
ties and applied Circular Hough Transform for wine grape berries 
recognition. A method for wine grape berries size estimation, 
based on Circular Hough Transform as well as histogram of gradi- 
ents and gist features and conditional random ﬁeld, is presented 
in Roscher et al. (2014) . The experiments showed on average, a 
1 mm difference of estimated size compared to manual measure- 
ment. Recognition and counting of immature citrus based on shape 
analysis and texture classiﬁcation is proposed in Senguptaa and 
Lee (2014) . In the paper, the authors used Circular Hough Trans- 
form, SVM, Canny edge detection, graph-based connected compo- 
nent algorithm, Hough line detection and SIFT features and ob- 
tained a recognition and counting rate of 80.4% on images captured 
in natural outdoor conditions. A method based on modeled shape 
and Generalized Hough Transform for eggplants recognition is pre- 
sented in Yao et al. ( 2008 ). The results show that, in the case of 
partially occluded objects, errors in position and pose estimation 
lower than 1.3% and 5.5% can be achieved. 
A two-step approach based on detecting peppers from mul- 
tiple views and applying a statistical method for recognizing 
and counting peppers in a cluttered greenhouse is proposed in 
Song et al. (2014) . Experiments are performed on 28,0 0 0 images of 
10 0 0 different plants and a 74.2% accuracy of detection is achieved. 
The authors in Cubero et al. (2014) developed a new method for 
grapevine berries size and weight determination and extended it to 
the detection of other fruits. The ﬁrst step of the proposed method 
is peduncle detection and it is based on a novel signature of the 
contour. A vision system for automatic apple harvesting based on 
median ﬁlter processing, seeded region growing and SVM is pro-

posed in Ji et al. (2012 ). The authors reported that 89% of apples

A method for apple recognition based on k-means clustering and 
genetic radial basis function (RBF) neural network is proposed in 
Ji et al. (2015) . The overall results showed that the recognition ac-

1.75 s. A visual system for litchi image acquisition and a method

average recognition accuracy of the proposed method is 93.75% 
with an average execution time of 0.516 s. 
A method for apple fruit counting and diameter estimation 
using thermal camera is presented in Stajnko et al. (2004) . The 
method is based on Normal difference index (NDI), morphologi- 
cal operations, feature extraction, particle selection and classiﬁer 
evaluation. The correlation coeﬃcient with manual measuring was 
0.88 for apple counting and 0.70 for diameter estimation. In Bu- 
lanon et al. ( 2009 ), the authors proposed a fusion of vision and 
thermal images for recognition of oranges by applying Laplacian 
pyramid transform (LPT) and fuzzy logic on registered images. 
Despite the high recognition rates achieved by the aforemen- 
tioned approaches, the methods based on 2D image processing 
are sensitive to lightening conditions, color difference between the 
fruit and background and especially to the amount of occlusion 
( Rakun et al., 2001; Xiang et al., 2014; Barnea et al., 2016; Mack 
et al., 2017 ). Hence, such methods can give satisfactory results 
only in structured environments. Furthermore, there is the prob- 
lem with accurate 3D pose estimation in 2D images due to in- 
suﬃcient information which is solved by depth information in 3D 
images ( Kapach et al., 2012 ). However, methods based on 2D im- 
ages are suitable for application in quality control after the crop 
has been harvested ( Sa et al., 2017 ). 
2.2. 3D image-based methods 
In addition to the approaches mentioned so far, which are 
based on 2D images, there are many methods in literature which 
use stereovision and 3D sensors for fruit and vegetables recogni- 
tion. The approach proposed in this paper belongs to this category. 
In this section, we give a review of the existing approaches for 
fruit recognition which use 3D sensor information and a theoreti- 
cal comparison of these methods with our approach. 
Most approaches considered in this section are based on the 
same pipeline consisting of four main steps: (i) image and depth 
data acquisition, (ii) segmentation, (iii) model ﬁtting or descrip- 
tor computation and (iv) classiﬁcation. In the rest of this section, 
different approaches to each of these steps used by researchers 
in this ﬁeld are reviewed. Furthermore, the techniques used in 
our approach for implementation of these steps are compared to 
the closely related methods, thereby explaining the motivation and 
contribution of our research. 
2.2.1. Image and depth data acquisition 
Different techniques have been used to obtain 3D information 
about the scene. One is the application of a laser scanner which 
provides accurate measurement of the scene geometry ( Eizentals & 
Oka, 2016; Mack et al., 2017; Paulus et al., 2013; Wahabzada et al., 
2015 ). However, the price of these sensors is very high in com- 
parison to consumer class RGB-D cameras, which are considered 
in our research. Furthermore, 3D point cloud acquisition by laser 
scanner is usually achieved by a time-consuming scanning proce- 
dure, where the 2D laser is moved in a particular way to cover the 
relevant part of the scene. For example, the total image acquisition 
time reported in Eizentals and Oka (2016) is 75.4 s. Another way 
to obtain a 3D point cloud is using stereovision ( Xiang et al., 2014; 
Font et al., 2014 ). Stereovision systems are signiﬁcantly more af- 
fordable than laser scanners but more prone to noise and outliers 
and less precise at greater distances. The third sensor type, which 
can be used for obtaining a 3D point cloud, is an RGB-D camera 
( Barnea et al., 2016; Méndez Perez et al., 2017; Sa et al., 2017; 
Yongting & Jun, 2017 ). In comparison to a 3D laser scanner system, 
RGB-D cameras provide a complete 3D scan of a scene in a frame 
rate but, analogously to stereovision, they are prone to noise and 
their precision falls rapidly with distance. In comparison to stereo- 
vision, RGB-D cameras have signiﬁcantly less outliers and are able 
to reconstruct non-textured surfaces. On the other hand, they are 
not applicable in bright daylight conditions. 
2.2.2. Segmentation 
In the context of fruit recognition, the task of image or point 
cloud segmentation is to subdivide the input image or point cloud 
into subsets of points, which are then analyzed in further algo- 
rithm steps. Segmentation is successful if each fruit is represented 
by a single segment, where this segment represents a complete 
visible part of the fruit surface. Segmentation can be performed us- 
ing either color information or depth information or a combination 
of both. Several color-based segmentation approaches are used for 
fruit recognition in the literature. Some of them are DBSCAN algo- 
rithm ( Eizentals & Oka, 2016 ), LCM segmentation ( Font et al., 2014 ) 
or ﬁxed thresholding ( Xiang et al., 2014 ). However, color-based 
segmentation is successful only if the fruit color can be clearly dis- 
criminated from its background, which is satisﬁed only in labora- 
tory and controlled conditions. Furthermore, segmentation in color 
space is highly sensitive to lighting conditions and uneven illumi- 
nation. 
In order to cope with these problems, in Mack et al. (2017) and 
Méndez Perez et al. (2017) , depth information is used. The ap- 
proach presented in Méndez Perez et al. (2017) assumes that fruits 
are positioned on a ﬂat surface, which makes the segmentation 
problem much easier. However, this approach, designed for classi- 
fying and characterizing fruits on a conveyor belt, cannot be ap- 
plied in fruit picking, where fruits hang on trees among leaves 
and branches. In Mack et al. (2017) , a region growing procedure 
is applied, where an adjacent point is added to the grown re- 
gion if the difference between its normal and the normal of its 
neighbor contained in the region does not exceed a user deﬁned 
smoothness threshold. However, this simple smoothness criterion 
often results in undersegmentation, i.e. in merging a region rep- 
resenting a fruit with adjacent regions representing other objects. 
In this paper, we investigate the application of a convexity con- 
straint in a region growing segmentation process. This constraint, 
which requires that the grown region must be an approximately 
convex surface, is much stronger than the smoothness constraint 
used in Mack et al. (2017) , thereby reducing undersegmentation. 
At the same time, since many fruits have an approximately con- 
vex shape, this constraint is not too strong to result in overseg- 
mentation. In some cases, combination of both color and depth 
information can enhance the segmentation results. This combined 
approach is used in Yongting and Jun (2017) , where the authors 
applied segmentation based on region growing which uses color 
and depth difference as the criterion for adding adjacent points to 
the grown region. Although it is advantageous to use color as one 
of the cues for region growing, the current implementation of our

2.2.3. Model ﬁtting and descriptors 
The next step, following segmentation, is model ﬁtting or de- 
scriptor generation . There are several attempts to model fruits us- 
ing spheres, circles, cylinders and similar simple geometric shapes. 
In Eizentals and Oka (2016) , a model based on a cylinder is ﬁtted 
to 3D points in order to estimate the pose of green peppers for 
automatic harvesting. In Mack et al. (2017) , a RANSAC based 3D 
sphere model ﬁtting is applied to point sets obtained by segmen- 
tation in order to detect grapevine berries. Sphere ﬁtting is also 
used in Rakun, et al. (2011) for preliminary research of estimating 
the number, diameter and yield of apples. Edge curvature analy- 
sis and circle regression is used in Xiang, et al. (2014) for tomato 
detection. 
A more general approach, which does not rely on a presump- 
tion about the similarity of the target fruit sort to a particular 
geometric shape, is to describe point sets obtained by segmen- 
tation using descriptors and train a classiﬁer to distinguish a 
target fruit sort from leaves and branches according to the applied 
descriptor. In Barnea et al. (2016) , the acquired image is pruned by 
highlights detection followed by symmetry detection and descrip- 
tor generation according to normal distribution. A disadvantage 
of this original idea is that highlights are not always detectable 
due to shadows and it is not applicable to some sorts of fruit 
which do not have a glossy surface. In Yongting and Jun (2017) , a 
color-FPFH descriptor is proposed, which is generated for detected 
NARF ( Steder et al., 2010 ) keypoints. This descriptor is compared 
to the standard FPFH ( Rusu, Blodow, & Beetz, 2009 ) descrip- 
tor, which does not use color information, as well as to SHOT 
( Tombari et al., 2010 ), VFH ( Rusu et al., 2010 ) and ESF ( Wohlkinger 
& Vincze, 2011 ) descriptors. FPFH is a vector composed of 33 
elements. In Yongting and Jun (2017) , 6 more elements, which 
describe the segment color are added. SHOT, VFH and ESF are 
rather complex, descriptors, having 352, 308 and 640 components 
respectively, which are developed for general purpose object 
recognition and classiﬁcation. Motivated by the fact that fruits are 
simple, approximately convex objects, we developed a novel de- 
scriptor, which is simpler than FPFH and much simpler than SHOT, 
VFH and ESF. The variant of our descriptor which does not include 
color information has only 25 elements, while the variant which 
includes color information has two more elements. Lower dimen- 
sionality of our descriptor allows faster classiﬁcation. Furthermore, 
an additional advantage of our descriptor is its fast computation. 
These properties are very important in real time applications such 
as automated fruit picking. 
2.2.4. Classiﬁcation 
The ﬁnal step of fruit recognition is the classiﬁcation of 
segments into fruit and other objects, e.g. leaves or branches. 
The most commonly used method is SVM, which is applied 
in Mack et al., (2017) , Barnea et al. (2016) and Yongting and 
Jun (2017) . In the last reference, the authors optimized SVM us- 
ing a genetic algorithm in order to achieve a higher recognition 
rate. In the proposed approach, we use a k-NN classiﬁer, although 
SVM, random forest (RF) or artiﬁcial neural networks could also 
be applied. It is important to notice that the considered classiﬁers 
require computation of descriptors, while model ﬁtting methods 
classify segments according to a particular ﬁtting score. 
2.2.5. Conditions and performance 
The considered approaches were tested in different conditions, 
which can be categorized into three main categories: (i) labora- 
tory conditions, where fruits are positioned in front of an artiﬁcial 
background ( Mendez-Perez et al., 2017; Font et al., 2014 ); (ii) con- 
trolled conditions, where a selection of images with clearly visible 
and properly lighted fruits are used for evaluation, and (iii) un- 
controlled conditions, where randomly acquired snapshots of fruit 
trees in a natural environment are used for evaluation. Fruit recog- 
nition in uncontrolled conditions is a challenging task, where seg- 
mentation is a critical step. Since our target application is auto- 
mated fruit picking in uncontrolled conditions, we evaluated our 
approach using a set of RGB-D images acquired according to the 
deﬁnition of uncontrolled conditions given above. It is hard to as- 
sess whether an approach proposed by other authors is evaluated 
using a dataset acquired in controlled or uncontrolled conditions 
if this dataset is not made publicly available but only few sam- 
ples are presented in a paper. To the best of our knowledge, our 
dataset is the only publicly available dataset containing RGB-D im- 
ages of fruit trees acquired in uncontrolled conditions with ground 
truth classiﬁcation. Although the absence of a standard bench- 
mark dataset and publicly available implementations of competi- 
tive algorithms prevents us from making a thorough comparison 
of the state-of-the-art approaches, in order to provide an insight 
into performances achieved in the ﬁeld of fruit recognition, we 
give a review of some reported results. In Yongting and Jun (2017) , 
the authors reported a classiﬁcation accuracy of 92.30%, 88.03% 
and 80.34% for apple, branch and leaf recognition, respectively. 
In the greenhouse test, the method presented in Eizentals and 
Oka (2016) successfully recognized 87 of 107 green pepper in- 
stances for aﬃne transformation and 66 of 107 instances for rigid 
transformation. The method proposed in Barnea et al. (2016) is 
tested on the sweet pepper dataset and the results show that 
an average precision of 0.55 is obtained. The recognition accu- 
racy of clustered tomatoes in Xiang et al. (2014) is 87.9% when 
the leaf or branch occlusion rate is less than 25%. The execu- 
tion times of the methods presented in Eizentals and Oka (2016) , 
Yongting and Jun (2017) and Barnea et al. (2016) are 12 s, 90 s and 
197 s, respectively. The execution time of the method proposed in 
Xiang et al. (2014) is around 500 ms, but the method has sev- 
eral limitations, reported in that paper. The method is sensitive to 
occlusion, lighting variations and defects of color and shape. The 
method proposed in this paper has a signiﬁcantly smaller execu- 
tion time, which is one of the contributions of our research. 
To summarize, to the best of our knowledge, this is the ﬁrst 
research which investigates the application of shape convexity for 
fruit recognition in RGB-D images, where convexity is used in 
both the segmentation step and descriptor computation. Further- 
more, we concluded that a lack of standard benchmark datasets 
for evaluation of different methods is a serious shortage in this re- 
search ﬁeld, which hinders a fair comparison between proposed 
approaches and assessment of their contributions. In the papers 
considered in this review, only authors in Sa et al. (2017) provided 
a set of manually annotated 3D images of sweet peppers and their 
peduncles. In this paper, we address this issue by presenting a 
publicly available benchmark dataset with four fruit sorts: tomato, 
nectarine, pear or plum. 
3. Methodology 
The fruit recognition approach proposed in this paper takes as 
the input an RGB-D image of a fruit tree. The image is segmented 
into approximately convex surfaces using the method described 
in Cupec et al. (2014) . For each convex surface, a descriptor is 
computed. We consider two descriptors: SHOT and CTI. Computing

Aggregation of triangles in approximately convex surfaces 
is performed by a region growing procedure proposed in 
Cupec, Nyarko, and Filko (2011) , which starts with one triangle, 
representing the initial convex surface, and expanding this surface 
by appending adjacent triangles. A new triangle is added to the 
grown surface if the surface obtained by this addition is also an 
approximately convex surface. This process is ﬁrst performed for 
the largest triangle in the image. After no more triangles can be 
added to the grown surface, another region growing procedure is 
started for the largest triangle, which is not already assigned to any 
convex surface. The result of this process is a set of approximately 
convex surfaces. Each of these surfaces mostly represent a single 
fruit, leaf or branch. 
3.2. Convex template instance descriptor 
We also investigate the application of a novel descriptor – Con- 
vex Template Instance (CTI) descriptor for fruit recognition. The 
basic idea of the proposed approach is to develop a descriptor of 
convex surfaces which allows eﬃcient comparison of their shapes. 
The proposed descriptor represents a convex surface by a point in 
an n -dimensional space and similarity of two convex surfaces is

3.3. Including color information 
In this section, we describe how the CTI descriptor can be aug- 
mented by color information. The vector of 25 components, com- 
puted for a particular convex surface S using the method described 
in Section 3.2 , is extended by adding two more elements describ- 
ing the dominant color of the considered surface. The colors of all 
points of S are represented in the HSV color space and a histogram 
of hue values is created from this point set. The contribution of 
each point to the histogram is weighted by its saturation value, 
where only the points with saturation greater or equal to a prede- 
ﬁned threshold τ s are considered. Then, the mean shift algorithm 
( Cheng, 1995 ) is used to segment the histogram into clusters. Let 
s ( h ) be the value assigned to the histogram bin corresponding to a 
particular hue value h from range 0 – 360 °. For each h , the mean 
shift procedure is performed by computing a sequence

hue value ≥τ s is assigned to a cluster according to its hue value. 
The cluster which is assigned the greatest number of points is de- 
clared as the dominant cluster. If the number of points assigned to 
the dominant cluster is at least 50% of the total number of points 
of S , then the CTI descriptor of this surface is extended by two val- 
ues: the center of the dominant cluster and the mean saturation of 
the points assigned to the dominant cluster. If the dominant clus- 
ter is assigned less than 50% of the total number of points of S , 
then the CTI descriptor is extended by two values 0. In the experi- 
ments presented in Section 4 , the parameters of this procedure are 
set to values τ s = 0.2 and w = 40 °. 
4. Experimental evaluation 
In this section, an overview of the experimental procedure and 
analysis of the obtained results are presented. RGB-D images of 
fruit trees are segmented into approximately convex surfaces. Each 
segment or convex surface is classiﬁed into two classes ( fruit or 
other ) using information provided by a descriptor generated for 
each surface. Classiﬁcation is performed using a k – NN algorithm. 
The suitability and eﬃciency of the CTI descriptor for fruit recogni- 
tion is assessed by comparing it with the two variants of the SHOT 
descriptor: the standard SHOT descriptor (hereinafter referred to 

460 
E.K. Nyarko et al. / Expert Systems With Applications 114 (2018) 454–466 
as SHOT) ( Tombari et al., 2010 ), and the SHOT descriptor which in- 
corporates color and texture information (hereinafter referred to as 
SHOTColor) ( Tombari, Salti, & Di Stefano, 2011 ). 
For a given keypoint, the SHOT descriptor proposed in 
Tombari et al. (2010) is created with the aid of a spherical vol- 
ume centered on the keypoint. The volume is ﬁrst subdivided into 
subvolumes by deﬁning an isotropic spherical grid, and then par- 
titioned along the radial, azimuth and elevation axes. For each of 
these subvolumes, a histogram of orientations of surface normals 
in all points within this subvolume is computed. The descriptor 
is then formed by concatenating the histograms generated for all 
subvolumes. 
In the research presented in this paper, the keypoint for gener- 
ating a SHOT descriptor is determined by computing the centroid 
of image projections of points belonging to the convex surface 
for which the descriptor is generated. If this centroid represents a 
point in the depth image which has a deﬁned depth value, then 
the 3D point corresponding to the centroid is used as the keypoint. 
However, not all points in depth images obtained by an RGB-D 
camera have deﬁned depth values since some surfaces absorb 
infrared light of the depth sensor or because of specular reﬂec- 
tions due to glossy surfaces. Hence, if the centroid does not have a 
deﬁned depth value, then the closest point in the 21 × 21 neighbor- 
hood of the centroid is identiﬁed and the 3D point corresponding 
to this image point is used as the keypoint. For the purpose 
of computational eﬃciency, the Manhattan distance is used for 
determining the closest point to the centroid.

4.2. Dataset 
With the aid of a hand-held PrimeSense Carmine 1.09 short 
range sensor operating in 100mm-mode, several sequences of RGB- 
D images of resolution 320 × 240 were captured in natural outdoor 
conditions in an orchard. 2 Each sequence contained images of one 
of the following four fruits: tomato, nectarine, pear or plum. For 
experimental purposes, images from these sequences were selected 
2 The dataset can be downloaded from www.ferit.hr/ARP3D/erdut-dataset . 
using two criteria. The ﬁrst criterion was that there was no mo- 
tion blur or misalignment of depth and RGB image. Such images 
were obtained when the camera was steady or moving very slowly. 
Since the image segments needed to be manually labeled for clas- 
siﬁer training and testing, the second criterion required that the 
fruit in the image was not deep in a shadow so that it could be 
visually and unambiguously categorized by a human evaluator. 
Each of the selected images was then segmented into ap- 
proximately convex surfaces using the procedure described in 
Subsection 3.1 and each segment was manually labeled by a 
human evaluator. Even though the segmentation procedure uses 
depth images only, the corresponding RGB images were used by 
the human evaluator for labeling purposes, thus the need for the 
aforementioned criteria used in image selection. Since the preci- 
sion of the RGB-D sensor used decreases with distance, the seg- 
mentation procedure was conﬁgured to consider only the points in 
the depth image within a distance of 0.6 m from the camera. 
The images and corresponding approximately convex segments 
were then divided into two datasets: a training or reference 
dataset, which was used for classiﬁer training, and a test dataset 
used to assess the discriminatory quality of each of the compared 
descriptors. An overview of the obtained sequences, images and 
dataset sizes is provided in Table 1 . Only segments belonging to 
fruits were labeled as fruit, while all other segments (e.g. those be- 
longing to leaves or branches) were labeled as other . 
Analyzing Table 1 , it can be noticed that the data is highly 
skewed, i.e. approximately only 4.4% of the total number of con- 
vex segments in either the reference dataset or the test dataset is 
labeled as fruit . 
Examples of RGB images of fruits captured during dataset gen- 
eration are shown in Figs. 3–6 , with approximately convex seg- 
ments obtained from the depth images projected onto the RGB im- 
ages and visualized in 3D. 
4.3. Classiﬁcation 
Five different classiﬁers were trained: four of which were fruit 
speciﬁc, i.e. a classiﬁer was determined for each fruit and a ﬁfth 
classiﬁer was trained using all fruit types. Classiﬁcation of the con- 
vex surfaces into two classes ( fruit and other ) was performed using 
k – NN algorithm. The value of k was determined by performing 
10-fold cross validation on the reference dataset. Fig. 7 . shows the 
results of the cross-validation procedure performed for the clas- 
siﬁer trained using all fruit types. It can be seen that an optimal 
value of k = 5 was obtained. 
It should be noted that the same value of k was obtained for 
all fruit speciﬁc classiﬁers. Using the original reference dataset and 
the 5-nearest neighbor classiﬁer, the quality of the descriptors was 
evaluated on the test dataset. Details of the results of the classiﬁca- 
tion procedure on the test dataset for all ﬁve classiﬁers are shown 
in Tables 2 and 3 . 
The classiﬁcation results provided in Tables 2 and 3 obtained 
using the test datasets indicate that there is virtually no difference 
between the SHOT and SHOTColor descriptors since the accuracy, 
precision and recall rates are almost the same, having values rang- 
ing from 98.5% to 99.6% with respect to accuracy, 82.4% to 87.5% 
with respect to precision, 85.4% to 91.3% with respect to recall and 
85.1% to 88.8% with respect to F 1 . Comparing the proposed CTI de- 
scriptors to both SHOT descriptors, it can be noticed that generally, 
the CTI descriptors have slightly lower values in all cases except 
in the situation where the tomato and plum classiﬁers are used. 
When using the CTI descriptor, accuracy values range from 98.1% 
to 99.8%, precision values have a range of 82% - 92.4%, recall is 
between 78.1% and 92.6%, while F 1 is between 80.1% and 91.5%. 
As expected, the results obtained by CTI are improved by adding 
color information to the descriptor, i.e., by implementing CTIColor.

A high accuracy for all the classiﬁers, as shown in Table 3 , is to 
be expected due to the highly skewed test dataset, i.e., due to the 
high number of segments in the test dataset labeled as other . This 
results in a high number of true negatives obtained compared to 
the total number of data measurements. 
A parameter that needs to be deﬁned for the computation of 
SHOT descriptors is the radius search . This parameter deﬁnes the 
size around each key point that is described by the descriptor. In 
all the experiments performed above, this parameter was set to 
20 mm. It was noticed that the tomatoes in the Tomato sequence 
were quite large in size compared to the other fruits. This parame- 
ter was then set to 50 mm and new SHOT descriptors were gener- 
ated. A suitable 5-NN classiﬁer was created and assessed using the 
test data. The results obtained only for the new SHOT descriptors 
are shown in Table 4 . It can be seen that the number (and per- 
centage) of incorrectly classiﬁed segments is less than previously 
obtained (compared to Table 2 ). The classiﬁcation metrics (accu- 
racy, precision, recall and F 1 ) are much higher and are now com- 
parable to those obtained by the classiﬁer using CTI or CTIColor 
descriptors. 
These results indicate that classiﬁcation using SHOT descriptors 
requires tweaking of the parameters to get suitable results depend- 
ing on the size of the fruit in question. The CTI descriptor, on the 
other hand, is generated based on the convex surface and does not 
require the user to provide an estimate of the expected convex sur- 
face.

In a recent paper ( Yongting & Jun, 2017 ), the authors com- 
pared an improved 3D descriptor (Color-FPFH) with four other 3D 
descriptors using four classic classiﬁers. This was done using a 
dataset of 52 point cloud images which were preprocessed and 
segmented using a region growing segmentation method to ex- 
tract independent data of apples, branches and trees which were 
then labeled and stored for training and testing purposes. In their 
paper, their proposed method outperforms the four other descrip- 
tors amongst which is the SHOT descriptor. Using the SHOT de- 
scriptor for their segmented images achieves between 70% and 82% 
accuracy depending on whether a leaf, a branch or an apple is 
being classiﬁed. In our paper, despite the fact that our dataset is 
more challenging, the use of the SHOT descriptor achieves a much 
higher accuracy rate. This might be attributed to the fact that our 
segmentation procedure is completely different thereby providing 
segments which are more suitable for fruit recognition. Further- 
more, in comparison to the execution time of 12 s, reported in 
Yongting and Jun (2017) , our algorithm is much faster, especially 
when using the CTI descriptor. Since the FPFH descriptor, used 
in Yongting and Jun (2017) can be computed very eﬃciently, we 
assume that the segmentation step of the algorithm ( Yongting & 
Jun, 2017 ) represents a bottleneck of their approach. Hence, our 
segmentation approach proposed in this paper represents a valu- 
able contribution. 
The results shown in Tables 2 and 3 , indicate that a classiﬁer 
can be trained to recognize various sorts of fruits at the same time. 
Improvement achieved by using color information is noticeable in 
the case of the CTI descriptor, especially when applied to images of 
plums. On the other hand, no signiﬁcant change, or improvement 
is observed when using SHOT descriptors. 
5. Conclusion 
In this paper, a novel approach to fruit recognition in RGB- 
D images is proposed. The ﬁrst step of the proposed approach 
is to segment the input RGB-D image into convex surfaces. Since 
two randomly positioned objects very rarely form one convex sur- 
face, by segmenting images into convex surfaces, the probability of 
merging multiple fruit or leaves into one segment is minimized. 
Furthermore, since many fruits sorts have approximately convex 
shape, a fruit is almost always represented by a single segment. 
Application of four descriptors is investigated. Two of them are 
variants of the SHOT descriptor and the other two are two vari- 
ants of the novel CTI descriptor, which is based on representing the 
shapes of surfaces by convex polyhedrons with quantized face ori- 
entations. The proposed CTI descriptor is signiﬁcantly simpler than 
SHOT descriptor, which is an important advantage because it is di- 
rectly related to computational eﬃciency of the fruit recognition 
system. 
The discussed approach is tested on a challenging dataset con- 
sisting of RGB-D images of four fruit sorts: tomato, nectarine, pear 
and plum. To the best of our knowledge, this is the only research, 
which considers recognition of plums, pears and nectarines using 
RGB-D camera. Our dataset is acquired in uncontrolled conditions: 
natural illumination, fruits are often in shadow and occluded by 
leaves. It should be noted that the images were acquired on the 
late afternoon of a cloudy day. Contrary to a common opinion that 
consumer grade RGB-D cameras are not applicable in outdoor en- 
vironments in daylight conditions, in our case the obtained depth 
information was not signiﬁcantly inﬂuenced by the sunlight. Nev- 
ertheless, if the recording was performed in the middle of a sunny 
day, we expect the results to be much worse. The recorded dataset 
is publicly available and the results obtained on this dataset by 
three approaches are reported in this paper. Hence, it can be used

as a baseline method for benchmarking of future fruit recognition 
approaches. 
The analysis of the experimental results show that, for the con- 
sidered dataset, combination of segmentation into convex surfaces 
and descriptors which rely on 3D geometry, provides a high fruit 
recognition accuracy. In the case of fruit sorts with distinguish- 
able color, such as tomatoes and plums, using color information 
can improve the performance. However, the color of some fruit 
sorts can be hardly distinguishable from the color of leaves. Fur- 
thermore, even if the fruit color is clearly different from the leaf 
color when exposed to direct light, in uncontrolled lighting condi- 
tions, the shadows cast by leaves and other fruits desaturate the 
color of fruits positioned deeper in the tree top. 
By comparing the results of the four considered descriptors it 
can be concluded that the CTI descriptor which uses color has 
slightly better performance than the SHOT in the case of tomatoes 
and plums, while in the case of nectarines and pears, the SHOT has 
slightly better performance. However, creation of CTI descriptor is 
signiﬁcantly faster than SHOT. Moreover, since it has less compo- 
nents than SHOT, classiﬁcation using the CTI descriptor is signif- 
icantly faster. Furthermore, the difference in the performance be- 
tween the system trained using images of a particular fruit sort 
and the system trained using the images of all considered sorts to- 
gether is not very large. This indicates that a possibility of creating 
one system which can be trained to recognize different fruit sorts 
is worth investigating. 
Although the developed fruit recognition approach gives inter- 
esting results, improvements are possible in all of its steps. In the 
rest of this section, we list some shortcomings of the presented 
method, noticed during the conducted experiments, and suggest 
possible research directions which address these shortcomings. 
Even though a convex surface detected in a depth image rarely 
represents multiple objects, there are cases where a fruit and a leaf 
positioned close to each other together represent an approximately 
convex surface and are detected as a single segment. In order to 
cope with this problem, a postprocessing step, which would sep- 
arate the fruit from the leaf, could be introduced. One approach, 
which could be applied for that purpose is to use the fruit color to 
extract the fruit from the false surface. This approach is expected 
to improve the recognition performance, but it will fail in the cases 
where the fruit color is not clearly visible due to shadows cast by 
other fruits and leaves. Another approach is to perform a RANSAC- 
based 3D model ﬁtting, as proposed in Mack et al. (2017) . In or- 
der to make the algorithm more general, instead of ﬁtting a ba- 
sic geometric shape like a sphere, convex shapes retrieved from a 
training set could be used as models. However, this additional step 
would surely increase the execution time of the fruit recognition 
algorithm. 
The current implementation of our approach considers only a 
single view of a particular scene. A natural extension of the pre- 
sented research is fruit recognition in sequences of RGB-D im- 
ages. By considering image segments with only one or two fruits 
as nearest neighbors in the training dataset, the recall can be in- 
creased, while decreasing the precision. This would result in more 
fruit candidates, some of which might be false. The false positives 
could then be rejected after approaching them by the camera and 
obtaining a more precise image of them. 
An interesting research direction is fruit recognition during 
night with aid of an artiﬁcial light source. While night conditions 
are favorable for active RGB-D cameras like Kinect, PrimeSense, Or- 
bec or RealSense sensors, color information can be made available 
by using a properly selected light source which does not interfere 
with the pattern projectors of the considered sensors. 
Improvements could also be made in the preprocessing stage 
of the algorithm. In the case where an image is acquired while 
the camera is pointed towards the sun, the color of fruits, leaves 
and branches is shifted towards the blue region of the spectrum, 
which degrades the performance of classiﬁcation based on color. 
An example is shown in Fig. 5 a. For such cases, color normalization 
techniques should be considered for preprocessing the image. The 
effect of color normalization on the fruit recognition performance 
is one future research option. 
Considering the classiﬁcation step, in addition to the applied k - 
NN approach, other classiﬁcation methods, commonly applied in 
machine learning, e.g. SVM, random trees or artiﬁcial neural net- 
works could be tried in combination with the proposed descriptor. 
Furthermore, impressive results obtained by deep neural networks 
(DNN) in the ﬁeld of object classiﬁcation indicate that application 
of DNNs for fruit recognition in RGB-D images is worth investigat- 
ing. 
Finally, although the evaluation dataset collected in the frame- 
work of the presented research is a valuable contribution to the 
related research ﬁeld, a more precise ground truth data could 
be generated. Since manual annotation of a large image set on 
the pixel level is beyond the capacities of our research group, the 
ground truth is created by manually annotating segments obtained 
by our segmentation method. This is a drawback of the ground 
truth provided with our dataset, because it cannot be used to eval- 
uate precision of segmentation algorithms on pixel level. Manual 
annotation of the images remains as a possible future extension of 
our work.
