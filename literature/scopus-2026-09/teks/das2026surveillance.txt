Puja Das1 · Chitra Jain2 · Kamal Kumar Gola3

Abstract
The paper describes the current state of development in multi-object tracking and comprehensively reviews the most recent
algorithmic achievements, applications, challenges, and directions for future research. This paper discusses different MOT
methods across various domains, including autonomous driving, surveillance, robotics, and intelligent transportation systems,
and how the paradigm switches from old tracking methods to deep learning-based approaches. The development of the
MOT models based on CNNs, RNNs, graph-based tracking, and transformer architectures is reviewed. This work critically
evaluates the problems related to MOT, including occlusions, MOTion blur, identity preservation, and real-time performance
constraints for demanding road applications in resource-scarce environments. Additionally, 152 MOT models and frameworks’
performances against several widely acknowledged benchmark datasets are evaluated, including MOTChallenge, KITTI,
Waymo Open Dataset, and nuScenes under standard metrics, Multiple Object Tracking Accuracy (MOTA), Multiple Object
Tracking Precision (MOTP), Identity Switches (IDSW), and Fragments (FRAG). Finally, emerging research directions, such
as multimodal sensor fusion in LiDAR and radar, self-supervised learning, domain adaptation, as well as the use of edge
computing to conduct real-time tracking, are also discussed. Hybrid tracking strategies to bridge the gap between Deep
Learning and probabilistic MOTion models are also discussed, and obtain better robustness but poorer scalability. This review
introduces the MOT research bar and poses strategic directions to scalable, efﬁcient, and interpretable tracking solutions for
intelligent perception systems for future innovations.
Keywords Deep learning · Edge computing · Multi-object tracking · Probabilistic MOTion models · Self-supervised learning ·
Sensor fusion

1.1 Scope of jointly detection and tracking
The main goal of MOT is to identify and track many objects
in the sequence of ﬁlms so that they can be extracted and
maintained their identity on the frame. MOT, unlike track-
ing individual objects, follows several categories, including
humans, cars, animals, and other moving objects, which
track a single moving object. One of the primary problems
is the lack of previous information on goods in context.
Systems that track objects use screens to maintain person-
ality, and each object gets an individual tag [2, 3]. Although
they have two different problems, the detection of courage
and object is sometimes confused. Since monitoring neces-
sitates the location of items in every frame, MOT heavily
depends on recognizing objects even though it is separate
from them. Conventional MOT systems may be divided into
two primary groups: one-stage and two-stage monitors. Real-
time performance is prioritized and detected by one-stage
trackers such as FairMOT, which combine monitoring and
recognition into one system and instantly deliver bound-
ing boxes and identiﬁcation in just one execution. However,
by combining identiﬁcation (through the use of YOLO or
Faster R-CNN and connection (using MOTion models or
appearance embeddings), two-stage trackers like DeepSORT
compromise speed for consistency [4]. Therefore, the effec-
tiveness of the MOT algorithm is signiﬁcantly impacted by
recent advancements in object identiﬁcation. MOT is utilized
by self-driving vehicles to ﬁnd cars, cyclists, and pedestrians
even as retaining secure mobility under converting situa-
tions. MOT is utilized by surveillance networks to reveal
public areas, discover criminal activities, and improve secu-
rity protocols. MOT allows robots to navigate appropriately
and effectively by helping to improve strategies and commu-
nicate with unpredictable surroundings. Figure 4 timeline of
object detection advancements from 2014 to 2024, show-
casing key models such as R-CNN, YOLO, DETR, Swin
Transformer, and Multi-Modal Object Detection.
Advances in Deep Learning have led to a paradigm
shift in MOT frameworks. Hand-crafted features and proba-
bilistic models dominated traditional tracking frameworks
earlier, but newer Deep Learning approaches now utilize
convolutional neural networks (CNNs), recurrent neural net-
works (RNNs), and attention mechanisms to achieve better
tracking accuracy. Recent innovations integrate foundation
models like the Segment Anything Model (SAM), which
provides zero-shot instance segmentation masks to enhance
occlusion handling and reduce identity switches. Similarly,
Spatio-Attentive Latent MOTion Models (SALM) leverage
attention mechanisms to resolve complex object interactions
in crowded scenes, minimizing fragmentation during abrupt
MOTion changes [5, 6]. CNN-based models learn robust
spatial features, while RNNs capture temporal motion pat-
terns of objects. Attention mechanisms, such as transformer
blocks, ensure better identity preservation even across long
trajectories and minimize errors in dense scenes. Although
signiﬁcant advancements have been achieved, MOT remains
a challenging problem with occlusions, MOTion blur, and
appearance variations. Occlusions occur when objects block
each other’s paths, leading to identity switches and track-
ing failures. Motion blur reduces object visibility, making
detection and feature extraction more difﬁcult. The appear-
ance variations caused by changes in viewpoint, illumination,
and background make tracking consistency even more chal-
lenging [7]. Researchers are now turning to new ideas like
self-supervised learning, graph-based tracking, and multi-
modal fusion to overcome these challenges and improve
MOT performance.
The performance of MOT is measured using various met-
rics like multiple object tracking accuracy (MOTA), multiple
objecttrackingprecision(MOTP),identityswitches(IDSW),
and fragments (FRAG). MOTA approximates overall track-
ing accuracy by accounting for detection and association
errors, and MOTP approximates localization precision. Iden-
tity switches deﬁne tracking failure when the ID of an
object is erroneously swapped, and fragments approximate

Multi-Object Tracking has been a keystone of recent
computer vision, triggering breakthroughs in various areas.

1.2 Organization
This paper uses a structured format to offer complete insights
into Deep Learning-based object detection and tracking. The
paper starts with a review of both traditional methods and
Deep Learning-based approaches, with a discussion of their
main advancements appearing in the Related Work segment.
The article examines the Challenges and Open Issues in the
Multi-Object Detection and Tracking section with an analy-
sis of the problems arising from occlusions and dimensional
changes in processing speed requirements and dataset avail-
ability. The research investigates Deep Learning Approaches
for Object Detection through CNN-based transformer-based
and region-based methods as well as single-shot detectors

anisms included in the section named "Loss Functions"

paper explores modern techniques for tracking and detection,
which include classic correlation ﬁlter-based trackers as well
as YOLO-based tracking and the Segment Anything Model
(SAM) alongside SLAM. The performance evaluation is per-
formed using MOTA and MOTP along with IDF1 and HOTA
as metrics. Deep Learning technology generates signiﬁcant
impacts in medical imaging, as well as self-driving cars and
trafﬁc monitoring and remote sensing operations. The paper
ends with Future Recommendations that focus on research
gaps and efﬁcient model improvements, as well as the future
impact of new architectures for object detection and tracking
systems.

The ﬁeld of Multi-Object Tracking (MOT) has undergone
a signiﬁcant evolution from classic methods to contempo-
rary Deep Learning approaches [16]. This evolution can be
attributed to the growing complexity of monitoring scenar-
ios and progress in calculation power. Historically, classic
methods were based on techniques such as Kalman ﬁlters,
data association, and movement models to follow several
objects [17]. These methods have laid the foundations for
subsequent developments in the region, establishing funda-
mental principles that are still relevant today. Classic world
techniques have mainly focused on linear movement mod-
els, which tried to predict the future position of an object
according to its past trajectory. The Kalman ﬁlter, a recursive
estimator, has acquired importance due to its effectiveness
in the treatment of measurements over time, thus adapting
to three-dimensional monitoring scenarios [18]. In addition,
data association techniques, such as the Hungarian algorithm
and the joint association of probabilistic data (JPDA), have
facilitated the correspondence of objects detected between
frames, allowing an improvement in monitoring performance
in environments cluttered [15]. However, these approaches
have often struggled with dynamic occlusions and history,
because they were strongly based on the hypotheses of con-
tinuity in the movement of objects and linearity in models of
movement. While precise monitoring demand in real-world
applications has intensiﬁed, the researchers began to explore
more robust alternatives. The introduction of more sophis-
ticated algorithms, such as monitoring multiple hypotheses
(MHT) and probabilistic graphical models, marked a change
toward the adaptation of non-linear movement models and
the manipulation of uncertainty in observations [19]. These
methods allowed better performance in scenarios where
objects were frequently occurring or interacting closely,
although they also introduced an increase in computational
cost. The advent of Deep Learning has catalyzed a trans-
formative change in the ﬁeld of the world, which gives
signiﬁcant improvements both in the accuracy of monitor-
ing and efﬁciency. The Deep Learning methods use neural
networks to extract representations of high-level functional-
ities from raw visual input, which can considerably improve
the robustness of monitoring systems [20]. A pivotal con-
cept in contemporary approaches is the use of convolutional
neural networks (CNNs) for the extraction of functionalities,
allowing algorithms to learn discriminating characteristics
directly from data, rather than relying only on handcrafted
characteristics as conventional approaches [21] show. This
progression has enabled Deep Learning systems to work well
in various and complex monitoring environments, including
crowded scenes and different lighting conditions. The emer-
gence of large sets of public data such as MOTchallenge and
KITTIplayedacrucialroleinthedevelopmentofword-based
systems. These data sets provide a reference to assess the
performance of various methods under standardized condi-
tions, thus proMOTing research and facilitating comparison
between different algorithms. The establishment of a clear
evaluation measures, such as the multiple object tracking
accuracy (MOTA) and the accuracy of monitoring multiple
objects (MOTP), also allowed researchers to quantitatively
assess the efﬁciency of their approaches [22].
Despite the promising capacities of Deep Learning world-
wide, several challenges persist in current research. Ques-
tions such as treating occlusions, maintaining a coherent
identity over prolonged periods, and managing dynamic
interactions between objects remain important obstacles
[23]. In addition, the computational demands of Deep
Learning models pose practical challenges when deploying
these systems in real-time applications, requiring continu-
ous research on light and effective architectures. Emerging
trends in the ﬁeld indicate an increasing interest in integrating
monitoring with other tasks, such as the detection and seg-
mentation of objects. This trend aims to create end-to-end
solutions that can contextualize the monitoring of objects
in their environment, allowing more precise performance
in complex scenarios. In addition, the combination with
reinforcement learning is explored to adaptively improve
monitoring strategies in response to changing environments,
which highlights the additional innovation potential in this
dynamic ﬁeld [24]. The evolution of tracking of multiple
objects (MOT) witnessed a signiﬁcant transformation with
the introduction of Deep Learning methodologies, which
fundamentally altered the approach to feature extraction and
representation of video data. The historical study conducted
by [22] illustrated how neural networks enhanced the ability
to automatically generate sophisticated features of resources
directly from raw video inputs. This change was critical to
address longstanding issues such as appearances on variable
objects, occlusions, and interactions within scenes that were
challenging for classic handmade MOT methods. The ini-
tial implementations of Deep Learning for MOTion focused
mainly on the integration of object detection structures
with tracking algorithms. These hybrid systems took advan-
tage of advances in convolution neural networks (CNN) to
ﬁrst detect objects in each table, followed by the appli-
cation of tracking methods to maintain object identities
between frames. Methods such as Sort (simple online track-
ingandinreal-time)andDeepSorthighlightedthisapproach,

3 Challenges and open issues in multi-object
detection and tracking
• Occlusion provides a prime challenge in Multi-Object
Tracking (MOT). It happens while objects in a scene
overlap or are temporarily obscured by other items,
complicating correct monitoring. Partial occlusions can
result in unreliable function extraction; at the same time,
whole occlusions can also cause a loss of item iden-
tity. To efﬁcaciously manipulate occlusions, advanced
monitoring models appoint re-identiﬁcation strategies,
MOTion prediction, and intensity estimation. However,
real-world conditions, such as crowded areas and con-
verting digicam angles, intensify this trouble. Creating
strong algorithms that can count on an object’s reappear-
ance after being occluded is a vital area of study in MOT
[73].
• Identity Switching takes place when a monitoring algo-
rithm mistakenly assigns a new ID to an item that is
already being tracked, frequently due to similarities in
appearance, occlusions, or inadequate feature matching.
This issue undermines monitoring consistency and over-
all accuracy. Identity switches are especially common in
crowded settings, where numerous objects display simi-
lar movement patterns and appearances. Advanced deep
learning models use appearance embeddings and spatial-
temporal constraints to reduce the likelihood of identity
switching. Nevertheless, challenges persist in real-time
applications, where fast-moving data and limited compu-
tationalresourceshinderconsistentidentitymaintenance.
Addressing identity switching is essential for improving
long-term tracking performance.
• Reliability and computational efﬁciency must be bal-
anced in real-time multi-object tracking (MOT). Appli-
cations such as robotic navigation, self-driving cars, and
surveillance demand strict real-time performance. Efﬁ-

Deciding on the SLAM technique is difﬁcult due to the
environmental factors. On the other hand, correlation ﬁlter
approaches are enough for the required task of being static
or less complex scenes, and provide a low-latency solution.

The difference is that Deep Learning-based approaches, such
as YOLO and SAM, are competitive in the ability to locate

the selection of a certain frame based on application-speciﬁc

Despite the development of the methods of the future, there
exist current techniques such as the correlation ﬁlters, and
the more advanced Deep Learning models, such as YOLO
(you only look at once) and SAM (segment all the models),
have been proven to be able to handle some most advanced
applications. The main idea of YOLO and SAM is still one
of detection and segmentation, but dedicated deep-learning
trackers are being realized for scenario-speciﬁc issues in
object tracking. SiamFC (Fully Convolutional Siamese Net-
works) uses a Siamese network to learn similarity metrics
from a target template and search regions such that track-
ing is possible without frequent model updates. MDNet
(Multi-Domain Network) is trained on multiple sequences
from different domains to generalize tracking across domains
and is excellent when object appearances change a lot in
long-term scenarios [131]. DeepSORT is one of the track-
ing algorithms that merges the detection model (i.e. YOLO)
with the calculation of motion statistics fueled by Kalman
ﬁlters. In this way, the appearance descriptors help decrease
the identity switch accurately when the occlusions occur.
Recently developed techniques, like TransT, that use the
transformer architecture and the self-attention mechanism
to model global dependencies between target and search
regions, can help us to overcome the problems of occlusions
anddeformations.Incomparisontopreviouscorrelationﬁlter
forcing only, the trained video trackers are capable of learn-
ing image features and temporal coherence, both of which
make them outperform the classical methods in dynamic
environments. Strictly speaking, conventional SLAM sys-
tems are customized for running sensors and the conditions
of the environment. ORB-SLAM is a feature-based approach
based on the ORB (Oriented FAST and Rotated BRIEF)
algorithm that allows an onboard processor to calculate the
localized features and move in real-time. As an observation,
LIDAR-SLAM is a class of SLAM systems based on a very
powerful point cloud registration method, that is, the itera-
tive closest point (ICP) registration, which has resulted in
precise 3D mapping in autonomous vehicles [131]. In very
dynamic situations, DynaSLAM and DS-SLAM can concur-
rently include semantic segmentation, for instance, object
instance segmentation using Mask R-CNN, to recognize and
reject the transient objects and thus keep the map consis-
tent by only focusing on the areas where the structures are
stable. Visual inertial fusion systems such as VINS-Fusion
can fuse the information from a camera and accelerometer
data to provide a reliable position estimate that is consistent
with textureless or fast-moving object environments. On the
one hand, adding DeepSORT tracking algorithms will both
maintain temporal coherence at the same time and provide
a more precise map by detecting only the dynamic entities
e.g. pedestrians being tracked by YOLO and DeepSORT and
continuously updated in the map, for instance, if they are
close to a collision. Semantic SLAM intersects with them
by not only going object level but also substituting it with
an object label via SAM’s segmentation masks (e.g. "door,"
"vehicle"). This, in turn, might allow for context-aware navi-
gation of human-robot interaction. Despite the advancement
in the ﬁeld of object tracking and SLAM technologies, other
problems have to be dealt with ethically. Models that are fed
with biased datasets may perform worse in different real-
world scenarios, and one example is their struggle to detect
darker-skinned pedestrians in low-light conditions and clas-
sify objects that are not well represented. There are also
privacy issues that are raised when SLAM is deployed in
public places, since tracking might be invasive. These prob-
lems can best be solved by curating a variety of training data
sets, blurring faces in public maps, and putting in place the
relevant regulatory frameworks to govern ethics. The tech-
nologies are moving cities that have always been the center of
human life from physical to digitized. Who are we to decide?
Either transparent algorithms in decisions, or user consent,
or not.
The improvement of efﬁciency can happen in a way by
the optimization of model architectures. For instance, mobile
nets are light and efﬁcient, which allows them to achieve
speed, although accuracy is partially lost. These innovations
are indispensable for the implementation of monitoring and
detection systems on resource-based devices like drones and
robotic platforms, which are typically used in SLAM appli-
cations. Model compression and pruning techniques, when
integrated, can further enhance real-time treatment capabil-
ities and thus, they can continuously keep an eye on several
objects in a changing environment with variability and unpre-
dictability. Further still, temporal information is capable of
being connected to the dynamic parameters to [132] ensure
robustness. Algorithms would positively defend the time
context by utilizing recurrent neural networks (RNN) or
transformers, as well as including object trajectories that
remain unaltered in the face of sudden variations. Ongo-
ing work may delve into the marriage between traditional
ﬁltering and automatic learning methods to exploit the pre-
dictive capabilities of Deep Learning while still maintaining
the stability of correlation ﬁlters. It is a major challenge to
outgrow the present data sets and to produce improvements in
the training and validation of robust monitoring algorithms.

9 Applications of deep learning-based
object detection and tracking
Object identiﬁcation, one of the three fundamental tasks
of artiﬁcial intelligence, has many practical applications.
Depending on the speciﬁc task, object identiﬁcation is imple-
mented differently in real-world applications. This section
reviews signiﬁcant applications of object identiﬁcation, such
as medical image analysis, satellite image detection, and
facial recognition for object detection [151].
9.1 Medical imaging
Deep Learning-based object recognition aids in medical
diagnosis by identifying abnormalities in X-rays, MRIs, and
CT scans. Artiﬁcial intelligence algorithms such as U-Net
and Faster R-CNN assist radiologists in accurately detecting
fractures, tumors, and other diseases. Computerized systems
reduce human error, accelerate evaluation, and support early
disease detection [152]. Technologies like this are especially
helpful for diseases like malignancy, where patient survival
rates are greatly increased by early detection. Furthermore,
identifying objects improves the precision of robotically
assisted procedures.
9.2 Self-driving cars
Algorithms such as YOLO and SSD analyze images from
car cameras to ensure safe navigation in unpredictable con-
ditions. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and related
models improve through training on large datasets, achieving
greater accuracy in recognizing diverse road environments
even under low light or adverse weather conditions [153].
Deep Learning-based recognition of objects is essential for
autonomous cars, allowing them to identify passengers,
motorists, roadway markings, and hurdles. Real-time object
detection aids in adaptive speed control, path identiﬁcation,
and the prevention of crashes.
9.3 Traffic monitoring and smart cities
The development of cities is being revolutionized by Deep
Learning-based object recognition, which enhances smart
cityefforts andtrafﬁccontrol. Artiﬁcial intelligence-powered
cameras help reduce trafﬁc by analyzing vehicle movement,
detecting violations, and adjusting signal timings [154].
Object detection is used by automated toll-collecting systems
to recognize license plates and classify vehicles. Smart park-
ing systems use AI to locate available spaces and efﬁciently
guide vehicles. Real-time pedestrian crossing surveillance
also improves trafﬁc safety by averting collisions. The use
of this technology is essential to improving the sustainability
and efﬁciency of cities.
9.4 Remote sensing image detection
The primary applications of remote sensing image identiﬁ-
cation are in transportation directions, planning for cities,
property and mineral surveys, and military observation.
Objects of interest include aircraft, ships, vehicles, highways,
airports, ports, and various man-made structures. The fol-

Multi-Object Tracking (MOT) has gained substantial recog-
nition due to its wide range of applications in both academic

a structured analysis of contemporary MOT techniques. The
evaluation begins by identifying the major challenges in the

tracking (DBT). A comprehensive classiﬁcation of tracking
methods based on their architectural design is presented,
followed by a detailed examination of their methodolo-
gies. An extensive performance assessment is conducted
using established metrics and publicly available benchmarks.
The paper also explores emerging research directions that
aim to advance the ﬁeld and highlight developing trends.
This survey focuses mainly on end-to-end tracking frame-
works, including transformer-based regression (TBR) and
transformer-based association (TBA) designs. This review
also analyzes recently released MOT datasets using HOTA
for detailed evaluation of tracking metrics. The study eval-
uates advanced algorithms across established datasets to
identify their performance characteristics, strengths, and lim-
itations. The study combines an analysis of modern methods
with prospective research directions, focusing on integrating
MOT with emerging technologies such as generative model-
ing, neuromorphic computing, and federated learning. These
emerging technologies have the potential to reshape MOT by
enhancing efﬁciency, ﬂexibility, and scalability. This survey
aims to address existing research gaps while encouraging
collaboration among experts. The analysis integrates current
innovations to identify future prospects that support both the
preservation and practical expansion of MOT technology.
Recent works from 2024 and 2025 emphasize lightweight
attention-based detectors such as YOLOv12 for real-time
and edge deployment, synthetic data generation for scal-
able training, and multimodal frameworks such as LiDAR
MOT-DETR for robust 3D tracking. In addition, compre-

fair comparison and reproducibility. These advances indicate
a future direction toward hybrid, efﬁcient, and adaptive MOT
