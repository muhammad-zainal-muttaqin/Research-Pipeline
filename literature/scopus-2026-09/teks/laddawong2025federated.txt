Te prevailing method for grading oil palm fruit bunches in mills relies on human graders conducting visual inspections, resulting
in frequent errors and inconsistent assessments. Tis is a signifcant open problem when developing a detector for oil palm fruit
ripeness and oil content, which are related factors. Current trends focus on computer vision techniques based on image processing
and machine learning to improve grading of oil palm fruit bunches at an individual factory, resulting in limited diversity of the
data used for evaluation. Collecting data from all factories ofers informational advantages but raises privacy concerns. Addressing
these challenges, this study proposes federated learning (FL) to develop local and global prediction models for grading oil palm
fruit ripeness while preserving data privacy. FL facilitates collaborative model training across factories, mitigating privacy risks
and enhancing model development efciency. Te proposed model uses the color of palm husks to determine the ripeness stage,
which is used as a factor in predicting the yield of oil from the crop. A predictive model was created using FL principles with
a training dataset of 5209 images, which was divided into two subsets: single-palm (2571 images) and multipalm (2638 images).
Te classifcation accuracy of a global model was 90.0%, while the local models were expanded to include private data for each of 4
testing clients. Te predictive global and local models from FL were used to implement the system in a web application form and to
validate its performance in calling the oil palm ripeness stage.
Keywords: federated learning; grading oil palm fruit; oil palm; ripeness
1. Introduction
Te oil palm species Elaeis guineensis is widely cultivated
across South-East Asia, notably in Indonesia, Malaysia, and
Tailand. In Tailand, oil palm serves various purposes,
being utilized in both dessert foods and in making biodiesel
for vehicles [1]. Farmers typically assess the ripeness of oil
palm and its yield by considering factors such as coloration
and fruit quantity per bunch. Subsequently, farmers
transport their harvest to the purchasing factory, where the
quality or oil percentage is visually evaluated based on the
coloration of the palm bunches, which are related factors.
Tus, this traditional method does not ensure optimal oil
palm quality in terms of oil percentage. Apart from infu-
encing the oil quality, a low oil percentage also decreases the
price paid. Tis traditional method does not ensure optimal

oil palm quality in terms of oil percentage. Te current
practice in grading fresh oil palm fruit bunches (FFBs) in
mills uses human graders for subjective visual inspection,
which can lead to repeated mistakes, inconsistent evaluation
results, and many other related losses. In addition, the fruit
color varies from very dark purple to orange, depending on
genotype and ripeness stage. Te oil content also varies by
ripeness stage of an FFB, and it is generally stated as oil-to-
bunch ratio [2]. As the oil content of an FFB is a function of
its degree of ripeness [3–5], it is crucial that the FFBs are
harvested at near optimal ripeness stage. Tis is a signifcant
open problem: to develop a detector for consistently de-
termining oil palm fruit ripeness.
In the past few years, several automated fruit grading
methods have been proposed and methods based on
computer vision are the most common in this context [6–9].
Such a system must support equipment such as an advanced
digital camera, as well as computer software and a trained
operator (human graders), and this is not suitable for ef-
cient on-site testing [10]. Te system can also apply artifcial
intelligence to classify the oil palm FFBs [11–14], and studies
have used neural networks and fuzzy regression models for
the classifcation [15–18]. It is known that this approach
requires a complicated algorithm and precise image col-
lection for the recognition or ripeness stages at each mill.
One challenge to enhance the performance of computer
vision is to curate a large image dataset for training the
classifer model. For this reason, a centralized data center to
collect the image dataset is required, which currently does
not exist because of the information privacy of mills. Tus,
this study applies federated learning (FL) to improve the
performance in grading oil palm fruit bunches for ripeness.
FL, or distributed machine learning, avoids the need for
data to be sent to a centralized server by involving the
transmission of models between the local computations [19].
Tis gradually lowers the costs and privacy hazards asso-
ciated with using centralized machine learning techniques.
FL introduces fresh avenues of exploration in the feld of
artifcial intelligence. It ofers an innovative training ap-
proach for constructing personalized models while up-
holding user privacy [20]. FL is applied in many research
areas including medical, IoTs, transportation, defense,
mobile apps, and so on [21–23]. Te machine learning model
is applied to generate global and local predictive models in
FL. As mentioned above, machine learning techniques are
widely used to classify the level of oil palm fruit bunch
ripeness, and the classifer types tested include SVM, ANN,
linear discriminant analysis (LDA), and so on [24, 25].
Moreover, deep learning techniques are currently widely
used to call the ripeness level, because these may provide
a high classifcation accuracy [26, 27]. Tus, this method is
applied in this study to generate global and local predictive
models in an FL scheme. Te proposed method will be
implemented on a web application platform that is accessible
across all types of devices: computers, laptops, or smart-
phones. Its primary purpose is to assess the ripeness stage of
oil palm fresh fruit bunches across diferent regions, pre-
senting the fndings in the form of percentages with a clear
and informative interactive dashboard. With the established
system, palm oil mills can swiftly and efectively respond to
dynamic situations and industry trends, all while safe-
guarding the confdentiality and privacy of their data. Te
breakthrough lies in handling the data securely without
centralizing it, ensuring the utmost confdentiality and
sensitivity of the information involved. Tis cutting-edge
approach is poised to revolutionize the industry and redefne
how we harness technology for sustainability with due
consideration of data security.
Te contributions of this research, in comparison to
previous methods and systems, include improvements in
data privacy, processing speed, accuracy, cost-efectiveness,
and real-time updates. Firstly, our approach utilizes FL,
enabling collaborative model training without sharing raw
data, ensuring the privacy of proprietary information; an
important concern in traditional centralized systems. In
terms of processing speed, unlike conventional systems that
process one image at a time, our FL-based system supports
the simultaneous processing of multiple fles, reducing
processing time and increasing efciency, especially in large-
scale industrial settings. Regarding accuracy, the integration
of deep learning with FL results in a model that achieves
90.0% accuracy in predicting oil palm ripeness, out-
performing previous machine learning methods that re-
quired more complex algorithms and data handling.
Additionally, the FL-OPI system eliminates the need for
centralized data infrastructure and reduces data transfer,
making it signifcantly more cost-efective than traditional
systems. Lastly, our system enables real-time model updates
across participating factories without exchanging raw data,
allowing continuous improvements. In contrast, traditional
methods rely on periodic updates, which can delay the
system’s adaptation to new data.
2. Related works
FL is a distributed method for developing machine learning
models, such that distributes and processes data across
several nodes or devices. It does not require sending in-
formation to a single center for training the model [28]. Te
global model is transmitted to the node or device that is
responsible for privately storing the data. At each node, the
private data will be used to improve the current iteration of
a global model in a local model. Te local model will then be
sent to update the global model for similar use by the other
distributed nodes. Tus, an efective model that efciently
predicts and regulates the correctness of calls across varied
data is created, by combining the fndings from each node
retaining privacy in processing of data, while maintaining
also stability and control. For this reason, FL is applied in
many tasks [29], for example in healthcare and medicine,
agriculture and agricultural processing, marketing and ad-
vertising, and so on.
FL is involved in the healthcare and medicine sectors
with hospital diagnostic model training by using dispersed
data to develop and train risk prediction models, based on
various patient datasets, in order to forecast or identify the
patient’s health status [22, 30]. Moreover, for predictions
and for categorizing patients from electronic health records,

Te above demonstrates that FL research is frequently
employed in many areas because of need for privacy of data,
and to improve predictive models. For this reason, this work
applied FL in oil palm industry (OPI) for classifying the stage
of oil palm fruit ripeness. Tis study utilizes FL to establish
a hub of knowledge while protecting individual privacy and
preventing the exchange of actual data, enabling use by all
participating organizations.
Oil palm holds signifcant economic importance in many
countries, serving as a key crop for producing a diverse range
of products in the food, energy, and agricultural industries.
High-quality ripe palm fruit are selected by the industry for
manufacturing various palm oil based products. Several
factors, including fruit color, reproduction, weight, shape,
and peel thickness, can be considered to assess the ripeness
of oil palm [28].
However, the traditional evaluation of oil palm quality is
predominantly conducted subjectively by experts, frequently
employed in OPI, who assess the ripeness of palm fruit
bunches through weight measurements and color obser-
vations. To address this, various image processing ap-
proaches have been introduced to aid in determining the
ripeness level of oil palm fruit [36].
An automated grading system for oil palm bunches using
the RGB color 130 layers has been devised. Tis system was
designed to distinguish three categories of oil palm bunches:
raw, semiripe, and ripe. Te ripeness index is determined
based on various color intensities [37]. To assess the ripeness
level using a similar approach, the study conducted by Fahmi
et al. distinguished between ripe and unripe fruits [38].
Additionally, a portable NIR spectrometer was employed for
the characterization of palm bunches, assessing both the
degree of ripeness and oil content in the bunches [25, 39].
Tis method utilizes hyperspectral imaging with invisible
light and NIR sensors to distinguish between diferent oil
palm fruit growths stages, employing LDA for classifcation.
Furthermore, the previous studies utilized image processing
and machine learning techniques to evaluate the ripeness
levels of oil palm fruit, as presented in Table 2.
Recent advances in deep learning strategies have shown
promising applications in various agricultural domains
beyond oil palm grading. For instance, hybrid attention
mechanisms have been successfully employed to predict
avocado ripeness with enhanced accuracy, leveraging both
spatial and channel-wise attention networks [42, 43]. Sim-
ilarly, spectrum-based hybrid deep learning models have
been utilized to predict intactness in agricultural products by
combining spectral imaging techniques with convolutional
neural networks (CNN) [44]. Tese approaches underscore
the versatility of deep learning methods in addressing
complex agricultural challenges, providing inspiration for
their application in oil palm fruit grading.
In order for the model to have the ability to efciently
analyze complex data that requires high accuracy, this study
used a deep learning model called the FL System, which is
a distributed learning system that uses data directly on each
device. Using a data-centered learning approach makes it
possible to learn and process according to the environment
of each device. We will encrypt the data so that the real data
are not revealed, to create a system model that will be used to
train oil palm yield prediction, supplying visualizations on
a web application platform, and reducing the time for
inspecting palm oil results from receiving the palm bunches.
Te quality and performance of the developed system are
evaluated using the indicators Root Mean Square Error
(RMSE), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), and Directional
Accuracy (DA).

3.2.1. Te Proposed Framework. Te proposed system ap-
plied FL to classify the ripeness level of oil palm bunches
using distributed data across the factories, and we estab-
lished the FL for OPI (FL-OPI) nodes. Te communication
structure of FL-OPI nodes will transfer models between
nodes in the system, as shown in Figure 2.
In Figure 2, there are fve tasks for each FL-OPI node. To
create a global model, a machine learning model is frst
chosen and trained on available datasets. Second, each
FL-OPI receives a copy of this initial global model. Tirdly,
the image database of the local palm fruit bunches is used to
train the local model. Tirdly, the image database of the local
palm fruit bunches is used to train the local model.
FL-OPI also modifes the parameters of the local models
to produce a new global model. It should be noted that this
task assignment does not share any palm fruit bunch color
image data, only the encrypted parameters of the local
model. Finally, by performing transfer learning from the
global model to the local model, each FL-OPI can obtain its
locally customized model.
In this current study, privacy is enforced by preventing
access to information about the precise origin of photos. Tis
could be strongly tied to the factory’s or company’s customer
information. Each FL-OPI in this study trains its local model
on a regional (local and private) dataset. Instead of sharing
any data, it sends only the local model’s updated parameters
to the cloud. Moreover, individualization provides a higher
level of predictability for each FL-OPI’s palm oil quantity
and quality based on palm color condition.

Tis work applied an FL framework. In this framework
the global machine learning model is updated and shared
with all clients for predictive classifcation use without the
raw data. For this reason, the connected clients can use the
global model and update their local models by using private
data for re-training. Te data sent back to the server are
encrypted and stored in encrypted fles, using only the
parameters of interest. Te system is also secure with au-
thentication token login session and data encryption of user
passwords. Afterward, the global model will be updated to
improve its performance, before sharing with the clients
again. Te main advantage of this approach is in the data
privacy, while at the same time the efciency of predictive
models is improved by the shared information. Moreover,
this approach is amenable for a cross-platform web appli-
cation that allows the users to access the system from
anywhere, or on any platform for internet use.
Regarding the assessment of accuracy, the traditional
models are derived from data collected and directly evalu-
ated by experts prior to model development. Te proposed
system is assessed for accuracy using training and test ac-
curacy values, and the models are improved by tuning their
parameters, along with the hyperparameters learning rate,
model training cycle number (epoch count), model batch
size, and the adjusted model structure. In each improvement
cycle, the model is improved according to the measurement
results of accuracy indicators, to increase the efciency of
prediction. In addition, the proposed system saves time in
classifying the ripeness stages of oil palm fruit bunches, with
improved performance and consistency. Te developed
system shows the time spent on image processing. Te re-
sults will be displayed together with the percentage of raw
ripeness stage in the oil palm fruit bunches.
Te primary objective of this study was to develop a FL
framework for grading oil palm fruit ripeness, addressing the

human inspection and centralized machine learning ap-
proaches that pose privacy concerns. Te global model
achieved a classifcation accuracy of 90.0%, demonstrating
the efectiveness of FL in maintaining high prediction ac-
curacy while preserving data privacy across diferent
factories.

 . Conclusion
According to published research, factors indicative of the palm
oil content of fruit bunches include the color coordinates and
ripeness stage of the palm fruit. Tis study proposes a cen-
tralized transfer learning framework named FL-OPI for col-
lecting image data to evaluate the ripeness of oil palm fruits in
the industry while safeguarding data privacy. Te proposed
model utilizes the color of the palm fruit to determine the
maturity stage, which serves as a factor in predicting oil yield
from crops. With a central collection of encoded model pa-
rameters from various FL-OPI nodes at diferent factories,
actual data privacy on agricultural raw materials remains
uncompromised. Te initial training dataset comprised 5209
images of palm fruit bunches, categorized by experts based on
ripeness degree, with photos of single bunches (2571 images)
and multiple bunches (2638 images), featuring oil palm va-
rieties such as CIRAD ‘Millennium’ (Deli X Lame), Spring
Black (Deli Nigerian Black), Surat Tani 2 (Deli X Lame), and
Surat Tani 7 (Deli X Tanza-NIA). During the data analysis
stage, the model achieved 90.0% accuracy after 50 training
cycles. Te usage entails users selecting images for input,
specifying basic information according to various topics, and
specifying oil palm fruit bunch weight. Te system then
processes and displays palm ripeness percentages graphically
on the dashboard of the web application. User models are
updated by a shared central model to enhance local accuracy.
However, each step adheres to the conceptual FL framework,
ensuring data privacy protection with no exchange of raw data.
FL-OPI was employed to test centralized transfer learning
accuracy, proving its efciency and superiority over conven-
tional methods in ripeness detection and quantifcation of oil
palm fruit bunches.
