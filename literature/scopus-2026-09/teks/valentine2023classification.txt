Classiﬁcation of Oil Palm Fresh Fruit
Bunches (FFB) Based on Its Maturity
Colour Using Convolutional Neural
Network (CNN) Approach
Stephenie Carmeline Valentine, Rayner Alfred, Florence Sia Fui,
Mohd Shamrie Sainin, and Zamhar Iswandono
Abstract It is very crucial to identify the maturity of Fresh Fruit Bunches (FFB)
of oil palm to ensure the appropriate timing is set for harvesting them and thus
maintaining a good quality of oil palm production. In Malaysia, Oil Palm Industry
is one of the biggest industries and has contributed a large amount of the country’s
income. Therefore, it is important to check the maturity/ripeness of oil palm fruits
before harvesting it. Currently, the grading of FFB based on its maturity is done
manually by human and sometimes human can make mistake in performing the
inspections. Hence, this paper proposes an automatic grading system of oil palm
FFB using a deep learning approach, speciﬁcally by using Convolutional Neural
Network (CNN). Most previously published works dealt with binary classiﬁcation
(Two classes classiﬁcation). However, this work investigates the effectiveness of
applying a deep learning approach to perform a multi-class classiﬁcation task. In
addition to that, not many works dealt with optimizing the deep learning model in
classifying oil palm fresh fruit bunches (FFB) based on its maturity colour. Thus, this
work will also evaluate the performance of the proposed CNN model with optimized
training parameters that includes the number of epoch and batch size. Based on the
results obtained, the number of epochs and the batch size have a great inﬂuence on
the performance of the CNN models. For instance, having a high number of epoch’s
value of 100 and bigger batch size’s value (e.g., 64), the CNN model can produce
higher performance in term of accuracy.

Introduction
Malaysia is one of the largest palm oil producers in the world. In 2020, Malaysia has
produced 19.14 million tonnes of palm oil [6]. Malaysia also produced and exported
41% of world production and 47% of world exports [5]. Hence, the production of
high-quality oil palm fruits is very important because most import countries of oil
palm fruit are very concerned about its quality [14]. The oil palm industry is also
one of the biggest contributors to the country’s economic development. Therefore,
the maturity identiﬁcation of Fresh Fruit Brunches (FFB) is very necessary in order
to produce high-quality oil palm fruit thus helping the country in increasing income
for this industry. However, there are some problems faced while trying to produce
good quality fruits. The main problem faced in the industry of palm oil production
is the accuracy in distinguishing or classifying oil palm fruits according to its matu-
rity [4]. Presently, the grading or classiﬁcation of FFB is done manually by qualiﬁed
graders (grading staffs of the mill or an agent appointed by the mill who has the
capability and experience in the grading of FFB) [12]. Basically, the grading will
be done in palm mills and graders need to follow the standard inspections given
by the authorities. Unfortunately, this method of classiﬁcation (manual grading) has
some limitations. One of the biggest limitations is the human’s ability to do exam-
inations [19]. Different human will have different grading classiﬁcation and they
can sometimes make mistakes too while examining. Disputes can occur between the
seller and the grader regarding the maturity and quality of the oil palm fruit as the
maturity of FFB will be assessed through its color. As human beings, we view things
differently and also similar to graders, they can also contradict with each other in
terms of color classiﬁcation of FFB.
Most previously published works are dealing with binary classiﬁcation (Two
classes classiﬁcation). Thus, this work will investigate the effectiveness of applying
a deep learning approach to perform the multi-class classiﬁcation task. In addition to
that, not many works are dealing with optimizing the deep learning model in classi-
fying oil palm fresh fruit bunches (FFB) based on its maturity. Thus, this work will
also investigate the effects of optimizing the parameters of the CNN models such as
the number of epoch and the batch size. The expected output of this work will be an
optimized prototype that can be used to perform the classiﬁcation of the maturity of
Fresh Fruit Bunches (FFB) of oil palm and thus overcome the limitations of manual
grading system. Section2 provides the detail of the literature review or related works.
Section3 presents the proposed approach, Sect.4 presents the results and discussion
and ﬁnally Sect.5 concludes this paper.

Related Works
Muchresearchonclassiﬁcationorgradingsystemhavebeendonesinceitisbecoming
a future trend because it has high potential for the study of new approaches [13].
There are several efforts done by some researchers to ensure the efﬁciency of the
grading or classiﬁcation of oil palm FFBs by using different approaches, such as
Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) [18]. The study aimed to classify FFBs into
two categories which are categorized into ripe and unripe bunches. They have trained
the deep CNN to classify 628 images and the result showed that the classiﬁcation
has 98% accuracy. A grading system of oil palm FFB ripeness has been developed
using supervised machine learnings that includes Artiﬁcial Neural Network (ANN),
K-Nearest Neighbours (KNN), and Support Vector Machine (SVM) [3]. Basically,
the processes involved are shown in Fig.1. The performance of CNN depends on
the number of training data and the number of layers used in the CNN model [9]. It
was suggested that using AlexNet, a pre-trained CNN was suitable for classiﬁcation
tasks where a huge amount of data was not available and also for tasks in which
higher processing time was not an issue. The CNN algorithm is considered as a
good approach for the FBBs grading system [18]. There are other related projects
that include the grading of maturity of oil palm fruits based on visible and NIR
Band [17], palm oil fresh fruit bunch ripeness grading identiﬁcation using colour
features with ANN approach [16], and evaluations of oil palm FFB maturity degree
using Multiband Spectrometer with ANN approach [20].
However, there are gaps based on the studies conducted by those researchers.
Some researchers used ANN model and others to grade FFBs while most used CNN
models. Based on the reviews, deep learning approach using CNN is the most suitable
method for classifying FFBs ripeness. But there are some enhancements that need to
be done to increase the efﬁciency of the grading system. For example, the research
by [2] has already achieved 98% accuracy but it only has two categories of ripeness.
Another class of ripeness can be added which is the overripe FFB. In other words,
this work deals with a multi-class classiﬁcation and focuses especially on optimizing
the training parameters such as number of epoch and batch size to improve the
classiﬁcation of the oil palm Fresh Fruit Bunches (FFB) based on its maturity colour
using a deep learning approach.
In this study, CNN will be used for the multi-class classiﬁcation task. A research
was done by Anupama Jawale and Ganesh Magar in 2019 to compare the image
classiﬁcation techniques which are Binary and Multiclass using CNN and SVM [10].
What they did was basically comparing CNN and SVM in terms of accuracy of
classiﬁcation images. Based on their ﬁndings, SVM is a better choice of algorithm
for binary image classiﬁcation with 80.95% accuracy while for CNN the accuracy
was only 75%. But for Multiple Class classiﬁcation, CNN shows higher accuracy
than SVM. Similarly, the CNN model is an effective and most suitable solution for
multi-class images classiﬁcation task where the Precision, Error, and Accuracy rates
are 90.81%, 4.25%, and 95.07% respectively [7].

In order to improve the classiﬁcation of FFBs based on its maturity colour using a
deep learning approach, optimizing the training parameters has to be done. Therefore,
a suitable optimizer should be chosen. There are several choices of optimizers such
as Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD), Momentum, RMSProp, Adam, AdaGrad,
and AdaDelta. The effectiveness of each optimizer depends on the CNN architec-
tures. Figure2 shows the CNN architectures over a timeline from 1998–2019 [1].
Adaptive Moment Estimation (Adam) optimizer is often used as the default opti-
mizer in machine learning. This optimizer has several advantages, including the fact
that it is simple to build and requires minimal tuning, that it is computationally efﬁ-
cient, memory efﬁcient, and has low memory needs, and that Adam performs well
in instances with huge datasets and high parameter values. The best combination of
CNN Architecture-Optimizer is the VGG16-Adam combination. Several optimizers
were used for VGG16 Architecture Network including NAG, AdaGrad, RMSProp,
Adam, Adamax, and Nadam [11]. The Adam optimizer was found to be the most
stable optimizer. It is because it has high results and low variance between learning
rates. Three optimizers were compared which were Adam, RMSProp, and SGD and
it was found that Adam was the most often used optimizer with the optimal learning
rate between 0.00005 and 0.0001 [15].

Datasets
In this work, images of FFBs with its actual ripeness level are acquired from Kag-
gle. The total images acquired for the dataset are 413 and the images are stored in
a folder named “Dataset” and they are separated into three sub-folders which are
“Training”, “Testing”, and “validation”. For each sub-folders there will be another
three folders named “Ripe”, “Unripe”, and “Overripe”. The name of the sub-folders
indicates the actual ripeness level of the FFB images. The dataset is retrieved using
the flow_from_directory method. The target size (128, 128), batch size (32),
classes(‘Overripe’,‘Ripe’,‘Unripe’)andclassmode‘categorical’aredeﬁnedrespec-
tively. The datasets are split into three sets which are training set, testing set, and
validation set.
For the image pre-processing, the FFN images are manually cropped to remove
unneededpartsoftheimagesuchasotherobjectssothatonlytheimageofFFBiskept.
Next, image data augmentation is used to expand the training, testing, and validation
dataset in order to improve the performance of the proposed deep learning model to
generalize. ImageDataGenerator class which is supported by the Keras library
will be used for image data augmentation. The augmentation techniques that are
used in this work includes shifting, rotations, zooming, and ﬂipping. This class also
provides a real-time data augmentation where it generates augmented images even
when the CNN model is still in the training stage. Figures3, 4 and 5 show the sample

Designing the Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)
Architecture
CNN has three main layers and every layer has its own function. These layers are
the convolution layer and pooling layer, which will deal with the feature extraction
while the fully connected layer is used for classiﬁcation task or predicting the output.
Hence, image of FFB will be used as the input and CNN model is expected to classify
the ripeness level of the FFB based on the given image.
Convolutional Layer Convolutional layer accepts an RGB picture or an output
from another layer. In order to produce a feature map, the incoming data is con-
verted into pixel values. This map represents low-level features such as edges and
curves. A sequence of extra layers of convolution may be used to ﬁnd higher level
unique characteristics. The image is modelled as a volume with the dimensions
Width×Height×Depth (Fig. 6).
1. The number of ﬁlters is a conﬁgurable parameter (k). More ﬁlters are required in
a more sophisticated model. To create an activation feature map, depth 3 convo-
lutional ﬁlters are dragged over the screen.

2. Depending on the application, the spatial scope of the ﬁlter (width and height)
can be changed. The most common ﬁlter sizes are 3 × 3, 5 × 5, and 7 × 7.
When a 3 × 3×3 ﬁlter is applied to a 32 × 32×3 picture, the resulting image
is 30 × 30×1. This is due to one pixel being deleted from the image’s left, right,
top, and bottom.
3. If S = 1, the kernel or ﬁlter window will be slid by one pixel by default. Addi-
tionally, the window may be moved by more than one pixel at a time. The stride
is deﬁned as the number of pixels slid. Because more sites are bypassed, larger
strides result in less overlap between the receptive ﬁelds and the smaller feature
map. As a result, the feature map is smaller than the input.
4. By injecting a number of zeros along the image’s border, padding is employed to
keep the dimension of the feature map the same as the input. As a result, it’s also
known as zero padding. The number of zeros added is determined by the size of
the convolution ﬁlter. If the ﬁlter size is set to 3, the number of zeros added will
be one, according to the formula.
Pooling Layer A down sampling layer is placed after the activation layer to lower the
spatial dimension while maintaining the depth constant. A 2 × 2 ﬁlter is frequently
used to the input in order to generate an output based on the pooling type. Maximum
or average pooling can be used, in which case the maximum or average value from
each ﬁlter-delimited subarea is used. There are no pooling options. The pooling layer
lowers the feature map’s length and breadth but not its depth. As a consequence, the
number of parameters and weights, as well as the training time and computing cost,
are decreased. In addition, overﬁtting is avoided. When a model achieves 100% or
99% accuracy on the training set but only 50% accuracy on the test data, this is
referred to as overﬁtting. Dropout layers, which remove a random set of activations
by changing the value to 0, can be used to circumvent this issue. Dropout is a pattern
generalisation function that discovers several representations of the same pattern.
Fully Connected Layer The Fully Connected layer identiﬁes extremely high-level
characteristics that are closely linked with a certain item or class. A fully connected
layer is fed a set of characteristics for categorizing photographs that do not take into
consideration the spatial structure of the images as input. Most of the models have
two totally connected layers. The output of the fully connected layer is a 1D vector

created by ﬂattening the preceding pooling layer’s output. Flattening is the technique
of converting a three-dimensional volume into a one-dimensional vector.
The initialization of the model can be made by specifying the model as a sequential
model and these layers are added.
1. 1×Convolution layer of 32 channel of 3 × 3 kernel
2. 1×Max pooling layer of 2 × 2 pool size
3. 1×Dropout layer of 0.25 value
4. 1×Convolution layer of 32 channel of 3 × 3 kernel
5. 1×Max pooling layer of 2 × 2 pool size
6. 1×Dropout layer of 0.25 value
7. 1×Convolution layer of 32 channel of 3 × 3 kernel
8. 1×Max pooling layer of 2 × 2 pool size
9. 1×Dropout layer of 0.25 value.
The ReLU (Rectiﬁed Linear Unit) is also added as the activation to each layer
to ensure that negative values are not passed to the next layer. After creating all the
convolution layer, ﬂatten layers are added to make the multidimensional input to
become a one-dimensional and also for the transition from the convolution layer to
the fully connected layer. Next, the dense layers are added as shown below:
1. 1×Dense layer of 64 layers, using ReLU as activation
2. 1×Dropout layer of 0.25 value
3. 1×Dense Softmax layer of 3 units.
The ReLU activation is used for the dense layer of 64 units and for the dense
layer that has the softmax activation, and only 2 units are used since there are only
3 classes to predict which are ripe, unripe, and overripe. The model is ﬁnally done
after the creation of the softmax layer. Next, the Adam Optimizer is used it is proven
to have the highest accuracy when applied with CNN model. The optimizer is also
deﬁned with the learning rate of 0.01. After that, the model will be complied using
model.compile() function. In the training process, the number of epochs is set
to 100 with 64 batch size. Figure7 outlines the coding used for creating the CNN
model. The Model.summary() function is used to summarize the model that has
just been created. In Fig.8, there are three layers of convolution layer, three layers
for max pooling layer, and one fully connected layer. Every layer has dropout layer
and the activation layer used are ReLU and Softmax in the last layer.
In this work, the CNN model parameters settings are varied in order to investigate
which setting produces the highest accuracy performance. Basically, the parameters
that are varied are the epochs (e.g., 25, 50, 75 and 100) and batch size (e.g., 32, 64)
while the other settings will be constant including:
• Height = 128
• Width = 128
• Learning rate = 0.01
• Adam optimizer
• CNN Layers

Conclusion
This work investigated the effectiveness of applying a deep learning approach to
perform a multi-class classiﬁcation task to classify the degree of ripeness for the
oil palm FBBs (e.g., overripe, ripe, unripe) based on its maturity. The number of
epochs and the batch size of the CNN model inﬂuenced the performance of the CNN
models. Having high number of epochs’ value of 100 and bigger batch size value
(e.g., 64), the CNN model can produce higher performance in term of accuracy. For
future works, there are several suggestions that can be made to ensure that the output
of this work can be further improved. These suggestions include using larger dataset
and also using a Cross Validation to help reducing overﬁtting of the model.
