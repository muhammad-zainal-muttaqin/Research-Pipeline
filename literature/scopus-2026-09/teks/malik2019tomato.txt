Abstract: Tomatoes are best known grown fruits in greenhouses. In order to decrease the high labor cost and increase the 
efficiency, recently many researches have tried to harvest tomatoes automatically. To help the harvesting robot picking the fruit 
precisely, the main challenges are to identify and localize red mature tomato fruit accurately. In this paper, we presented a 
robust and effective method for challenging task of segmentation and localization of on-plant tomatoes in the greenhouse, for 
automatic tomato harvesting purpose. A low-cost RGB-D Kinect V2 camera was used to accomplish the task. Color images and 
depth images of tomatoes on- plants in the greenhouse were captured in day time using Kinect V2 camera. Along with color 
and depth images, the corresponding color coordinates, depth coordinates, and camera coordinates were also saved. The 
proposed method was divided into two stages: fruit segmentation and fruit localization. At the first stage, an improved HSV 
algorithm and watershed algorithm were used to segment mature fruits from a complex background. An improved HSV 
algorithm was used to enhance the feature of red mature fruits. After that, Otsu method and morphological operations were used 
to segment mature fruits from the background and then watershed was implemented to further separate touched fruits. 
Subsequently, each detected fruit was separately labeled to calculate the pixel coordinates values in color image. At the second 
stage, a fusion of color and depth information was carried out on the basis of the mapping relationship between color, depth and 
camera coordinates, to map the obtained fruit pixel coordinates in color image with acquired 3D point cloud data. Then the 
corresponding point cloud data of each labeled fruit were generated. To remove the noise in the cloud data, a filtration process 
was performed, and the final point cloud was generated. To verify the proposed method, 18 pairs of images were experimented 
containing the different numbers of measured fruits with measuring distance, ranging from 0.58 to 1.50 m. The average fruit 
localization error rate was 1.2 cm for depth distance and 1.9 cm for ground distance. Final results showed that the proposed 
method has good performance and robustness for segmentation and localization and have good potential to be implemented on 
the tomato harvesting robot in the greenhouse. 
Keywords: segmentation; localization; RGB-D Kinect V2 camera; point cloud; HSV; watershed

Tomato is a very popular and vital fruit all around the 
world (Berova & Zlatev, 2000). Since 1995, China has

tomato accurately in a greenhouse using a harvesting 
robot is a challenging task because of the complexity of 
the background, the touched fruits, and also the difficulty 
of localization of the fruits. Therefore, to solve this issue, 
a segmentation and localization method is needed for 
tomato fruits.  
For fruit harvesting robot, it is difficult to harvest the 
fruit precisely in two-dimension (2D) environment. 2D 
depicts as length and width and object on an image, has 
plane graphics. The accuracy to correctly identify and 
localize each fruit can be increased by segmentation and 
localization using three-dimension (3D) information. 3D 
image contains another dimension: depth and known as a 
stereoscopic image. At present, to achieve better 3D 
localization, stereo vision technology (Ehlert, Heisig, & 
Adamek, 2010; Sui, Fisher, & Reddy, 2012; Yamamoto 
et al., 2014), laser scanning technology (Chang et al., 
2017; Sharma et al., 2016) and 3D digitizer technology 
(Andújar et al., 2016; Li et al., 2015) were used. Stereo 
vision technology has passive 3D depth estimation while 
Kinect has active 3D estimation and performs better for 
the indoor environment than stereo vision technology. 
And while comparing with laser scanning technology, 
Kinect can get more precise data and also it has a much 
lower cost. 
The Kinect V2 camera has a wide range to be used in 
many machine vision applications. Besides RGB images, 
it also provides depth information while generating the 
depth images (Andújar et al., 2016; Li et al., 2015). 
Moreover, Kinect provides 3D views at the relatively fast 
sampling rate. This aspect is essential for simultaneous 
localization and mapping (SLAM); used to build 
environment map in a real time environment (Kamarudin 
et al., 2014). Therefore, the Kinect camera has become a 
popular tool for fruit localization. Xia et al. (2015) used 
the Kinect V2 camera for segmentation of individual 
plant leaves in a greenhouse by using depth information. 
However, it still requires to improve the stability of 
segmentation by combining the color and depth images. 
Kusumam et al. (2016) proposed a 3D vision-based 
detection and localization method to pick up the broccoli 
heads in the field using Kinect V2 camera, and the results 
can be further improved by adding RGB images instead 
of working only with depth images. Wang, Walsh, & 
Verma (2017) used Kinect V2 camera to estimate the size 
of the mango fruit on the tree. The result, regarding fruit 
size, can be improved by using high-resolution depth 
camera. For indoor based robots, Jiang et al. (2013) used 
the Kinect sensor for multiclass fruit classification, and 
Random Forest classifier needs some improvement to 
localize each fruit in the field correctly. Wang & Li (2013) 
used the Kinect camera to estimate the diameter and 
volume of Vidalia sweet onions and volume can be 
improved further by using a color image with depth 
image instead of only depth image. Fu et al. (2014) 
researched automatic detection and segmentation of the 
stems of the potted tomato plant, the implementation of 
the proposed method was limited to only one greenhouse 
tomato plant, and also the plant was not put in an actual 
greenhouse environment. Li et al. (2015) used Kinect 
camera for the digitization and visualization of tomato 
plants in a greenhouse, and this method is limited to only 
four samples and needs to be implemented in a real 
greenhouse world. Martínez-Guanter et al. (2017) used 
3D Laser Imaging Detection and Ranging (LiDAR) 
sensor which is used to measure distance by illuminating 
laser light and Kinect camera for the optical sensing to 
determine the tomato plants spacing for precise 
agrochemical applications and three optical sensors were 
used, which made the system complicated and even then 
false detection occured. 
Further, for touched fruits segmentation, only relying 
on 2D image information is not enough and also not 
enough to detect the 3D pose of each fruit. A dense 
textured based segmentation, a color and elliptical-based 
model, and a simple color and smoothness filter based 
method were used for fruit detection in the past (Payne et 
al., 2014; Payne et al., 2013; Qureshi et al., 2016). The 
results can be improved by using a Kinect camera for less 
mature fruits. Yamamoto et al. (2014) proposed a method 
for on- plant touched fruits using image analysis and 
machine learning methods. However, to get better results 
for detection of intact tomatoes, it still needs 
improvement, and better results can be achieved by using 
red, green, blue plus depth (RGB-D) camera instead of 
RGB camera. RGB-D camera contains color camera 
besides with a depth sensor to get the depth information 
of the object. Nguyen et al. (2014) used an RGB-D based 

280   December, 2019                   International Agricultural Engineering Journal                      Vol. 28, No. 4 
camera (Xtion PRO Live, ASUS, Taipei, Taiwan) to 
detect the apples for harvesting robot, Kinect V2 camera 
can help to improve results to detect the high number of 
green apples and also with implementing a good color 
segmentation method. 
Based on the above overview, a conclusion can be 
drawn: because of the complexity of the greenhouse 
background and the touched fruit, fruit separation and 
localization are very challenging. No effective method 
has been proposed yet for tomato segmentation and 
localization in the complex greenhouse environment. Hue, 
Saturation, Value (HSV) a color-based segmentation and 
watershed algorithm was used to work in 2D in the 
previous paper (Malik et al., 2018). In the proposed 
method, HSV and watershed-based segmentation method 
were used to complete the segmentation process with 
improvements to work with 3D. 
This study aimed to put forward a robust and effective 
method for challenging task of segmentation and 
localization of -plant mature tomatoes, for automatic 
tomato harvesting purpose, based on RGB-D Kinect V2 
camera. Through data analysis, depth distance from a 
camera to fruit, ground distance from the projected 
camera to projected fruit and angle of a camera were 
precisely measured.

2.2.1. HSV algorithm 
Color feature is an important attribute to differentiate 
between fruit with respect to its background. Tomato 
changes its color from mature green, yellow and mature 
red. Therefore, the harvesting robot should be able to 
detect only red tomatoes. This purpose was achieved by 
using HSV algorithm. HSV is a color-based segmentation 
method in which hue is described as a color component 
with a range from 0 to 360°, for saturation, the amount of 
gray in color has range from 0 to 100°, and value 
describes the brightness of color component from 0 to 
100°.  
2.2.2. Watershed algorithm 
For harvesting robot, it is difficult to localize each 
connected fruit, and in result, robot will not be able to 
pick the right fruit. Such kind of issue was also faced at 
the sorting of rice, citrus, and wheat (Courtois, Faessel, & 
Bonazzi, 2010; Rosell-Polo et al., 2017). To overcome 
such problem, the watershed algorithm is a good 
segmentation technique because tomatoes are like circular 
objects and watershed is the best to use with such kind of 
objects (Faessel & Courtois, 2009). The watershed lines 
divide an image into catchment basins, associate with one 
minimum in the image. Watershed usually applied on the 
gradient function of the image. The gradient defines the 
transformation among regions, and it has high values at 
borders between objects and minima in the homogeneous 
regions (Tarabalka, Chanussot, & Benediktsson, 2010). 
Mostly, the result of watershed segmentation on the 
gradient image without any preprocessing will lead to a 
severe over segmentation. Over segmentation is a term 
used in watershed segmentation and produced when 
implemented on very complex image. It can be described 
as the partitioning of segments into many sub segments. It 
occurs due to the presence of large number of local 
minima and then forms its own catchment basin.  Matlab 
was used as a programming tool for segmentation 
purpose in this study.  
At first, the color segmentation method based on HSV 
algorithm was used to eliminate the background such as 
leaves, branches, green tomatoes, and other greenhouse 
spaces, as shown in Fig. 4. The threshold is a widely used 
procedure to segment the objects. In this proposed 
method the segmentation process of fruit was conducted 
on the base of threshold values which was given to Hue, 
Saturation, and Value separately. Threshold values for H, 
S, and V were selected on the basis of region of interest 
from 0 to 1. Otsu and structure methods were also 
implemented for the thresholding of objects to get good 
fruit 
size 
and 
remove 
the 
background. 
After 
implementing thresholding and morphological operations, 
the binary image of mature fruit was detected, and a 
complex background was removed. At the end, R, G and 
B channels were masked on binary image to form an 
RGB image of segmented red tomato. After completing 
the whole processing, only red tomato fruit was detected. 
 
Fig. 4.  Diagram of mature tomato detection. 
 
Watershed-based algorithm was then implemented to 
separate the touched fruit, as shown in Fig. 5. In this 
proposed method, the first grayscale conversion and 
gradient magnitude were implemented on the extracted 
red fruit and then applied the first watershed. At this stage 
result was over segmented. So, to overcome this kind of 
issue, the morphological marker was imposed and then 
implemented a second watershed. To get a good resultant 
image, firstly the structuring element was applied after 
the reconstruction operation of open and close was 
imposed. Then morphological erosion and dilation 
operation were implemented. Dilation operation made the 
fruit foreground bigger and large number of pixel values 
were generated, and the regional maximum of fruit was 
calculated. Furthermore, the result was superimposed on

the grayscale image. For the binary image, the Euclidean 
matrix was calculated. The distance transform was 
implemented to each pixel of a binary image. The rigid 
lines were generated and superimposed to separate each 
fruit. Finally, the label matrices were converted into true 
colors to display the label matrix as a color watershed 
image. In the end, a watershed image was generated.

3D localization of tomato fruit is another important

2.3.1. Generation of 3D point cloud data 
For the purpose of 3D localization, generation of 
point cloud data was needed. In order to generate, the 
software development kit provided by Microsoft Kinect 
for Windows SDK 2.0 and application programming 
interface (API) were used. API is defined as a set of 
programming 
instructions 
and 
tool 
for 
building 
application software’s such as graphical user interface 
(GUI). At the time of data capturing, along with color and 
depth images, the color image pixel coordinates, depth 
image pixel coordinates and camera coordinates were 
saved. The XYZ coordinate information related to the 
target object in a camera coordinate system was 
calculated according to the mapping relationship among 
these three coordinates system.  
After the HSV and watershed segmentation, the fruit 
area pixel coordinates values in RGB image were 
obtained for mature red tomatoes. The Bounding box and 
Region props techniques were used to label each fruit and 
to get the pixel column-row coordinates values. A 
mapping 
relationship 
based 
algorithm 
was 
then 
implemented among color, depth, and camera coordinates, 
to map with pixel column-row coordinates of separated 
fruits on RGB image. After that fruits'3D point cloud data 
were generated and stored as a *.txt file which contained 
X, Y, and Z under the camera coordinate system, R, G, B, 
and point cloud data corresponded to color image 
coordinates, Xcolor and Ycolor.  For this VS used a 
programming tool. 
For removal of noise, the filtration process was 
implemented on point cloud data, and then final 3D point 
cloud data for each fruit were obtained. The depth and 
ground distance values were calculated for each fruit. 
Matlab was used for filtration process. Generation of 
point cloud data also helped in error analysis, when point 
cloud of one fruit was divided into two or more parts and 
were compared with the measured data. Z-axis of Kinect 
V2 camera was along with the camera lens direction, Z 
value can be used for depth information calculation of the 
tomato fruit. After that, the system generated data were 
compared with greenhouse measured data of respected 
tomato fruits. Thus, fruit separation and localization were 
completed as is shown in Fig. 6.

2.5. Method for error analysis of fruit recognition and 
localization 
A specific method was used for error analysis of fruit 
recognition and localization in the greenhouse. To 
complete the analysis, a Kinect V2 camera was vertically 
projected to the fruit. One wire was dropped from the 
fruit center to the ground and the other was from the 
center of the camera to the ground and the ground 
distance was measured. The vertical distance of the 
surface projection line from the center of the tomato to 
the camera plane was measured by steel measuring tape 
and this distance was used as the true depth distance and 
angle of Kinect was also measured. In addition, this linear 
fit analysis was used to check the system accuracy.

4. Conclusions 
This paper has proposed an on- plant tomato fruit 
recognition and localization method, based on Kinect 
RGB-D V2 camera. The 3D localization of fruit can be 
obtained even under complicated greenhouse condition.  
(1) By using the segmentation method based on HSV 
color space, the background of the image was completely 
removed even with a lot of noises, and the mature red 
tomato fruit was detected.  
(2) By using the watershed algorithm, the fruits were 
separated correctly, and coordinates of each pixel for 

286   December, 2019                   International Agricultural Engineering Journal                      Vol. 28, No. 4 
every fruit were saved efficiently to map with the point 
cloud. 
(3) The final result showed that the use of Kinect 
RGB-D V2 Camera with this proposed method can 
generate the 3D point cloud data of tomato fruit, identify 
and locate each fruit effectively and efficiently. 
The proposed method has great potential to help the 
harvesting robot to effectively remove the background 
effect and make accurate and efficient separation and 
localization of red mature tomato.
