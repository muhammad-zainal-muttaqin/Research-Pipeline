Extraction of Fruit Thorns and Supervised Machine Learning 
Classifiers Using Image Processing Technique

Keywords: image processing technique, fruit thorns, Gabor, GLCM, BGLAM, ANN 
 
Abstract 
There is a processing need for a fast, easy and accurate classification system 
for oil palm fruit ripeness. Such a system will be invaluable to farmers and 
plantation managers who need to sell their oil palm fresh fruit bunch (FFB) for the 
mill as this will avoid disputes. In this paper the authors have investigated and 
presented the way of grading the oil palm FFB automatically by using the digital 
image processing techniques based on the Gabor, GLCM and BGLAM texture 
feature extraction and K nearest neighbourhood (KNN) and artificial Neural 
Network (ANN) supervised machine learning. The results show that the oil palm 
fruit bunch maturity inspection system using GLCM technique based on the ANN 
classifier with ROI 1 yields the best quality classification result at 91.3% compared 
with the systems of the BGLAM based ANN classifier with ROI 3 and Gabor based 
KNN classifier with ROI 1 which obtained results with AUC test accuracy up to of 
91.3 and 88.7%, respectively. 
 
INTRODUCTION 
There is a need for higher quality inspection of agricultural crops worldwide, 
especially for the medical and food industry. Since the traditional methods of the 
agricultural product’s quality assessment are manual, there is an issue of subjectivity and 
it is subject to disputes which can be tedious and costly. Fruit maturity is influenced by 
physiological factors which produces subjective and inconsistent evaluation results (Sun, 
2006). While current emphasis is on quality of agriculture product the world over, a fast, 
easy and accurate classification mechanism of agricultural crops is certainly an important 
task for high quality products (Alfatni et al., 2011). The inspection system in general 
utilizes improved engineering designs and image processing techniques to convey and 
grade the products (Alfatni et al., 2008; Leemans and Destain, 2004; Saeed et al., 2012). 
Furthermore, the external inspection stage is one in which a surface grading system is 
achieved through processing the sample’s external features and characteristics (Du and 
Sun, 2004; Malamasa et al., 2003; Njoroge et al., 2002). The external grading system is 
an integration of software as image processing techniques, as well as hardware which 
comprises a computer, CCD camera and data acquisition (Abdullah et al., 2004, 2005; 
Ghazali et al., 2008). 
In this work the authors have investigated and presented the ability of the image 
processing technique based on the BGLAM, BGLAM and Gabor thorns feature extraction 
and SVM, KNN and ANN machine learning techniques in order to get high accuracy FFB 
ripeness classification.  
 
MATERIALS AND METHODS

categories of fruits ripeness – under-ripe, ripe and overripe based on the variation in the 
thorns features during the oil palm FFB ripeness stages as illustrated in Figure 1.

Image Acquisition 
The images are collected under a controlled illumination system by using the oil 
palm grading system bunch housing material as illustrated in Figure 2. 
 
Image Segmentation 
The fruit image segmentation is used to segment the fruit image based on its 
original parts in order to implement specific image processing on return on investment 
region of interest (ROI) for the desired target. Accordingly, the segmentation technique 
permits partitioning off of images into homogeneous regions, which may have particular 
common attributes, such as gray level of the thorns. In this work, the color-based 
segmentation was done to segment the image from its background as illustrated in  
Figure 3.  
Texture is an important image feature that has been applied widely in the food 
industry for quality evaluation and inspection. The four different types of texture are 
statistical, structural, model-based, and transform-based (Zheng et al., 2006). Statistical 
texture is most widely used in agricultural crops because of its performance accuracy and 
short computation time. Transform-based textures are also widely used, but are not as 
popular as statistical textures or those compared to other texture feature types. 
 
Texture Feature Extraction Technologies 
Based on the statistical texture analysis, the texture feature can be classified into 
three different levels which are the first-order statistic such as variance which do not pixel 
neighbouring relationships. The second and higher-order statistic measures the 
relationship between groups of two neighbouring pixels in the original image. The third-
order statistic considers the relationships among three or more pixels, but these are not 
commonly implemented due to calculation time and interpretation difficulty. In order to 
analyse, extract and measure the external texture image features, three texture feature 
techniques were used, namely Gray Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM), Basic Gray 
Level Aura Matrix (BGLAM) and Gabor wavelet transform. These were applied on the 
texture value of the pixels in the matrix of the segmented image with the oil palm FFB 
types recognition system as shown in Figure 3.  
 
System Decision (Classification) 
Image classification, which ultimately leads to the identification of the FFB 
ripeness level, is the final image-processing step of the oil palm FFB grading system. In 
image classification, the effects of using GLCM, BGLAM and Gabor feature extraction 
techniques with three different supervised machine-learning techniques were evaluated. 
The three supervised machine-learning techniques, namely ANN and KNN, were used on 
the three different ROIs for each input image, including ROI 1 (300×300 image size), 
ROI 2 (50×50 image size), and ROI 3 (100×100 image size). The classification part was 
performed in two general stages, namely, training and testing. 
 
Artificial Neural Network (ANN) 
An artificial neural network with a feed-forward neural network model was used 
in the current study. Figure 4 shows the design of the ANN structures as input, hidden, 
and output layers (Unay, 2006). This section aims to perform multi-layer preceptor 
(MLP) models which then classify the oil palm FFB ripeness based on the statistical, 
histogram, Gabor, GLCM and BGLAM features. The MLP training stage, the MSE of the 
training process did not exceed 0.003. The higher efficiency score observed revealed the 
measurement for choosing the MLP model architecture of 20×14×1, 256×128×1 and 
300×100×1 at all the trials with GLCM, BGLAM and Gabor respectively, for the oil palm 
FFB ripeness grading system based on the texture of the thorns features.  
In addition, the normalization of the GLCM, BGLAM and Gabor features 
extracted from 180 samples of known ripeness classes of the oil palm FFB was used to 
fine-tune the system using the ANN Model. A trial-and-error experiment was conducted

to minimize the mean square error (MSE) between the targets and the output and the

the desired output was specified as 0.5, 0, and 1 for under-ripe, ripe, and overripe,

efficiency of the training. 
 
K-Nearest Neighbour (KNN) 
The KNN is based on the principle that the instances within a dataset generally 
exist in close proximity to other instances with similar properties. Moreover, the distance 
and the value of k play key roles in KNN, and the position vectors in a multidimensional 
feature space represent each object in the space (Kotsiantis, 2007). The steps for 
constructing k-nearest neighbor’s algorithm areas follows: 
- Determine the proper value of k, which is the number of nearest neighbors;  
- Calculate the distance between the query instance and all training samples using City-
block using the Equation 6; 
- Sort the distance and determine the nearest neighbors based on the kth minimum 
distance;  
- Gather the category of the nearest neighbors;  
- Use simple majority of the category of nearest neighbors as the prediction value of the 
query instance.  
In this study, the investigation shows that the proper metric distance that 
minimizes the distance between two similarly classified instances is City-block, as 
indicated in Equation 6 (Portegys, 1995). Figure 4 (Liu, 2007) shows an example of the 
KNN with k=6.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
The effectiveness of the GLCM, BGLAM and Gabor texture feature extraction 
method were trained and tested using three different supervised machine-learning 
classifiers, namely, ANN and KNN. The dataset used for this experiment consisted of  
180 images, including 60 for under-ripe, 60 for the ripe as well as 60 for the overripe set 
of images used for training, and 90 used for validation. Moreover, 270 images were used 
for system testing. The effectiveness of the three different feature extraction methods 
based on the three different image sizes, which respectively were ROIs 1, 2, and 3 with 
300×300, 50×50, and 100×100 input image sizes, were evaluated and compared with one 
another to improve the quality of the performance of the methods.  
Depicted in Figure 6 a, b and c are the ROC graphs of FFB ripeness classification 
performed by the ANN and KNN classifiers with the higher AUC score obtained at ROI 
1, ROI 2 and ROI 3 to recognize GLCM features FFB dataset using the texture of thorns 
model. The achieved results in Figure 6a indicate that the GLCM feature extraction-based 
texture model with ANN classifier is capable of performing perfect classification at ROI 1 
with AUC test accuracy up to 91.3%. Moreover, using the BGLAM texture features with 
the ANN classifier improves the performance rate at ROI 3 with AUC test accuracy of 
FFB ripeness up to 91.1%, as illustrated in Figure 6b. The results in Figure 6c indicate 
that the Gabor feature extraction-based texture of thorns model with the KNN classifier 
performed a proper classification rate at ROI 1 with AUC test accuracy up to 88.7%.  
Generally, the FFB ripeness classification performed by the oil palm grading 
system to recognize oil FFB ripeness classes (under-ripe, ripe and overripe) features using 
GLCM, BGLAM and Gabor representations showed good performance results (all cases 
AUC>88%) for FFB ripeness classification in the FFB texture of thorns model datasets. 
 
CONCLUSIONS 
In the current study, an algorithm for the oil palm fruit bunch maturity inspection 
system based on the GLCM, BGLAM and Gabor of thorns feature extraction algorithm 
and two different supervised machine learning classifiers, namely, ANN and KNN, as 
well as the image processing techniques for pre-processing and segmentation, were 
investigated and implemented for FFB ripeness classification. The FFB oil palm grading 
system was tested with different mask size ROIs, which indicated the different image 
sizes 300×300 (ROI 1), 50×50 (ROI 2), and 100×100 (ROI 3). These ROIs were used to 
achieve the accuracy of FFB ripeness, which was determined using the area under the 
ROC curve. The results of the area under the ROC curve for the two classifiers were 
compared with one another. The results show that the oil palm fruit bunch maturity 
inspection system using GLCM technique based on the ANN classifier with ROI 1 yields 
the best quality classification result at 91.3% compared with the systems of the BGLAM 
based ANN classifier with ROI 3 and Gabor based KNN classifier with ROI 1, which 
obtained results with AUC test accuracy up to of 91.3 and 88.7%, respectively.
