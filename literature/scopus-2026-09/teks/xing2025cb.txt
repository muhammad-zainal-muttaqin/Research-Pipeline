Abstract—To address the challenge of maturity monitoring 
and fruit quantity estimation in tomato yield prediction under 
greenhouse conditions, this paper proposes the CB-YOLO-
DeepSORT framework for continuous tracking and dynamic 
yield estimation. Built upon YOLOv5s, the framework 
incorporates the CBAM attention mechanism to enhance 
tomato maturity recognition in occluded environments and 
integrates the CIoU loss function to improve localization 
accuracy, ensuring precise ripeness mapping for reliable yield 
estimation. Then, the DeepSORT-based multi-object tracking 
algorithm is applied to continuously monitor tomatoes at 
different maturity stages, maintaining identity consistency to 
enhance the stability of real-time tomato yield estimation. 
Validation confirms the enhanced CB-YOLO attains 99.5% 
mAP@0.5 on test data, surpassing YOLOv5s by 2.5 
percentage points. The proposed system achieved MOTA and 
MOTP scores of 82.35% and 84.5% in medium-density 
scenarios, demonstrating robust tracking performance under 
moderate target congestion. Finally, a web-based monitoring 
system deploys autonomous robots with depth cameras to 
track tomato maturity in greenhouses. Real-time data is 
visualized through daily snapshots (pie charts and counts of 
ripe/unripe fruits) and weekly maturation trends (line charts 
and histograms), providing actionable insights to optimize 
harvest scheduling and resource allocation for precision 
agriculture.  
Key words: Tomato maturity recognition, Multi-object 
tracking, Yield estimation, Inspection platform 
 
I. INTRODUCTION 
Tomatoes, as globally significant economic crops, have 
been widely cultivated in greenhouses due to the 
environmental control advantages they offer. To achieve 
automation and precision management in agricultural 
production, growth monitoring, maturity assessment, and 
yield estimation are essential components for greenhouse 
tomatoes. However, current greenhouse tomato inspection 
primarily relies on manual labor, which suffers from 
inefficiency, high costs, and substantial subjective errors, 
failing to meet the demands of large-scale cultivation 
management [1]. In recent years, deep learning-based 
object detection algorithms such as YOLO have 
demonstrated promising performance in agricultural 
visual monitoring tasks. Nevertheless, the complex 
greenhouse environment imposes additional constraints 
on the robustness of visual algorithms, while persistent 
challenges remain in small target recognition, dynamic

Existing research has achieved notable progress in 
tomato fruit maturity recognition. Zhang et al. [2] 
developed a maturity detection method combining 
machine vision with tactile sensing technology using 
transfer learning strategies. Miao et al. [3] proposed an 
optimized object detection approach based on improved 
YOLOv7 to address issues of fruit occlusion and 
indistinct maturity feature differences. Hao et al. [4] 
introduced GSBF-YOLO, a lightweight detection 
model incorporating GSim, C3Ghost, and BiFPN

employing FocalEIoU Loss to enhance detection

the LOX-SE-GIoU model, which integrates SE 
attention modules and achieves a 92.17% mAP in 
distinguishing tomato fruits from stems. Despite these 
advancements in detecting tomatoes at various maturity 
stages, limitations persist regarding small target 
detection and maturity classification stability in 
complex greenhouse environments.  
Accurate counting of tomatoes at different maturity 
stages is crucial for intelligent yield estimation [6]. 
Zhang et al. [7] proposed a tomato counting method 
using ultra-deep masking and improved YOLOv8, 
enhancing counting accuracy through optimized 
convolutional structures and robust loss functions. An 
et al. [8] developed StraTracker, a multi-object tracking 
algorithm combining YOLOv8n detectors with feature

strawberry counting with an 8.7% error rate by

optimize fruit tracking and counting stability in dense

II. MATERIALS AND METHODS 
A.  Dataset Collection and Annotation 
Tomato fruit images were collected from the Jingpeng 
Smart Greenhouse at the Agricultural Machinery Research 
Institute in Tongzhou District, Beijing, and a greenhouse 
tomato plantation in Rongcheng, Weihai, Shandong 
Province. To enhance the diversity of the dataset, images 
were captured under various lighting conditions Fig. 1(a. b. 
c), different shooting angles Fig. 1 (d. e. f), and multiple 
illumination directions Fig. 1(g. h. i). Additionally, complex 
occlusion scenarios, including fruit occlusion, leaf occlusion, 
and stem occlusion, as shown in Fig1(j. k. l), were 
specifically targeted for data collection. 2116 high-quality 
images in sRGB format were collected, with resolutions of 
4928×3264 pixels and 4032×3024 pixels, respectively. A 
representative set of greenhouse tomato images captured 
under complex environmental conditions is shown in Figure

To achieve high-precision object detection, the 
LabelImg annotation tool was used to manually label the 
pixel regions of tomato fruits in the RGB images [11]. Based 
on maturity stages, the tomatoes were classified into two 
categories:ripe tomatoes(ripe_tomatoes), characterized by a 
fully red surface with a firm texture, and unripe 
tomatoes(unripe_tomatoes), which appear dark green and 
remain hard. Each tomato was labeled with a minimum 
enclosing bounding box to ensure accurate localization 
while minimizing background interference. Partially visible 
tomatoes were annotated only if at least 50% of their area

B.  Data Preprocessing and Image Augmentation 
To improve the model’s adaptability to complex 
environments and enhance dataset diversity, preprocessing 
and data augmentation were applied to the raw images. Due 
to the large image size and memory constraints, cropping, 
compression, and resizing were performed. Due to the large 
image size and memory constraints, cropping, compression, 
and resizing were applied [12]. The images were converted 
to JPEG format with a standardized resolution of 640×640 
pixels, balancing computational efficiency and adaptability 
to low-quality images in real-world greenhouse scenarios.  
In order to mitigate illumination variations, adaptive 
histogram equalization and random saturation adjustment 
(±5%) were applied to improve color consistency and 
enhance the contrast between fruits and the background. To 
accommodate variations in fruit growth postures, scaling, 
horizontal/vertical flipping, and ±90° rotations were 
performed, alleviating sample distribution imbalance. 
Additionally, Gaussian noise and 0.5-pixel Gaussian blur 
were introduced to mitigate overfitting, while multi-scale 
transformations were employed to improve the model’s 
robustness to varying object sizes and distances.  
After preprocessing and augmentation, the dataset was 
expanded to 4260 images, containing 14995 labeled 
instances, of which 9858 were “ripe_tomatoes” and 5137 
were “unripe_tomatoes.” The dataset was split into training 
(80%), testing (10%), and validation (10%) subsets, 
ensuring that the validation and test sets remained 
independent to maintain objective model evaluation. The 
dataset distribution across different maturity stages is

C. YOLOv5 Object Detection Model 
YOLOv5 [13] demonstrates the best inference speed and 
recognition performance within the YOLO series. 
Particularly, the YOLOv5s model, due to its smaller weight 
and fast inference speed, exhibits stable performance and is 
capable of meeting the real-time and accurate recognition 
requirements for tomatoes at different maturity stages [14]. 
Therefore, in this study, we select YOLOv5s as the base 
model for our experiments.

A. Enhanced Model with CBAM Attention Mechanism
To overcome the challenges posed by the high visual 
similarity between unripe green tomatoes and foliage 
backgrounds, as well as the loss of small-object features in 
greenhouse tomato images, this study proposes an improved
model based on the YOLOv5 architecture, referred to as CB-
YOLO. The CB-YOLO model integrates the Convolutional 
Block Attention Module (CBAM) and the Complete
Intersection over Union (CIoU) loss function to enhance
detection performance. The architecture of the improved 
YOLOv5 model is illustrated in Figure 2. In order to 
mitigate the loss of small-object features in complex
backgrounds, the CBAM dual-channel attention mechanism 
is embedded after the CSP1_X and CSP2_ 1 modules in the 
backbone network. The CBAM module performs adaptive
feature refinement through channel attention and spatial 
attention mechanisms.

B. Yield Estimation Model Based on CB-YOLO and 
DeepSORT
DeepSORT [17] is a multi-object tracking method 
based on the SORT algorithm. By introducing cascade 
matching and deep learning feature extraction, it optimizes
motion and appearance features, effectively reducing ID
switching issues for tomatoes in long-term occlusion
scenarios [18].

Considering the complex factors in greenhouse 
environments, such as wind influence, robotic arm collisions, 
and gravity-induced branch load changes, this paper 
proposes a tomato tracking and counting strategy combining 
CB-YOLO and DeepSORT, as shown in Figure 3. The 
strategy uses CB-YOLO to detect the number of tomatoes in 
each frame and applies DeepSORT for inter-frame tracking, 
ensuring consistent tomato identity and accurate counting, 
providing reliable data support for yield estimation. 
The proposed method consists of the following key steps:
1. Online Object Detection: A high-quality tomato image 
dataset is constructed by collecting and augmenting tomato 
images. The enhanced dataset is then used to train the 
improved CB-YOLO detection network to obtain the 
optimal detection weight model. 
2. Multi-Object Tracking: The improved CB-YOLO 
detection model is integrated with the DeepSORT tracking 
model to construct a multi-object tracking network. This 
network leverages the Kalman filter algorithm [19] and the 
Hungarian matching algorithm [20], significantly enhancing 
tomato fruit tracking performance in complex environments. 
3. Tomato Yield Estimation: The improved CB-YOLO 
and DeepSORT models are applied to detect and track fully 
ripened tomatoes in video streams, enabling accurate fruit 
counting for yield estimation. During tracking, each detected 
tomato is assigned a unique ID, starting from 1, ensuring ID 
consistency across consecutive video frames. Thus, the 
maximum ID value in the video provides an accurate 
estimate of the total tomato yield. 
C. Web-based Tomato Inspection Platform
An automated monitoring framework enables web-
controlled robots to conduct daily greenhouse inspections, 
transmitting real-time tomato maturity analytics and yield 
estimates to a centralized dashboard. By combining remote 
control with visual feedback (ripeness percentages, harvest-
ready quantities), the system reduces manual labor and 
improves harvest predictability through data-driven crop 
management. 
The system consists of three components: robot 
hardware, backend service module, and frontend interface. 
The robot hardware, based on ROS Noetic and Jetson Xavier, 
performs motion control and environmental perception, 
supporting synchronized localization and mapping for stable 
tomato detection, even in complex occlusion scenarios. The 
backend service, running on Ubuntu with the Python Flask 
framework, communicates with ROS via rosbridge and 
stores maturity and yield estimation data in an SQLite 
database. The frontend interface, built with HTML5 or 
JavaScript and roslibjs, provides an interactive control panel 
for robot control and displays tomato maturity and yield 
trends. 
The integration of the improved CB-YOLO algorithm 
with DeepSORT enhances tomato detection and tracking, 
ensuring precise fruit counting. The overall platform

V. CONCLUSION 
This paper addresses the challenges of tomato fruit 
detection and yield estimation in complex greenhouse 
environments by proposing an integrated CB-YOLO and 
DeepSORT algorithm. An inspection system was developed 
for tomato maturity classification, tracking, and yield 
estimation in greenhouse settings. The key findings are as 
follows: 
1)By embedding the CBAM attention mechanism into 
the YOLOv5s backbone network, the ability to distinguish 
between tomato fruits and the background was significantly 
improved. Coupled with the CIoU loss function, the issue of 
small object localization bias was resolved, achieving a 
mean average precision (mAP@0. 5) of 99.5%.  
2)The DeepSORT algorithm is employed, utilizing a 
dual matching strategy of appearance features and motion 
models to maintain ID consistency even after brief 
occlusions of the fruit. The MOTA reaches 87.5% in low-
density scenarios, effectively ensuring tracking continuity.  
3)The inspection platform, built on the ROS framework, 
successfully realized tomato maturity grading, real-time 
counting, and yield visualization. The practical applicability 
of the algorithm was validated in a real greenhouse 
environment, providing technical support for dynamic 
agricultural robot inspections.
