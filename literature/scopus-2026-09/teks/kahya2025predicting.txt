Predicting Grain Count and Weight of Grape Clusters by Image
Processing with Deep Learning
Erhan Kahya1

Abstract
This study aims to evaluate the effectiveness of deep learning techniques in predicting grape number and weight. Specif-
ically, grape detection and weight estimation were performed using machine vision processes with the object recognition
algorithm You Only Look Once Version 8 (YOLOv8). Tests performed with different model sizes (nano, small, medium,
large, xlarge) showed that the YOLOv8L model had the highest accuracy rate. The dataset was augmented with 120
photos taken in the ﬁeld and 540 images obtained from the internet, divided into 80% for training and 20% for testing.
The data augmentation techniques applied improved the generalization ability of the model and minimized the problem
of overﬁtting. The results obtained show that the YOLOv8L model performs better than other models on metrics such as
mAP50-95, precision and recall. In addition, the model developed for grape weight prediction has the potential to be used
in agriculture for object recognition and weight estimation. This study aims to support sustainable production processes
by providing data-driven decision-making mechanisms to overcome challenges in modern viticulture.

Introduction
This study aims to evaluate the effectiveness of deep learn-
ing techniques in estimating the number and weight of grape
seeds. In this ﬁeld, advanced algorithms such as Convolu-
tional Neural Networks (CNNs) and You Only Look Once
(YOLO) models are used to automate tasks related to grape
maturity assessment and grape detection, with the aim of
optimizing vineyard management and improving wine pro-
duction. The integration of these technologies facilitates
the non-destructive evaluation of grape quality and enables
real-time decision-making processes in agricultural appli-
cations (Yanling et al. 2023; Badeka et al. 2023; Huang
et al. 2024). In particular, deep learning has revolutionized
traditional methods by demonstrating superior performance
in analyzing complex data sets and enabling signiﬁcant ad-
vances in crop yield prediction. Studies have shown that

Deep learning provides a multi-layered processing capa-
bility that enables in-depth model inference from complex
data sets, making it an effective tool, especially for im-
age-based applications in agriculture (Yanling et al. 2023;
Badeka et al. 2023). In viticulture, deep learning models
such as CNNs are used to recognize and classify grape
varieties and assess the ripeness of grapes. These models
learn from labeled datasets and adjust their internal param-
eters through backpropagation, improving their accuracy
over time (Huang et al. 2024). The YOLO model has been
used to optimize the detection of grape ripeness. Impres-
sive accuracy levels with fast recognition times have been
achieved, facilitating the automation of robots in grape har-
vesting (Badeka et al. 2023; Khaki and Wang 2019). Ma-
chine learning techniques also play an important role in
grape recognition, which is a crucial element in predict-
ing grape yield. This process not only counts the grapes
but also evaluates the health and ripeness status of the
grapes to enable more effective yield prediction (Rajarshi
et al. 2024; Muruganantham et al. 2022). Recent studies
have shown that the transition to more automated methods
using image analysis and deep learning frameworks ad-
dresses the challenges associated with manual counting due
to the occlusion and dense distribution of grapes in the ﬁeld
(Badeka et al. 2023). Advances in computer vision technol-
ogy combined with deep learning have provided innova-
tive approaches to grape maturity assessment. Researchers
have developed systems that can non-destructively mea-
sure total soluble solids and titratable acidity, which are
indicators of grape ripeness, by combining color imaging
techniques with neural network regressors, signiﬁcantly im-
proving grape harvest decision mechanisms (Badeka et al.
2023; Meghraoui et al. 2024). Huang et al. (2024) inves-
tigated the implementation of an advanced Mask region-
based CNN (R-CNN) model speciﬁcally suited for grape
cluster detection and segmentation in orchards. In their re-
search, a carefully selected dataset of grape images was
used to develop a robust detection system capable of ef-
fectively identifying clusters. The results emphasized the
potential of deep learning techniques in agricultural ap-
plications and achieved high accuracy rates (Huang et al.
2024). Wang et al. (2024) investigated machine learning
algorithms for evaluating the quality of dried grapes by ex-
tracting image features. In this study, a holdout validation
strategy was used to evaluate the performance of the model.
A remarkable accuracy of 98.40% was achieved with the
LightGBM algorithm. This case demonstrates the effective-
ness of machine learning techniques in food quality assess-
ment and illustrates how these technologies can improve
the traditional labor-intensive processes. Additionally, en-
vironmental factors must be considered in predicting grape
bunch weight. In their research, Filho et al. (2020) found
signiﬁcant increases in bunch weight with increased irriga-
tion levels. Such environmental variables can be integrated
into deep learning models to enhance their accuracy. Zhang
et al. (2022) noted that deep learning-based models make
the detection of grape bunches more reliable under differ-
ent lighting conditions and from various angles. The impor-
tance of environmental factors was investigated by de Leão
et al. (2022), who studied the effects of different rootstock
varieties and irrigation levels on grape weight. According
to their results, the average bunch weight was 220g when
the ‘BRS Magna’ variety was grafted onto the rootstock
‘IAC 572’. Such effects of environmental factors should be
taken into account so that deep learning models can make
more accurate predictions. Deep learning techniques offer
a powerful and high-potential tool for grape weight pre-
diction. The combination of image analysis and machine
learning methods provides farmers with more effective de-
cision-making tools by increasing agricultural productivity.
It is expected that further research in this area will con-
tribute to the development of more accurate and reliable
prediction methods. Marani et al. (2020) segmented grapes
from images captured by general purpose cameras using
deep neural networks (DNN) and achieved high accuracy
rates with four pre-trained DNNs for grape recognition in
this study.

Materials and Methods
Material
Grapes are economically valuable fruits that are divided
into table, wine and wild varieties, each with unique proper-
ties and functions. This diversity reﬂects the many uses of
grapes in the kitchen and agriculture and at the same time il-
lustrates the challenges associated with their cultivation and
harvesting. Their ability to adapt to different climatic and
soil conditions makes grapes a globally preferred product,
important for both fresh consumption and wine production.
The ripening process of grapes is decisive for the time of
harvest and has a direct inﬂuence on the quality and ﬂavor
characteristics. Ripeness is generally assessed by key in-
dicators such as sugar accumulation and acidity. Vineyard
managers use various techniques to analyze these factors.
Environmental factors such as soil structure, climate and
weather also play a crucial role in the timing of harvest.
Climate change challenges this process and requires grow-
ers to update their methods to achieve the highest yield
and quality by adapting to changing conditions. Harvest-
ing methods have a signiﬁcant impact on the quality of the
grapes (Lorenzis et al. 2022). Although mechanical har-
vesting increases efﬁciency in large vineyards, it carries the
risk of damaging the fruit. In contrast, manual harvesting
allows for careful selection of ripe grapes, which improves
quality but requires a lot of labor. The decision as to which
harvesting method is preferable depends on the size of the
vineyard, the soil structure and the grape varieties grown.
Discussions about the sustainability and economic viability
of these two methods continue. Discussions about viticul-
ture and harvesting practices often revolve around the use
of labor, the environmental impact of agricultural practices
and the effects of climate change on traditional viticulture.
Despite these challenges, the wine industry continues to
develop innovative solutions that balance agricultural and
environmental efﬁciency and sustainability (Rogiers et al.
2022; Arias et al. 2022).
Method
In this study, different image processing techniques were
applied to determine the number of grapes and to calcu-
late the grape weight. First, pre-processing steps such as
resizing and normalization of the images were performed.
These steps are crucial for optimizing the performance of
the model. In particular, resizing the images to a speciﬁc
size speeds up the training process while increasing accu-
racy. Segmentation operations were performed to accurately
recognize the grape berries and clusters. Techniques such
as color-based segmentation and edge detection were used
to make the objects in the images more distinct. The choice
of these techniques is based on the ability to distinguish the
different colors and textures of the grape berries. Further-
more, features such as color histograms and edge features
obtained from the images contribute to the learning process
of the model and allow for more accurate predictions. The
grape images were taken with a Nikon D3100 camera with
a resolution of 1920× 1080. The images were in JPEG for-
mat. The images were saved in JPEG format (lossy) with
the maximum quality setting of the digital camera. In June
2024, a total of 120 photos were taken at a distance of 1.0m
from the vine. In addition, 540 images from various inter-
net sources were used. The images from the internet and
from the ﬁeld were divided in a ratio of 8:2. Of the images
from the Internet, 432 were used for the training set, while
72 were from the ﬁeld. For the test set, 108 images from
the internet and 18 photos from the ﬁeld were used. To
increase the diversity of the training set and prevent over-
ﬁtting, four different random extension combinations were
used. These enhancements include horizontal and vertical
ﬂipping, contrast adjustment, brightness adjustment and the
addition of Gaussian noise. To improve the generalization
ability of the model, various data augmentation methods
were applied. These methods include techniques such as
image rotation, mirroring, scaling and brightness adjust-
ment. Data augmentation minimizes the problem of overﬁt-
ting the model and provides more robust results under dif-
ferent conditions. Each data augmentation technique was
favored for speciﬁc scenarios, leading to diversity in the
training process of the model. The effectiveness of the data
augmentation methods was demonstrated by experimental
results. It was found that the applied data augmentation
strategies increased the accuracy of the model and reduced
the training time. In this context, the performance differ-
ences between the cases where data augmentation methods
were used and the cases where they were not used were
compared in terms of accuracy and loss metrics. The train-
ing set consists of 1730 images. Figure 1 shows the process
of data set augmentation.

YOLOv8 YOLOv8, known as “You Only Look Once Ver-
sion 8,” is the latest version of the widely recognized
YOLO framework for real-time object recognition and was
released on January 10, 2023. This algorithm was origi-
nally introduced by Joseph Redmon and his team in 2015
and has since been continuously developed with various
technical innovations (Redmon et al. 2016). Building on
the advances of its predecessors YOLOv5 and YOLOv7,
YOLOv8 maintains a lightweight model size while offering
improved accuracy and performance. The improvements
made in YOLOv5 and YOLOv7 have resulted in signiﬁ-
cant advances in accuracy and performance, while retaining
the lightweight structure. These features make it an ideal
model for use in edge devices. The architecture of YOLOv8
signiﬁcantly improves the efﬁciency and robustness of ob-
ject detection and localization through innovations such
as recognition mechanisms and advanced augmentation
methods (Sun et al. 2024). YOLOv8 offers lower latency
and faster processing times compared to previous versions,
making it an ideal solution for real-time object recognition
applications (Wang et al. 2023). Layers developed with
a deep network architecture improve the ability to recog-
nize objects with high accuracy, even in more complex
images (Lin et al. 2017). An improved YOLOv8 model for
the detection of diseases in greenhouse crops was designed
to help detect vegetable diseases more accurately (Wang
et al. (2024). An improved YOLOv8 algorithm for the de-
tection of diseases and pests was investigated. In particular,
improvements have been made in the extraction of low-
level features (e.g., edge texture) (Zhang et al. 2023). An
improved YOLOv8 algorithm has been applied to real-
time and precise segmentation of crown regions of fruit
trees in natural environments, addressing autonomous tree
detection and segmentation to optimize pesticide use and
minimize off-target spray release. This approach aims to
increase efﬁciency and reduce environmental impacts in
agricultural applications Khan et al. 2024). With its signif-
icant advances in object recognition, YOLOv8 enables its
users to achieve faster and more reliable results. Thanks
to its innovative design and features, it is becoming an
increasingly preferred model.

Discussion
Agricultural production plays a crucial role in food security
and economic sustainability worldwide. Grape production
in particular represents a large market for both fresh con-
sumption and the wine industry. The productivity of grape
bunches is a crucial element that has a direct impact on
farmers’ income. Therefore, accurate prediction of the num-
ber of grape grains and grape weight enables optimization
of agricultural management strategies. In recent years, ar-
tiﬁcial intelligence and deep learning methods have led to
signiﬁcant changes in the agricultural sector. These tech-
nologies enable the automation and optimization of agri-
cultural processes thanks to the ability to analyze large data
sets. In particular, object recognition algorithms offer an
effective solution for the automatic identiﬁcation and eval-
uation of agricultural products. Deep learning-based ob-
ject recognition systems such as YOLO are attracting at-
tention with their real-time image processing capabilities
and increasing the effectiveness of applications in this area.
Various deep learning architectures are used to overcome
the challenges of grape group segmentation and ripeness
detection. The integration of deep learning technologies
into vineyard management represents a signiﬁcant departure
from traditional, labor-intensive methods, as it enables au-
tomation and greater accuracy in yield prediction and grape
maturity assessment. The use of deep learning in this area is
driven by the ability to analyze large image datasets to make
more informed decisions about grape harvest timing and
crop management. Notable advances include the use of at-
tention mechanisms such as the Convolutional Block Atten-
tion Module (CBAM) and the Residual Attention Network

Research shows that these models can associate morpholog-

vineyard practises and potentially improving wine quality.
Deep learning-based grape weight prediction uses advanced
machine learning techniques, in particular CNNs, to accu-
rately predict the weight of grape clusters. This application
is an important aspect of precision viticulture, which aims
to improve agricultural efﬁciency and productivity through
the use of data-driven insights. In particular, models such
as YOLO v3, Mask R-CNN and U-Net have been used for
grape cluster segmentation, and the modiﬁed U-Net model
has shown improved accuracy in classifying pixels as part
of or outside of grape clusters (Shook et al. 2021). Olen-
skyj et al. (2022) used deep learning approaches to estimate
grape yield directly from ground-based image data and gen-
erated a large dataset in a commercial vineyard with me-
chanical management. The research shows that the appli-
cation of object detection, CNN regression and transforma-
tion models provides promising results. Shen et al. (2023)
developed a computer model that automates the tracking
and counting of grapes in real time, optimized with the
YOLOv5s algorithm, with 84.9% accuracy and 50.4 frames
per second. Chen et al. (2024) emphasized the efﬁciency of
the developed YOLOv8-GP model and the experimental re-
sults provided an accuracy of 89.7%. Sneha et al. (2024)
developed a system that provides farmers with real-time
estimation of the harvest weight of grapes. Aguiar et al.
(2021) conducted a study to identify grape clusters using
deep learning and achieved an accuracy of 66.96% with
a new dataset. Pinheiro et al. (2023) pointed out that pro-
ductivity can be increased by classifying the health status
of grape clusters using YOLO models, while Aristizabal
et al. (2024) emphasized the importance of yield prediction
for strategic decision-making processes in viticulture and
discussed the potential of deep learning to increase accu-
racy. Coviello et al. (2020) presented a cost-effective and
fast yield estimation using the Grape Bunch Counting Net
(GBCNet) tool. Du and Liu (2023) developed a new method
for urgent fruit counting with higher accuracy. Amemiya
et al. (2022) developed a colour prediction-based support
system for determining the harvest time of grapes. Overall,
these studies show that deep learning methods have the po-
tential to signiﬁcantly improve yield prediction and harvest
management in viticulture. Lopes and Cadima (2021) fo-
cus on the use of the YOLO object detection algorithm for
real-time grape cluster detection. The experiments investi-
gated the challenges of discriminating grape berries against
a leafy background in white grape varieties. The YOLOv5x
and YOLOv4 models achieved successful results with F1-
scores of 0.76 and 0.77 at speeds of 31 and 32 FPS, respec-
tively. The YOLOv4-TINY model showed the best balance

Conclusion
In this study, the data processing process using the ob-
ject detection algorithm You Only Look Once Version 8
(YOLOv8) was investigated to determine the number of
grapes in the grapes and calculate the weight of the grapes.
The tests carried out with different YOLOv8 models (nano,
small, medium, large, xlarge) showed that the YOLOv8L
(large) model has the highest accuracy compared to the
other models. By applying various data augmentation tech-
niques and model improvements, the generalization ability
of the model was improved and the problem of overﬁt-
ting was minimized. The results of the study show that the
YOLOv8L model showed high performance in grape berry
detection and performed better than other models on met-
rics such as mAP50-95, precision and recall.
The richness of the dataset was ensured with 120 photos
taken in the ﬁeld and 540 images obtained from the in-
ternet. The dataset was split 80% for training and 20% for
testing, and four different random data augmentation strate-
gies were applied. These strategies ensured that the model
produced more successful results under different lighting,
directional and exposure conditions. The accuracy of the
system’s weight prediction was also tested. First, the weight
of the grape was assumed to be a ﬁxed value of 3.2g and the
results were tested on 20 samples. The data obtained was
used to create a new model for weight calculation. New
measurement coefﬁcients based on the grape weight were
determined using SPSS. The formula “Y= –3.213+ 2.273×
computer_bunch” was found. Using this coefﬁcient and the
model formula, the weight calculation was compared with
the weights of the 20 samples used in the trials and a high
success rate in weight prediction was found. It was assumed
that this formula and the coefﬁcients can be used for object
recognition and weight prediction in agricultural applica-
tions and may also be suitable for the recognition of other
fruit varieties and various agricultural products in the future.
