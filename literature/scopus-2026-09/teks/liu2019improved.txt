ABSTRACT. This study presents a novel approach for kiwifruit detection using deep convolutional neural networks (CNN). 
It aims to build an accurate, fast and reliable fruit detection system, which is a vital element for fruit yield estimation and 
automated harvesting. Recent work in deep neural networks has led to the development of a state-of-the-art object detector 
termed Faster Region-based CNN (Faster R-CNN). A common Faster R-CNN model VGG16 is adopt through transfer 
learning, for the task of kiwifruit detection using imagery obtained from two modalities: RGB (red, green, blue) color and 
Near-Infrared (NIR) images. Kinect v2 is used to take NIR image and its aligned RGB image from bottom view of the kiwifruit 
canopy. NIR image (1 channels) and RGB image are aligned and arranged side by side into a 6-channel data. The input 
layer of the VGG16 was modified to receive 6-channels data. Two different fusion methods were used to extract features: 
Image-Fusion (fusion of the RGB and NIR images on input layer) and Feature-Fusion (fusion of feature maps of two VGG16 
models where the RGB and NIR images were input respectively). The modified models were trained end-to-end by using 
back-propagation and stochastic gradient descent techniques and compared to original VGG16 models with RGB and NIR 
image input. Results showed that the average precision (AP) of the original VGG16 with RGB and NIR image input is 88.4% 
and 89.2% respectively, the 6-channel VGG16 using the Feature-Fusion method reached 90.5%, while that using Image-
Fusion method reached the highest AP of 90.7%. The results indicated that the proposed kiwifruit detection approach shows 
a promise for better fruit detection.  
Keywords. Multi-modal faster R-CNN; deep convolutional neural network; kiwifruit detection; VGG16; RGB-D 
Introduction 
China is the largest country producing kiwifruits worldwide, with a yield of 2,390,287 tons in 2016 from a cultivated area 
of 197,048 ha (UN FAO, 2018). Within China, Shaanxi Province has the largest production, accounting for approximately 
70% and 33% of the Chinese and global productions, respectively (Hu et al., 2017). Harvesting kiwifruits in this region 
relies mainly on manual picking which is labor-intensive (Fu et al., 2015). Therefore, the introduction of robotic harvesting

Kiwifruits are commercially grown on sturdy support structures, such as T-bar and pergola systems (Huang & Ferguson, 
2001). T-bar trellis is common in China because of its low cost. It consists of a 1.7 m high post and an approximately 1.7 m 
wide cross arm, which may vary slightly in width according to the shape and size of the orchard. Wires run on the top of the 
cross arms connecting them to each other in the middle and on both edges of the cross arms. The upper stems of the kiwifruit 
are tied to the top wires so that the egg-sized kiwifruits would be hanging downward, which makes them visible and 
accessible for manual picking (Mu et al., 2018). This canopy structure also provides relatively simpler and structured 
workspace for mechanized or automated field operations such as robotic picking compared to other fruit trees such as apples. 
It is highly desirable to investigate robotic harvesting systems for this crop due to a large labor force required in the 
manual picking operation. Fast and effective detection of kiwifruit in the field under natural environment is the foundational 
step for robotic harvesting (Fu et al., 2017). Cui et al. (2013) used Otsu threshold in 0.9R-G component to segment side-
viewed kiwifruit images, and detected each fruit using Canny operator and elliptical Hough transformation, which reached 
89.1% detection rate. Mu et al. (2014) employed L*a*b* color space to extract side-viewed kiwifruits fruit edge with Canny 
operator and elliptical Hough transformation for fruit recognition, which achieved 88.5% accuracy rate but spent 3.976 s for 
each fruit. Fu et al. (2015) segmented bottom-viewed kiwifruit images using Otsu threshold in 1.1R-G color component and 
detected each fruit using minimal bounding rectangle and elliptical Hough transform, which achieved 88.3% correct 
detection but spent 1.51 s on each fruit. Fu et al. (2017) developed a kiwifruit detection system at night using artificial 
lighting by identifying the fruit calyx, which reached a success rate of 94.3% and took 0.5 s in average to recognize a fruit. 
These studies primarily used color and shape of calyx and fruit to detect the kiwifruit, which was limited to detect fruit in a 
single cluster with few fruit and were less effective for multi clusters in the field. To overcome these limitations, a well 
generalized model that is invariant and robust to brightness and viewpoint changes and highly discriminative feature 
representations is desired. 
Recently, deep learning has made considerable progress in object detection and classification. In particular, convolutional 
neural networks (CNN) showed superior performance in target detection tasks. There have been some researches using 
different CNN architectures for fruit detection, such as apple, mango and kiwifruit. Bargoti et al. (2017) applied VGG16 to 
detect mangoes and apples in orchards, which achieved an F1-score more than 0.9. Häni et al. (2018) employed U-Net and 
ResNet 50 for apple detection and yield estimation which can achieve 97.83% accuracy. Fu et al. (2018) employed LeNet 
to detect kiwifruits in orchard, which reached 89.29% detection rate and cost 0.27 s in average to recognize a fruit. Fu et al. 
(2018) presented the kiwifruit image detection using ZFNet, which achieved 92.3% detection rate and cost 0.50×10-2 s in 
average to detect a fruit. These studies showed good promising for fruit detection in RGB images using CNN.  
However, those studies only used common RGB images as input data for the CNNs. In uncontrolled field conditions, a 
single sensor modality can rarely provide the needed information to detect the target fruits under a wide range of variations 
in illumination, partial occlusions and different appearances. This makes a great potential to use multi-modal sensors because 
varying types of sensors can provide complementary information regarding different aspects of the fruits. Sa et al. (2016) 
proposed early fusion and late fusion methods of RGB and NIR images based on VGG16 to detect rockmelon, strawberry, 
apple, avocado, mango, orange, and sweet pepper, of which the F1-score reached 0.838. Zhan et al. (2011) employed RGB 
and NIR fusion algorithm to distinguish the chestnut quality based on BP network, of which the discriminating rate is 
improved by 3.75% and 6.25%, respectively, compared to using NIR and RGB alone. Abdelsalam et al. (2017) extracted 
seven color components from RGB and NIR citrus images and a voting process algorithm is applied to detect citrus defects, 
of which the accuracy is more than 95%. These studies achieved higher accuracy after using multi-modal information fusion. 
However, these studies do not take into account the impact of information fusion on the operation speed in the actual 
mechanical picking process. and when CNN network was used, the two fused images (RGB and NIR) are not aligned. 
Therefore, in order to enable the CNN network to learn useful kiwifruit features from multi-source information, two novel 
methods of detecting kiwifruits on aligned RGB and NIR images from Microsoft’s Kinect v2 (Microsoft, Redmond, WA, 
USA) are introduced in the paper. The two different fusion methods based on VGG16 are used to extract features of kiwifruit 
images: Image-Fusion and Feature-Fusion. For achieve better kiwifruit detection performance, the Image-Fusion and 
Feature-Fusion models are initialized by the parameter of RGB and NIR models trained firstly, and the SGD (stochastic 
gradient descent) was used to update the initialized parameters per iteration. The proposed methods using RGB and NIR 
images aims to achieve higher detection rates of kiwifruit by information fusion, thus provide a theoretical basis for 
achieving more accurate and faster automated picking of kiwifruit.  
Materials and methods 
Image Acquisition 
All the data were captured in late October during the harvesting season on the most common cultivar ‘Hayward’ at 
Meixian Kiwifruit Experimental Station (34°07'39''N, 107°59'50''E, and 648 m in altitude) at the Northwest A&F University.  
The data acquisition equipment consisted of a RGB-D camera (Kinect v2) mounted on a mobile tripod at height of 1.5 m

and a NIR image for the same scene. The captures were carried out during the all day, and artificial lighting is needed when

Image Preparation  
The data included a 3D point cloud (with RGB and depth information) and the corresponding NIR image, which is 
captured from the same scene based on the perspective of depth sensor. A pre-processing was carried out to align these 
corresponding RGB extracted from 3D point cloud and NIR images as input data of the convolutional neural network. Data 
preparation included 2D projection of the 3D point cloud, RGB image flipping and aligning NIR to RGB image. Fig. 1 
illustrates a flowchart of the data preparation steps. 
 
Figure 1 Flow chart to prepare the 6-channels kiwifruit image dataset  
In total, the image dataset consisted of 500 6-channel images with 424×512 pixel resolution. Each image included 
around 30 to 50 target kiwifruits. Since the NIR images are aligned with RGB images after pre-processing, ground truth 
kiwifruit locations were manually annotated in RGB images using the rectangular annotations, labeling a total of 19,839 
kiwifruits in all the dataset. The labelled dataset of 6-channel images for kiwifruit was divided into training, and testing 
groups. 80% kiwifruit images are randomly selected from the dataset as a training set, and the remaining kiwifruit images 
as a test set. The training images were randomly obtained from the independent and uniform sampling of the whole 
dataset. The training images and test images were mutually exclusive, which ensured the reliability of the later evaluation 
standards. 
Fruit Detection Using Faster R-CNN 
The Faster R-CNN architecture merges region proposals and object classification and localization into one unified 
deep object detection network. Two networks (RPN and Faster R-CNN) are concatenated as one network that can be 
trained and tested through an end-to-end process (Bargoti & Underwood, 2017). In this paper, a state-of-art Faster R-
CNN model VGG16 (Simonyan & Zisserman, 2014) was employed for kiwifruit detection. VGG16 model is the second 
place in the 2014 ILSVRC (ImageNet Large Scale Visual Recognition Challenge competition), however it performed 
better than the first place (GoogleNet) in multiple migration learning tasks (Deng et al., 2009). The convolutional layers 
of original VGG16 model are pre-trained with ImageNet dataset and fine-tuned with our training dataset.

Multi-Modal Fusion 
Here, two specific VGG16 models can be denoted as Image-Fusion and Feature-Fusion that are used to combine the RGB 
and NIR images. Since the original implementation of VGG16 uses RGB color images, the input layer of 3-channel image 
was modified to work with 6-channel image to meet the requirement of multi-modal input. Image-Fusion alters the structure 
of the first convolution layer of the VGG16 network so that 6 channels, rather than 3, are provided. Feature-Fusion method 
feed RGB and NIR images to two VGG16 models respectively and then fuse on the feature map. 
Image-Fusion 
Image-Fusion alters the structure of the input layer of the VGG16 model so that the input data layer has 6 channels (3 
channels from RGB and 3 channels from NIR). The VGG16 network is modiﬁed and adapted to receive RGB and NIR 
information simultaneously. An overview of this is provided in Fig. 3(a). To achieve this, we average the R, G, B responses 
from the first convolutional parameter of RGB model trained firstly and initialize the extra 3 channels (the first convolutional 
layer of Image-Fusion has 6 channels). 
Feature-Fusion 
Feature-Fusion alters the structure of the input layer so that RGB and NIR images are fed into two VGG16 models and 
combines the feature map after VGG16 models. To achieve this, a Concat Layer is added to concatenate feature maps of 
RGB and NIR images after convolution, as shown in Fig. 3(b). The VGG16 models are initialized by the parameter of RGB 
and NIR models and then ﬁne-tuned as original VGG16 model.

Network Training  
The training platform included a desktop computer with Intel Xeon E5-1650 (3.60 GHz) six-core CPU, and a GPU of 
NVidia TITAN Xp 6GB GPU (3840 CUDA cores) and 16 GB of memory, running on a Windows 7 64 bit system. The 
software tools used included CUDA 8.1, CUDNN 7.5, python 2.7 and Microsoft Visual Studio 12.0. The experiments were 
implemented in the Caffe framework (Jia et al., 2014). 
To train the deep learning networks, two sets of information were required including the images to train with, and a 
corresponding label for each image. The SGD was used to train the four different models and the momentum of the network 
was set to a fixed value of 0.9 and a weight decay of 0.0005. An optimal learning rate was employed to help neural network 
to be quick enough to be trained with significant features. In this work, the learning rate of 0.001 was reduced by changing

it to 10% of the current rate per 10, 000 iterations. It took about 12 hours to perform 100,000 iterations over the training set.  
During training, firstly, RGB and NIR models are initialized by the parameters of ImageNet model and updated 
parameters by training our RGB and NIR training dataset respectively. And then, the parameters of two VGG16 models in 
Feature-Fusion model are respectively initialized by the parameters of RGB and NIR models trained before, and trained by 
our six-channel dataset. Since the network structure of Image-Fusion is similar to that of RGB (the first convolution layer 
of Image-Fusion has 3 more channels), the parameters of Image-Fusion are initialized by RGB model trained, and the extra 
3 channels of first convolution layer are initialized by averaging the R, G, B channels. Finally, the SGD was used to update 
the initialized parameters of fusion models and train by the way of training the original VGG16 model. The method of 
training on the trained model aims to accelerate model convergence and achieve better detection performance by further 
iterations.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
Evaluation of different modalities 
The detection results of the modalities were evaluated in terms of AP and number of inferred images per second 
(processing on a Titan X GPU), as shown in Table 2, and the P-R curve of the modalities were shown in Fig. 4. As can be 
seen in Fig. 4, the P of single modalities are lower than the P of fusion modalities under the same R condition. Comparing 
detection results using single modalities, NIR modality gave the best performance with a AP of 89.2%, followed by the RGB 
modality with a AP of 88.4%, as shown in Table 2. Although NIR images have never been directly used for kiwifruit 
detection, the results using this modality are superior with that of RGB modality. Better results were obtained when 
combining the RGB and NIR modalities on the convolution layer (Feature-Fusion), achieving an AP of 90.5%. The Image-
Fusion has roughly equivalent performance as the Feature-Fusion, however, the Feature-Fusion has a more complex network 
structure, it needs more time to identify an image. Overall, from the Fig. 4, we can see that the evaluations of information 
fusion modalities were higher than that of single modality, which means the information fusion modalities have better 
performance in kiwifruit recognition than single modalities.

Under the different environment conditions, the overall detection rate of Image-Fusion modality was 91.7%, which was 
higher than other four algorithms (RGB, NIR, Feature-Fusion; Fu et al. 2018a), a little lower than that of Fu et al 2018b 
(92.3%). For different types of kiwifruit, the detection rates of Image-Fusion modality are higher than those of single 
modality which confirm the improvement of information fusion in detection rate for complex condition of kiwifruit. 
Compared with Fu et al 2018b, the occlusion and overlap kiwifruit detection rate of Image-Fusion modality is higher and 
the adjacency kiwifruit is a little lower. In addition, the detection speed is also an important factor for realizing automatic 
picking of kiwifruit. Since the images taken by Kinect v2 is 512×424 pixel, the resolution is lower than that of images taken 
by Cannon s110. The one of advantage of low resolution is that the detection speed is much more faster, which is 0.36×10-2 
(s/fruit), compare with Fu et al. 2018a and Fu et al. 2018b. The detection speed of Image-Fusion modality is comparable 
with that of single modalities. This is because the addition of channels only affects the number of operations on the first 
layer, which is insignificant with respect to the whole network. Furthermore, the use of Kinect v2 has the advantage of 
inferring straightly to 3D location of each detection although detecting fruits in 2D images. These provide the possibility to 
achieve kiwifruit positioning directly from 2D images. Overall, compare with other modalities, Image-Fusion has state-of-
art performance and fast detection speed, which provides a theoretical basis for more efficient and accurate detection of 
kiwifruit. 
CONCLUSIONS 
According to characteristic of kiwifruit images in in field, two different improved kiwifruit detection algorithms based 
on VGG16 with RGB and NIR information fusion is proposed. VGG16 is modiﬁed and adapted to receive RGB and NIR 
information simultaneously in Image-Fusion modality. The input RGB and NIR images are fed to two VGG16 models and 
combines the feature map in Feature-Fusion modality. The 6-channel image datasets provided by Microsoft’s Kinect v2. 
were captured at different time and lighting conditions. The performance of Image-Fusion and Feature-Fusion modality was 
compared with the single modalities (RGB and NIR), and reference algorithms (Fu, Feng, Elkamil, et al., 2018; Fu, Feng, 
Majeed, et al., 2018). The AP, detection speed and detection rates of different kiwifruit types were used to evaluate the 
performance of those modalities. The detection rate of Image-Fusion modality is significant improved in adjacent fruit, 
compared with the single modalities. Furthermore, AP (91.05%) of the Image-Fusion modality was higher than the other 
modalities, and it maintains the fast detection speed of single fruit (0.36×10-2 s/fruit). Overall, the results demonstrated that 
the improved algorithm of Image-Fusion has better detection accuracy and faster recognition speed. Besides, Kinect v2, 
used in the paper, can infer the position in 3D space directly from the 2D image. All these have laid a theoretical foundation 
for realizing automatic picking of kiwifruit.
