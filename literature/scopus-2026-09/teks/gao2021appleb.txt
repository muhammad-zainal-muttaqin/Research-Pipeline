ABSTRACT. Accurate estimation of apple yield is important for producers to make informed decisions when managing their 
orchards and be competitive when selling their products. Yield estimation is a key for predicting stock volumes, allocating 
needed labor, and planning harvesting operations. Manual visual yield estimation has traditionally been employed, resulting 
in inaccurate and misleading information. Although some studies have developed related algorithms based on machine 
vision to count fruits in tree images, they are difficult to apply in large orchards. The main goal of this study is to develop 
an automated video processing methodology to detect and count the apple fruits in modern vertical fruiting-wall architecture 
using deep learning techniques and object tracking. Existing researches using tracking technology to count fruits based on 
videos have all put the tracking target on the tracking fruit target itself. However, the small size and large number of video 
fruit targets have brought great obstacles to the realization of tracking technology. Therefore, according to the fact that all 
relatively stationary targets in the video have the same displacement trajectory, this study select tree trunk as the tracking 
target to obtain the displacement trajectories of all detected fruits. A detection model of apples and tree trunks was trained 
used YOLOv4-tiny network, which integrated with the tracking algorithm to develop a counting algorithm for video fruits. 
Combining the detected position information of the apple and the trunk with the displacement track obtained by tracking 
can establish the connection between the fruits in the video frame, thereby realizing the counting of fruits. The developed 
counting method was used to detect and count the images and videos in testing dataset, respectively. An AP of 99.35% for 
apple and trunk detection was obtained in this study, and over 90% apples were counted correctly using proposed method 
compared with manual counting results. These promising results demonstrate the potential of the present method to provide 
yield estimates for apple fruits or even other types of fruit. 
Keywords. yield estimation; orchard video; object tracking; fruit position; reference displacement; fruit ID

Introduction 
Knowledge of tree fruit crop yield such as apples can guide agronomic treatments, harvest scheduling making, and labor 
resource allocation. Yield estimation in an apple orchard is important for growers as it facilitates efficient utilization of 
resources and improves returns per unit area and time (Bargoti and Underwood, 2017; Koirala et al., 2019). Despite the 
importance, the estimation is generally carried out in a method of manual counting combined with the orchard agronomic 
conditions and historical fruit yield data due to the lack of a more automatic and precise fruit yield estimation method (Payne 
et al., 2014; Dorj et al., 2017; Apolo-Apolo et al., 2020). However, manual counting is a labor-intensive, expensive, and 
often destructive method, which becomes virtually infeasible to be considered an effective tool in the decision-making 
process. Therefore, developing an automatic and highly accurate apple counting method to estimate apple yield before 
harvest is the key to helping farmers make decisions. 
Fruit detection is a precursor for growers to automatically count fruits for yield estimation and numerous precision farming. 
In recent years, a wide range of methodologies have been developed to detect fruits. Traditional methods such as K-means 
clustering, simple linear iterative clustering, circular Hough transform, directional gradient histogram, etc. rely on the color, 
shape, and texture characteristics of the fruit can only achieve fruit detection with poor robustness (Qureshi et al., 2017; 
Wang, 2018; Liu et al., 2019b). The deep learning method based on the convolutional neural network with advanced 
semantic expression ability has good robustness, because it can autonomously learn the shallow features and deep features 
of the target (Fu et al., 2018; Zhou et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021). The two-stage detection network including Faster R-CNN 
(Faster Region Convolutional Neural Networks) has high detection precision (Gené-Mola et al., 2019; Gao et al., 2020), 
while Fu et al. (2020a) and Kuznetsova et al. (2020) proved that YOLO (You Only Look Once), which belongs to the one-
stage detection network, has a faster detection speed. Therefore, YOLOv4-tiny (Wang et al., 2020), released with YOLOv4 
(Bochkovskiy et al., 2020) in 2020, is considered to have the potential to achieve high-precision detection at a faster detection 
speed. 
Some work has been achieved to count the number of tree fruit crops based on fruit detection. Stein et al. (2016) used 
Faster R-CNN based on VGG16 to count the obtained images of a mango tree from multiple angles. Compared with the 
results based on manual counting, the error rate of a single fruit tree is only 1.36%. Bargoti and Underwood (2017) and Dorj 
et al. (2017) achieved a correlation coefficient of 0.826 and 0.93 between the fruit count results of the algorithm developed 
based on watershed segmentation and manual counting for apple images and citrus images, respectively. Koirala et al. (2019) 
developed a network ‘MangoYOLO’ based on YOLOv3 and YOLOv2-tiny to detect and count mango fruits by images, 
which achieved orchard fruit load estimates of between 4.6 and 15.2% of actual fruit numbers. Although these methods 
obtained good fruit counting results, most of these methods are based on fruit tree images, which is time-consuming, labor 
intensive, costly and difficult to implement for apple orchards. Video of multiple fruit trees, which can be easily obtained 
by a self-built cart, is a convenient alternative that can help quickly achieve fruit counting. However, to realize the fruit 
counting in the video, a method is needed to correlate the same fruits between consecutive video frames to avoid over-
counting in addition to realizing the detection of fruits. 
Tracking, a fundamental task in any video application requiring some degree of reasoning about objects of interest, can 
establish object correspondences between video frames. Given the position of any object of interest in the first frame of the 
video, the purpose of visual object tracking is to estimate its position in all subsequent frames with the highest possible 
accuracy (Q. Wang et al., 2019). Tracking is widely used in the fields of military aerospace, security surveillance and 
intelligent driving (Lee et al., 2019; Ngo et al., 2019; Wawrzyniak et al., 2019). In recent years, tracking algorithms based 
on discriminative correlation filter (DCF) have developed into the mainstream video tracking method due to relatively stable 
tracking performance and fast speed (Kalal et al., 2012; Danelljan et al., 2014). A CSR-DCF (channel spatial reliability-
discriminative correlation filter) tracking algorithm have been proposed on recent tracking benchmark that could achieve 
state-of-the-art performance where channel spatial reliability concepts to DCF tracking (Lukežič et al., 2018). Farhodov et 
al. (2019) integrated Faster R-CNN with CSR-DCF algorithm to track pedestrian and vehicle and demonstrated outstanding 
real-time detection and tracking performance. 
Existing researches on video-based fruit counting are mostly implemented by tracking all fruits detected in a video frame. 
Liu et al. (2019a) developed a mango fruit counting system based on the Kalman filter to track all fruits, and a R2 value 
related to the field count reaches 0.88. Z. Wang et al. (2019) used a Kalman filter-based tracker to track all mango fruits 
detected in the video using the ‘MangoYOLO’ network with a bias corrected Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of 18.0 
fruit/tree. Vasconez et al. (2020) used Gaussian estimation multi-object tracking algorithm developed by Milan et al. (2017) 
to track all apple fruits detected by Faster R-CNN with Inception V2 in the video, which reached a 93% of counting 
performance. Although the above research has good results for fruit counting, the algorithms need to track all the detected 
fruits, which cause the algorithm to take up a lot of computing resources. Therefore, realizing fruit video counting through 
resource-saving single-target tracking may be more in line with actual application requirements. 
By tracking the trunk of apple tree trained in vertical fruiting-wall architecture, the motion trajectories of all relatively 
stationary objects in the video frame, including trunks, branches, leaves and fruits, can be obtained. The existing video 
tracking technology mainly focuses on tracking some large targets with obvious characteristics, such as pedestrians (Wojke

et al., 2018), cars (Li et al., 2014), or humans with actions (Jain et al., 2014), and has not reached the accuracy of human 
marking after decades of research (Milan et al., 2017). Therefore, it is difficult to track one of the apple fruits that have 
similar characteristics to each other in an unrestricted environment. However, the trunk target is larger and has the same 
trajectory as the fruit, which is more suitable for tracking than the fruit target. In the modern vertical fruiting-wall architecture, 
the trees have obvious characteristics and are planted close together with interplant spacing generally ranging from 0.3 to 
1.5 m, while inter-row spacing varies typically from 2.5 to 4.0 m (Gao et al., 2020). Therefore, the relationship between the 
fruits of the upper and lower frames of the video can be established by tracking the tree trunk, thereby eliminating the 
repeated counting of fruits. 
This study aims to design and implement a real-time apple video automatic counting system based on YOLOv4-tiny 
detection method and single-target tracking method for estimating the yield of apple orchards. This work is organized as 
follows: Section 2 mainly describes the design of the software algorithm to detect and count apples from the videos. Results 
and discussion for fruit detection on images, object tracking and counting for frame sequences, and future work are presented 
in Section 3, and the Summary and Conclusions in Section 4. 
Materials and methods 
This study focused on counting apples in vertical fruiting-wall tree architecture, which is one of the modern orchard 
systems. This section describes the self-built portable device that was used to acquire sensor data, the apple orchard where 
experiments were performed and the algorithmic pipeline that is proposed to process the data to detect, track, and count the 
fruits and trees. An overview of the pipeline is illustrated in Figure 1. Fruits and tree trunks are detected in individual color 
images and videos using YOLOv4-tiny, a state-of-the-art object detection network. The detection result of the tree trunk can 
be used as the input of the target tracking network to realize the association between the same targets in the video frame. 
Fruit counting algorithm was developed by integrating detection and tracking algorithms, which then enables the unique 
count. This section describes the equipment and all of the steps in Figure 1 in detail. 
 
Figure 1. Proposed pipeline. To count fruits on one side of a specific row of trees, our pipeline takes in videos recorded from one side of the tree 
row. Firstly, fruits and tree trunks are detected using YOLOv4-tiny in frame n. The trunk detection result from the YOLOv4-tiny detector is 
input into the CSR-DCF-based tracker to track the trunk in subsequent video frames. Calculate the movement displacement of the tree trunk 
between consecutive video frames to match the same fruit. Based on the detected fruit positions, fruit IDs are given in order to achieve fruit 
counting, and the maximum ID of the fruit in the last frame of the video is obtained as the number of fruits in the video. 
Data acquisition 
Image and video data were collected using a self-built portable device in a commercial fruiting-wall Scifresh apple 
orchard with dense-foliage canopy. A Microsoft Kinect V2 sensor (Microsoft Inc., Redmond, WA, USA), a camera clamping 
device, and a support frame were mounted on a portable cart to form the device for acquiring fruit-on-plant images, while a 
field remote control trolley was used to get orchard videos. All image and video data in this study were acquired in a 
commercial, vertically trellis-trained apple orchard (Figure 2a, 2b) located in north Prosser, WA, USA. The tree spacing and 
row spacing are 1.5 m and 2.7 m, respectively, and tree height is about 4.0 m (Zhang et al., 2018; Fu et al., 2020b). In the 
orchard, regular canopy management is conducted annually and equally to all blocks and it is normally maintained by the 
same group of trained laborers. 
A total of 800 RGB (Red, Green, and Blue) images and 10 videos were acquired during two harvesting seasons (2017 
and 2018). All the apple images were obtained using the Kinect V2 sensor with a JPEG image format and a resolution of 
1920 × 1080 pixels for developing an algorithm for fruit and trunk detection. During the apple videos acquisition, the trolley 
was driven along the rows of the apple tree and is tried to maintain at an almost constant speed. The time of video acquisition 
was random, so the numbers of fruit trees and fruits contained in the video were also different. The total numbers of fruit 
and fruit trees in each video were counted manually. Every fruit in each video including tree origin (where adjacent canopies 
inter-meshed) was counted, except for the fruits in the background. Uncertainty in manual count typically occurred around 
dimly lit fruit on the canopy margin and background and around highly occluded fruit (Figure 2c) (Qureshi et al., 2017). 
The RMSE for manual counts of the video set (mean of 43 fruit per image) by three different operators was 2.7 fruits per

Dataset building 
The dataset is constructed using image data and video data to train and test network performance. The overall image data 
was randomly divided into raw training dataset (80% of the images) and testing dataset (20% of the images) to train and test 
the detection network, respectively. For each image, an XML annotation file (filename=image name) was created containing 
the image attributes (name, width, height) and the object attributes (class name, object bounding box co-ordinates). Apples 
and tree trunks were labeled as two different classes with rectangular annotations in the XML file. While all the videos were 
used to test the performance of the counting algorithm. 
Data augmentation technique was used to enlarge the image dataset which helped to reduce over-fitting in supervised 
learning algorithms. To achieve sensitive detection of apple in the orchard, this study took into consideration most kinds of 
interference that may occur when detecting fruits. Data augmentation, including brightness transformation, adaptive 
histogram equalization, motion blur transformation and image mirroring in horizontal axis on the dataset, were implemented. 
A total of 800 images were augmented to 6400 images. Although many studies have proved that rotation can greatly improve 
network performance, considering that the angle of the fruit trees in the image generated by rotation does not conform to 
the actual situation, this study did not choose rotation as the augmented method. 
YOLOv4-tiny detection network 
In terms of fruit detection using CNN-based network, YOLOv4-tiny can be used to develop a network model that is faster 
than other methods. YOLO was first proposed in 2016 (Redmon et al., 2016) and constantly improved recent year giving 
rise to YOLOv2 (Redmon and Farhadi, 2017), YOLOv3 (Redmon and Farhadi, 2018), and YOLOv4 (Bochkovskiy et al., 
2020). Corresponding lightweight networks, such as YOLOv3-tiny and YOLOv4-tiny have also been proposed. YOLOv4-
tiny, a network scaled down by the YOLOv4 object detection neural network, is designed for low-end GPU devices and 
achieved extremely high 1774 FPS speed on RTX 2080Ti by using TensorRT-FP16 (Wang et al., 2020). YOLOv4-tiny 
achieved a 40.2% mAP@0.5, which is 7.1% higher than YOLOv3-tiny in the MS COCO dataset (Han et al., 2020). 
Nevertheless, the processing speed of YOLOv4-tiny is slightly faster than that of YOLOv3-tiny. 
Structure of YOLOv4-tiny is shown in Figure 3. YOLOv4-tiny had fewer layers of 37 (starting from 0) compared to 
YOLOv4 with more than a hundred. Therefore, it made the training process faster with fewer computations without too 
much trade in detection performance (Montalbo, 2020). The extractor begins with an input layer that takes an 𝑁×𝑁 image, 
which is 416×416 in this paper. The backbone comes with an interchanging 3×3 and 1×1 receptive filters that strides over 
the input to produce a new set of feature maps or filters that pass through the network. Detector uses both the feature map 
of the final level and the feature map of the previous level to detect various size objects by applying the concept of feature 
pyramid network. Because many convolutional layers cannot be used on lower-level feature maps, it is difficult to extract 
enough feature maps. Therefore, YOLOv4-tiny uses only the two feature maps for object detection, unlike the YOLOv4.

CSR-DCF-based tracker 
Object tracking is an open and challenging problem for which discriminative correlation filter have shown excellent 
performance. The basis of the proposed tracking method is based on the CSR-DCF algorithm, which has achieved the latest 
results on VOT 2016, VOT 2015 and OTB100, and can be run in real-time on a CPU (Lukežič et al., 2018). CSR-DCF 
performed on par with the VOT2016 best-performing CCOT (Danelljan et al., 2016), while being 20 times faster than the 
CCOT. The speed of real-time trackers like DSST (Danelljan et al., 2014) and Struck (Hare et al., 2011) is comparable to 
CSR-DCF, but their tracking performance is significantly poorer. KCF (Henriques et al., 2015) can run nearly 10 times faster 
than CSR-DCF, but delivers a significantly poorer performance than CSR-DCF, with an average failures of 2.03, while CSR-
DCF is only 0.85. 
The CSR-DCF algorithm has been implemented in C++ and integrated into the Open CV library as a DNN (Deep Neural 
Network) module (Farhodov et al., 2019). The tracker trains the DCF template based on two simple standard features, HoGs 
and Colornames, so as to realize the tracking of the target in the video sequence. In the tracking process, the filter is adjusted 
to the part of the object suitable for tracking by establishing a spatial reliability map, which can not only enlarge the search 
region, but also improve tracking of non-rectangular objects. Reliability scores reflect channel-wise quality of the learned 
filters and are used as feature weighting coefficients in localization, thereby reducing the noise of the weight-averaged filter 
response. The area where the maximum response point is the new position of the target, and then the predict area is used to 
train and update the filter for subsequent prediction. 
Fruit counting algorithm 
This research matched the same fruit between video frames by tracking tree trunk targets in the field of view. The 
YOLOv4-tiny network model trained in Section 2.3 can detect orchard video obtained in Section 2.1, thereby finding the 
number and position of the fruit and tree trunk in each frame. The fruit and the trunk were distinguished based on the class 
tag index, and the first trunk target in the video motion direction was selected as the tracking target. Initialize the tracker 
using the detected position of the tracking target to get the predicted position of the target in the next frame. Calculate the 
difference between the predicted position and the initial position to obtain the reference displacement between video frames. 
Because the displacement between video frames is not large, the new position obtained by adding the reference displacement 
to the initial position of most apples should ideally be the same as the position of a certain fruit detected in the next frame, 
thereby realizing the matching of the same fruit between consecutive frames of video. 
The existence of detection error and tracking error makes it difficult to match the same fruit between consecutive frames 
of the video. Although both the YOLOv4-tiny and CSRT networks have good performance, there is a certain gap between 
the positions of the fruits output by the two networks and ground truth. However, there should be the closest Euclidean 
distance between the calculated new position of the fruit and the detected new position of the same fruit, so that preliminary 
matching of the same fruit can be achieved. Video quality problems or the loss of the tracking target field of view will cause 
tracking errors or even tracking failures. In order to avoid tracking errors resulting in large errors in the calculation of the 
reference displacement, the study will promptly adjust the position of the tracked tree trunk; at the same time, in order to 
avoid the inability to obtain the reference displacement due to the tracking failure, the study will also change the tracking 
target in advance. 
Each frame of fruit detection results and tree trunk tracking results were used as input to the fruit counting algorithm.

Experimental platform and related settings 
The platform for training the detection network included a desktop computer equipped with a CPU of Intel Core i5-6400 
(2.70 GHz), 16 GB of RAM, and an NVIDIA GTX 1080 8 GB GPU, running on a Windows 10 64-bit system. The software 
tools included CUDA 9.0, cuDNN 7.1.3, Microsoft Visual Studio 2015, CMake-3.16, Python 3.6, and OpenCV 3.1.0. The 
experiments were implemented on the Darknet framework. The network input size was 416 × 416, and 64 was for batch 
size. The stochastic gradient descent was applied for training with a momentum of 0.9 and a weight decay of 0.0005. An 
initial value of 0.001 and 50,000 were set as the learning rate and iterations of the network, respectively. Transfer learning 
technique was performed to train the network by importing weights from previous training on COCO dataset, which contains 
91 objects types with 2.5 million labeled instances in 328k images (Lin et al., 2015). 
The platform for debug the counting algorithm included a laptop computer equipped with a CPU of Intel Core i7-8565U 
(1.80 GHz), 8 GB of RAM, and an NVIDIA GeForce MX250 2 GB GPU, running on a Windows 10 64-bit system. The 
software tools included CUDA 10.0, cuDNN 7.6.5, Microsoft Visual Studio 2017, Python 3.8, and OpenCV 4.4.0. The 
algorithm was developed based on Python language with OpenCV library. The CSR-DCF algorithm and Darknet framework 
were integrated in the OpenCV library of 4.0 and later versions, which supports this research to realize the establishment of 
the CSRT tracker and the loading and detection of the YOLOv4-tiny network. 30 and 37 network layers were set as the 
YOLOv4-tiny network output layer, forward propagation to obtain the detection results of fruits and trunks. One of the 
detected trunks was selected as the tracking target to create a tracker for follow-up research.

Results and discussion 
In this section, the relationship between the information obtained by the algorithm and the real information measured on 
each video was evaluated and verified. Initially, the detection model of fruit and trunk trained based on YOLOv4-tiny 
network was evaluated according to test dataset with ground truth. Afterwards, the correctness of fruit IDs obtained based 
on proposed counting algorithm in each frame of the video were evaluated according to the fruit ID manually labeled. Then, 
a validation of the count of fruits in videos is presented. Finally, the shortcomings of this research and the future direction 
of the research are analyzed. 
Performance of the detection network 
The training loss curve based on YOLOv4-tiny on the training dataset converged within 50,000 iterations (Figure 4), and 
excellent detection results were obtained on the corresponding testing dataset (Table 1). The loss value is generally stable 
when the number of iterations reaches 43,000 iterations, and approached the lowest value of 0.90. The curve demonstrates 
that the network efficiently learns the features with good convergence ability, and the data in Table 2 is also sufficient to 
prove it. There are a total of 1280 images in testing dataset, including 37,642 targets. The AP of apples and trunks both 
reached more than 99%, and the mAP value was 99.35%, which provided a good basis for fruit counting. Besides, YOLOv4-
tiny only took 0.022 s on average to detect apples or trunk in one image with the resolution of 1920 × 1080 pixels. The 
network trained in this work can quickly and effectively detect apples and trunks, which may achieve the desired effect in a 
real orchard. 
The YOLOv4-tiny model achieved a good performance for detecting both apples and trunks under natural orchard (Table 
1). Most apples were successfully detected from the background, although there were false negative (FN) and false positive 
(FP) detection boxes. However, the FN detection boxes would not make a significant negative effect on further apple 
counting because the fruit counting algorithm had great potential to correctly count the apple by detecting multiple video 
frames containing incorrectly classified apple (Figure 5). Because the trunks belong to the large target in orchard image and 
have obvious features, all trunks in the testing dataset are detected, even the incomplete trunks located at the boundary of 
the image. Although the detection rectangle of the tree trunk is not always the minimum bounding rectangle of the target,

Performance of the fruit counting algorithm 
Figure 6 shows the overall performance of the method presenting the results of the linear regression. Each dot in the chart 
represents the maximum number of the fruit ID obtained in the last frame of a video found by the algorithm in the x axis (Pt) 
and the summation of the visual manual counts in the same video found by the inspectors in the y axis (Nt). This model fit 
was obtained from 15 orchard videos, which obtained a R2 = 0.9875. The number of frames of these videos is between 10 
and 104, which contains 76-478 fruits. Thus the model performed well, regardless of the number of video frames. Pc values 
of above orchard videos were all calculated (Figure 6), which reached an average value of 91.30%. For the selected videos, 
there was an average of 38 fruit detected per frame, of which about 0.01 false positive and 0.19 false negatives. The false 
positive errors resulted in an over-count while the false negative errors resulted in an under-count. The numbers of false 
positives and false negatives were similar, resulting in a low bias and a high Pc value.

The counting algorithm proposed in this paper can give the correct ID to most fruits in the video frame. Figure 7 and

can detect the fruits that are occluded severely or located on the boundary in the frame, which is also one of the reasons for 
the emergence of RMSE. Besides, the unsuccessful detection of some fruits is another main reason for the emergence of 
RMSE. Based on Eq. (9) and Eq. (10), the relevant MIDE and RMSE are calculated to be 0.199 and 2.387, respectively, 
which indicates the reliability of the algorithm in short-term video fruit counting. 
Among the factors that could interfere with the algorithm performance, major importance was given to the switch of fruit 
ID. The fruit ID changed mainly because the fruit was not successfully detected continuously between video frames. The 
system assigns a fruit ID when the fruit is successfully detected. If the fruit is successfully detected in subsequent frames, 
the original ID will remain unchanged; but when the fruit is not detected, the ID will be cancelled, regardless of whether the 
detection fails or the fruit field of view disappears. Therefore, the fruit that fails to be detected will be given another ID 
when it is successfully detected again, causing the ID of the fruit to change (Figure 8a). Besides, the close Euclidean distance 
between the fruits will also cause the fruit ID to change, provided that one of the two fruits has not been successfully detected 
in a certain frame (Figure 8b). However, the probability of this phenomenon occurring in the video is extremely low.

Comparison with other methods 
Although there are some reports of apple fruit counts with good results, it is difficult to compare with this research 
because the data processed by each research were all captured images or videos from different orchard environments, and 
even the quality of captured orchard videos varies greatly. Vasconez et al. (2020) used a multi-object tracking method based 
on Gaussian estimation to simultaneously realize video counting of three fruits including apples, and reported that up to 93% 
of the fruit counting performance can be achieved. However, the highest detection rate of apple in this study was only 54%, 

ASABE 2021 Annual International Meeting 
Page 10 
which means that there will be a large number of fruits that will not be counted in the study. Roy et al. (2019) proposed a 
method of combining multiple frame counts of videos based on camera motion, which can achieve a count accuracy of 89–
98% on multiple datasets, but the research does not show that the processing fruit variety. 
An apple counting algorithm based on the popular multi-target tracking algorithm ‘deep sort’ was developed to compare 
the performance of the algorithm proposed in this research. The basic idea of the deep Sort algorithm is tracking-by-detection. 
The core is the recursive Kalman filter and the Hungarian algorithm, which uses a metric that combines motion and 
appearance information to perform data association frame by frame. The algorithm detects the target in each frame of the 
video, and realizes the counting of the target by matching the detected target and the motion trajectory. In order to better 
compare the counting performance of deep sort and the algorithm proposed in this research, the YOLOv4-tiny network 
trained in this research is used to perform the detection work of deep sort algorithm. The same video was used to test the 
two algorithms. 
Same videos are used to judge the performance of the two algorithms, which are shown in Figure 9a. The proposed 
algorithm has an average counting accuracy of 91.60% for these 5 videos, but the deep sort algorithm has serious over-
counting. Although the deep sort algorithm has shown good tracking and counting performance for pedestrians, it is not 
ideal for apple video obtained in this study. This is because the fruit is not only much smaller than pedestrians or vehicles, 
and there is no obvious difference in appearance between individuals. Besides, although the researcher consciously 
controlled the average speed of the vehicle, the speed of the obtained video still varied greatly, making the movement in the 
fruit video basically irregular. This not only makes the metric that combines motion and appearance information basically 
unable to provide an effective function of fruit counting, but also leads to the failure of apple tracking, as shown in Figure 
9b. In terms of speed, the algorithm proposed in this study can be implemented on CPU at a 2-5 fps near real-time system, 
but deep sort is only 0-2 fps.

Future works 
In the future, the fruit counts on both sides of the fruit tree row would to be merged to achieve an accurate estimation of 
orchard yield. In the modern orchard with vertical fruiting-wall architecture, some apples can be seen from both sides of the 
tree row, which results in repeated counting for those fruits when calculating the number of fruits in the tree row. Although 
there are some studies to reduce the existence of this error by establishing mathematical models(Chen et al., 2017; Koirala 
et al., 2019), this method can hardly cope with sudden changes in orchard yield. Roy et al. (2018) proposed a method for 
judging the visible fruits on both sides of a tree row based on three-dimensional reconstruction technology, which can help 
to achieve accurate estimation of orchard yield, but requires high configuration. Therefore, we plan to develop an algorithm 
that can identify the fruits visible on both sides of the tree row, combined with the current fruit counting algorithm to obtain 
the number of fruits in the orchard tree row in real-time. Besides, we also plan to develop technologies based on the depth 
camera to measure the diameter and volume of the fruit for accurate estimation of orchard yield.

Conclusion 
This research examined automatically counting apples (during harvest season) on vertical-fruiting-wall trees for 
estimation orchard yield using algorithm developed by YOLOv4-tiny and CSRT in orchard videos. A total of 800 images 
were acquired and then augmented to 6400 images, where 80% of them were used to train the YOLOv4-tiny model. Both 
apples and trunks were labeled as study targets, reaching AP values of 99.59% and 99.10%, respectively. The detected trunk 
was used as the tracking target in CSRT tracker to obtain the reference displacement between video frames. Apples detected 
between adjacent video frames are combined with reference displacement information to achieve matching of the same fruit. 
Assign the same fruit unique ID to complete the fruit count. The algorithm counts fruit videos in a 2-5 fps real-time system, 
and achieved a counting accuracy of 91.30%, which provides the possibility for real-time yield estimation of the orchard.
