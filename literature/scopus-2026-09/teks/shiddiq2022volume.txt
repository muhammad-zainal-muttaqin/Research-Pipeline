Abstract. Volume measurement has used computer vision to estimate the mass of many types of fruits. Capacity 
determination of a crude palm oil refinery involves the measurement of weight and mass of oil palm fresh fruit bunches, 
which is usually measured using a weighbridge. Automation is possible using a fast sorting and grading machine vision 
where computer vision is applied. This study aimed to use a computer vision method with a cross-line laser beam to 
measure the volumes of oil palm FFB using a segmentation process and an ellipsoid equation. Samples were 40 oil palm 
FFB of Tenera Variety. The volumes measured using two other methods, the water displacement method (WDM) and the 
manual method, were used for validation.  The average difference of the oil palm FFB volumes measured manually and 
by computer vision was  0.77 x 103 cm3 with R2 of 0.9336. The volumes measured using the WDM method and computer 
vision has an average difference of 0.81 x 103 cm3 with an R2 of 0.9074. Comparison of manual to computer vision 
method and WDM to computer vision method show the volumes measured are within 95 % spread using Bland-Altman 
plot. The FFB volumes measured using the computer vision method range from 3.92 × 103 cm3   18.02 × 103 cm3 which 
relate to 2.4 - 17.9 kg of masses. These results show the potential use of the computer vision method for oil palm FFB. 
Keywords: Computer vision, volume, ellipsoid algorithm, oil palm fresh fruit bunches, cross-line laser beam 
INTRODUCTION 
 
Volume and mass are two significant physical quantities in agricultural industries, especially for sorting and 
grading fruits and vegetables. Measurements of volume and mass can obtain fruits at a particular size according to 
production standards hence can be included in the grading or classification of fruits besides chemical contents such

as sugar content or oil content [1]. Sorting based on volume and mass is often done traditionally using human vision 
or using digital measuring tools. More accurate measurement of volume uses the method of water displacement 
based on Archimedes' Law or air displacement, while mass can be measured using a digital scale [2]. Volume and 
mass can also be measured using electronic sensors [3,4]. For fast measurements of volume and mass, machine 
vision and computer vision have been used. 
Fruit volumes can be estimated using either two-dimensional or three-dimensional imaging methods that utilize 
one or two cameras and image processing. Image processing has been utilized to estimate the volume of tangerine 
[5], apples [6], and tomatoes [7], while the mass and weight of fruits can be measured from fruit volume using 
images [8, 9]. Therefore, measuring fruit volumes using computer vision and image processing is essential for 
weight or mass estimation. It enables quick fruit sorting and grading process at factories [10]. 
The shapes of fruits are similar to geometry shapes such as a spheroid, a cylinder, or an ellipsoid.  Therefore, the 
volume of the fruits can be estimated using the geometrical approach. Measurement of fruit volume both manually 
and computationally using image processing can be carried out using the geometrical shape approach such as citrus 
fruits are round-shaped, watermelons are ellipsoid, and zucchinis are cylindrical-like [9, 11, 12, 13]. Computer 
vision has been used to determine the volume or area of an object by measuring fruit geometry parameters such as 
the length of the semi-major, semi-minor, and semi median axis [14, 15, 16]. Furthermore, the volume estimation 
can be validated using manual volume measurements [17] and a water displacement method [18]. 
Oil palm fresh fruit bunches as the source of crude palm oil (CPO) is a significant agricultural product. The FFB 
sorting in palm oil mills is done traditionally by collecting the right FFB, sorting according to its ripeness levels, 
sizes, and oil and free fatty acid contents. Measurement of various physical quantities of oil palm fruits has been 
done manually, such as measuring fruit density and average volume [19]. The information is crucial for milling 
processing steps. Computer vision has been used for oil palm fresh fruit bunches, such as predicting ripeness [20, 
21] and oil content [22]. Physical and optical characteristics of oil palm FFB on two different plantings have been 
analyzed using three methods. The methods were manual method, machine vision, and optical sensors application. 
The physical characteristics measured manually were the weight, length, width, and circumference of the oil palm 
FFBs [23].  
A physical parameter of oil palm fresh fruit bunches, such as volume, can also be measured using computer 
vision methods, especially for automatic sorting and grading before the FFB bunches enter the boiling process. 
Measuring volumes of fruit bunches using computer vision and Moiré Technique is possible, such as grape bunches  
[24].  In this study, the measurements of oil palm FFB volumes used computer vision with a diode laser cross beam.  
A laser triangulation method with a line laser beam will create a broken line at an object and represent the object 
height.  The object volume can be estimated using the laser triangulation technique by utilizing the contour area and 
depth obtained from images [25].  The volume and weight of an object can also be estimated using a computer 
vision system and a structured light [26].  In this study, the laser cross-beam was used to simplify the segmentation 
process for obtaining the length of the semi-major and semi-minor of the fruit bunch [27, 28].  The volume of FFB 
was approximated as an ellipsoid volume. The steps of acquiring and processing images of oil palm FFB for volume 
measurement used a Python-based computer program. Manual and water displacement measurements would 
validate the results. Bland-Altman plot would analyze the comparisons of the three methods [29]. 
 
RESEARCH METHODS 
 
The experiment for volume measurement of oil palm fresh fruit bunches (FFB) was carried out in several stages. 
The first stage was preparing the samples of FFB. The second stage was setting up the computer vision (CV) system 
and creating a Python-based program for the volume measurement. The third stage was measuring the volumes of 
oil palm FFB manually.  Before measuring the volume using the CV system, two processes were performed, 
calibration of pixel to cm and validation of the program using a known shape. The next stage was the measurements 
using the CV system. The oil palm FFB images were recorded using a colour camera and processed to obtain the 
semi-major, semi-median, semi-minor lengths.  Measurements using Water Displacement Method (WDM) was done 
afterwards.  Comparison analysis for three measurement methods used linear regression and Bland-Altman Plot. 
There were 40 oil palm FFBs used in this study which were Tenera variety and Nigrescence in colour. The 40 
samples were in unripe and underripe ripeness levels to avoid loose fruitlets during the volume measurements. The

fruit bunches varied in mass which ranged from 2.40 - 17.85 kg measured using a digital scale with an accuracy of 
0.1 kg. 
Imaging System 
The volume measurement of oil palm FFB was using a computer vision (CV) system assisted by a crossline 
diode laser. The processes of acquiring an FFB image, segmentation, extracting the length of the semi-major, semi-
median, and semi-minor axis, and calculating the FFB volume were performed automatically using the Python-
based program. Figure 1 shows the CV system that consisted of a colour camera and diode laser, a dark box, a 
computer, and Python-based software. The camera was a CMOS colour camera with a resolution of 640 × 480, 15 
frame/sec, and connected using a USB cable to the computer. The cross-line laser was a Roithner Laser diode 
module with 635 nm (red) wavelength, about 2,5 mW laser power, operated in continuous mode.  The system was in 
a dark box to block room light.

The volume measurement of oil palm FFB used a Computer Vision (CV) system with a cross line laser beam.  
Python-based software was used for automatic image acquisition, contour detection, measurement of the three axial 
lengths, and calculating the oil palm FFB volumes.  Figure 3 shows the volume measurement algorithm of oil palm 
FFB. The first step was setting up the color camera with a cross-line diode laser in the CV system, then image 
acquisition started. After the image acquisition stage, the image processing extracted some features needed. The 
Hue, Saturation, and Value of HSV color space were used to obtain the crossline laser beam information. The image 
on the computer screen consisted of broken lines of the cross-line laser beam with oil palm bunches image on the 
background.   
 The next step was a contour detection process that aimed to obtain the coordinate of a pixel in the image frame. The 
number of contours detected is limited to 5 for calculating the surface area of the object. If the contour number was 
exactly 5, the program immediately measured the length of the semi-major axis, semi-median axis, and semi-minor 
axis based on the pixel coordinates of each contour. After the contour detection process and obtaining length 
conversion from pixel to cm, each parameter of a, b, and c can be calculated. The conversion value was found 
previously using an image of a ruler and a known object. In this measurement,  the parameter c  represented the 
object height obtained using the surface area. After attaining the three parameters of an ellipsoid, the volume of oil 
palm FFB resulted in cm3. 
 
Manual Volume Measurement 
Manual volume measurement was done before image acquisitions of oil palm FFB were conducted. The manual 
measurement was to validate the volume measurement using the CV system. The oil palm FFB shape is 
approximated as an ellipsoid. Therefore its volume is estimated as an ellipsoidal volume. The manual measurement 
was conducted by measuring the diameter of each axis of the oil using a measuring tape [23]. Each parameter of a, 
030007-4

b, and c in Eq.2 was calculated as half of each axial diameter. The oil palm FFB volume then was calculated using 
Eq.2. The measurement was done twice for each oil palm FFB and averaged.  There were 40 oil palm FFB samples 
for this measurement labelled as S1 to S40. 
 
Image Acquisition 
Computer Vision (CV) system was built using a color camera and a cross line laser beam of a diode laser. The 
working distance for the camera to the object was 87 cm. The diode laser was placed 4 cm to the side of the camera 
created a triangulation between the object, camera, and the laser beam [25]. The calibration was also conducted 
using a plastic ball with its known axis diameter shone by the crossline laser beam. The calibration process resulted 
in 14.28 pixels/cm.   
Image acquisition was the next step for a CV system to be implemented in the volume measurement of oil palm 
FFB. The process started by adjusting the camera focus and laser beam position. The processes of image acquisition, 
color space conversion, contour detection, finding the lengths of ellipsoidal axis, and volume calculation were 
performed in real-time using a Python-based computer program. The image acquisition results were saved for 
further analysis. Image acquisition took two images for each sample of oil palm FFB, which is on the front and back 
part of the oil palm FFB. The volume obtained later will be averaged. 
 
Water Displacement Measurement 
Water displacement measurement is one of the manual methods used to validate the results of the computer 
vision method. The water displacement method (WDM) used the change of the water surface before and after the oil 
palm FFB was immersed in water. This measurement is the standard method, even though it is destructive, wet, and 
tedious [2]. This measurement was done after recording images of oil palm FFB. Due to the unavailability of a 
bucket suitable for oil palm FFB that vary in mass and volume, some considerations were made. Figure 4 shows the 
simulated situation of a container that has been used to measure the oil palm FFB volumes using the WDM method. 
The available large bucket was a truncated cone shape which does not have the same diameter at the top and bottom 
of the bucket. Therefore, Eq. 4 calculated the volume of oil palm FFB using the WDM method. The t1 and t2 
represented respectively as the height of the water before and after the oil palm FFB was immersed.  It shows that 
the radius of R1 and R2 and the height of water in the container change when the oil palm FFB become larger or 
smaller while the value of r as the radius of the bottom part is constant.  In this study, a long plastic ruler was used to 
measure the water height of t1 and t2. The diameter of the water surface before and after the oil palm FFB submerged 
was measured using rolling tape, then parameters R1 and R2 were obtained.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
This study aimed to use the computer vision system to measure the volumes of oil palm fresh fruit bunches.  The 
oil palm FFB volume was estimated using an ellipsoidal volume. The cross-line laser beam was proposed to add 
contour to the oil palm FFB images, hence easier to measure the lengths of three ellipsoid axes. The image 
acquisition and processing used a Python-based program. Comparison has been made to the results of the oil palm 
FFB volume measured by the CV system to the resulting volumes measured using the manual method and WDM. 
Image Segmentation Results 
 
The computer vision system with a cross-line laser beam has been used to find the volume of oil palm fresh fruit 
bunches. The volume information will be essential in estimating the mass of fruits using image processing [1, 8, 9]. 
The cross-line laser beam offers the easiness to obtain contours of the fruit as a substitute for segmentation using 
edge detection. Figure 5 shows the cross-line laser beam across the oil palm FFB (a) and five contours obtained for 
an oil palm FFB (b). Broken lines on the image display the length of each contour that shown by the pixel numbers. 
The length of semi major-axis was found by subtracting the contour pixels of #3 to #4 on the x-axis. In the 
meantime, the median axis length was calculated by subtracting contours of #1 and #5 on the y-axis. The object 
height was obtained by applying the linear regression model from the area of contour #2.  The reason is due to the 
area change as the object height changed. Parameters a, b, and c from the ellipsoid volume formula as in Eq. 1 then 
resulted by dividing the length of major, median, and minor axes by two, respectively. Room lighting also affects 
the shape of the contours produced by the laser beam, so it is necessary to use a dark room or dark box.

  Comparison of Volume Measurement Methods 
Comparisons have been made for the volume measurements of 40 samples of oil palm FFB using the CV 
method, manual method, and water displacement method (WDM). The CV method uses an image intersected by a 
laser beam in the form of a cross line to determine the semi-major, semi-median, and semi-minor length of an 
ellipsoid shape. The manual method used a measuring tape to measure the semi-major, semi-median, and semi-
minor axes of the ellipsoid. At the same time, the WDM method uses Archimedes' law with buoyancy force. Figure 
6 shows the volume of 40 oil palm FFB resulting from three measurements. As information, the masses of the 
samples range from 2.40 - 17.85 kg. The CV method results were set in the x-axis for reference. The comparison 
used analysis of Linear Regression lines.

Figure 6 shows the relationship between the volumes of oil palm FFB measured using the computer vision on the 
x-axis with WDM on the left y-axis and the CV method with the manual method on the right y-axis. Figure 6 shows 
the R2 values of 0.9074 for CV to WDM and 0.9366 CV to the manual, respectively. Linear regression results also 
show the root mean square error (RMSE) of 1.087 for CV to WDM and 0.775 for the CV to the manual method. The 
lowest volumes of oil palm FFB obtained using the CV method, WDM method, and the manual method are 3.92, 
2.29, 3.22 (× 103 cm3) and the highest volume measured for the three measurements are 18.02, 17.03, 18.16 ( 103 
cm3) respectively. The results show that the CV method has a larger volume measured for each oil palm FFB. 
The comparison between the cv and manual volumes shows a higher coefficient of determination R2 value and a 
lower RMSE value because it uses the same ellipsoid formula, but only different in the measurement method, 
namely computationally and manually. Errors obtained in the CV method could be due to a slight error in the pixel 
to cm conversion and the center position of the cross line beam changes in each FFB oil palm measurement. The 
error in manual measurement could be due to inaccuracy in measuring the diameter of the semi-major, semi-median, 
and semi-minor because of the parallax effect. Comparison between CV volumes to WDM volumes has a lesser R2 
value than volumes of CV to the manual method. The difference could be because not all oil palm FFB has an 
ellipsoidal shape. The shapes of oil palm FFB depends on several factors, such as variety [23]. The oil palm FFB 
shape is somewhat flat on the sides compared to the front and backside. The flatter sides are due to infertile fruitlets 
between two fronds [30].

Figure 7 shows the Bland-Altman plot for comparing the measurement results of the computer vision method 
with the manual and WDM methods. Bland-Altman plot presents the agreement between two measurement methods 
by plotting an XY plot where the y axis represents the difference between two paired measurements data and the x-
axis represents the average of both measurement data [29]. There are three lines in the plot representing the mean 
030007-7

lines and ±2SD lines of standard deviation. Bland-Altman plot can show the difference between two measurement 
methods based on the three lines. The mean line closer to zero describes that the results of the two methods have low 
deviation. Plot Bland and Altman plot also illustrate whether the data spread lie between the ±2SD lines. 
Figure 7 also shows the data spread of volumes for CV and manual method comparison (a) and CV and WDM 
method comparison (b). Figure 7 (a) shows a lower data spread where the mean difference between CV and manual 
was 0.768 cm3 with 95% limits of agreement (-1.211 to 2.708). Figure 7 (b) shows the comparison between the CV 
and WDM methods. The mean difference for both measurements was 0.806 cm3 with 95% limits of agreement (-
1.153 to 2.776).  The higher mean difference for the CV and WDM method shown by the Bland-Altman plot agrees 
with the larger coefficient determination R2. However, both parameters show higher confidence for the potential use 
of the computer vision method assisted by cross-line laser beam for nondestructive volume measurement of oil palm 
fresh fruit bunches. 
 
 
CONCLUSION 
Volume measurement of oil palm fresh fruit bunches using Computer Vision (CV) method has been carried out by 
using a color camera and a cross laser of 635 nm. The results were validated using manual measurements and the 
Water Displacement Method (WDM). The volume measurements using CV and manual method resulted in a mean 
difference of 0.768 cm3 while using CV and WDM method obtained 0.806 cm3 of the mean difference. The 
coefficient of determination R2 between CV and manual was 0.94, while CV and WDM had 0.91. Lower coefficient 
of determination and higher mean difference for CV and WDM comparison was due to the inhomogeneous shapes 
of the oil palm FFB samples that can be an ellipsoid, a truncated ellipsoid, and a spheroid. The computer system 
assisted by a cross-line shape laser beam was shown capable of estimating the volume of oil palm fresh fruit 
bunches with easiness in the image processing algorithm. This system can be embedded in an automatic sorting and 
grading machine vision complementary to ripeness level detection.
