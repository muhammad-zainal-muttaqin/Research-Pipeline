A B S T R A C T  
Indonesia’s economic progress is closely linked to palm oil, a crucial plantation commodity. The price of this oil 
relies on the oil content found in the fruit of the oil palm, which is gauged by the fruit’s maturity level, a task 
usually undertaken by experts. The maturity of oil palm FFB is generally divided into several classes, commonly 
unripe, under-ripe, ripe, and over-ripe. However, the detection of maturity still uses human vision, which causes 
detection errors. Therefore, this research will use the computer vision method with the YOLOv4 model to detect 
the maturity of the oil palm fruit. YOLOv4 itself is a real-time object detection model that has been widely used to 
detect fruit maturity, including oil palm fruit; this research uses 4,160 images of oil palm fruit maturity which is 
divided into six categories, namely empty fruit, underripe, abnormal, ripe, under-ripe, and over-ripe. The 
YOLOv4 model trained using the dataset will be optimized using GA for looking at the optimal hyperparameter to 
produce a better model. The result is YOLOv4 model experienced an increase in mAP compared to before using 
GA hyperparameter optimization.   
Introduction 
Palm oil is one of the world’s most important oil-producing crops, 
and Indonesia is one of the world’s largest palm oil suppliers [1]. The 
cost of palm oil is determined by the oil content in the fruit, a mea­
surement assessed by an expert. The primary factor taken into account is 
the fruit’s level of maturity [2]. The correlation between the maturity 
level of oil palm fruit and its oil content is such that unripe fruit contains 
a small amount of oil. In contrast, ripe fruit has a significantly higher oil 
content [3]. In general, the detection of oil palm fruit maturity is carried 
out by an expert using human observations, which is very subjective and 
can cause errors in the detection process [4]. Along with the develop­
ment of technology, many methods have been developed to detect oil 
palm fruit maturity that is more objective and effective. One way to 
detect maturity in fruit is to use a computer vision approach. 
Numerous studies on the maturity level of oil palm fruit have been 
conducted using the Computer Vision approach, commencing with 
machine learning research from [3] and [5]. Further research using deep 
learning conducted by [4,6,7], and [8] has developed a deep learning 
model for detecting the maturity of the oil palm fruit. Starting from this 
deep learning, an object detection method was developed to detect or 
classify fruit maturity in real-time. Object detection is a method used to 
locate and identify specific objects within an image by placing a box or 
bounding box around the target object that needs to be detected [9]. One 
of the algorithms often used for the object detection method is You Only 
Look Once, usually called YOLO. YOLOv1 [10] was first introduced in 
2015, YOLOv2 [11] in 2016, YOLOv3 [12] in 2018, and YOLOv4 [13] 
was developed in 2020. YOLO is a single-stage object detection model 
that performs detection in a single pass, starting with YOLOv1 with 24 
Convolution layers, improvements in YOLOv2 and YOLOv3 that uses 
Darknet-19 and Darknet-53 also able to detect 9.000 objects, and lastly 
YOLOv4 which focuses on improving performance from previous 
models with some term such as BoS (Bag of Specials) and BoF (Bag of 
Freebies). YOLOv4 itself has been widely used in research to detect the 
maturity level of various fruits such as bananas [14], cherries [15], 
citrus [16], coffee [17], strawberries [18], walnuts [19], grapes [20], 
and blueberries [21]. 
Detection of oil palm fruit maturity using YOLOv4 has also been 
carried out. In 2022, [22] used the YOLOv4 model to detect the maturity 
of oil palm fruit which is divided into two classes, namely positive and 
negative, where positive are images that have ripe FFB, and negative are 
images that do not have ripe FFB. The YOLOv4 model used is YOLOv4,

In the same year, [23] used YOLOv3, YOLOv3-tiny, YOLOv4, and 
YOLOv4-tiny models, this YOLO model was developed only to detect 
four levels of oil palm maturity. In 2023, [24] modified YOLOv4 with

From the research above, the YOLOv4 model that has been devel­
oped can already detect the maturity of oil palm fruit with an accuracy 
above 95 %. However, the hyperparameter tuning method that has been 
used can still be developed to get a more optimal hyperparameter. We 
can use optimization algorithms, such as Genetic Algorithm [25,26] 
known as GA to get optimal hyperparameters. Genetic Algorithm is an 
optimization algorithm inspired by the concept of “survival of the 
fittest” from the evolution of organisms [27] and has found extensive 
application in optimizing various parameters within neural network 
models. These parameters include the number of nodes, activation 
functions, feature selection, learning rate, momentum, neural layers, 
and other hyperparameters that can be fine-tuned to enhance the neural 
network’s performance [28–32]. Based on the background above, pre­
vious research still used manual hyperparameter tuning to detect FFB 
maturity using YOLOv4 model. Therefore, this research will use the 
YOLOv4 model, which aims to obtain the most optimal hyperparameter 
learning rate using the GA algorithm to detect the maturity of oil palm 
fruit to produce the best model in terms of efficiency and accuracy. 
Related works 
The related work in this research is a review of the previous research 
that has been conducted using YOLOv4 to detect the maturity of various 
types of fruits such as oranges, bananas, cherries, and oil palm FFB. Also, 
previous research used GA to optimize various types of hyper­
parameters. In 2021, [16] researched detecting citrus fruits’ maturity 
using the YOLOv4 method. They categorized the maturity levels into 
four groups: yellow orange, green orange, yellow-green orange, and 
rotted orange with a total of 549 labeled images, achieving an mAP of 
88.36 %. In the same year, [14] researched banana maturity by classi­
fying them into two categories: ripe and unripe with a total of 369 
labeled images. Their YOLOv4 model achieved a mAP of 87.6 %. 
Additionally, [15] performed cherry detection, dividing cherries into 
three maturity categories: immature, semi-ripe, and ripe with 400 
labeled images. The YOLOv4 model used in their study had modified 
backbones, switching from CSPDarknet53 to Densenet, and produced a 
research result with F1 Scores of 94.7 % and an improvement in mAP of 
0.15 %. 
In 2022, there was significant research focusing on fruit maturity 
assessment using the YOLOv4 technique and its variations. [19] con­
ducted a study on walnuts, utilizing 2880 training images, 320 valida­
tion images, and 400 testing images. The maturity of walnuts was 
categorized into four groups: Maturity-1 (Unripe), Maturity-2 (Mid-­
ripe), Maturity-3 (Ripe), and Maturity-4 (Over-ripe) with a total of 3600 
images. This research employed the Lightweight YOLOv4 model with 
MobileNetV3 as the feature extractor. Comparisons were made between 
Lightweight YOLOv4 and YOLOv4 modification methods. The study 
findings revealed that Lightweight YOLOv4 achieved a higher mAP of 
94.05 %, surpassing YOLOv4 by 0.06 %, a reduced file weight of 84 %, 
and 2.5 times faster. The detection of strawberry fruit maturity has also 
been studied [18] by dividing it into two maturity types, namely ripe 
and immature with a total of 112 images. The models used are also 
various variations of YOLOv4, namely YOLOv4, YOLOv4-tiny, and 
YOLOv4-scaled as well as modifications on the dataset side in the form 
of traditional augmentation and camouflage augmentation. This study 
showed that YOLOv4 camouflage augmentations got the highest mAP of 
99.97 %, 99.18 %, and 99.90 %. In the journal by [20], grape ripeness 
was also detected using YOLOv4. The ripeness levels of the grapes were 
divided into four categories: young grapes, near young grapes, near 
mature grapes, and mature grapes, with a dataset containing 1000 
labeled images. To enhance feature extraction, the YOLOv4 model un­
derwent a slight modification by replacing CSPDarknet53 with Mobi­
lenetv3 and incorporating the SEnet mechanism. This modified model 
was referred to as SM-YOLOv4. A comparison was conducted among 
several models, including YOLOv4, SE-YOLOv4, Mobilenetv3-YOLOv4, 
SM-YOLOv4, YOLOv5, YOLOv4-tiny, and Faster_R-CNN. The study 
revealed that SM-YOLOv4 achieved the highest mAP of 93.52 %. 
In the article of [17] also researched detecting maturity in coffee 
beans. The maturity was categorized into three types: immature, ripe, 
and overripe. The dataset used in the study comprised 387 image data, 
with 310 images used for training and 77 images for testing. Four YOLO 
models, namely YOLOv3, YOLOv3-tiny, YOLOv4, and YOLOv4-tiny, 
were employed in the experiments. For each YOLO model, several ex­
periments were performed by varying the input sizes, specifically 320, 
416, 512, 608, 704, and 800 pixels. The results indicated that YOLOv4 
with an input size of 800 × 800 pixels achieved the highest mAP of 81 %. 
[21] researched blueberries, categorizing them into two datasets based 
on color and ripeness. The color categories consisted of green, red, and 
blue, while the ripeness categories included ripe and immature. Ripe 
blueberries were categorized as blue, while green and red blueberries 
were considered unripe. The study utilized six YOLO models: YOLOv3, 
YOLOv3-SPP, 
YOLOv3-tiny,

YOLOv4-tiny. The research findings indicated that YOLOv4 achieved the 
highest mAP in both datasets, obtaining 88.12 % and 79.79 % for 
datasets with two and three categories, respectively. 
Several research studies have been conducted on oil palm fruit. A 
study by [22] focused on oil palm fruit and organized the image dataset 
into positive and negative categories. In this context, positive referred to 
images containing ripe oil palm fruit, while negative represented images 
without ripe oil palm fruit. The total dataset used in the research was 
490 images, with 240 images in the positive and 250 in the negative 
datasets. The researchers employed various versions of the YOLOv4 
model, namely YOLOv4, YOLOv4-CSP, and YOLOv4-tiny, and adjusted 
the input sizes to 512 × 512 pixels and 608 × 608 pixels, respectively. 
During testing, the YOLOv4-CSP model achieved the highest mAP of 
96.43 %, while during field testing, the YOLOv4 model obtained an mAP 
of 87.9 %. During the same year, [23] researched oil palm fruit with four 
maturity levels: immature, underripe, ripe, and overripe. The study used 
various versions of the YOLO model, including YOLOv3, YOLOv3-tiny, 
YOLOv4, and YOLOv4-tiny. Among these models, YOLOv4 achieved 
the highest accuracy of 98.7. 
In 2023, [9] conducted research using YOLOv4 to detect six maturity 
categories of oil palm fruit, which included empty, under-ripe, 
abnormal, ripe, un-ripe, and overripe. The research utilized a video 
dataset, and three variations of the YOLOv4 model were employed: 
YOLOv4-CSPDarknet53, YOLOv4-tiny, and YOLOv4-Tiny 3L. The 
training process included data augmentation, hyperparameter tuning, 
and frozen layers. The YOLOv4-Tiny 3L model achieved the highest 
results during testing, with a mAP (mean Average Precision) of above 70 
%. [24] used the YOLOv4 model with various hyperparameter tuning in 
width and height, specifically 320 × 320 pixels, 416 × 416 pixels, and 
512 × 512 pixels. They used a dataset containing a total of 4160 images 
and classified the oil palm fruit into six maturity categories: empty, 
under-ripe, abnormal, ripe, un-ripe, and overripe. The research results 
demonstrated that all versions of the YOLOv4 model achieved a mAP 
higher than 99 %.

Proposed methods 
In this research, the proposed method consists of five stages which 
can be seen in Fig. 1. Starting from planning, a literature review was 
carried out on previous research for YOLOv4 model described in Chapter 
2, followed by research problem formulation. The next stage is Initia­
tion, where the YOLOv4-tiny model is modified to have an Early Stop­
ping mechanism, hyperparameter setting, and dataset configuration. 
The third stage is Model Training, where the baseline model: YOLOv4- 
tiny and YOLOv4-tiny ES (with Early Stopping) models, is trained and 
evaluated. The fourth stage is Hyperparameter Optimization, where 
Genetic Algorithm is applied to the baseline model to get the optimal 
learning rate hyperparameter. After that, the baseline model will be 
retrained with the new learning rate and evaluated. The last stage is 
Model Testing, where the YOLOv4-tiny and YOLOv4-tiny ES models will 
be tested using a testing dataset and the results will be reported. So in 
total, this research will consist of four models: YOLOv4-tiny, YOLOv4- 
tiny ES, YOLOv4-tiny after GA, and YOLOv4-tiny ES after GA. The 
models are trained and tested using Google Colaboratory Pro + with 
NVIDIA A100-SXM4–40GB GPU. 
YOLOv4 model and early stopping 
YOLOv4 Model used in this research is YOLOv4-tiny, the small 
version of YOLOv4 developed for faster training and detection but 
usually has lower accuracy. YOLOv4 architecture consists of three parts: 
Backbone, Neck, and Head, where each part’s function is extracting 
features, collecting feature maps, and detecting class and bounding box, 
respectively. The YOLOv4-tiny architecture has fewer layers and the 
head only uses two layers for detection whereas YOLOv4 has three 
layers. The architecture is shown in Fig. 2. In this research, YOLOv4-tiny 
baseline models will be configured using the hyperparameter in Table 1 
according to recommendations from [13] to get the best results. YOLOv4 
and YOLOv4-tiny do not have an Early Stopping mechanism, a method 
used to reduce computational costs on model training. Therefore, this 
research will use Early Stopping inspired by previous research from [33] 
that uses patience and counter parameter where patience is used for the 
tolerance value of the reduction in the mAP model. The counter is a 
value of how many times the model has decreased mAP compared to the 
best mAP during the training process. If the counter value has reached 
the patience value, then the model training process will be stopped. Still, 
if the counter value is smaller than the patience value, then the training 
will continue. With the Early Stopping method, YOLOv4-tiny model 
training using GA can be accelerated, reduce cost, and get optimal mAP. 
There will be two baseline models: YOLOv4-tiny and YOLOv4-tiny with 
Early Stopping using a 5 patience parameter value.

Genetic algorithm 
The Genetic Algorithm consists of stages: initial population, fitness 
evaluation, selection, crossover, and mutation. The initial Population is 
the value of individuals or genes that will be created randomly, where 
the higher the individual the better the solution, fitness evaluation is 
where all the genes will be evaluated in this research. The fitness eval­
uation will be the mAP of the model. Selection is to select several best 
genes based on the fitness evaluation, the crossover is where the best 
genes that have been selected will be crossed over to have better genes, 
and mutation is the stage where the new genes will have a mutation to 
increase the search of finding a better solution. The stages of GA and 
Parameter will be used for GA as shown in Fig. 3 and Table 2, 
respectively. 
The initial population is a random value of learning rate within the 
range of 0.01 – 0.0001, this value is based on hyperparameter tuning of 
previous research [9]. The population initiation value defines how many 
individuals or genes (learning rate) will be created randomly, the 
crossover and mutation probability will determine whether the genes 
will be crossed over and mutated, GA will keep training and evaluating 
the model until the max generation or iteration is achieved. The selec­
tion mechanism for what genes will be crossover or mutation is that the 
genes that have been evaluated will be masked with a random float 
value ranging from 0 to 1. If the masked value is lower than the prob­
ability, the genes will be selected for crossover and mutated. In the case 
of mutation, the new mutation will be the new random value of the 
hyperparameter (learning rate). (Fig. 4) 
Simple Arithmetic Crossover is a method by combining the elements 
of two-parent individuals using a mixing ratio (alpha), alpha determines 
how much influence each parent has on the offspring. The alpha ratio 
value itself is a randomly generated float number in the range of 0.00 – 
1.00, so when a gene is selected to be crossover, it will be divided into 
head and tail where the divided point will be a random value between 
0 and the length of the gene. The crossover will be done on the tail side 
using each parent’s tail side to create a new offspring or genes, an 
example can be seen in Fig. 5. 
Dataset 
The dataset used in this research is an image of oil palm fruit that was 
used in previous research [24], which was collected for two months from 
12.00 to 13.00 PM from several palm oil mills in South Kalimantan, 
Indonesia. This dataset has six maturity classes, namely empty, 
under-ripe, abnormal, ripe, un-ripe, and overripe with a resolution of 
416 × 416 which can be seen in Fig. 6. 
This dataset is image data that has been annotated using DarkLabel, 
which is a tool for annotating images. DarkLabel will generate txt files 
with the same name in the image data which contain the coordinates of 
the bounding box object as shown in Fig. 7. In this context, the initial 
number represents the class ID, while the second and third numbers 
indicate the coordinates. On the other hand, the fourth and fifth 
numbers specify the dimensions of the bounding box, namely its length 
and height. The image data in this dataset also has multi labels in an 
image, where the image may have more than one label class as seen in 
Fig. 7, where the image is annotated with four label classes. The label 
class mapping and classes distribution can be seen in Table 3. The total 
amount of images data in this dataset is 4160 images, divided into three 
parts, where 2908 for training, 835 for validation, and 417 for testing.

Conclusion 
This research involved conducting experiments on the YOLOv4-tiny 
model and the Genetic Algorithm to achieve a highly accurate model. 
The results of this research are an mAP improvement of YOLOv4-tiny 
compared to previous research and before applying the Genetic Algo­
rithm, the mAP improved by 0.1 % and 0.5 %, and succeeded in 
obtaining the optimal hyperparameter learning rate with value of 
0.007465. 
Employing Genetic Algorithm to optimize hyperparameters in 
YOLOv4 model can be computationally intensive due to its repetitive 
nature aimed at obtaining optimal results. Future investigations could 
explore Genetic Algorithms to find other optimal hyperparameters for
