Kiwifruit yield estimation using deep learning and binocular 
stereo vision on Android smartphones

ABSTRACT. Knowing beforehand yield of kiwifruit to be harvested leads to better logistics and decisions making in 
agricultural industry. Fruit yield estimation has been relied on time-consuming manual assessment, which is thus highly 
desirable to be automated. Deep learning has achieved outstanding results in the field of fruit detection. Binocular stereo 
vision has widely been used to measure distance and object’s size. Besides, easy-carry smartphones are getting powerful 
and often have two or more rear lenses. In this paper, deep learning and binocular stereo vision technology were employed 
to develop an Android APP for detecting and counting kiwifruits in real-time videos and calculating their sizes. Existing 
studies calculating size all used two same lenses to construct a binocular stereo vision system. However, lenses on 
smartphone were different from each other, which usually included a standard lens and a wide-angle lens. This paper first 
got intrinsic and extrinsic parameters matrix of the two lenses by calibrating them using Zhang’s calibration method, then 
generated disparity map by rectifying image pairs and SGBM stereo matching algorithm, and final calculated kiwifruit size. 
A lightweight model trained by SSD with quantized MobileNetV2 and a detection line method were used to detect and count 
kiwifruits in the images. Results showed that the APP obtained average counting error rate of 15.2% and counting speed of 
30 ms per image. Average depth and diameter error between the APP and manual measurement was 6.92% and 6.58%, 
respectively. These results indicated that the proposed Android APP is promising for kiwifruit yield estimation. 
Keywords. Android; binocular stereo vision; deep learning; yield estimation

Introduction 
China is the largest cultivator and producer of kiwifruits in the world. In 2018, the cultivation area of kiwifruits in China 
exceeded 1.68 × 105 ha with the yield of 2.04 million tons. Within China, Shaanxi Province has the highest yield of 
approximately 9.48 × 105 tons from a cultivation area of 5.3 × 104 ha in the same year (UN Food & Agriculture Organization, 
2020). With such a large cultivation area and yield, knowing beforehand the amount and size of fruit to be harvested leads 
to better logistics and decisions making in the agricultural industry. 
Fruit yield estimation will allow growers to plan fruit storage and sales more precisely and has been relied on time-
consuming manual counting and measurement, which is thus highly desirable to be automated. Fruit yield estimation before 
harvest is a crucial step to predict the required resources for workers such as packing and storage houses for the harvest and 
distribution resources for marketing (Dorj et al., 2017; Feng et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2020). Fruit quantity and size are two 
important indicators of fruit yield. Traditional methods for fruit counting and size measurement are primarily manual, which 
are laborious and inaccurate. Accordingly, an automated yield estimation approach is necessary. 
Deep learning as a powerful technique in the artificial intelligence field is becoming a prevalent way of fruit detection 
and counting, and achieved promising results (Fu et al., 2020a; Fu et al., 2020b; Gao et al., 2020; Majeed et al., 2020). Chen 
et al. (2019) detected and counted strawberries using Faster R-CNN (Region-based Convolutional Neural Network) with 
ResNet-50 in field, which gained counting correct rate of 84.1%. Hani et al. (2020) combined U-Net with Faster R-CNN for 
apple detection and counting in an orchard and reported counting accuracy of 97.8%. Gan et al. (2020) applied Faster R-
CNN and Lucas-Kanade optical ﬂow tracking method to detect and count citrus in an orchard, which obtained counting 
accuracy of 96%. These studies showed that deep learning approach can detect and count lots of fruits in a complex 
background of natural environment and obtained remarkable results. However, these convolutional neural networks (CNNs) 
have too many convolutional layers and parameters, and thus require specific equipment to perform the huge calculations. 
Therefore, these CNNs has been mostly applied on desktop computers, whereas they are very inconvenient to be employed 
in orchard, especially difficult for farmers to estimate yield in orchard by themselves. 
Easy-carry smartphones are getting powerful and can already run some lightweight CNNs. He et al. (2019) detected 
oilseed rape pests using single shot multibox detector (SSD) with MobileNetV1 and InceptionV3 on an Android smartphone, 
which achieved average precisions (APs) of 67.4% and 62.9% at IoU of 0.6, respectively. Liu et al. (2019) employed SSD 
with MobileNetV1 and InceptionV3 to develop an Android APP for plant disease classification, which correctly classified 
95.02% and 95.62% of plant diseases on the PlantVillage dataset, respectively. Zhou et al. (2020) used SSD with quantized 
MobileNetV2 and InceptionV3 to detect kiwifruits on an Android smartphone, which reached detection rate of 89.7% with 
detection speed of 103ms per image. The above studies proved that SSD with MobileNet can be run on smartphones. 
Binocular stereo vision has been widely applied in object size measurement. Liu et al. (2018) used binocular stereo vision 
and structure from motion vision algorithm to obtain tree information, which reached measurement accuracy of tree diameter 
was 97.11%, and the accuracy of tree height measurement was 94.72%. Li et al. (2020) utilized binocular stereo vision to 
measure size of fish by reconstructing three-dimensional (3D) information of fishes, which obtained average estimation 
errors of length and width were about 4.7% and 9.2%, respectively. In addition, a rear camera with double or more lenses is 
embedded in most of the recent smartphones, thus potentially enabling binocular stereo vision. 
In this study, a kiwifruit yield estimation Android APP based on deep learning and binocular stereo vision techniques has 
been developed. An SSD-MobileNetV2 model was employed for kiwifruit detection and counting. Binocular stereo vision 
system constructed by rear lenses of smartphone was used to calculate kiwifruit size. The performance of the APP was 
evaluated by counting error rate, depth error rate, and diameter error rate. 
Materials and methods 
Image acquisition 
To obtain depth information of objects in images, binocular stereo vision is often considered to be used. Given the recent 
spread of a quite large amount of dual lenses camera smartphones, it is quite clear that using two lenses of smartphone to 
construct stereo vision system shall be considered (Konrad and Padmanaban, 2017). However, to the best of our knowledge, 
until now most of the smartphone producers did not provided complete access to the dual lenses cameras of their devices, 
hence making hard the development of smartphone-based binocular stereo vision system. Thanks to the Multi-Camera API 
released by Google, a binocular image acquisition APP was developed by our team. The interface of the APP is shown in 
Fig. 1.

Binocular image data were collected by a smartphone (Huawei P30, Huawei Inc., Shenzhen, China) in an orchard under 
different illumination conditions. A tripod was placed at around 1 m underneath the kiwifruit canopy. The smartphone was 
fixed on the tripod with its rear camera facing up the canopy to collect images. Huawei P30 is provided of a standard and a 
wide-angle lens. Binocular images were taken in late October 2020 during the harvesting season on the most common 
cultivar ‘Hayward’ at Meixian Kiwifruit Experimental Station (34°07'39''N, 107°59'50''E, and 648 m in altitude) of the 
Northwest A&F University, Shaanxi, China. In total, 800 image pairs (400 pairs each in the morning and afternoon) with 
1,920 × 1,080 pixels were collected, as shown in Fig. 2. To train deep learning model, ground truth kiwifruit targets were 
manually annotated in the 800 original images collected by the standard lens using rectangular annotations and saved in 
‘xml’ files.

Kiwifruit detection by SSD with MobileNetV2  
Meta-architecture of SSD 
SSD has fast speed and high accuracy for detecting objects by a single deep neural network. Feature extraction network, 
SSD feature layers and detection layer are the main components of SSD. For this study, MobileNetV2, were employed as 
the feature extraction networks to extract kiwifruit features. The speciﬁc implementation process of SSD is shown in Fig. 3. 
Firstly, SSD feeds input kiwifruit images through the feature extraction network and SSD feature layers to generate feature 
maps at different scales for the detection layer. Then, the detection layer extracts anchor boxes from different scale feature 
maps for kiwifruit detection. Lastly, SSD uses the detection layer to produce a series of predicted bounding boxes and 
confidence scores for kiwifruits that exist in each anchor box, and then generates the final output images. As shown in Fig. 
3, an essential character of SSD is that each feature layer is connected to the detection layer. Furthermore, no resample of 
feature maps is required, which enabling SSD to operate in an entire feedforward manner.

Feature extraction network: MobileNetV2 
MobileNetV2 is a network released by Google in 2018, designed for mobile and embedded devices (Sandler et al., 2018). 
Inverted residual module (IRM) is the basic and key unit of MobileNetV2. Figure. 4 showed the diagram of MobileNetV2 
that includes 17 IRMs. A complete IRM is ﬁrstly performing a pointwise convolution to expand the number of feature map 
channels to 6 times of the input number, and then operating a depthwise convolution and another pointwise convolution. 
The depthwise convolution changes the width and height of the input feature maps (Yu et al., 2020). In the second pointwise 
convolution, the number of feature map channels is decreased to the input number (Buiu et al., 2020). Moreover, batch 
normalization (BN) layers are attached to each convolution layer to improve the model convergence speed and avoid 
gradient explosion (Liu and Wang, 2020). ReLU6 (rectiﬁed linear unit 6) non-linearity activation function increases the 
sparsity of the network and reduces the interdependence between parameters, thus being widely used after BN layers 
(Krizhevsky et al., 2012). However, the ReLU6 function will lead to signiﬁcant information loss in the case of low-
dimensional input (Wang et al., 2020). Therefore, the feature maps are directly passed to the next convolutional layer without 
using a RuLU6 after the second pointwise convolutional layer and the BN layer. 
 
Fig. 4 Diagram of MobileNetV2, which includes 17 inverted residual module (IRM). 
Network training 
The training hardware included a desktop computer equipped with a CPU of Intel Core i5 6400 (2.7 GHz), 16 GB of 
RAM, and an NVIDIA GTX 1080 8GB GPU, running on a Windows 10 64-bit system. The software tools included CUDA 
9.0, CUDNN 7.5, Python 3.6, and Microsoft Visual Studio 14.0. The experiments were implemented on the TensorFlow 
platform. 
BN was applied after every layer, and the standard weight decay was set to 0.00004. The learning rate decayed by 0.05 
per epoch with the initial learning rate of 0.003, and a batch size of 12. Two hundred thousand iterations were set to analyze 
the training process. 
The model was trained based on pre-trained models that have been trained the COCO (Common Objects in COntext) 
dataset to reduce the training time. TensorFlow provides a fine-tuning strategy to use the transfer learning to retrain the ﬁnal 
layer of the feature extraction network for new objects (Majeed et al., 2020a; Zhang et al., 2020). In our experiment, the 
fine-tuning strategy was applied to decrease the time taken to train from scratch by taking a fully trained model for COCO 
dataset and retraining for new classes from existing weights. 
Kiwifruit counting 
A detection line method was utilized to realize real-time video kiwifruit counting. While counting kiwifruits, an 
experimenter walked straight ahead in the orchard with a selfie stick fixed a smartphone below the kiwifruit canopy. 
Therefore, it was often the situation that every kiwifruit passed through the detection line once. When center point of 
detection box of a detected kiwifruit passes over the detection line, the kiwifruit will be counted otherwise not. 
Kiwifruit size calculating 
Binocular camera calibration 
Cameras need to be calibrated for calculating intrinsic parameters and extrinsic parameters. We adopted a calibration 
toolbox that used Zhang’s calibration method (Zhang, 2000) to calibrate the lenses. This approach uses a pattern of the

the chessboard at each scene by changing the chessboard orientation were captured, as shown in Fig. 5. Then, grid corners 
were extracted as the calibration pattern by applying the toolbox.

Distortion rectification 
Distortion rectification aims to remove the lens distortions and turn the image pairs into a standard conﬁguration in which 
the optical axes of the two cameras are parallel and the image planes are row-aligned. As shown in Fig. 2 (b), there was ﬁsh-
eye effect in the image. The distortion rectiﬁcation used the parameters that were acquired in the previous step. Firstly, the 
wide-angle images were rectified to correct lens distortion. Then, image pairs were normalized to obtain similar image views 
by compensating the different focal lengths. Lastly, image pairs were cropped to remove the severe distortion area around 
the image boundary. 
Disparity map generation 
Disparity map generated by means of binocular stereo matching, which ﬁnds corresponding points between the two 
images. Because the accuracy of disparity image determines the accuracy of disparity map, the stereo matching algorithm 
plays a key role in the binocular stereo vision system. SGBM (semi-global matching method) algorithm (Hirschmuller, 
2008) was used to implement binocular stereo matching, which utilized mutual information of image pairs rectified in the 
last step for pixel matching and approximated a global two-dimensional smoothness constraint.

APP development 
The kiwifruit yield estimation APP contains two functions, which are kiwifruit counting and size calculating. The APP 
development environment is based on the Windows 10 operating system and Java language and is configured by Android 
Studio 4.0.1, JDK 1.8.0. 
The initial interface of the kiwifruit counting function is shown in Fig. 7. The interface includes an image display unit, a 
red detection line, an album button, a video button, and a text display unit. Users can use camera of smartphone for real-
time counting, or choose an image or a pre-recording video to be counted by the two buttons. The kiwifruit counting results 
will be shown in the image display unit and the text display unit immediately.

Results and discussion 
Kiwifruit counting 
Performance of detection line counting method 
The detection line counting method obtained an acceptable performance. The APP counted the number of kiwifruits pass 
through the detection line, and displayed the counting results in the image display unit and the text display unit, as shown 
in Fig. 9. To demonstrate the performance of the proposed method, we tested three real-time videos with durations of about 
one minute. CNs was compared with the ANs in the three real-time videos. As reflected in Table 1, the average CER is 
15.2%. The reason why leaded to such high average CER was that some repeatedly detected kiwifruits were also counted, 
as shown by red circle in Fig. 9. There was a similar result for kiwifruit counting in orchard (Mekhalfi et al., 2020), which 
gained a counting error rate of 15%.

Comparison with other studies on fruit counting 
Comparing with the previous fruit counting method, the APP showed encouraging results. Gan et al. (2020) counting 
citrus utilizing Faster R-CNN and Lucas-Kanade optical ﬂow tracking method and obtained counting accuracy of 96%. 
Mekhalfi et al. (2020) incorporated image stitching and Viola and Jones object detection algorithm to count kiwifruits, which 
acquired CER of 15%. These studies obtained better fruit counting results than our study. It was because these studies were 
working on images or frames captured from videos. However, our APP counted kiwifruits using real-time videos. In the 
aspect of counting speed, Gan et al. (2020) and Mekhalfi et al. (2020) spent 1.6 s per image using AMD Ryzen 3007 CPU 
and 0.18 s per image applying NVIDIA Quadro P4000, respectively. Nonetheless, the counting APP counted kiwifruits took 
only 30 ms per image on average on a Huawei P30 smartphone. 
Kiwifruit size calculating 
Performance of proposed binocular size calculating method 
The kiwifruit size calculating function was able to obtain 3D coordinate of kiwifruit and calculating kiwifruit diameter 
successfully. Calculating result display interface is shown in Fig. 10. Disparity map generated by binocular image pair, 3D 
coordinate of two points on the edges of kiwifruit and the CDia were shown in the interface. 
 
Fig. 10 Kiwifruit size measurement result display interface. 
The CDep obtained by our binocular stereo vision system was satisfactory. In order to quantitatively calculating DepER, 
we tested with 10 kiwifruits at different locations in the orchard and compared their ADep to the smartphone with CDep 
calculated by the proposed method. The ADeps between kiwifruits and smartphone varied in the range of 800 mm to 1102 
mm and were measured by laser rangefinder. Test results of depth are tabulated in Table 2. The DepER is deﬁned as the 
difference between the actual depth and the depth computed by triangulation. As shown in Table 2, it can be observed that 
the DepER of the sixth and seventh kiwifruit are 17.92% and 12.67%. Some pixel points mismatched in the binocular stereo 
matching process, which was the main reason leading to the quietly high DepER. The average DepER reaches 6.92%. There 
was a similar result in Yu et al. (2016), which obtained average depth error of 6%. Additionally, Yu et al. (2016) used two 
same RGB cameras to construct binocular stereo vision system. Therefore, the DepER of our system is tolerable.

The proposed binocular size calculating method gained content results. The error of 3D coordinate computed by the 
method may influence the final size calculating results. To analyze the impact of the 3D coordinate error generated by the 
proposed binocular stereo vision, the results were examined. As aforementioned, 10 kiwifruits in different position were 
randomly selected as test samples. ADias of the 10 kiwifruits had been measured by vernier caliper. As shown in Table 2, it 
can be observed that the DiaER of the seventh kiwifruit was 16.61%. The reason was that the location of the kiwifruit was 
at edge of the image, which produced a large angle with the camera causing the calculated Euclidean distance to be not the 
ADia. The average DiaER achieved 6.58%. Similar as Li et al. (2020), which obtained average estimation error of 6.95%. 
The DiaER of our proposed method is lower than Li et al. (2020), which proved that the binocular stereo vision system was 
reliable. 
Results from other binocular size calculating method 
The precision of ours and three other binocular stereo vision system constructed by Liu et al. (2018) and Li et al. (2020) 
was concluded and analyzed. The APP were tested on images with resolution of 1,920 × 1,080 pixels on a HUAWEI P30 
smartphone. As shown in Table 2, the proposed method obtained DiaERs between 0.24% - 16.61%, and average DiaER of 
6.58%. Liu et al. (2018) employed a computer using their binocular stereo vision system to estimate tree height, achieving 
average accuracy of 95.9%. Li et al. (2020) reported average error of fish length utilizing a laptop, which acquired average 
length error of 6.95%. According to the above results, although our binocular stereo vision system had higher calculating 
error than the others, ours was constructed by a standard lens and a wide-angle lens instead of two totally same lenses. 
Moreover, our binocular stereo vision system was running on a smartphone, which was portable and efficient. Based on the 
above results, the APP has the potential for further kiwifruit yield estimation. 
Conclusion 
Fast and accurate detection of kiwifruit in orchard under natural environment is the primary technology for yield 
estimation. Deep learning has become a prevalent way of fruit counting and achieved outstanding results. Besides, easy-
carry smartphones are getting powerful and including multiple rear lenses, which enable smartphones to run deep learning 
model and develop binocular stereo vision system. 
This study developed a kiwifruit yield estimation Android APP using SSD-MobileNetV2 model and binocular stereo 
vision. The model obtained an average counting error of 15.2%. Moreover, the binocular size calculating method got a 
satisfactory performance, which only gained an average depth and diameter error of 6.92% and 6.58%. Based on the results, 
it can be concluded that the APP has a high efficiency for kiwifruit yield estimation. 
However, the performance of the kiwifruit counting and size measurement method are still insufficient. The model usually 
leads to overlapped detection boxes, which cause counting number to be higher than actual number. In addition, the SGBM 
binocular stereo matching algorithm sometimes leads to wrong depth calculating. Solving these problems will be our future 
works.
