Abstract: Due to the short time, high labor intensity and high workload of fruit and
vegetable harvesting, robotic harvesting instead of manual operations is the future. The
accuracy of object detection and location is directly related to the picking efficiency, qual-
ity and speed of fruit-harvesting robots. Because of its low recognition accuracy, slow
recognition speed and poor localization accuracy, the traditional algorithm cannot meet the
requirements of automatic-harvesting robots. The increasingly evolving and powerful deep
learning technology can effectively solve the above problems and has been widely used in
the last few years. This work systematically summarizes and analyzes about 120 related
literatures on the object detection and three-dimensional positioning algorithms of harvest-
ing robots over the last 10 years, and reviews several significant methods. The difficulties
and challenges faced by current fruit detection and localization algorithms are proposed
from the aspects of the lack of large-scale high-quality datasets, the high complexity of
the agricultural environment, etc. In response to the above challenges, corresponding
solutions and future development trends are constructively proposed. Future research
and technological development should first solve these current challenges using weakly
supervised learning, efficient and lightweight model construction, multisensor fusion and
so on.
Keywords: object detection; object localization; deep learning; computer vision; fruit-
harvesting robots
1. Introduction
With the deterioration of climatic conditions in recent years, many regions of the
world are facing extreme weather events such as droughts, high temperatures, floods and
wildfires [1,2]. In addition, the world’s population is increasing and the area of arable
land is decreasing. This requires the production of more grain, vegetables and fruits in
the agricultural system to meet the needs of the people [3,4]. Fruit-harvesting work still
requires a large number of people to perform it manually. In order to reduce costs and
improve labor efficiency, many intelligent vegetable- and fruit-harvesting robots have
been developed [5–8]. The fruit- and vegetable-harvesting environment is complex and
susceptible to rain, snow, light and other uncertain factors, which brings greater difficulties
to automated-harvesting operations. The efficient fruit- and vegetable-harvesting robot
requires accurate object recognition and three-dimensional positioning support, because
the accuracy of object detection and positioning directly affects the automated-harvesting
efficiency, the degree of crop harvest loss and whether the harvesting robot itself is damaged
by collision [9].

Since the 1990s, the harvesting robot has been rapidly developed and a large num-
ber of new methods have emerged in the field of object recognition. In the early days,
researchers used traditional digital image processing technology to achieve object de-
tection and recognition based on color (RGB, HSV, Lab, HSI), texture, shape and other
features extraction [10,11]. Thanks to advances in machine learning, some detection al-
gorithms based on K-means clustering, K-nearest neighbor clustering, Bayes Classifier,
Haar-like features and the AdaBoost algorithm, and the support vector machine (SVM) al-
gorithm have been proposed [12–18]. Deep learning, which originated from convolutional
neural networks (CNNs), can acquire more abstract categories or features of high-level
attributes [13,19]. In terms of fruit detection, some people have developed novel algorithms
for an automated vegetable- and fruit-harvesting robot based on Alex Krizhevsky networks
(AlexNets), visual geometry group networks (VGGNets), regions with convolutional neural
networks (R-CNNs), faster region-convolutional neural networks (Fast R-CNNs), faster
region-convolutional neural networks (Faster R-CNNs), the single-shot multibox detector
(SSD), You Only Look Once (YOLO) and so on [20–27].
The representative research results of object recognition and localization of picking
robots in recent years are introduced in this paper. Kapach et al. briefly described the
vision system of automatic fruit-harvesting robots, and comprehensively summarized the
strengths and weaknesses of various methods in current research areas. Most of the research
works involved the color of the object, but little research works were carried out on the
detection and recognition of objects with inconspicuous color such as bagged fruits [28]. Fu
et al. summarized the application of various RGB-D cameras and related algorithms for fruit
detection and localization, pointing out the challenges faced by vision systems based on
RGB-D cameras as well as future directions [29]. Wang et al. summarized the application of
a detection algorithm based on CNNs in fresh fruits, compared different detection methods
in different production stages and found that it possessed good application prospects [30].
Dhanush et al. provided a systematic summary of a vision system and related algorithms
used in the production and inspection of agricultural products, detailing the role played by
smart agriculture in agricultural production [31].
This paper focuses on the study of fruit detection and localization algorithms in
harvesting robots based on DL methods. Firstly, the papers were retrieved from Google
Scholar, ScienceDirect, Web of Science and other databases for the last decade using a
combination of fruit recognition, fruit detection, fruit localization, harvesting robots and
computer vision. After that, the above papers were evaluated and filtered to match the
topic. Finally, the papers were classified and analyzed based on object detection and object
localization. In this paper, the problems and challenges of fruit recognition are analyzed,
and the future development trend is predicted, in order to provide some suggestions for
future research on fruit detection and localization.
2. Fruit Detection Method
Compared with traditional algorithms, fruit detection methods based on deep learning
have obvious advantages in object classification, detection and recognition. They utilize low-
level features to form high-level features for the final tasks by introducing the multilayer
perceptron structure. Since fruits in natural scenes have unstructured features such as
random spatial distribution, presence of overlapping occlusion and diverse shapes, deep
convolutional network methods can automatically learn features from training data [32].

2.1. Dataset Preparation Method
Datasets act as a decisive role in model training and evaluation. They help DL models
gain more powerful capabilities in object recognition, classification and segmentation tasks
by training with diverse and widely covered image datasets. The first step is to collect
data through different devices. The fruit images collected in the field are processed and
analyzed in order to achieve the task of fruit detection in different scenarios, different
varieties and different growth stages. In most cases, RGB cameras are used to obtain the
color, texture, shape and other features of fruits, which is a more effective and less costly
data acquisition tool. Zhao et al. used Canon cameras and Motorola smartphones to take
pictures of grapes in Brazilian vineyards [33]. However, RGB cameras can only obtain the
color information of the object, and cannot directly obtain the depth information of the
object and the camera. To solve these problems, a number of RGB-D cameras are used to
acquire color and depth images [29]. Kaukab et al. used an Intel RealSense D455 camera to
collect images in high-density apple orchards under a number of different light conditions
and angles [34]. However, most depth cameras can only acquire data up to 3.5 m, and
longer distance data needs to be acquired through a robot such as light detection and
ranging (LiDAR) scanners. Data preprocessing is performed for the features of different
object recognition tasks, and the process includes both image augmentation, image labeling
and so on. Image augmentation techniques are used to adjust the color, brightness, contrast
etc. of the image, or to rotate, flip, crop etc. the image, so that the image input to the
network is more suitable for a specific object recognition task or to achieve the expansion
of the size of the dataset. Recently, some image enhancement based on deep learning such
as adversarial training, GAN, CycleGAN and CVAE-GAN has also been proposed [35–38].
These algorithms usually contain the generator network and the discriminator network. The
generator network is able to generate realistic enhanced images, while the discriminator
network is able to distinguish the enhanced image from the original image. Through
adversarial training of generators and discriminators, GANs can generate realistic images,
repair low-quality images, expand datasets, perform style transfer and finally achieve the
purpose of image enhancement. Image labeling, including object localization and category
information, helps the model to accurately identify and locate various objects in the image.
Object recognition tasks based on deep learning require a certain scale of datasets
for training. At present, most fruit detection methods based on deep learning are based
on supervised learning, which trains the model by inputting a certain number of images
and corresponding label files into the network to extract the features of the object and
realize the task of object recognition [39]. Supervised learning is a common method to solve
classification and regression problems. For the field fruit detection task, the training data
determines the effect of the object detection task to a certain extent, and its scale depends

on the visual complexity of the fruit image and the type of deep learning model. The data-
labeling process is time-consuming and inefficient, and for some specific recognition tasks,
the data-labeling process requires guidance from relevant experts, so it is a meaningful
problem to achieve faster and more accurate labeling of data samples and efficient training
with small amounts of data in supervised learning. The unsupervised learning model only
uses images as training data. It can avoid the above problems by learning the structure of
the data, extracting distinguishable information or features from the data and mapping the
input to a specific output, which is often used in the field of image segmentation [40].
2.2. Fruit Detection Method Based on DL
The historical evolution of different object detection algorithms based on DL is shown
in Figure 2. The object detection method based on DL can be divided into the two-stage
method (The algorithms highlighted in red in Figure 2) and the single-stage method. In the
two-stage detection method, the region proposals are generated, and then they are precisely
classified and located by a CNN. The typical methods are R-CNN, Fast R-CNN, Faster
R-CNN and Mask R-CNN [41–44]. The single-stage object detection method eliminates
the process of generating region proposals and directly generates the class probability and
position coordinates of the object, which is simpler than two-stage object detection. The
representative algorithms of single-stage object detection are SSD series, YOLO series and
RT-DERT [45–47]. The related studies on the application of DL-based detection models
in fruit harvesting are summarized in Table 1. Overall, two-stage detection methods
have higher detection accuracy and one-stage detection methods have faster real-time
detection. For some small fruits like apples, tomatoes, etc., which need to be detected in
larger quantities and require high detection speeds, it is better to use single-stage detection
algorithms for robotic harvesting. For some fruits that are heavily shaded and require high
detection accuracy such as coconuts, it is better to use two-stage detection algorithms such
as the Faster R-CNN.

2.2.1. Two-Stage Fruit Detection Method
Combining traditional machine learning methods with CNN, the two-stage fruit
detection framework is proposed to obtain as many region proposals as possible through
a selective search, and use a CNN instead of manually extracting the features of the
regions and classifying them. Due to the fine-grained processing of candidate regions,
those algorithms usually have high detection accuracy and strong robustness in complex
scenarios [65].
To enhance detection capabilities, reduce the missed detection rate of the object and
improve the robustness of the model under complex scenes such as the occlusion of the
object and the difference in the shape and size of the object, some scholars have improved
the two-stage fruit detection algorithm. Tu et al. improved the Faster R-CNN structure
by incorporating feature maps from shallower convolution feature maps for regions of
interest (ROI) pooling. Its precision and recall of detecting the small object passion fruit
with the precision, recall and F1 values were 93.10%, 88.50% and 94.60%, respectively [23].
To improve detection accuracy, Fu et al. improved the Faster R-CNN detection model by
two pre-trained network architectures (ZFNet and VGG16) and developed an apple detec-
tion algorithm for dense tree canopies. The algorithm structure is shown in Figure 3 [48].
The results displayed that the algorithm based on the VGG16 backbone network performed

0.181 s, which fully satisfied the requirements of real-time detection by the picking robot.

Instance segmentation is the novel approach in object detection that extends the ob-
ject detection framework by adding an extra branch at the end of the model, enabling
instance segmentation for each output suggestion box, allowing segmentation, recognition
and localization tasks to be carried out in parallel. As one of common instance segmen-
tation network methods, the mask region convolutional neural network (Mask R-CNN)
is composed of Faster R-CNNs and fully convolutional networks (FCNs). During the
apple picking process, leaf occlusion and fruit overlap have the most serious impact on
the accuracy of apple detection and positioning. The improvement and application of
the Mask R-CNN has largely solved the problem of difficulty in recognition caused by
fruit occlusion and overlap. To accurately and quickly detect overlapping apples, Jia et al.
reported an improved detection model on the basis of Mask R-CNN. The method combined
the ResNet and the DenseNet as the backbone network for feature extraction, which could
greatly reduce the complexity of the model, and the detection speed was faster with an
accuracy of 97.31% [49]. Fruit harvesting needs to be completed at a specific time, otherwise
it will affect the quality and storage time of fruits and vegetables. Detection algorithms for
agricultural-harvesting robots need to ensure accurate recognition in complex and changing
operating environments while ensuring image processing speed. When optimizing the
two-stage object detection algorithm, it is necessary to consider how to optimize the model
structure and the setting of model parameters while ensuring the accuracy of the model, so
that the algorithm cannot only obtain higher detection accuracy but also meet the real-time
requirements in real application scenarios [51,61,62,64,66–68].
2.2.2. Single-Stage Detection Method
Although the two-stage object detection algorithm has high detection accuracy, it
has a large number of parameters and a large amount of calculation, and the detection
speed is slow, making it difficult to complete real-time detection tasks, which affects the
working efficiency of the picking robot. In the single-stage detection algorithm, it only uses
one CNN to directly predict the classes and locations of different objects for a test image,
which greatly reduces the consumption of computing resources and improves the speed of
detection, making it very suitable for online real-time detection [69–72].
SSD model is a single-stage detection model based on the VGGNet network and the
ResNet network, which has good detection effect and fast speed in object detection and
recognition [73,74]. Liang et al. proposed a novel detection algorithm for real-time mango
detection. The algorithm was improved on the SSD model of VGG16 and ZFNet for feature
extraction network, and the accuracy of the improved algorithm was higher than that of
the Faster R-CNN. However, the layer number of the improved network structure was
deep and the number of parameters was large, resulting in a slow operation speed [56].
Yuan et al. used inception V2 network as the backbone network and combined it with the
SSD model to propose an algorithm for the detection of cherry tomatoes in a greenhouse.
The map of this model was 98.85%, which was 6.43% higher than the SSD base model [52].
Agarwal et al. developed an intelligent model for detecting mangoes on trees based on the
Darknet-19 backbone network and SSD, as shown in Figure 6. They improved the stability
of the work for different lighting conditions by augmenting the input images with data. The
mAP of the model was 92.43% in the field [26]. Magalhães et al. compared the detection
performance of SSD and YOLO models for tomatoes in greenhouses and found that the
SSD model based on the Inception v2 backbone network has better detection performance
with an F1 value of 66.15% [53].

3. Fruit Localization Method
Fruit localization is another important part of the vision system of the fruit- and
vegetable-harvesting robot. After the fruit is detected, it is necessary to accurately locate
it. In order to help the end-effector realize accurate gripping operation, it is necessary to
acquire the data of the picking point of the fruit apple, the three-dimensional position of the
fruit and the attitude. In recent years, scholars have proposed many effective localization
methods based on stereo vision system, RGB-D camera, etc. in [84–97].
3.1. Fruit Localization Method Based on the Stereo Vision System
It is common to use a binocular or multicamera combination of the stereo vision system
to localize the fruit due to the large error of the monocular camera. In the stereo vision
system, common stereo matching methods include feature-based methods, region-based
methods and so on. Feature-based methods can obtain sparse disparity maps, and dense
disparity maps can be obtained by difference estimation, but it is easily affected by fruit
occlusion, light, texture repetition, etc., and the difference estimation is computationally
large. Region-based methods can obtain dense disparity maps, but it is affected by the affine
distortion and radial distortion of the image, and it is difficult to choose the size and shape of
the pixel constraint window. Stereo vision matching methods often use binocular cameras,
which utilize the principle of triangulation to obtain three-dimensional information about
an object through the parallax of two images taken in different orientations [93]. The
schematic diagram of binocular stereo vision 3D positioning is shown in Figure 10. Liu
et al. recorded the left and right images of pineapple fruit with binocular cameras, and
obtained the accurate three-dimensional coordinates of the fruit based on the principle of
triangulation. Test results displayed that the absolute positioning error of this method was
about 25 mm and the relative positioning error was 1.17% at the distance of 1.7–2.7 m [98].
Figure 10. Schematic diagram of binocular stereo vision 3D positioning [98].
In order to accurately locate the fruit and picking points, Luo et al. used two CMOS
color cameras to calculate the cluster boundary volume by extracting the 3D spatial co-
ordinates of the detection of the pedicel and the central cut point of the grapes [87]. To
address the effects of background and fruit overlap on citrus localization, Hou et al. used
Otsu thresholding algorithms and morphological processing methods to restore the full
morphology of the fruit. In addition, they obtained the spatial localization information of
the fruit based on the Kriging algorithm and combined it with the geometric features of
citrus. The results showed that the average error of the localization algorithm was less than
2.75 mm [92]. Considering that multiple cameras can increase the perception range and
obtain more image feature information, Chen et al. established four color camera vision

systems, designed a point cloud correction algorithm to calibrate the camera system and
developed a point cloud splicing algorithm to improve the accuracy of the reconstructed
fruit surface [89].
3.2. Fruit Localization Method Based on RGB-D Cameras
RGB-D cameras can acquire color images and depth images at the same time, and
their typical representatives are structured light cameras, time of flight (TOF) cameras,
etc. Structured light cameras obtain the position and depth information of an object by
emitting an infrared laser of a specific wavelength, projecting it on the object after a certain
encoding and calculating the distortion of the returned encoded pattern. TOF cameras emit
continuous laser pulses to a target, receive the reflected light with a sensor and obtain the
distance to the target based on the round-trip time of flight of the detected light pulses [29].
When the fruit is successfully detected, its 2D information can be obtained. After that, the
distance from the camera to the fruit can be obtained from the depth information or the
point cloud information to complete the 3D localization of the fruit. Arad et al. used a
TOF camera to acquire RGB image information and depth information of bell peppers, and
obtained the localization information of the fruits based on the depth information extracted
from the detected regions through an associated pixel-world conversion procedure [95].
Li et al. proposed a localization algorithm for occluded apple fruits based on the frustum-
based point cloud processing method and YOLACT++ model. The YOLACT++ model was
used to locate the fruit in the RGB image. The frustum-based method used the output of
the mask and 2D bounding box to estimate the depth of the center of the fruit. Robotic-
harvesting results showed that the algorithm was able to reduce the average positioning
error by 43% [91]. Bortolotti et al. developed the localization algorithm for apples based
on the RGB_D images from the Intel RealSense D435i. The algorithm incorporated the
modified YOLOv5 model, the circle detection algorithm and the triangulation method
based on depth information. Experimental results displayed that the overall positioning
error of the algorithm was 5.7 mm [99]. Lin et al. proposed an algorithm for citrus detection
and localization of RGB-D images. The algorithm filtered the image and detected and
localized the citrus using the density clustering algorithm and SVM, with an average
localization error of about 13 mm in all directions [100]. Accurate positioning of green
apples is tricky due to the light, the environment and the fruit color. To address the above
problem, Sun et al. proposed a green apple localization algorithm [101]. The gradient field
of the target image was analyzed using the image depth information, and the vorticity
center and two-dimensional plane projection were built to achieve the localization of the
apple center. Then, the apple image was segmented based on the density peak clustering
algorithm, and the precise segmentation region of the target image was efficiently obtained
using the double sort algorithm. Finally, the above two results were fused to achieve
the localization of the target fruit. GAO et al. used the RANSAC algorithm to perform
spherical fitting on the point cloud of the fruit surface to obtain the center of the mass
of the fruit and improve the accuracy of fruit positioning. The standard deviation of the
radius of the sphere fitted by the algorithm for apples was 1.79 mm, and the standard
deviation of the center coordinate of the sphere was less than 2.5 mm. The algorithm has
an average time of 0.543 for apple localization, which meets the working requirements of
apple picking robots [84]. Liu et al. tested the localization performance of several RGB-D
cameras, including the ZED 2i, in a complex kiwifruit orchard. The results showed that the
RealSense D435 camera performed the best, with a localization speed of 0.062 s per image
and an average error within 19 mm [96].

3.3. Other Fruit Localization Methods
Although there are many ways to obtain image depth information, laser active vision
is a better choice considering some characteristics of the perceived image. LiDAR can
scan the entire harvest scene and provide relevant ranging information, which has been
used by researchers for agricultural automation and robotics. In order to guide the end-
effector to grasp the fruit, Tanigaki et al. used an infrared laser ranging sensor to obtain
the 3D positions of the target fruit and obstacles and then determine the trajectory of
the manipulator’s end-effector to avoid collision with obstacles [102]. Considering that
RGB cameras are susceptible to factors such as lighting conditions, Genemola et al. used
mobile terrestrial laser scanners to detect and locate apples. The positioning accuracy of
this method is comparable to that of the RGB system, but it can directly obtain the three-
dimensional information of fruit, and is not affected by the change of sunlight [86]. Zhang
et al. designed an active positioning system for high-precision fruits, which consisted of
an RGB camera, an RGB-D camera, a red line laser, and linear moving parts (Figure 11).
It can achieve high precision positioning within 0.6 m–1.2 m and the depth positioning
error is less than 4 mm [94]. Experiments have proved that it can locate apples and other
fruits more accurately than commercial RealSense cameras [97]. Liu et al. proposed a real-
time dynamic fruit detection system based on the ORB-SLAM3 and YOLOv5 algorithms.
The ORB-SLAM3 was used to estimate camera attitude in real time. LiDAR was used to

4. Challenges
The research of fruit recognition and localization based on DL has made great progress,
but the existing models still face many problems and challenges before large-scale field
application:
(1)
High-quality and large-scale fruit datasets are scarce. Datasets play an irreplaceable
role in the development of current object detection algorithms [104]. In order to obtain
better and faster detection results, large and comprehensive datasets are indispensable.
The number of data samples collected in the fruit detection is required to be large
enough for the purpose of excellent detection accuracy. To improve and validate
the robustness of the model, the images in the datasets should also contain a variety
of scenarios such as single-targeted fruits, multitargeted fruits, branch and stem
foliage occlusion, occlusion between fruits and different lighting conditions [105,106].
Fruit growth environments are mostly complex and unstructured and fruit growth is

Agronomy 2025, 15, 145
13 of 19
cyclical, and data collection must be completed within a specific time frame, which
brings great challenges to the establishment of large-scale datasets [39,70,107].
(2)
The stability, generalization and robustness of detection and localization algorithms
in complex scenarios need to be further improved. Because the area occupied by
small fruits in the image is small, the features that can be extracted after the convo-
lution operation are limited and easily lost, and some algorithms perform poorly in
dense and occluded scenes. Due to the existence of various complex and variable
scenarios in the natural environment where the automated-harvesting robot operates,
the model used for fruit detection and localization needs to have both high stability,
generalizability and robustness to ensure its stable operational effect and high op-
erational efficiency [108–114]. Therefore, improving the performance of models in
complex scenarios while ensuring the detection and localization accuracy is currently
a challenge in the field of object recognition.
(3)
There is a high degree of complexity and uncertainty in the agricultural environment.
Although CNNs based on DL can solve the object detection and recognition problem
well, the neural network has a huge demand for the training dataset, and the model
training is computationally intensive and time-consuming. In addition, although
the correct rate of object recognition is relatively high, due to the complexity and
uncertainty of the agricultural environment, the convolutional neural network still has
a large error in object localization [85,115]. As the growth state of fruits and vegetables
is affected by the environment and changes in the characteristics of the fruits and
vegetables themselves and other factors, the detection and localization algorithms
need to take into account more factors and the corresponding CNN structure will
be more complex, which will result in longer model processing times, poorer real-
time performance and lower operational efficiency of the robot [32,33]. There are
many external disturbances and obstructions in the fruit-picking and -harvesting
environment, which can cause large random errors to the machine vision localization
system, and the pattern of these errors is difficult to obtain and compensate.
5. Future Trends
(1)
Research and development of a weakly supervised model. Due to the time-intensive
and labor-intensive nature of the data collection and labeling tasks, and the difficulties
associated with large-scale data collection tasks, utilizing fewer sample data for model
training and reducing the amount of labeled data is important for reducing labor costs
and increasing the flexibility of detection. For some fruit datasets that are difficult
to collect and small in number, their intrinsic connection with common large-scale
datasets can be utilized for migration learning. Relatively few models for small-scale
datasets have been conducted on the semi-supervised and unsupervised models
for fruit detection. The data-labeling process based on supervised learning is time-
consuming, labor-intensive and inefficient. In addition, for certain specific recognition
tasks, the data-labeling process requires guidance from relevant experts. Using small-
and medium-scale data for semi-supervised learning can obtain good detection results.
Therefore, weakly supervised models will be the future development direction of fruit
recognition models [39,116–118].
(2)
Development of lightweight detection and localization model for automated fruit-
and vegetable-harvesting robot. Researchers are actively exploring lightweight tech-
nologies such as compression algorithms, quantization methods and depth-separable
convolution to reduce the computational complexity and storage space occupied by
the model, so that object detection technology can better adapt to the needs of actual
scenarios. Under the premise of accuracy, models for embedded devices not only

14 of 19
require a small model size, but also ensure speed to achieve real-time detection and
localization [119]. Therefore, the subsequent research should focus on improving the
detection and localization speed based on lightweight models such as MobileNet,
ESPNet and GhostNet on embedded devices with limited computing power [120–123].
(3)
Fruit detection and localization based on multiple sensor fusion. Stereo vision devices
provide high detection accuracy, but they are susceptible to light conditions in outdoor
environments and the vision-matching process is complex and takes a long time to
compute. LiDARs have excellent localization accuracy and high reliability compared
to other sensors, but have the disadvantages of slow speed and high cost. The use of
multisensor information fusion technology can greatly improve the detection accuracy
and reduce the localization error [6]. By extracting information from multiple sensors,
the information of the automated fruit- and vegetable-harvesting robot can be more
comprehensively obtained, and combined with the optimal object recognition and
localization algorithm, the recognition and localization accuracy can be effectively
improved with high reliability and robustness. In recent years, new visual sensors
such as TOF cameras, light field cameras and chlorophyll fluorescence cameras have
attracted more and more researchers’ attention. Researchers can expand the image
dataset of fruit and vegetable images or extract fruit signature features and make
target region judgment, then make decision fusion according to the influence of
occlusion or lighting conditions and finally merge to complete object detection and
positioning, which can effectively reduce the demand for data and the calculation
amount of the neural network, and shorten the network training time [22].
6. Conclusions
Accurate identification and precise localization of fruits is crucial for fruit-harvesting
robots. In the past, the traditional fruit detection technology had poor accuracy and a
low detection speed, which cannot meet the needs of harvesting robots for real-time fruit
detection and localization. Deep learning technology can solve the above problems and
play a unique role in automated agricultural harvesting. The methods in data collection,
data enhancement and data annotation for the dataset production are summarized and
data enhancement techniques based on DL are widely used. Currently, the stereo vision
system and RGB-D cameras are often used to localize the position of fruits, which can be
used in indoor or outdoor scenes and are less affected by changes in lighting conditions
and object features, while meeting the demand for a higher localization accuracy of picking
robots. It is shown in studies that light variations, variable crop growth states, shading
and clustering in natural environments are the main challenges for accurate recognition
and localization. Currently, deep learning algorithm training often requires a large number
of samples. In the future, researchers need to propose algorithms with higher accuracy, a
faster processing speed and lighter weight based on geometric features, image features,
etc. In addition to this, deep learning algorithms that deal with small datasets and weakly
supervised algorithms need to be further developed. Due to the complexity and uncertainty
of agricultural environments, harvesting robot vision systems still have large localization er-
rors. This requires the utilization of multisensor fusion technology and artificial intelligence
technology to improve the integrated visual error tolerance and anti-interference capability.
