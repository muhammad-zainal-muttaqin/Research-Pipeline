Abstract— Despite Indonesia's leading position in palm oil 
processing, the inaccurate assessment of oil palm fruit maturity 
poses challenges in determining the optimal harvest timing and 
maintaining 
product 
quality. 
With 
advancements 
in 
information technology, including image processing and object 
detection techniques like YOLO and EfficientDet, there is 
potential for automated and precise identification of palm fruit 
maturity. To explore this, the authors conducted research to 
identify the most suitable algorithm for detecting the ripeness of 
palm oil fruit. The study utilized a dataset consisting of 8299 
images with six maturity levels, employing augmentation 
techniques to increase image variability. Results show that 
YOLO V8s outperformed EfficientDet Lite 4 in terms of object 
detection precision, achieving higher mean average precision 
(mAP) scores. YOLO V8s showed a 4% improvement in 
mAP@50 and a 16% improvement in mAP@50-95 compared to 
EfficientDet Lite 4. YOLO V8s achieved a mAP of 0.978 for 
mAP@50 and 0.765 for mAP@50-95, surpassing EfficientDet 
Lite 4. However, EfficientDet Lite 4 had an advantage in terms 
of class accuracy and bounding box placement, as indicated by 
smaller box_loss and cls_loss values. YOLO V8s also 
demonstrated better frames per second (FPS) performance, 
ranging from 10 to 15 FPS, compared to EfficientDet Lite 4. 
Keywords— Oil Palm Fruit Bunches, YOLO, EfficientDet 
Lite, Object Detection 
I. INTRODUCTION 
Indonesia holds the distinction of being the largest palm 
oil processing country worldwide. The country has 
experienced a significant and continuous increase in palm oil 
production in recent years. According to the 2019 report by 
the United States Department of Agriculture, Indonesia stands 
as the top global producer of palm oil. In that year alone, 
Indonesia produced a staggering 42.50 million metric tons of 
palm oil, accounting for 58% of the world's palm oil 
production [1]. Malaysia follows in second place with 19 
million metric tons, contributing 26% of global palm oil 
production. 
In the palm fruit processing industry, accurate assessment 
of fruit ripeness plays a vital role in determining the optimal 
harvest time and ensuring the quality of the final product. With 
the advancements in information technology, it has become 
increasingly feasible to achieve this through automation. 
Image processing techniques and object detection offer 
effective solutions to address this challenge. Object detection, 
a subfield of computer vision, involves the application of 
computer algorithms to detect and identify specific objects. It 
enables computers to mimic human vision and recognize 
objects, making it a valuable tool in analyzing videos or 
images for various purposes. 
Object detection algorithms such as YOLO (You Only 
Look Once) and EfficientDet enable fast and accurate 
identification of the ripeness level of oil palm fruits. Each 
algorithm has its own advantages and disadvantages, and it is 
important to compare their performance in specific object 
detection tasks. In several articles, it has been noted that 
"YOLO has gained popularity in real-time object detection 
applications due to its speed. However, EfficientDet is 
emerging as an alternative to YOLO because it offers better 
speed and higher accuracy" [2]. YOLOv8 is an object 
detection algorithm developed by Joseph Redmon and Ali 
Farhadi. YOLO adopts a single-shot approach, allowing 
objects to be detected in a single pass of the image without the 
need for separate feature extraction stages [3]. YOLOv8 is one 
of the latest versions of YOLO and brings several 
improvements in performance and accuracy. This algorithm 
utilizes a convolutional neural network to perform real-time 
object detection on images or videos. YOLOv8 is designed to 
be fast, accurate, and user-friendly, making it an excellent 
choice for various tasks such as object detection, image 
segmentation, and image classification.  YOLOv8 is one of 
the versions of YOLO released by Ultralystic in 2023. 
Following the one-stage model architecture paradigm, similar 
to other YOLO variants, it utilizes fewer parameters compared 
to two-stage models, resulting in a relatively lighter model for 
training purposes. 
EfficientDet is a real-time object detection model initially 
implemented in TensorFlow and Keras, and now also 
available in PyTorch. This model utilizes the EfficientNet 
backbone and custom detection and classification networks. 
With its EfficientNet backbone, EfficientDet is designed to 
scale efficiently from small to large model sizes while 
maintaining scalability. EfficientDet Lite 4 is a variant of the 
EfficientDet object detection model architecture developed by 
Google AI. It aims to provide a lighter and more efficient 
version of EfficientDet, specifically optimized for resource-
constrained devices like mobile phones and IoT devices. 
EfficientDet Lite 4 has lower specifications compared to other 
variants of EfficientDet, including fewer options and smaller 
model sizes, enabling easier deployment on limited devices. 
The EfficientDet model was released in 2019 by the Google 
Brain team. It follows a one-stage model detector architecture 
similar to YOLO, which offers efficiency and simplicity 
compared to more flexible and accurate two-stage models. 
In this research, a comparison is made between two object 
detection models to determine the most optimal algorithm for 
detecting the ripeness of palm fruits. The YOLO and 
EfficientDet models utilize their latest versions, which may 
yield different outcomes compared to previous studies. The

objective of this study is to identify the best object detection

aspects: (1) A diverse dataset consisting of 8,299 oil palm

In a study [4], the objective was to evaluate the 
performance of ResNet, YoloV3, and TinyYoloV3 object 
detection models on Image Detection using Python. The 
experimentation aimed to gain further insights into achieving 
highly accurate object detection results in images. The study 
found that the YOLOv3 model achieved an average accuracy 
of

The research described in [5]proposed the YOLOv4 
architecture with three variations to develop a video-based 
object detection model for detecting the maturity of palm oil. 
Model development was carried out through hyperparameter 
tuning, frozen layer training, and data augmentation 
techniques, 
including 
geometric 
and 
photometric 
augmentation. To validate the effectiveness of the YOLOv4 
model, a comparison of models with the SSD-MobileNetV2 
FPN and EfficientDet-D0 architectures was performed. The 
results show that YOLOv4-Tiny 3L produces the best 
performance in real-time detection of oil palm fresh fruit 
bunch (FFB) objects, with an mAP of 90.56% in the single-
class category and 70.21% in the multi-class category. 
Additionally, the YOLOv4-Tiny 3L model also exhibits 
approximately 4 times faster detection speed than YOLOv4-
CSPDarknet53, 5 times faster than FPN SSD-MobileNetV2, 
and 9 times faster than EfficientDet-D0. 
In a study conducted by [6], the YOLOv4 model was used 
to detect objects in real-time. The study utilized data input 
directly from the camera into the deep learning model, 
specifically YOLOv4, to detect the maturity level of Fresh 
Fruit Bunches (FFB) on oil palm trees in real time. The 
research aimed to develop a model for detecting the maturity 
of oil palm FFB on trees, which could be used to activate 
harvester robots. YOLOv4 was trained using FFB images of 
ripe oil palms taken with the Intel Real-sense Camera D435, 
with a resolution level of 1920×1080. During the data 
collection process, an expert was involved to help classify the 
maturity level of the oil palm in the labeling process.  The test 
results showed that the average value of Average Precision 
(mAP) and Recall is 87.9% and 82%, respectively, with an 
Intersect over Union (IoU) value of 0.5, which met the 
threshold in 2000 iterations. The proposed model can operate 
in real-time at 21 FPS (Frames Per Second). 
In article [7], a study is described which aims to compare 
the performance of the Faster R-CNN, SSD, and YOLO 
models in recognizing or detecting the ripeness level of 
tomatoes with input from fruit images. In this study, model 
training was carried out for 5 hours to produce a total training 
loss value below 0.5. The results of the test show that the 
accuracy value of the Faster R-CNN model is better than the 
YOLO model. and the SSD model with an average accuracy 
value of 99.55%, 94.6% and 89.3%, respectively. 
Lastly, in the article [8], the objective of the study was to 
propose an efficient computer vision model for real-time weed 
detection in crops. The experiment utilized a dataset 
consisting of images of two weed species commonly found in 
the researchers' region with a temperate climate: Phalaris 
Paradoxa and Convolvulus. This image dataset was taken 
manually using a professional camera with different lighting 
conditions, from morning to noon, whether in sunny or cloudy 
weather. Four open source computer vision models that have 
been configured to detect objects were used for this test: 
Detectron2, EfficientDet, YOLO, and Faster R-CNN. Among 
the models, YOLOv5 demonstrated the best performance, 
achieving 82% precision and 30% error in just 2 hours of 
training over 200 epochs. It outperformed the other models in 
terms of both accuracy and efficiency, making it a suitable 
choice for object detection tasks. 
III. RESEARCH METHOD 
A. Dataset Preparation 
The dataset was collected by capturing videos using 
smartphones, which provided a 360-degree view of oil palm 
fruits. Subsequently, the video dataset was organized into 
folders based on different categories. In this case, the dataset 
was divided into six categories: unripe, underripe, ripe, 
overripe, abnormal, and empty fruit bunches (Figure 1). The 
data collection took place in an oil palm plantation located in 
Kalimantan. Several individuals working in the plantation 
participated in the data collection process, using their 
smartphones as cameras [9]. 
The collected dataset in the form of videos will be 
processed into image frames. This process is facilitated by the 
web platform Roboflow. Roboflow offers an upload feature 
that allows users to upload images or videos. If the dataset is 
uploaded as a video, it can be converted into image frames by 
adjusting the desired frame rate, specifying the desired 
number of frames per second, as shown in Figure 2. The total 
dataset has been converted into 8299 images.

B. Data Pre-Processing 
After converting the dataset into images, the subsequent 
steps of the process are illustrated in Figure 3. The images are 
labeled through annotation using bounding boxes and smart 
polygons to expedite the labeling process. After annotating 
and labeling the entire dataset, the next step is to split the 
dataset into training, validation, and test sets. In this case, the

for training, 20% for validation, and 10% for testing.

The dataset is divided into two versions of sizes: 640x640 
for the YOLOv8s algorithm model dataset and 1024x1024 for 
the EfficientDet Lite 4 algorithm model dataset, according to 
the documentation of each algorithm. Augmentation is applied 
to increase the variation of images. According to a study 
conducted by researchers at the University of California [10], 
data augmentation can help improve object detection 
performance by introducing variations in the training dataset 
and reducing overfitting. 
In this study, two augmentation methods provided by 
Roboflow were used for both versions of the dataset, namely 
90-degree rotation and rotation (Figure 4). The augmentation 
process resulted in an increased dataset size of 19,843 images. 
The dataset export process is adjusted according to the chosen 
algorithm model.

C. Model 
The models used in this research are described below:  
1. YOLO V8 
YOLOv8 is one of the latest versions of YOLO and comes 
with several improvements in terms of performance and 
accuracy. This algorithm employs a convolutional neural 
network to perform real-time object detection in images or 
videos. Like its earlier model, YOLOv8 utilizes a one-
stage model, dividing the image into regions and making 
predictions for bounding boxes and probabilities in each 
region. The probabilities associated with each bounding 
box are then used to classify whether it contains an object 
or not [3].  YOLOv8 is an advanced and state-of-the-art 
(SOTA) model that builds upon the successes of previous 
YOLO versions by introducing new features and 
enhancements to improve performance and flexibility. 
Like YOLOv5, its architecture includes a backbone, head, 
and neck (Figure 5). With a new and improved backbone 
consisting of convolutional layers, as well as a more 
sophisticated detection head, YOLOv8 has become a 
leading option for real-time object detection.  Moreover, 
YOLOv8 supports the latest computer vision algorithms, 
such as instance segmentation, enabling detection of 
multiple objects in images or videos. The model uses the 
faster and more precise Darknet-53 backbone network, 
replacing the previous YOLOv7 network. In YOLOv8, 
bounding boxes are predicted through an anchor-free 
detection head. The model's larger feature map and 
enhanced convolutional network contribute to its 
improved precision and speed.  Furthermore, YOLOv8 
incorporates feature pyramid networks to recognize 
objects of varying sizes effectively. Its user-friendly API 
makes it easy to implement in various applications, 
adding to its appeal and usability. Overall, YOLOv8 is a 
cutting-edge model that represents a significant 
advancement in real-time object detection.  
The model version used in this research is YOLOv8s. 
The YOLOv8s model can run smoothly on mobile 
devices, both Android and iOS. Besides the YOLOv8s 
model, the models that can be executed on mobile devices 
currently are YOLOv8n and YOLOv8m.

2. EfficientDet Lite 
EfficientDet is an object detection model that leverages 
EfficientNet as its backbone network and is enhanced with 
the integration of the Bidirectional Feature Pyramid 
Network (BiFPN) as a feature network [2] (as depicted in 
Figure 6). BiFPN incorporates 3 to 7 features from the 
backbone network and iteratively applies top-down and 
bottom-up bidirectional feature fusion. The outcomes of 
this feature integration are then fed into the network class 
and network box, enabling the model to generate object 
class predictions and bounding box predictions. 
 
 
Figure 6. EfficientDet architecture 
EfficientDet-Lite is a variant of EfficientDet, specifically 
trained on the COCO 2017 dataset, and optimized for 
Tensorflow Lite, making it well-suited for deployment on 
mobile devices.  The model version used in this research 
is EfficientDet-Lite 4. EfficientDet-Lite 4 is the most high-
performing and closest in performance to YOLOv8s 
among the efficientdet lite models. 
 
 
D. Evaluation Method 
In the context of comparing EfficientDet Lite-4 with 
YOLOv8s, there are several commonly used metrics to

compare the performance of both models in object detection 
tasks. Some of the commonly used metrics include: 
1. mAP/AP 
This evaluation method is the most used method in object 
detection research. mAP measures the accuracy of object 
detection based on precision and recall, calculated from the 
predicted object locations and labels. This method is suitable 
for use in multi-label datasets such as palm fruit ripeness 
detection, where objects can have multiple ripeness labels. 
mAP (mean average precision) is the average of AP. In some 
contexts, AP is calculated for each class and then averaged. 
However, in some contexts, they have the same meaning. 
According to COCO in its article [11], "AP is averaged over 
all categories. Generally, this is called 'mean average 
precision' (mAP). We do not distinguish between AP and 
mAP (as well as AR and mAR) and consider the difference 
clear from the context." Therefore, it can be concluded that 
AP and mAP are the same and can be used as a comparative 
metric. 
There are two commonly used mAP/AP metrics: 
mAP/AP 0.5 and mAP/AP 0.5-0.95. mAP 0.5 is the average 
of the average precision (AP) across all object classes when 
the Intersection over Union (IoU) is greater than 0.5. On the 
other hand, mAP 0.5-0.95 is the average of the average 
precision (AP) across all object classes when the IoU is in 
the range of 0.5 to 0.95 with a 0.05 interval. 
 
Figure 7. Measurement used to characterize object detector 
performance on COCO [12]. 
According to the COCO mAP (Figure 7), an interpolated 
definition of AP is used, computed at 101 points. COCO 
defines AP as the average of several IoU (Intersection over 
Union) values, which determines the minimum IoU required 
for considering a positive match. AP [.5:.95] represents the 
average AP for IoU values ranging from 0.5 to 0.95 with a 
0.05 interval. In the COCO competition, AP is calculated as 
the average of 10 IoU thresholds across 80 categories 
(AP@[.50:.05:.95]), covering the range of IoU from 0.5 to 
0.95 with a 0.05 interval [12]. 
2. cls_loss 
Cls loss measures the prediction error in classifying 
objects for each anchor or bounding box prediction. It 
typically uses loss functions such as Cross-Entropy Loss 
to compute the difference between the predicted 
probabilities and the true class labels. 
3. box_loss 
box loss quantifies the error in estimating the location 
and size of the bounding box. One commonly used 
method for box loss is Smooth L1 Loss or Huber Loss, 
which assigns different weights to small and large 
errors. In short, the box loss measures how accurate the 
predicted bounding box coordinates are compared to the 
ground truth coordinates. The lower the difference 
between the predicted bounding box and the ground 
truth, the lower the value of the box loss.

V. CONCLUSION 
The research findings indicate that YOLO V8s 
outperforms EfficientDet Lite 4 in terms of fps and all mAP 
evaluations, including mAP 50, mAP 50-95, and class-
specific mAP. This demonstrates the superiority of YOLO 
V8s in terms of accuracy and performance. However, 
EfficientDet Lite 4 also has advantages in terms of model size, 
cls_loss, and box_loss. Although the model size difference is 
only 1.9mb, this advantage may not be significantly impactful. 
This is because the model size of YOLO V8s is already small, 
making it easy to implement on mobile devices. 
In conclusion, the comparison between the YOLO V8s 
and EfficientDet Lite 4 models reveals that YOLO V8s excels 
in object detection precision and real-time detection 
performance, while EfficientDet Lite 4 excels in class 
accuracy and bounding box placement based on the values of 
Box_loss and Cls_loss. To achieve the best model for palm 
fruit maturity detection, it is important to consider specific 
application requirements. If precision and real-time object 
detection are the main concerns, YOLO V8s may be the 
preferred choice. However, if bounding box placement and
