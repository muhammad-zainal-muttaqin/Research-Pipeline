Abstract-In recent years, the utilization of deep learning 
technology for fruit yield prediction methods using robot 
dogs equipped with cameras, along with target detection 
and tracking algorithms, has emerged as a research focus. 
However, the movement of robot dogs through undulating 
orchard terrain often induces vibration in the mounted 
cameras, which can significantly impact the performance of 
tracking algorithms. To address this issue, this paper 
proposes a tracking algorithm integrated with a camera 
motion compensation module. This module applies image 
rotation, scaling, and translation parameters to the 
tracking algorithm, enhancing its performance and 
counting accuracy in vibrated scenes. Utilizing nectarine 
data collected by a robot dog equipped with a gimbal 
camera, the proposed algorithm achieves a Multiple Object 
Tracking Accuracy (MOT A) of 63.4%. In ablation 
experiments, integrating the camera motion compensation 
module into the tracking algorithm improves MOTA by 
2.9%. These results validate the efficacy of the proposed 
algorithm in mitigating the effects of camera vibration and 
enhancing tracking performance in dynamic orchard 
environments 
Keywords-fruit count, target tracking, robot dog, camera 
motion compensation

INTRODUCTION 
With the modernization and intelligent development 
of agricultural production, orchard management has put 
forward higher requirements for the accuracy and 
efficiency of fruit counting. Yield statistics through fruit 
counting plays a crucial role in the development of fruit 
harvest operation plans and marketing strategies [l]. At 
the present stage, accurate estimate of orchard yields 
before harvesting fruits helps managers to rationally 
allocate labor according to the distribution of fruits in the 
park [2]; in the future, it helps to deploy harvesting robots 
working strategies to efficiently complete the harvesting 
tasks and realize intelligent production in the orchard. 
Traditionally, the prediction method using manual 
counting usually relies on manual field observation and 
counting, and then prediction is made based on the 
observation results. The problem with this method is that

With the development of computer vision, the method of

robot dogs have been widely used in the fields of

development of smart agriculture [8]. The application of 
this technology demonstrates the trend of digitalization 
and automation in the field of agriculture, which helps to 
improve the efficiency of orchard management and 
promote the development of agriculture towards 
intelligence and sustainability. Therefore it is of far-
reaching research significance to utilize smart mobile 
devices to improve the efficiency of orchard counting 
while improving the counting accuracy in complex 
orchard scenarios. 
Currently, the methods for fruit counting are mainly 
based on the fruit videos collected by cameras, and the 
detection algorithms and tracking algorithms of deep 
learning are utilized for fruit counting [9]-[12]. 
With the development of deep learning, detection and 
tracking algorithms are becoming more and more mature, 
the counting work using detection and tracking methods 
has become a mainstream method nowadays, there is no 
expensive equipment cost at the same time, but also real-
time detection and tracking counting can be carried out on 
the limited resources of the equipment. Parico et al. [9]

frame-by-frame detection and tracking of fruity pears and 
finally count the number of fruits. Wang et al. [10] 
proposed a mango counting method for multi-target 
tracking, using Mango YOLO to detect the fruit targets in 
each frame, then Kalman filtering and Hungarian 
matching algorithm to realize the tracking offruit targets,

In current fruit counting studies, when utilizing smart 
mobile devices such as robot dogs to collect data in 
orchards, the camera lenses often experience vibrating 
due to walking in complex terrain. Existing mainstream 
counting methods, namely detection and tracking 
methods, mostly rely on Kalman filtering algorithms and 
Hungarian matching algorithms to match and correlate 
the detected results. Kalman filters are essentially linear 
gaussian systems and are not suitable for predicting fruit 
positions in vibrated scenarios. In complex orchard 
environments, camera movements lead to decreased 
tracking performance, affecting the accuracy of the final 
count. Therefore, this paper addresses the issue that 
existing Kalman filters are not suitable for predicting fruit 
positions in vibrated scenarios, which affects the 
performance of the tracker and the accuracy of the final 
count. This paper proposes a fruit tracking algorithm 
based 
on 
camera 
motion 
compensation, 
named 
StableSort-CMC (Camera Motion Compensation). This 
algorithm combines camera motion compensation 
algorithms with Kalman filters. Traditional image 
registration techniques are used to obtain camera motion 
parameters for calibrating and updating the predicted 
values of the Kalman filter, ultimately achieving fruit 
counting in vibrated environments. 
II. 
METHOD 
The methodology of this paper is mainly divided into 
two parts. The first part introduces the camera motion 
compensation algorithm, which innovatively addresses 
1699 
the issue of inaccurate counting caused by vibrating in 
actual orchard scenes by combining detection and 
tracking algorithms. The second part outlines the overall 
process of fruit counting method. In this part, the input 
fruit image sequence data is obtained by cutting frames 
from the collected fruit videos, thus obtaining continuous 
fruit image data. Through three modules: camera motion 
compensation module, detection module, and tracking 
module, the number of fruits contained in the fruit image 
sequence is obtained. The overall process is illustrated in 
Fig. 1. 
A. Camera Motion Compensation Algorithm 
The tracking method based on detection results is 
essentially a data association process, where the detection 
results of the previous frame are used to determine 
whether the fruits in the next frame are associated with 
those in the previous frame. Fruits that are associated are 
assigned the same ID to represent the same fruit. For 
newly appearing fruits, it is first determined whether they 
have appeared in previous frames. If they have, they are 
assigned the previous ID to prevent duplicate counting. If 
not, a new ID is assigned. The output of the data 
association, i.e., the tracking algorithm, is the result of 
Hungarian matching. The input matching algorithm 
includes the predicted position information from the 
Kalman filter and the detected position information from 
the next frame. Therefore, to obtain ideal tracking results, 
the predicted values of the Kalman filter are crucial. 
In scenarios without vibration, such as uniform 
motion, the Kalman filter typically exhibits good 
predictive ability because it is essentially a linear 
Gaussian system. In such scenarios, the image sequences 
captured by the camera only undergo translation. 
However, in vibrated scenarios, the image sequences also 
undergo rotation and scaling transformations, which are 
difficult for the Kalman filter to predict. Therefore, this 
paper proposes using a camera motion compensation 
algorithm to calculate the rotation, scaling, and translation 
transformations between image 
sequences. 
These 
transformations are then incorporated into the Kalman 
filter to enhance its predictive accuracy.

B. Overall Method of Counting 
The counting process of this paper is shown in Fig. I, 
which is mainly divided into three modules camera 
motion compensation module, detection module and 
tracking module. First, for the input fruit image sequence, 
the camera motion compensation module and the 
detection module calculate the camera motion parameters 
and detect the fruit position information frame by frame, 
respectively; then, the tracking module performs data 
correlation of the detection results of the neighboring 
images, and at the same time, the camera motion 
parameters are used to improve the accuracy of the data 
correlation of the tracking module, and finally, the 
number of correctly tracked fruits is obtained. 
The camera motion compensation module is shown as 
CD in Fig. 1, the main method is to detect the feature points 
frame by frame for the input fruit image sequence, match 
the detected feature points of two adjacent frames with 
the sparse optical flow method, and calculate the affine 
matrix of the image transformation, i.e., the camera 
motion parameter, with the matched feature points. The 
obtained camera motion parameters are used to correct 
and update the mean and variance of the Kalman filter, 
where the correction and updating process of the mean 
and variance of the Kalman filter in this paper follows the 
method in the literature [14], i.e., the camera motion 
parameters are used to do the corresponding matrix 
operations on the mean and variance of the Kalman filter. 
The detection module is shown as ® in Fig. I, and the 
detection algorithm DomAda-FruitDet [15] proposed in 
the team's previous research work is used as the detection 
algorithm for fruit counting in this paper, which is an 
Anchor-free domain-adaptive fruit detection algorithm 
improved based on CenterNet [16], and performs good 
detection accuracy on a wide range of fruits. The main 
method is to cut frames from the captured orchard fruit 
video data to obtain fruit image sequences, then scale 
normalize the fruit images and feed them into the 
DomAda-FruitDet detection model to obtain fruit 
location information. 
The tracking module is shown as @ in Fig. 1. In this 
paper, we use the OrangeSort [12] algorithm proposed in 
the early-stage research work of the team as the tracking 
algorithm. The OrangeSort algorithm is an improved 
tracking algorithm based on the Sort algorithm [ I 7] and 
designed using motion displacement estimation method. 
It can effectively improve the tracking quality, and the 
specific region counting strategy it employs reduces the 
problem of repeated counting. It has shown good 
counting performance on the citrus dataset. At the same

Camera Motion Compensation (CMC) module to achieve 
fruit counting in vibrated scenes. The specific process is 
shown in Fig. 2, and the specific steps are as follows: 
First, the detection results and camera motion 
parameters of the fruit image sequence are obtained. Then, 
the detection results and camera motion parameters are 
utilized to calculate the motion matching degree between 
the Kalman filter predicted tracking results and the 
detection results of the current frame. Calculate the IoU 
cost matrix based on the detection boxes and tracking 
boxes. Finally, the Hungarian matching algorithm will be 
utilized to associate the detection results with existing 
tracking results and output three types of results: 
"unmatched detection targets," 
"correctly matched 
detection targets," and "unmatched tracking targets." For 
the correctly matched detection targets, estimate the 
position of the unmatched tracking targets in the current 
frame using the average motion displacement of the 
tracked fruit targets and then update the Kalman filter 
state accordingly. Create new Kalman filters for 
unmatched detection targets. For fruits exceeding the set 
counting region, delete the corresponding trackers. The 
counting region strategy in this paper follows the setting 
in literature [12], which designates the middle 3/5 region 
of the image as the counting region. For fruits within the 
counting region, the total number of fruit image 
sequences is obtained by detecting and tracking frame by 
frame. 
In summary, this paper proposes a counting method 
that integrates camera motion compensation. By utilizing 
the obtained camera motion parameters to correct and 
update the Kalman filter's prediction of target motion 
states, the performance of the tracker is improved. This 
further achieves accurate fruit counting in actual orchard 
scenarios with vibrated scenes. 
Ill. 
RESULT 
A. Data Acquisition 
This paper collected the nectarine data on June 16, 
2023, at the Beijing Alumni Base of Jilin University in 
Shunyi District, Beijing. Data was collected in the 
orchard through the robot dog CyberDog (Xiaomi Inc., 
Beijing, China) equipped with a gimbal camera, and 
eleven nectarine videos were collected. 
The following acquisition method was followed: 
1) The CyberDog traveled among the fruit tree ridges 
and captured bilateral video sequences of each row of 
fruit trees. 
2) Set the camera to record the video frame rate to 
ensure that approximately 30 images are captured on each 
side of each tree, for a total of approximately 60 images 
on the left and right sides of a tree. 
B. Experimental Details 
In this paper, a computer equipped with GEFORCE 
GTX 3090 type GPU and Intel i7 8th model CPU is used 
for training and testing validation of fruit detection and 
tracking algorithm. For the detection dataset, a total of 
678 nectarine images were produced and randomly 
disrupted to divide the training and validation sets by 7:3. 
For the tracking dataset, a total of 1061 nectarine image 
sequences were produced.

To verify the effectiveness of the counting method 
proposed in this paper, we primarily utilize the counting 
evaluation metrics from literature [12], comparing the 
counting errors derived from manual counting and 
algorithmic counting to assess the counting performance. 
The evaluation metrics also include accurate counting, 
erroneous counting (mistaking the leaf background as 
tracking targets for counting), double counting ( counting 
the same fruit target multiple times), and missed counting 
( omitting tracked fruit targets). 
To validate the effectiveness of the tracking algorithm 
presented in this paper, the evaluation metrics proposed 
in the MOT (Multiple Object Tracking)[l 8].challenge 
were used as the standard for assessment. In the 
evaluation of multi-object tracking algorithms, the 
primary focus is on the MOTA 
(Multiple Object 
Tracking Accuracy) and MOTP (Multiple Object 
Tracking Precision) metrics. The MOTA mainly concerns 
the number of errors made by the tracker, with fewer 
errors leading to a higher MOTA score, indicating better 
tracker performance. Its formula is given by: 
MOT A = 1 -

where FN represents the number of times the tracker 
failed to detect a target, F P represents the number of 
times the tracker falsely predicted a target, / DS represents 
the number of times an identity switch occurred, and GT 
represents the total number of actual targets. 
C. Experimental Results and Analysis 
1) Comparative Experiment 
In this section's experiment, to demonstrate the 
effectiveness of the proposed multi-object tracking 
algorithm StableSort-CMC, two tracking algorithms, 
ByteTrack [19] and Bot-Sort [14] were chosen for 
comparison. ByteTrack is an algorithm that relies solely 
on the Kalman filter and Hungarian matching algorithm 
for tracking, similar to other non-RelD algorithms, using 
only the bounding box (bbox) obtained from object 
tracking. The feature of the ByteTrack algorithm is its use 
of low-confidence boxes, performing a secondary 
matching for 
the 
tracking 
algorithm, 
effectively 
optimizing the tracking process and addressing ID 
switching issues due to occlusions. Bot-Sort is a new 
robust tracking algorithm that combines the advantages of 
camera motion and image appearance information, 
introducing camera motion compensation into the 
tracking algorithm for the first time and setting a more 
accurate Kalman filter state vector. 
The comparison experiment utilized the DomAda-
FruitDet algorithm for detection, with a detection 
algorithm accuracy metric mAP_0.5 of 0.783. The 
counting comparison results are shown in Table 1, where 
our algorithm counted 132 nectarines, closest to the 
manual count of 84, with the lowest counting error of only 
0.571. Next, the Bot-Sort algorithm counted 138, with a 
counting error of0.642.

IV. 
CONCLUSION 
Building upon previous research, this paper proposes 
a tracking algorithm called StableSort-CMC, combined 
with adetection algorithm called DomAda-FruitDet, to 
address the issue of inaccurate fruit counting in vibrated 
scenarios due to the performance degradation of tracking 
algorithms. The StableSort-CMC algorithm utilizes 
traditional image registration methods to obtain camera 
motion parameters, which are then used to correct and 
update the Kalman filter with the rotational, scaling, and 
translational relationships between image sequences. 
Through ablation experiments, the effectiveness of the 
camera motion compensation module is demonstrated, 
and comparative experiments confirm that the proposed 
tracking algorithm effectively improves the performance 
of the tracker in vibrated scenarios, thereby enhancing the 
accuracy of fruit counting in orchards under vibrated 
conditions. 
In the future, we aim to design lightweight model 
architectures 
for 
deployment 
on 
edge 
devices. 
Additionally, we plan to integrate these edge devices onto 
mobile robot dogs to achieve real-time fruit counting in 
orchards. To mitigate the impact of vibrated scenarios on 
the tracker, we intend to utilize image registration 
methods as an alternative to tracking algorithms, thus 
avoiding frame-by-frame detection and tracking and 
further reducing counting errors caused by vibration.
