Abstract— Image quality significantly affects the results of 
automatic classification of oil palm maturity levels. However, 
image quality often decreases due to noise, uneven lighting, or 
differences in image capture conditions in the field. This study 
combined two stages of image enhancement using the Rolled 
Guided Filter (RGF) and the Enhanced Super-Resolution 
Generative Adversarial Network (ESRGAN). RGF is used to 
handle noise in images. However, the use of RGF can cause some 
detailed features in the image to become blurred. ESRGAN was 
used to correct these deficiencies by restoring details and 
improving the visual quality of the image. The image 
enhancement results successfully obtained a PSNR of 32.26 dB, 
indicating 
that 
the 
image 
enhancement 
results 
are 
mathematically similar to the original image. The SSIM of 0.94 
indicates that the image enhancement results look almost 
identical in structure and visual patterns to the original image. 
The FID of 6.78 indicates that the image enhancement results 
have good generative quality. Based on these results, it can be 
concluded that the combination of the RGF-ESRGAN method 
is successful in improving image quality while maintaining 
feature information in oil palm images. 
Keywords— ESRGAN, Palm Oil, Image Enhancement, RGF 
I. INTRODUCTION 
Oil palm is a major tropical plantation commodity and the 
world's largest source of vegetable oil [1]. By 2024, global 
palm oil production is projected to exceed 80 tons/year and 
contribute more than 35% of total global vegetable oil 
production [1]. The amount of vegetable oil produced by oil 
palms is highly dependent on the ripeness of the fruit. Ripe oil 
palms, which have a uniform reddish-orange color, produce 
better oil quality and quantity than unripe oil palms, which are 
reddish-orange with a black base, and overripe oil palms, 
which are dark red [2]. The similarity in color between ripe, 
unripe, and overripe oil palms is one factor that makes it 
difficult for farmers to select oil palms with the correct 
ripeness level. Automatic classification of oil palm fruit based 
on ripeness can help oil palm farmers separate fresh fruit 
bunches to obtain optimal oil quality and quantity [3]. Many 
automatic classification technologies are developed using 
deep learning methods [4]. 
Deep learning is a method that works through a deep 
learning process to extract detailed features, so image quality 
significantly influences classification results [5]. However, oil 
palm fruit images sometimes have poor quality, such as high 
levels of noise and low resolution, due to uneven lighting 
conditions, low camera quality, or suboptimal image capture 
and storage processes [6]. Image enhancement is one of the 
stages in image preprocessing carried out to obtain optimal 
image quality to improve automatic classification results [7]. 
Image enhancement can be achieved by handling noise and 
increasing resolution. Noise handling aims to eliminate 
interference that arises from the image acquisition or 
transmission process [8]. One widely used noise handling 
method is the Rolling Guidance Filter (RGF) [9]. 
Rolling Guidance Filter (RGF) is a noise management 
method that applies an iterative guided filter process to an 
image. The goal is to remove noise without damaging the 
shape, edges, or important details in the image. In the first 
iteration, the filter functions to remove large noise. This 
process produces a smoother and more stable image for use in 
the next stage [9].. In subsequent iterations, the filter ensures 
that noise that was not completely removed in the initial stage 
is removed through a further filtering process. This process 
produces an image with a smoothing level that is not 
excessive, and the image does not become blurry. Lin et al. 
[10] used RGF combined with the Wiener Filter on brain 
tumor images and obtained a fairly good SSIM of 0.8. Prema 
& Arivazhagan [11] used RGF on facial images combined 
with the Nonsubsampled Contourlet Transform on house 
images and produced an excellent PSNR value of 67.7. 
Although RGF is effective in reducing noise and maintaining 
sharp image edges, this method can cause fine details that may 
be important to appear blurred, especially in low-resolution

image quality can be improved. 
One image enhancement technique that can be used to 
restore features that appear blurred due to noise processing is 
resolution enhancement. Resolution enhancement can clarify 
fine details and improve the visual structure of an image by 
adding high-frequency spatial information. One method 
widely used today for resolution enhancement is the Enhanced 
Super-Resolution 
Generative 
Adversarial 
Network 
(ESRGAN) [13]. ESRGAN is a development of the Super-
Resolution Generative Adversarial Network (SRGAN) that 
aims to increase image resolution with sharper, more realistic, 
and natural results [14]. ESRGAN uses a Residual-in-
Residual Dense Block (RRDB) that is able to extract deeper 
features without losing important details in the image. The 
GAN architecture in ESRGAN consists of two main 
components, namely the generator and the discriminator, but 
has been refined to make the training process more stable and 
the texture reconstruction results smoother. The generator 
plays a role in producing high-resolution images from low-
resolution images, while the discriminator functions to assess 
the authenticity of the image by distinguishing between the 
reconstructed image and the original image. Yue et al. [1] 
applied ESRGAN to remote sensing imagery and produced 
excellent PSNR and SSNR values of 31.2 and 0.822, 
respectively. Xu et al. [15] also used ESRGAN on building 
imagery and obtained SSIM and PSNR values of 34.83 and 
0.92, respectively. ESRGAN is capable of improving image 
resolution well. However, if the image being enhanced 
contains noise, this method tends to magnify and amplify the 
existing noise, so the resolution enhancement results appear 
rough and less natural [14]. 
This study applies a combination of image quality 
improvement, namely noise handling and image resolution 
enhancement. The method used for noise handling is the 
Rolling Guidance Filter (RGF), which can perform iterative 
adaptive filtering to smooth homogeneous areas while 
maintaining significant edge structures in the image. The 
method used for resolution enhancement is the Enhanced 
Super-Resolution 
Generative 
Adversarial 
Network 
(ESRGAN). ESRGAN is intended to restore and clarify fine 
details that are potentially lost after the filtering process in 
noise handling with RGF. RGF plays a role in removing noise 
in oil palm images, while ESRGAN increases image 
resolution while maintaining the sharpness and visual 
structure of the RGF results. The combination of these two 
methods aims to produce oil palm images with more optimal 
quality so that they can support the automatic classification 
process of oil palm maturity levels. Performance evaluations 
used to measure the success of RGF and ESRGAN include 
Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), Structural Similarity 
Index Measure (SSIM), and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID). 
PSNR is a quantitative and mathematical measure of how 
close the color values of each pixel in the enhanced image are 
to the pixel values in the original image. SSIM is a measure 
that takes into account image structure and human visual 
perception, with values close to 1 indicating a high degree of 
similarity between two images. FID is a metric that measures 
how realistic and diverse the GAN-generated images are 
compared to the original images used to train the GAN model. 
II. RELATED WORK 
The Rolling Guidance Filter (RGF) method has been 
applied in various studies. Lin et al. [10] utilized RGF in 
conjunction with a Wiener Filter on brain tumor images. The 
resulting SSIM value was quite good, at 0.8. Prema & 
Arivazhagan [11] used RGF on facial images combined with 
the Nonsubsampled Contourlet Transform on house images, 
resulting in an excellent PSNR value of 67.7. 
The Enhanced Super-Resolution Generative Adversarial 
Network (ESRGAN) method has been applied in various 
studies. Yue et al. [1] applied ESRGAN to building images 
and produced excellent PSNR and SSIM values of 31.2 and 
0.822 dB. Xu et al. [15] also used ESRGAN on building 
images and obtained SSIM and PSNR values of 34.83 and 
0.92 dB.

Fig. 2 shows that the ESRGAN method consists of a 
convolution layer, two residual blocks, a GAN operation, and 
upsampling. The initial convolution layer extracts basic 
features such as edges, textures, and color patterns. The 
residual block consists of a convolution layer, batch 
normalization, and ReLU. The residual block contains a skip 
connection to preserve important information and avoid 
vanishing gradients during training. The residual block learns 
a more complex feature representation without losing the 
original image structure. The results of the two residual blocks 
are recombined through an additional convolution layer to 
balance the feature maps, then passed to an upsampling stage 
to increase the spatial dimension of the image using 
techniques such as sub-pixel convolution or pixel shuffle. 
After the upsampling process, a final convolution layer is used 
to smooth artifacts and adjust color channels before producing 
a high-resolution output image that resembles the actual 
reference image. The ERSGAN process is defined in

D. Evaluation 
The performance evaluation stage is carried out to 
measure the success rate of the proposed image quality 
enhancement method. The evaluation metrics used in this 
study include Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), Structural 
Similarity Index Measure (SSIM), and Fréchet Inception 
Distance (FID). MSE measures the mean squared error 
between the enhanced image and the original image. PSNR is 
used to measure the effectiveness of noise reduction [19].

contrast and structure, between the enhanced image and the 
original image [20]. Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) is used 
to measure the distribution distance between the original

IV. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
The palm oil ripeness image dataset consists of three 
classes, namely unripe, ripe, and overripe. Each image in the 
dataset underwent two stages of enhancement, which are noise 
handling using RGF and resolution enhancement using 
ESRGAN. The image and its corresponding histogram after 
the RGF enhancement can be seen in Fig. 4. 
Based on Fig. 4(a), the application of the RGF to the palm 
oil image effectively enhances visual quality by reducing 
noise while preserving essential edges and structural details. 
The filtered image appears smoother and more visually 
balanced, allowing clearer distinction between ripe and unripe 
fruits without over-blurring. In Fig. 4(b), the corresponding 
histogram shows three main peaks representing dark, medium, 
and bright regions, indicating that contrast and dynamic range 
are well maintained after the RGF process. Overall, RGF 
performs effective edge-preserving smoothing, producing a 
cleaner image with improved visual uniformity suitable for 
further enhancement.

The image resulting from the RGF process served as the 
input for the ESRGAN stage, which aims to further improve 
the spatial resolution and visual clarity of the RGF-enhanced 
image. The image and its corresponding histogram after the 
combined RGF–ESRGAN enhancement can be seen in Fig. 5. 
Based on Fig. 5(a), the application of ESRGAN to the RGF-
enhanced image produces a significant improvement in visual 
sharpness and detail representation. The resulting image 
exhibits higher clarity, with fruit textures and fine edges 
appearing more distinct and realistic. Compared to the RGF 
output, the ESRGAN result shows enhanced contrast and 
richer color tones, contributing to a more vivid and natural 
appearance. In Fig. 5(b), the histogram distribution becomes 
slightly wider, suggesting an expanded dynamic range and 
better differentiation of pixel intensities. Consequently, 
ESRGAN effectively enhances the spatial resolution of the 
RGF-processed image, making it more suitable for subsequent 
segmentation and feature extraction tasks. 
Based on Fig. 4 and Fig. 5, the RGF method shows that the 
image undergoes effective smoothing while preserving 
essential structural details. Fine textures and noise are 
reduced, resulting in a cleaner and more visually coherent 
image, though with slightly diminished sharpness and contrast 
due to the smoothing effect, as reflected in the narrower 
histogram distribution. In contrast, the ESRGAN-enhanced 
image demonstrates notable improvements in sharpness, 
detail recovery, and contrast balance. The textures appear 
more natural, and the histogram shows a wider spread of pixel 
intensities, signifying a richer dynamic range and improved 
visual realism. Overall, the combination of RGF and 
ESRGAN forms an effective image enhancement pipeline, 
where RGF successfully removes noise and smooths the 
image, while ESRGAN restores fine details and contrast, 
resulting in a visually enhanced and high-quality image. In 
addition, to measure the success rate of the RGF-ESRGAN 
image enhancement method, the PSNR, SSIM, and FID 
values were also calculated. A comparison of the performance 
evaluation on the image resulting from the enhancement using 
the RGF-ESRGAN method can be seen in Table I 
Based on Table I, the image enhancement results using 
either the RGF or ESRGAN method alone have PSNR 
performance evaluation values below 30, SSIM below 0.9, 
and FID above 10. The RGF and ESRGAN methods that are 
not combined show quite good performance in image 
enhancement, but have lower performance evaluation values 
than RGF-ESRGAN. The method proposed in this study, 
namely the combination of the RGF and ESRGAN methods, 
produces PSNR evaluation values above 30, SSIM values 
above 0.9, and FID below 10. This shows that the combination 
of image enhancement methods successfully improves image 
quality with a low error rate, high structural similarity, and 
feature distribution that remains close to the original image. 
The performance evaluation results of the image enhancement 
method using RGF-ESRGAN are also compared with 
previous studies. A comparison of the performance evaluation 
between the proposed RGF-ESRGAN method and other 
studies can be seen in Table II. Based on Table II, it can be 
seen that the method used [6] obtained a PSNR value above 
25, an SSIM value of 0.8, and an FID value above 10. This 
indicates that the image enhancement results still have a 
relatively high error rate and moderate structural similarity. 
The method used [1] obtained a good PSNR value above 30, 
but with an SSIM value below 0.9 and an FID above 10.

V. CONCLUSION  
Based on the results of image quality enhancement using 
a combination of RGF-ESRGAN, both methods demonstrated 
good performance based on image evaluation parameters. A 
PSNR value above 30 dB indicates that the error rate between 
the reconstructed image and the reference image is very small, 
and the pixel intensities of both have high similarity. An SSIM 
value close to 1 indicates that the structure, contrast, and 
luminance of the improved image are very similar to the 
original image, thus maintaining its visual quality. In addition, 
an FID value below 10 indicates that the distribution of the 
enhanced image features has a very high similarity to the 
reference image, resulting in a natural and realistic 
appearance. This indicates that the proposed combination of 
RGF and ESRGAN methods is proven effective in improving 
image quality, thus potentially supporting the process of 
classifying the level of maturity of oil palm more accurately. 
For future work, further research can focus on optimizing the 
RGF-ESRGAN architecture using larger and more diverse 
datasets, integrating the enhancement process with automated 
classification models, and exploring real-time implementation 
to improve efficiency and applicability in field conditions.
