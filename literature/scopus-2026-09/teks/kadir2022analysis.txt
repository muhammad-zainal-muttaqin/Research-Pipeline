Abstract— Applications utilizing 3D Camera technologies 
for detecting fruit in the agriculture sector continue to expand. 
The Intel RealSense is one of the leading 3D depth sensing 
cameras, currently available on the market and aligns itself for 
use in many applications, including robotics, automation, and 
agriculture sectors. University Putra Malaysia, in collaboration 
with oil palm industries, has developed an autonomous fresh 
fruit bunch (FFB) collector machine equipped with an object 
detection system capable of detecting and collecting the FFB on 
the ground using RealSense camera technologies. We applied 
deep learning models to detect and collect oil palm fresh fruits 
bunch (FFB). Our FFB datasets were trained using the open- 
source YOLOv4, and the trained weight file was sent to our 
tractor to detect and collect the FFB autonomously. This 
research focuses on the performance of the detection method of 
YOLOv4 in detecting and collecting FFB on the ground via a 
RealSense camera. Based on the research carried out, we found 
that the reduce in network architecture will reduce the total 
detection time. The recognition performance is improved 
significantly from YOLOv4 to YOLOv4-tiny in term of the FPS 
and the time taken for the detection.

I. INTRODUCTION
Researchers have favoured the object detection algorithm 
as one of the most important areas of computer research, 
whether they use the old method of image processing and 
computer vision or the new method of combining deep 
learning. After years of research and development, the Object 
Detection algorithm has been used in UAVs [1], driverless 
cars, medical image systems, agriculture sector, and other 
applications. 
Malaysia is one of the world's sustainable producers of 
palm oil. In the next five years, they aim to use a machine 
made to collect fresh fruit bunches (FFB) from oil palm trees. 
They are leveraging robotics technologies to design a machine 
for harvesting oil palm fresh fruit bunches (FFBs) [10], which 
they expect to deploy within the next five years. 
University Putra Malaysia (UPM) has developed an 
autonomous FFB collector machine in collaboration with oil 
palm industries for use in the oil palm plantation sector. The 
autonomous FFB collector application to carry out the 
collection of Fresh Fruit Bunch (FFB) autonomously is 
equipped with a kinematic algorithm, a detection system, and 
an integrated system for an autonomous system. This system 
is capable of detecting the FFB and extracting its coordinates. 
Based on this measurement, the system will signal the machine 
arm, which is equipped with sensors, to move or extend 
towards the fruit, collect the FFB, and place it in the tank 
As the primary focus of current research [2], there are two 
types of deep learning based object detection algorithms [3]: 
two stage and one stage methods. The two stage algorithm is 
based on region based convolution neural networks and 
region-based fully convolutional networks. The object 
information must be obtained through the regional proposal 
network by these algorithms before they can produce the 
projected location and category results. There are two parts to 
the detection step. Even though the accuracy is higher in the 
two stage method, the one stage method operates more 
efficiently and has a faster detection speed than the two-stage 
method. In a real-time application, a one-stage method is more 
suitable for some applications that require high real-time 
detection speed. 
The single-stage method is represented by You Only Look 
Once (YOLO) series [4], Single Shot Multibox Detector 
(SSD) series, and CornerNet. This method can directly output 
location and category information without requiring region 
proposals to obtain candidate object information. It has higher 
efficiency compared with two-stage method. 
II. YOU ONLY LOOK ONCE (YOLO)
The You Only Look Once (YOLO) method is the first 
regression-based one-stage method [5] proposed by Redmon, 
et al. The You Only Look Once version 5 (YOLOv5) is the 
latest version of YOLO. YOLOv5 has the advantages of 
YOLOv4 with the CSP backbone and PA-NET neck. The 
most significant improvements in YOLOv5 include mosaic 
data augmentation and auto-learning bounding box anchors. 
In this paper we will use YOLOv4 and YOLOv4-tiny 
network to complete the detection of the Fresh Fruit Bunch 
(FFB) by using RealSense Camera.

B. YOLOv4-tiny 
YOLOv4-Tiny is the small version of YOLOv4, has a 
more straightforward design, and performs well in the object 
detection algorithm [2]. It has faster speed of object detection. 
Using a 1080Ti GPU, Yolov4-tiny can detect objects at a rate 
of 371 frames per second with an accuracy sufficient for real- 
world applications [3]. It greatly increases the feasibility that 
the object detection method can be implemented on embedded 
or mobile systems. To improve the speed of object detection 
with slight impacting accuracy, an improved Yolov4-tiny is 
proposed in this paper. 
The Yolov4-tiny method uses the CSPDarknet53-tiny 
network as its backbone, as opposed to the CSPDarknet53 
network used by the Yolov4 method. The CSPDarknet53-tiny 
network substitutes the ResBlock module in residual network 
with the CSPBlock module in cross stage partial network. The 
CSPBlock module can enhance the learning ability of 
convolution network comparing with ResBlock module. To 
reduce the amount of calculation, it eliminates the 
computational bottlenecks in CSPBlock module with the 
highest amount of calculation. It increases the precision of the 
Yolov4-tiny method for constant or even reduced 
computation.

B. Intel RealSense RGBD Camera 
As the demand for the development of Intel RealSense and 
SDK has increased, there has also been a greater demand for 
depth sensing cameras that can operate at both short and long 
ranges. This increased demand led to the creation of the Intel 
RealSense Cameras ZR300, D415, and D435, which are 
available now. 
Intel has released the D400 camera series, which features 
the most sophisticated depth perception capabilities to date. 
The depth sensing cameras D415 and D435 contain the most 
recent Intel RealSense vision processor and module. The Intel 
RealSense Depth Camera D415 features a rolling shutter and 
a standard field of view, making it ideal for general-purpose 
applications. 
Nevertheless, the D435 has a global image shutter and a 
larger field of view, as well as the ability to capture and stream 
the depth data of moving objects, resulting in a high level of 
depth perception accuracy in motion [9]. In this project, we 
will use Intel RealSense Depth Camera D435i. The ideal range 
of this camera is .3 m to 3 m, making it an ideal choice for the 
FFB detection system.

C. RealSense Depth Camera 
Intel's RealSense R200 is a 3D somatosensory camera 
with an image processor, colour image sensors, an infrared 
light projector, and two infrared image sensors. The effective 
distance of the camera is between 0.5 and 4 metres, and the 
infrared sensor detects the depth of the scene and converts it 
into a 640480-pixel depth image. 
RealSense camera ranging principle: the infrared emitter 
emits infrared "structured light. [7]" The infrared sensor 
receives the structured light after it has been reflected by the 
obstruction. Due to the different distance of the infrared light 
reflected from the obstacle's surface, the position and shape of 
the "structured light" pattern captured by the infrared receiver 
will change. We can calculate the object's spatial information 
and then perform a detailed calculation based on the 
triangulation principle [8].

V. SIMULATION RESULTS AND ANALYSIS 
In this paper, we use a custom dataset of Fresh Fruit Bunch 
(FFB) as a train and test dataset. Roboflow platform is being 
used to label the unlabeled dataset. It contains 1104 training 
images, 105 validation images and 47 testing images with only 
1 class (FFB). The training, validation, and test sets are 
divided according to 0.9:0.1:0.05. The aim of data 
augmentation is to make the input pictures more variable so 
that the object detection model will be more resistant to 
images taken in different situations. Two common data 
augmentation techniques are photometric distortions, and 
geometric distortions, greatly enhance the object detection 
process. When addressing photometric distortion, we modify 
an image's brightness, contrast, hue, saturation, and noise. 
Geometric distortion is produced using arbitrary scaling, 
cropping, flipping, and rotation. 
All experiment has been carried out on the platform 
configured Win 11 operating system, equipped with AMD 
Ryzen 5 3500U with Radeon Vega Mobile Gfx (2.10 GHz). 
In order to make full use of the GPU to accelerate the network 
training, the CUDA 11.2 and its matching CUDNN are 
installed into the workspace. We are using Google Colab to 
train our custom data set. We use different parameters for both

Average Precision (AP) and mean Average Precision (mAP) 
are usually used evaluation indexes in the field of object 
detection, that can thoroughly describe the model's 
performance. 
FPS denotes the number of images that can be detected 
successfully in one second. FPS is currently a common 
indicator for evaluating model efficiency [11]. The number of 
true objects present in an image is not taken into account by 
precision. Recall, on the other hand, is the ratio of true 
predictions to the total number of objects in an image. For an 
object detection algorithm to be considered accurate, it must 
have a balance of precision and recall. As a result, we utilise 
the F1-score to assess whether or not a model is correct based 
on a variety of indicators, which include true positive (TP), 
true negative (TN), false positive (FP), and false negative 
(FN). This was done by calculate precision and recall in (1) 
and (2) before get F1-score as shown in (3). 
 
 
We compare both of the proposed method with YOLOv4 
and YOLOv4-tiny to test their performance in mAP and FPS. 
The results are shown in Table 1. YOLOv4-tiny have a fairly 
simple network architecture with few parameters compared 
with YOLOv4. Therefore, it has better performance in FPS

other studies in which an increase in FPS decreases the mAP

VI. CONCLUSION 
In this paper, we anticipate that the reduce in network 
architecture will reduce the total detection time. The 
performance parameter was analyzed in order to provide an 
insight on how well the algorithm is performed on different 
method of detection It is noted that the recognition 
performance is improved significantly from YOLOv4 to 
YOLOv4-tiny in term of the FPS and the time taken for the 
detection. This will eventually improve the FFB detection 
system by reducing the time taken to detect and collect the 
FFB.
