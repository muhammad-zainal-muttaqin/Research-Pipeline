In recent years, fruit harvesting robots have become a research hotspot for agricultural
machinery. To be effective, robots should quickly and accurately detect fruits on trees in
the complex environments. In this study, a method using an RGB-D (Red, green, blue, and
depth space) camera combining colour data and 3D contour features for the detection of
juicy peach was developed based on clustering and model segmentation. The point cloud
of fruits was acquired in realistic ﬁeld conditions. The pre-processed point cloud was
divided into clusters by the conditional Euclidean clustering algorithm, and the 3D contour
features were then used to extract fruits. The random sample consensus (RANSAC)
spherical segmentation method was applied to clusters considered to contain multiple
fruits that have close distance with one another. The fruit point cloud obtained is
incomplete due to the limited range of camera view and actual occlusions in orchard, these
clusters are considered to contain only one fruit. The progressive sample consensus
(PROSAC) circular segmentation method was exploited to improve accuracy. Experiments
were conducted under three situations with different occlusions. The total recognition
accuracy reached 88.68%, and the average recognition processing time for a single fruit was
about 292.4 ms, it can meet the requirements for fruit detection considering that the
harvesting robot mainly takes the ripe peaches as object.

Fruit harvesting is an important part of orchard production
and management (Li, Zhu, Huang, Guo, & Qin, 2018). Tradi-
tional manual harvesting is not only inefﬁcient but also incurs
high cost due to the need to employ a large number of
labourers (Li, Lee, & Hsu, 2011). Also, high-intensity work can
also cause harm to workers (Fathallah, 2010). Farmers there-
fore constantly seek for new methods for upgrading their
production methods to reduce production costs and improve
the efﬁciency of orchard management. The rapid develop-
ment of automation and robotic technology has led to
increasing application of automatic harvesting robots, such as

fruit harvesting robot is mainly based on a visual system for

ing, the robotic system should ﬁrst detect the fruits on tree,
and the correct recognition rate determines the ﬁnal success
rate of picking. Although numerous studies have been con-

fruits, the detection system should also overcome the impact

should be developed. It can not only provide an auxiliary tool
for harvesting robots but also count the number of fruits to
estimate orchard yields. It is therefore of great signiﬁcance to
improve the efﬁciency of production and management in
orchards.
The concept of automated harvesting, as an alternative to
mechanical harvesting can be dated back to 1968 (Schertz &
Brown, 1968). As early as 1987, black and white monochrome
cameras were used to detect fruits based on geometric fea-
tures by Whittaker, Miles, Mitchell, and Gaultney (1987). The
major disadvantage of monochrome cameras is the lack of

used by researchers working in machine vision systems for
robotic

gorithm for automatically identifying apples on tree. Zhao,
Tow, and Katupitiya (2005) proposed a vision-based apple
locating algorithm in a single image. Patel, Jain, and Joshi
(2012) developed an automatic fruit segmentation and yield

fruit detection in colour images and have not utilised depth
information.

other objects in the real working environment nor can it meet
the requirements of locating fruits. Combining depth infor-
mation, based on the three-dimensional (3D) space of an ob-
ject while using colour imaging has been studied by many
researchers in recent years.
To improve the applicability of clustering tomato recogni-
tion, Xiang, Jiang, and Ying (2014) obtained the depth images
of tomato by the stereo matching technology and proposed a
clustering tomato recognition method. The practical applica-
tion of stereovision system is limited due to its complexity and
long computation time, the system is often unsuitable to be
applied to systems that require real-time performance. Laser
range ﬁnders can directly provide depth information. Bulanon
and Kataoka (2010) used a laser sensor to measure the dis-
tance of fruits in an apple orchard, but the laser scanning
system is bulky and expensive, and data acquisition is
complicated. In recent years, consumer-grade RGB-D cameras
have been favoured by many researchers. This kind of depth
camera can acquire colour images, depth images, and 3D
point cloud data from the target scene. They are small in size,
simple in structure, and easy to operate. Xia, Wang, Chung,
and Lee (2015) introduced the depth information of plant
leaves, used a low-cost RGB-D camera to obtain depth images
and colour images, and segmented the occluded individual
plant leaves in a complex natural environment. Wang and Li
(2014) used an RGB-D camera to measure the maximum
diameter and the volume of sweet onions and used the
measured parameters to estimate the density of onions.
Nguyen et al. (2016) used an RGB-D camera to detect red apples
on tree, and the recognition accuracy of the partially occluded
apples reached 82%, the point cloud was still converted into a

Fruit detection under complex conditions mainly aims to
solve the problem of natural light changes, fruit clustering,
fruit occlusion, and colour consistency between the fruit and
its background. Although many methods for fruit detection
are available, the requirements for fruit detection have
continuously increased and the detection method should be
updated. Thus, an effective method for fruit detection re-
quires development. In this study, an RGB-D camera was used
to detect juicy peaches on trees and attempt to solve problems
such as fruit clustering and blocking.
In section 2, the materials and methods used are pre-
sented, including data acquisition and method for solving
fruit clustering and blocking. Section 3 provides the results
and discussion and section 4 discusses our conclusions and

Materials and methods
This study mainly used Microsoft Kinect 2.0 RGB-D camera to
build a camera operating environment and conducted data
acquisition under Ubuntu 14.04 system. Visual Studio 2013
and PCL 1.8.0 operating environments were conﬁgured in
Windows 7 64-bit system. The development of related algo-
rithms was completed using the Point Cloud Library (PCL,
http://pointclouds.org/) (Rusu & Cousins, 2011) and written in
Cþþ language.
2.1.
Data acquisition
Kinect 2.0 is a 3D motion-sensing camera launched by
Microsoft in 2014. This camera was an upgraded version of
Kinect 1.0 and can provide high-quality colour and depth
images (Vazquez-Arellano et al., 2018). The experiment was
conducted at a juicy peach orchard in Yangshan Town,
Huishan District, Wuxi City, Jiangsu Province, China, with
cloudy weather on July 26, 2018. The variety of juicy peach is
Hujin, which is red or bicoloured at maturity. Colour images,
depth images, and 3D point clouds of the fruit trees were
obtained.
2.2.
Fruit detection overview
The overview of fruit detection method is shown in Fig. 1.
Point cloud pre-processing mainly removed the background
and other interference. In conditional Euclidean clustering,
the pre-processed fruit point cloud was clustered into several
clusters, and each cluster was segmented separately accord-
ing to the preset segmentation judgment strategy. Random
sample consensus (RANSAC) spherical segmentation method
was applied to clusters considered to contain multiple fruits,
and progressive sample consensus (PROSAC) circular seg-
mentation method was applied to clusters that were consid-
ered to contain only one fruit. The total number of fruits
detected by the two segmentation methods was then counted.
2.2.1.
Point cloud pre-processing
The acquired 3D point cloud of the fruit tree contained scene
information, such as fruits, leaves, and branches, so that the
background information should be removed and the fruit re-
gions could be determined to accurately detect fruits. Colour
information is usually used to distinguish between fore-
ground and background as an important feature (Barnea et al.,
2016). Many studies on fruit detection have proved that colour
information can be used to remove the background.
Point cloud pre-processing mainly includes three parts:
pass-through
ﬁltering,
background
removal,
and
outlier
removal. The pass-through ﬁltering is equivalent to distance
ﬁltering (Bae, Belton, & Lichti, 2009). Considering the large
amount of point cloud data acquired in the target scene and
the fruit harvesting robot mainly works at a close distance.
The range of z-axis direction was set to 0.5e1.5 m to preserve
the speciﬁed range of point cloud. This process can greatly
reduce the number of points, and accelerate subsequent
processing.
In the section on extracting fruit regions, a cross section is
made in the area containing fruits, leaves, and branches in the
acquired colour image, and the distribution of grey values of R,
G, and B channels in the cross section is observed (Fig. 2b). The
difference between R and G values in fruit regions is > 0, while
it is < 0 in leaf regions, R-G colour difference method could
thus be applied to obtain fruit regions. However, the R and G
values of many other areas were very close and the difference
was not obvious. If the method is directly used to remove the
background, then the threshold is hard to determine and the
ideal effect cannot be achieved. Zhao et al. (2015) proposed a
night recognition method for apple picking robot and an
improved R-G segmentation algorithm, the image enhance-
ment method of gamma transformation stretches the value of
the R channel, increases the difference between the R and G
components of target fruit, and decreases the R component of
other scenes to less than the G component. With this idea, the
R channel of point cloud colour information was adjusted
properly to make the difference between R and G components
of foreground and background more obvious.

2.2.2.

each of them dealt with separately to avoid detection errors

certain distance was still left in real space. Given that the
point cloud contains the position information of object, such

2.2.3.
Judgment strategy
After clustering, some clusters contained multiple fruits. The
amount of points in such clusters was sufﬁcient to support
sphere model in most situations, thus fruits could be extrac-
ted by spherical segmentation. Also, some clusters contained
only one fruit, the amount of points might be insufﬁcient, and
even in some cases, only the outline of fruits can be obtained.
If the spherical segmentation method is used directly, it is
difﬁcult to extract fruits because of the limited number of
points. A circle ﬁtting method can be applied to such cluster
only by contour features without requiring too many points.
Meanwhile, shape features could be exploited to ensure that
such region is indeed a fruit region rather than something
else.
The point cloud contained colour and position information
of the spatial object. In this study, a segmentation judgment
strategy was proposed based on the distance between points
in 3D space. The point with the maximum and minimum
coordinate value in x, y, and z directions could be found and
the difference between the maximum and minimum values
reﬂected the maximum length of the object in three di-
rections. If the value in a certain direction was greater than
the preset threshold, then the cluster was considered to

2.2.4.
Point cloud spherical segmentation
The parameters of a mathematical model can be estimated
iteratively by the RANSAC algorithm from a set of datasets
containing outliers (the data that do not ﬁt the model). This
indeterminate algorithm has a certain probability to obtain a
reasonable
result.
The
number
of
iterations
should
be
increased to improve the probability of correctly estimating
the model. The greatest advantage of this algorithm is its
robustness in model estimation. The input of the RANSAC
algorithm is a set of observations and a parametric model that
can be adapted to the observed data. In this section, the set of
observation data was the point cloud cluster containing
multiple fruits and the parametric model was a spherical
equation. The goal was achieved by repeatedly selecting a set
of random subsets in the dataset, then the selected subset was
assumed to be inliers, as veriﬁed by the following method
(Luchowski & Kowalski, 2013):
Step 1: The spherical equation can be adapted to the
hypothetical inliers, and the parameters of the spher-
ical model can be calculated from the hypothetical
inliers.
Step 2: The model obtained in Step 1 is used to test all
other data. If a point is suitable for the estimated model,
then the point is considered as an inlier.
Step 3: If there are enough points to be classiﬁed as
hypothetical inliers, then the estimated model can be
considered reasonable.
Step 4: The model is re-estimated with all hypothetical
inliers.
Step 5: The model is evaluated by the error rate between
the inliers and the model.
The above process was repeated for a ﬁxed number of
times, that is, the number of iterations of the algorithm. The
model generated each time was either abandoned because of
there were too few inliers or adopted because it was better
than the existing model.
In our work, the number of iterations was 10,000, the range
of sphere radius was set to 10e50 mm according to the
measured radius of fruits, and the distance threshold was set
to 10 mm, which is the allowable maximum distance between
the inliers and the model. The minimum number of points
required to segment a sphere model was 100, it could thus
avoid segmenting the same fruit multiple times. These pa-
rameters were tuned for many times based on training im-
ages. The point cloud cluster after spherical segmentation is
shown in Fig. 6. The ﬁrst cluster contained four fruits and
could segment four sphere models; the second cluster con-
tained two fruits and could also segment two sphere models;
and the last cluster, which was considered to contain multiple
fruits, only contained one fruit in reality and could still
segment a one sphere model. This method could thus solve
the problem of close inter-fruit distances and mutual contacts
between fruits.

2.2.5.
Point cloud circular segmentation
In the processing of 2D images, circular Hough transformation
is usually used to detect circles, however, this method is
computationally intensive and time-consuming. Therefore,
the idea of detecting circles in 2D space and 3D space was
applied. PROSAC is a random parameter estimation algorithm
and is an improved version of RANSAC. Unlike RANSAC,
which draws random samples uniformly from the full set,
PROSAC samples are drawn from progressively larger sets of
top-ranked correspondences (Chum & Matas, 2005), it can
thus save computation time and improve operational speed in
theory. However, its robustness is not as good as RANSAC. The
edge of object is extracted ﬁrst when circle ﬁtting is carried
out, but it will cost time. In general, circular segmentation is
considered easier than spherical segmentation. Thus, the
PROSAC algorithm was adopted to compensate for this time
consumption.
In a 2D image, the edge of object is deﬁned based on the
gradient, while the points with sharp changes in the geo-

in the point cloud. Whether a point can be considered as a
boundary point or not depends on the angles formed by the
point and the adjacent point. Let q ¼ fq1/qng be the list of

The distribution of points in z-axis direction is not orderly,

Circular segmentation is similar to spherical segmenta-
tion, thus the parametric inputs are same as for spherical
segmentation. The difference is that to estimate the param-
eters of a circle model, there is no need to set the minimum
number of points to satisfy a circle model.
The boundary of the clusters considered to contain only
one fruit was extracted and then the PROSAC circular seg-
mentation method was applied to ﬁt the circle model (Fig. 8).
When the fruit had sufﬁcient points, the circle model can be
segmented completely, and if the number of fruit points is
low, then circle model can also be ﬁtted successfully. In this
way, even when the number of fruit points is too small due to
occlusions, the probability of successful fruit detection can be
improved.

randomly selected (Table 3). Some clusters containing only
one fruit were difﬁcult to be extracted with a spherical seg-
mentation method, but circular segmentation method can
solve this problem. Although circular segmentation can also
process clusters containing multiple fruits in some situations,
it takes more time to extract boundaries. By contrast, spher-
ical segmentation directly utilises the position information of
the contour points, which can obviously improve the pro-
cessing speed. Different models could be used to segment
these clusters so that more fruits can be detected. However,
the two sampling methods had almost the same real-time
performance using spherical segmentation. Considering that
RANSAC is more robust when applied to spherical segmen-
tation, it is better to adopt it to clusters containing multiple
fruits. In addition, the extraction of a single fruit is easier since
it can be processed by both two sampling methods. However,
PROSAC is a little faster in processing circular segmentation.
Thus this method can be used to compensate for time
consumed in boundary extraction.

3.3.
Discussion
Some fruits cannot be detected accurately, and the reasons
might be different. Undetected fruits in some situations were
labelled manually in Fig. 9. The shape of undetected fruit is
incomplete (Fig. 9a) and it might be ﬁltered after clustering.
When the camera was affected by light conditions (Fig. 9b),
such as overexposure, the colour of some fruits changed and
they might be ﬁltered after pre-processing. In Fig. 9c, because
of severe occlusions, the amount of points of blocked fruits
was too small and segmenting the model became difﬁcult.
Moreover, when the fruits were immature, their colour was
similar to background, and they might be more easily ﬁltered
at the pre-processing stage.
Thus, three factors affect fruit recognition. The ﬁrst reason
is serious occlusions, making it difﬁcult to segment the model
successfully based on shape analysis. Secondly, some fruits
are immature and their colour is similar to obstacles, they are
easily ﬁltered out when mature fruits are extracted. Thirdly,
the camera was easily affected by light conditions and some
fruits were difﬁcult to detect because of light reﬂection, thus
reducing the overall detection accuracy.
4.
Conclusions
This study was aimed at the problem of fruit recognition by a
harvesting robot. In this study, a fruit detection method was
proposed based on 3D contour features and colour data. After
removing background, the fruit point cloud was divided into
clusters based on position information. Contour information
was exploited to extract fruits from these clusters by a
spherical or circular segmentation method. Experiments
showed that the proposed method could identify more fruits
than the traditional method. The total recognition accuracy
reached 88.68%, and it only took 292.4 ms on average to detect
a fruit. Thus, the method can be used for real-time fruit
detection and counting to estimate yields in orchards.
In cases where more immature fruits are on trees, recog-
nition accuracy is relatively low. Hence, this method is more
robust when used for detecting mature red fruits. In the future
work, pre-processing of the original point cloud should be
improved, and immature green fruits could be considered
when detecting mature red fruits. The method is suitable for
fruits whose shape close to spherical, such as peaches, apples,
and oranges. The method should be improved to deal with
fruits with complex shapes. Because light conditions had an
effect in our experiment, overcoming the inﬂuence of illumi-
nation variations on fruit detection is a signiﬁcant task which
should be researched in future studies.
