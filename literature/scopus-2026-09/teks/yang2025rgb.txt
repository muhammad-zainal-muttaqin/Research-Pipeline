Abstract—Accurate localization of apples during harvesting is 
a critical step towards automated and intelligent orchard 
picking. However, the prevalence of 
occlusion 
and 
backlighting in orchard environments poses significant 
challenges to model precision. Sole reliance on RGB-based 
object detection models often fails to provide the precise 
target positioning required for successful grasping and 
picking. To address this, this study proposes an RGB-D dual-
branch instance segmentation model based on YOLOv11n-
seg. Firstly, an improved CSPDarknet-based backbone from 
YOLOv11 and an enhanced MobileNetV4 are employed to 
construct a dual-branch feature extraction network, which 
independently extracts features from the RGB and depth 
modalities. Secondly, a Bidirectional Feature Fusion 
Attention mechanism is employed to improve the integration 
of information across different modalities. Furthermore, the 
adaptive dynamic upsampling module, DySample, is 
incorporated to dynamically generate upsampling kernels, 
thereby improving the quality of segmentation mask 
boundaries. Finally, experimental results demonstrate that 
compared to the original model, the proposed improved 
model achieves increases of 2.9% and 2.7% in Box mAP50 
and Mask mAP50, respectively.  
Keywords-Agricultural Intelligence; Apple Harvesting; 
Multimodal; RGB-D; Instance Segmentation 
I. 
 INTRODUCTION  
As one of the most widely cultivated fruit species in the 
world, apples possess both great nutritional value and 
economical significance[1]. Currently, apple harvesting in 
orchards primarily relies on traditional manual labor, which 
is characterized by high labor costs and low levels of 
mechanization and intelligence[2]. To promote industrial 
upgrading, the adoption of robots to replace human pickers 
has become an industry trend. With the advancement of 
deep learning techniques, vision-based solutions for fruit 
localization have been widely adopted. However, complex 
orchard environments—featuring uneven fruit distribution, 
substantial occlusion, and challenges such as an overhead 
perspective with backlighting during picking—pose 
significant obstacles. In these scenarios, the object detection 
accuracy and localization performance of models remain 
considerable challenges. 
In recent years, fruit harvesting algorithms for complex 
orchard environments have been widely applied. To address 
the issue of fruit mutual occlusion in identifying and 
localizing clustered fruits, Wang et al.[3] proposed an 
adaptive parameter clustering algorithm, KGAP-DBSCAN, 
for localizing lychee clusters, achieving a precision of 
94.20%. Meanwhile, Li et al.[4] enhanced the traditional 
DQN algorithm to tackle the low efficiency caused by 
tightly arranged kiwifruits during harvesting. 
To achieve precise harvesting, researchers have adopted 
cascaded strategies. For instance, He et al.[5] categorized 
strawberry targets into occluded and non-occluded states, 
employing a two-stage architecture to accomplish high-
precision picking. In another study, Qifan Wang et al.[6] 
utilized two separate models to identify grape berries and 
stems respectively, enabling the robotic arm to perform 
rapid approach and precise detachment. 
The YOLO object detection model has been widely 
adopted for fruit recognition and localization due to its high 
speed and lightweight architecture[7]. For instance, 
Hongxiong Su et al.[8] presented a keypoint detection 
framework for simultaneous localization of zucchini fruits 
and their corresponding picking points. Their YOLOv8n-
Pose-DSW model, developed from a YOLOv8n-pose 
baseline, attained performance levels of 94.0% mAP@50 
and 71.4% mAP@50-95, demonstrating its effectiveness in 
precise agricultural target localization. Furthermore, 
building upon the YOLOv11 architecture, the GIA-YOLO 
model developed by Longlong Ren et al.[9] integrates an 
attention mechanism, attaining a mean average precision of 
96.2% for nectarine fruit detection. In a related effort, 
Jinpeng Wang et al.[10] enhanced the YOLOv7 algorithm, 
attaining an average precision of 91.7% for localizing 
camellia oleifera fruits. 
Compared to object detection methods, instance 
segmentation is characterized by its higher precision and 
more detailed informational output[11]. For example, Yuan 
Fang et al.[12] employed a modified Mask R-CNN instance 
segmentation algorithm, utilizing morphological cropping 
and concave point segmentation to address the adhesion 
problem caused by overlapping or occlusion between 
persimmons, achieving a mean average precision of 
94.25%. In another study, through the integration of the 
SimAM attention mechanism with the Mish activation 
function, enhancements made by Xinzhao Zhou et al.[13] to 
the PG-YOLACT++ network subsequently led to 
performance gains of 2.2% and 2.6% in mean precision for 
object detection and mask segmentation, respectively. 
Pengjun Xiang et al.[14] proposed a CNN-Transformer 
hybrid network model named MixSegNext for the 
segmentation of prickly ash fruits and flower clusters, 
reporting significant improvements in segmentation 
accuracy and pick-point localization precision. Similarly, 
Han Li et al.[15] enhanced the YOLOv8n-seg model for 
segmenting citrus fruits and stems, achieving a mean 
average precision of 94.43%. 
A significant gap exists between the predominant focus 
on 2D fruit detection in research and the actual 3D

Consequently, many researchers have adopted RGB-D

network to localize picking points on target fruits.   
The aforementioned methods have achieved significant 
progress in fruit localization for harvesting in various 
aspects. However, when dealing with the complex 
environments of orchards, each approach presents certain 
limitations. Traditional clustering algorithms are primarily 
suitable for locating and harvesting clustered fruits. While 
cascade strategies can adapt to various scenarios, they 
improve accuracy at the cost of increased system 
complexity. Single-stage object detection algorithms like 
the YOLO series offer faster speeds, yet their detection 
accuracy is generally inferior to that of instance 
segmentation models, which is detrimental to precise fruit 
grasping. Conversely, two-stage instance segmentation 
algorithms, which prioritize accuracy, demand considerable 
computational resources and memory, resulting in slower 
inference speeds. Although employing RGB-D cameras for 
assisted perception aligns better with real-world harvesting 
conditions, it often requires separate task-specific 
processing, lacking the advantages of an end-to-end 
framework, such as lower cost and architectural simplicity. 
Therefore, this study proposes an end-to-end dual-
branch RGB-D multimodal instance segmentation model 
based on YOLOv11n-seg. The main contributions are 
summarized as follows: 
(1) Incorporation of Depth Data: A dual-branch feature 
extraction network is designed to process both RGB and 
depth modalities. The star operation is integrated into the 
C3K2 module of the network to enhance the model's feature 
representation capability. 
(2) Construction of a multimodal feature interaction 
module: This module enhances information interaction and 
fusion between the different RGB-D modalities. By 
employing a cross-attention mechanism, it fully exploits the 
complementary nature of deep-level multimodal features, 
achieving effective multi-scale feature fusion. 
(3) Integration of an adaptive dynamic upsampling 
module: This module dynamically generates upsampling 
kernels, improving the quality of segmentation boundaries 
and effectively boosting the segmentation accuracy for 
apples in orchard environments. 
II. 
ATERIALS AND METHODS 
A. Dataset 
The 
experiments 
utilized 
the PApple-RGB-D-
Size dataset[18]. This dataset comprises 3,925 PNG-format 
RGB images with a resolution of 1200×1200 pixels, 
captured from six Fuji apple trees at different growth stages. 
Each RGB image has a corresponding depth map stored in 
NPY format. In total, the dataset contains 15,335 annotated 
Fuji apples with ground truth annotations. The imaged trees 
include three at a late maturation stage (exhibiting a reddish 
tint) and three that were imaged when the apples had 
reached approximately 70% of their final size. 
The dataset was partitioned into training, validation, and 
test subsets in an 8:1:1 proportion. The depth data was 
aligned with the RGB images during preprocessing. As 
shown in Figure 1, the dataset encompasses various 
challenging scenarios found in orchard environments, 
including occlusion between fruits, occlusion of fruits by 
trunks and leaves, and challenging perspectives such as 
upward views under backlit conditions during harvesting.

The C3K2 module within YOLOv11 backbone has been 
redesigned by incorporating StarNet[21]. The processing 
pipeline is as follows: the input features are first split, with 
one portion preserved as a residual connection for later use. 
The other portion undergoes a 1×1 convolutional layer for 
linear transformation, generating two separate feature 
streams. A star operation—defined as an element-wise 
multiplication between these two feature streams—is then 
performed to achieve a mapping into a high-dimensional, 
non-linear feature space. The result of this star operation is 
subsequently combined with the initially preserved residual 
via a residual connection. Finally, the integrated feature map 
is output. 
This operation fundamentally differs from traditional 
approaches that increase network width to enhance 
dimensionality. 
Instead, 
it 
achieves 
remarkable 
performance while maintaining a compact network 
structure. 
To ensure dimensional alignment between the two 
feature pathways and to facilitate subsequent feature fusion 
operations, the MobileNetV4 network architecture was 
optimized. It was restructured into a 4-stage pyramid, 
retaining its core components of depthwise separable 
convolutions and Universal Inverted Bottleneck (UIB) 
blocks. This optimized architecture extracts and outputs 
features from the depth data at multiple scales, specifically 
tailored to align with the P3, P4, and P5 feature pyramid 
levels of the YOLOv11 backbone.

E. Dynamic Upsampling Module 
DySample is a dynamic upsampling module[23], whose 
core structure is illustrated in Figure 5. The feature ! with 
dimensions  × U × V (where U is height, V is width, 
and  is the number of channels) is fed into two parallel 
linear layers for spatial dimension mapping. Based on the 
module's predefined number of groups W and upsampling 
scale factor 	, these linear layers output features with a 
dimension of 2W	X . The outputs from both layers then 
undergo a sigmoid activation function, scaled by 0.5, to 
adjust their dimensions, producing the final upsampling 
offsets Y with dimensions 	U × 	V × 2W. 
Subsequently, to satisfy the requirement for element-
wise addition, the original sampling grid Z, which has the 
same dimensions 	U × 	V × 2W , is combined with the 
offsets Y through element-wise summation. This generates 
the dynamically adjusted sampling point set, referred to as 
the dynamic sampling set D. Finally, using D as the precise 
sampling guide, a resampling operation is performed on the 
input feature map !, yielding the upsampled output feature 
map !L. The dimensions of !L are  × 	U × 	V, achieving 
both an 	 -fold increase in spatial resolution and the 
complete preservation of the original feature map's channel 
count and critical semantic information. 
This module departs from the traditional paradigm of 
dynamic kernel convolution, fundamentally rethinking 
upsampling from the perspective of point sampling. It 
dynamically selects kernel sizes and resolutions, and 
optimizes the sampling strategy based on target distance 
information from the depth data. This capability effectively 
mitigates feature ambiguity for distant targets in RGB-D 
tasks, thereby enhancing the segmentation accuracy of 
small objects. Furthermore, performance and flexibility are 
improved by grouping the feature channels and generating 
independent sampling sets for each group.

III. 
RESULTS AND ANALYSIS 
A. Evaluation Metrics 
To evaluate the performance of ours model, Box mAP50 
and Mask mAP50 were used as metrics. In this context, 
mAP50 refers to the mean Average Precision over all 
categories at an IoU threshold of 0.5. 
A Bounding Box(Box) is a fundamental output in object 
detection tasks within computer vision, used to delineate the 
approximate location of a target in an image. In contrast, an 
Instance Mask(Mask) serves as the core output for instance 
segmentation, providing a pixel-wise distinction between 
the target and the background, thereby offering a more 
precise delineation than a bounding box. 
B. Experimental Setup 
To ensure the fairness of the experiments, all trials were 
conducted under identical environmental conditions and 
parameter settings. The hardware configuration utilized 
an NVIDIA RTX 3090(24 GB) GPU and an Intel Xeon 
Platinum 8352V CPU @ 2.10GHz. The experiments were 
conducted in a software environment consisting of Ubuntu 
20.04, Python 3.8, PyTorch 2.0.0, and CUDA 11.8. The

IV. 
CONCLUSIONS 
This study proposed a dual-branch RGB-D multimodal 
instance segmentation model based on YOLOv11n-seg. 
The designed dual-branch feature extraction network, 
combined with the introduced cross-modal interaction 
attention module and dynamic upsampling module, 
achieved improvements of 2.9% and 2.7% in Box mAP50 
and Mask mAP50, respectively, on the public PApple-
RGB-D-Size dataset compared to the base model. The 
proposed model demonstrates robust capability in complex 
orchard 
environments, 
effectively 
handling 
various 
challenging conditions such as occlusion and backlighting 
to achieve accurate apple localization. This work provides a 
solid technical foundation for automated and intelligent 
robotic harvesting in orchards. 
Subsequent research will aim to refine the model 
architecture and implement it on edge devices for evaluating 
its real-time capabilities. We will also expand the dataset to 
accommodate nocturnal harvesting scenarios, thereby 
enhancing the model's adaptability and generalization 
capability in practical orchard harvesting environments.
