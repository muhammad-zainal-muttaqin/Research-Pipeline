A B S T R A C T
The present study proposes a passion fruit yield prediction model based on camera features and seman-
tic segmentation to address challenges in fruit recognition and yield estimation. The model integrates a 
density-attention mechanism and cross-multi-scale feature fusion, enhancing detection accuracy in complex 
backgrounds, small fruit instances, and high-density fruit scenarios. Experimental results demonstrate that 
the proposed model outperforms existing state-of-the-art (SOTA) models across multiple metrics, achieving a 
precision of 0.88, a recall of 0.83, and an accuracy of 0.85, with an F1-score of 0.85. These results indicate 
superior robustness and generalization capability, providing novel methodological and theoretical support for 
fruit detection and yield estimation in smart agriculture.
1. Introduction
Passion fruit (Passiflora edulis) is a tropical fruit of significant 
economic value, widely utilized in the food processing, beverage, and 
pharmaceutical industries (He et al., 2020). Its unique flavor and rich 
nutritional composition have driven increasing market demand, leading 
to a global expansion in cultivation area (Ribeiro et al., 2019). The 
yield of passion fruit not only affects the economic returns of growers 
but also directly influences the stability of the entire industry chain. 
However, passion fruit production is subject to multiple influencing 
factors, including environmental conditions, management practices, 
and varietal differences, resulting in substantial yield fluctuations (de 
Jesus et al., 2022). Accurate yield estimation plays a crucial role in 
optimizing orchard management, enhancing the reliability of harvest 
predictions, and facilitating rational market supply planning (Cao et al., 
2021). As shown in Fig. 1, several key challenges are encountered in 
the current estimation of passion fruit yield. First, occlusion of fruits 
and background interference increase the difficulty of fruit identifi-
cation (Tang et al., 2023a). Passion fruit grows on a vine structure, 
with fruits often covered by leaves, branches, or other fruits. Moreover, 
variations in fruit color and texture at different growth stages further 
complicate the accurate localization and quantification of fruits (Ou 
et al., 2023). Second, the difficulty in detecting small fruits affects the 
accuracy of yield estimation (Xiao et al., 2023). Passion fruit exhibits

duplicate counting affects the reliability of yield prediction (He et al., 
2022). Due to the dense growth of fruits and variations in viewing 
angles, the same fruit may be recorded multiple times across different 
frames or perspectives. Effectively distinguishing previously counted 
fruits and preventing redundant counting remains a critical challenge 
in improving yield estimation accuracy (Xia et al., 2022).
Significant progress has been made in fruit detection and yield esti-
mation research in recent years. Traditional manual counting methods, 
which rely on visual inspection or manual measurement, have long 
been employed by growers. While intuitive, these methods are time-
consuming, labor-intensive, and prone to human error, making them 
unsuitable for the precise management of large-scale orchards (Shafiee 
et al., 2021). With advancements in computer vision, image process-
ing techniques based on color and shape have been applied to fruit 
detection. These methods identify fruits by extracting color features, 
edge information, or morphological parameters, demonstrating feasi-
bility in controlled environments. However, their accuracy is signifi-
cantly reduced under conditions involving illumination variations, fruit 
occlusion, and complex background interference (Barbole et al., 2022).

In recent years, deep learning techniques have emerged as powerful 
tools for fruit detection and yield estimation. Object detection models 
such as YOLO and Faster R-CNN have achieved promising results in 
fruit detection for crops like apples and citrus (Chu et al., 2021; Tang 
et al., 2023b). For instance, Gao et al. proposed an apple fruit detection 
and counting method based on deep learning and trunk tracking, 
integrating the YOLOv4-tiny network with the CSR-DCF (Channel Spa-
tial Reliability-Discriminative Correlation Filter) tracking algorithm to 
enhance fruit counting accuracy in modern orchard environments. Liu 
et al. introduced a dynamic fruit localization system, ORB-Livox, based 
on ORB-SLAM3 and YOLO-V5. By incorporating LiDAR depth measure-
ments and Gaussian distribution clustering optimization, this system 
achieved high-precision fruit detection and localization in dynamic or-
chard settings (Liu et al., 2023). Wu et al. developed an automatic fruit 
counting system, NDMFCS, for apple orchards, utilizing YOLOv4-tiny 
and an anomaly fruit detection suppression mechanism. This system 
eliminates background and ground fruits using a thresholding approach 
and employs trunk tracking with ID assignment to improve fruit de-
tection, tracking, and counting accuracy, demonstrating effectiveness 
in modern orchards (Wu et al., 2023). Despite these advancements, 
existing methods still face limitations in passion fruit yield estimation. 
Severe false detections and missed detections occur due to fruit oc-
clusion in complex backgrounds, while densely distributed small fruits 
remain difficult to accurately identify. Additionally, variations in fruit 
size and uneven distribution density pose challenges for feature ex-
traction. Furthermore, conventional object detection methods struggle 
to directly estimate the actual size-to-quantity relationship of fruits, 
restricting the accuracy of yield estimation. Therefore, more precise and 
robust computer vision methods must be further explored to address 
the unique growth characteristics and yield estimation requirements of 
passion fruit.
To address the aforementioned challenges, this study proposes an in-
telligent detection framework for precise passion fruit yield estimation, 
in which a tailored data augmentation strategy mitigates the effects 
of occlusion and insufficient small-fruit samples, a density-attention 
mechanism enhances the recognition of densely distributed small fruits, 
a cross-scale feature fusion module addresses size variation and uneven 
distribution density, and a camera-parameter-based yield prediction 
network establishes an accurate mapping from pixel measurements to 
fruit quantity. The primary innovations include:
1. To mitigate the issues of fruit occlusion and insufficient small 
fruit samples, a data augmentation strategy tailored for complex 
orchard environments is introduced. By innovatively leveraging 
the cutout method, realistic occlusion by leaves is effectively 
simulated, improving the model’s robustness to occlusions. Ad-
ditionally, the copy-and-resize technique is employed to gener-
ate multi-scale small fruit samples and high-density fruit dis-
tributions, significantly improving the model’s adaptability and 
robustness in real orchard conditions.
2. To address the challenge of densely distributed small fruits that 
are difficult to accurately identify, a density-attention mecha-
nism is designed. By enhancing feature representation in dense 
regions and optimizing attention weight allocation, the percep-
tion and recognition of small fruits in high-density areas are 
strengthened, leading to a significant improvement in small fruit 
detection accuracy.
3. To overcome the variations in fruit size and the uneven dis-
tribution density of passion fruit, a cross-scale feature fusion 
mechanism is developed. By integrating multi-scale information 
through interactive fusion, the detection performance for both 
large and small fruits, as well as in dense and sparse regions, is 
simultaneously optimized, enhancing the model’s generalization 
and accuracy.
4. To resolve the limitations of traditional methods in accurately 
mapping fruit size to quantity, a passion fruit yield prediction 
network based on camera characteristics and semantic segmen-
tation is proposed. By extracting the pixel count of fruits through 
semantic segmentation and establishing a precise pixel-to-actual-
size correspondence using camera parameters, high-precision 
fruit quantity estimation and yield prediction are achieved.
In this study, a passion fruit yield estimation method based on the 
density-attention mechanism and cross-scale feature fusion mechanism 
is proposed and systematically evaluated in orchard environments. 
Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method outper-
forms existing approaches in terms of recognition accuracy, small 
fruit detection capability, and duplicate counting elimination, signifi-
cantly enhancing the reliability and practicality of passion fruit yield 
estimation. The structure of this paper is organized as follows: Sec-
tion 1 introduces the research background, challenges, and objectives. 
Section 2 provides a detailed description of data collection, model 
architecture, and experimental design. Section 3 presents an analysis of 
the experimental results to validate the effectiveness of the proposed 
method. Section 4 summarizes the research findings and discusses 
potential future directions.

hance its adaptability under varying lighting conditions, occlusions, 
and changes in fruit density, as illustrated in Fig. 3. Brightness adjust-
ment, hue adjustment, and saturation adjustment were employed as 
key augmentation methods to enable the model to adapt to different 
lighting conditions and color deviations caused by imaging devices. 
For instance, under strong illumination, fruits may appear overexposed,

eralization capability under complex lighting, diverse occlusions, and 
high-density fruit distributions was significantly improved, ensuring 
stability and accuracy in practical applications.
2.3. Proposed method
The passion fruit yield prediction method proposed in this study 
begins with image data input, where the semantic segmentation module 
first separates the passion fruit from the background and calculates the 
number of fruit pixels. Subsequently, the physical size of the fruit is 
obtained by mapping the pixels to the actual object size, using camera 
features. The density-attention mechanism then optimizes the detection 
of fruits in dense areas, enhancing the model’s ability to recognize 
small fruits. Finally, the cross-multi-scale feature fusion module merges 
feature information from different scales, comprehensively optimizing 
the recognition of large and small fruits, as well as dense and sparse 
scenarios. Through the effective integration of these modules, the 
model is capable of performing efficient and accurate passion fruit yield 
prediction.

2.3.1. Passion fruit yield prediction network based on camera features and 
semantic segmentation
As shown in Fig. 4, the passion fruit yield prediction network based 
on camera features and semantic segmentation is the core method of 
this study. It aims to accurately identify passion fruit using seman-
tic segmentation technology from deep learning and combine camera 
feature parameters to convert image pixels into actual physical sizes, 
thereby providing accurate yield estimation. The network is primar-
ily divided into three parts: image semantic segmentation, pixel-to-
physical-size mapping, and yield prediction. In terms of image pro-
cessing, a convolutional neural network (CNN)-based semantic seg-
mentation module is used, specifically implemented with the U-Net 
structure, which has demonstrated strong performance in medical im-
age segmentation, with strong feature extraction and reconstruction 
capabilities.
The U-Net architecture consists of an encoder and a decoder. The 
encoder extracts features from the image through convolution and 
pooling operations, gradually reducing the spatial resolution of the 
image to capture abstract high-level features. The decoder then restores 
the spatial resolution of the image through upsampling, progressively 
reconstructing the detailed information of the image, ultimately gener-
ating a segmentation result that matches the original image size. The 
output of the semantic segmentation is a binary map of the same size 
as the input image, where the passion fruit region is marked as 1, and 
the background as 0. This step provides accurate regional information 
for subsequent fruit detection and yield estimation. Next, based on the 
segmented passion fruit pixel area, the pixel-to-physical-size mapping 
is performed using camera feature parameters. To achieve this, the 
camera’s intrinsic and extrinsic parameters must be considered. The 
intrinsic parameters include the focal length, sensor size, etc., while 
the extrinsic parameters describe the position and orientation of the 
camera. By mapping the pixel coordinates in the image to the actual

2.3.3. Cross-multi-scale feature fusion mechanism
The cross-multi-scale feature fusion mechanism is designed in this 
study to address the challenges of feature extraction in passion fruit at 
different growth stages and distribution densities, particularly in opti-
mizing scenarios involving large fruits, small fruits, and dense or sparse 
regions, as shown in Fig. 6. Traditional convolutional neural networks 
often struggle to handle features from multiple scales simultaneously. 
However, this mechanism effectively captures features of fruits with 
varying sizes by fusing multi-scale features within a unified framework, 
thereby improving the model’s recognition accuracy and robustness. 
The mechanism adopts multi-scale convolution operations and feature 
fusion methods. Specifically, different feature maps are first extracted 
through convolution layers, with each layer having varying kernel sizes, 
strides, and channel numbers to capture features at different scales. In 
the initial design, a 7 × 7 kernel is used to extract low-level image 
features, with the resulting feature map having a size of 480 × 480 
and 256 channels. A max pooling layer is then applied to reduce 
the dimensionality, decreasing the feature map size to 240 × 240. 
Subsequently, multiple 1 × 1 convolution layers are employed to extract 
more refined feature information, with each layer having 256 channels. 
Through multiple convolution and pooling operations, the network 
extracts feature maps containing rich information at different scales. 
To further enhance feature diversity and adaptability, the next step 
involves upsampling the lower-resolution feature maps to restore their 
size to the original image dimensions. This process allows the network 
to focus on detailed features at high resolution while retaining the 
contextual information extracted at lower resolutions. This operation 
ensures effective feature extraction in both dense small-fruit regions 
and sparse large-fruit regions. In terms of feature fusion, a cross-
multi-scale fusion method is applied, which involves weighted fusion 
of feature maps at different scales. This process ensures that fruits 
of different sizes receive appropriate attention, preventing small fruit 
details from being overlooked while also capturing the overall features 
of large fruits. Specifically, assuming two feature maps 𝐹1 and 𝐹2 with 
sizes 𝑊1 × 𝐻1 × 𝐶1 and 𝑊2 × 𝐻2 × 𝐶2, where 𝑊 and 𝐻 represent width 
and height, and 𝐶 denotes the number of channels, the fused feature 
map 𝐹𝑓𝑢𝑠𝑖𝑜𝑛 is computed as follows: 
𝐹𝑓𝑢𝑠𝑖𝑜𝑛= 𝛼⋅𝐹1 + 𝛽⋅𝐹2,

where 𝛼 and 𝛽 are weight coefficients that control the contribution of 
each scale’s feature map to the final output. In this study, through cross-
multi-scale feature fusion, features at different scales are fully utilized, 
ensuring that the network maintains high recognition accuracy in both 
large and small fruit scenarios. These fused feature maps are then 
fed into the subsequent semantic segmentation network for passion 
fruit recognition, followed by yield prediction combined with camera 
features. By fusing features from different scales, not only is the model’s 
ability to recognize fruits at different growth stages improved, but its 
adaptability in varying fruit distribution densities is also enhanced. In 
dense regions, where small fruits may be occluded by other fruits, the 
multi-scale feature fusion allows the network to effectively differentiate 
these fruits. In sparse regions, the fusion of features helps the network 
better capture the characteristics of large fruits. When combined with 
the ‘‘passion fruit yield prediction network based on camera features 
and semantic segmentation’’, the cross-multi-scale feature fusion mech-
anism greatly enhances the network’s recognition capability. In the 
passion fruit yield prediction task, this mechanism aids the network 
in accurately identifying fruits in complex growth environments, pro-
viding more precise input for subsequent yield estimation. Ultimately, 
through these designs, the network maintains high precision and ro-
bustness in handling various growth environments, fruit sizes, and 
densities, improving the overall reliability and effectiveness of the yield 
prediction.
2.4. Experimental configuration
The dataset was partitioned into training, validation, and test sets 
using a 7:2:1 ratio, a partitioning strategy widely adopted in previous 
studies for similar deep learning tasks (Ranjan et al., 2023; Biswal et al., 
2020). Specifically, 70% of the data was allocated to the training set 
for model parameter optimization and feature learning, while 20% was 
designated as the validation set to fine-tune hyperparameters and moni-
tor overfitting during training. The remaining 10% was assigned to the 
test set for evaluating the final model performance. This partitioning 
strategy was chosen to ensure that the model is exposed to a sufficiently 
diverse set of passion fruit samples during training while preserving an

A stratified sampling strategy was adopted to maintain a balanced

biases introduced by random partitioning, multiple rounds of random 
resampling were conducted after the initial split. Training results under 
different partitioning strategies were compared to verify that model 
performance remained unaffected by variations in data partitioning.
For network training, the initial learning rate was set to 1 × 10−4
and was decreased by a factor of 0.1 every 50 epochs using a step decay 
schedule. The batch size was fixed at 16, and the model was trained for 
200 epochs in total. The Adam optimizer was employed for parameter 
updates with default momentum parameters (𝛽1 = 0.9, 𝛽2 = 0.999) 
and a weight decay of 1 × 10−5. All convolutional and normalization 
layers were initialized using the Xavier uniform initialization strat-
egy. The loss function combined binary cross-entropy loss and Dice 
loss to balance region classification and boundary accuracy. Training 
was conducted on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU (24 GB VRAM) with 
CUDA 11.8, and the implementation was developed in Python 3.10 
using the PyTorch 2.0 framework.
In addition, a camera calibration protocol was implemented to

2.6. Baseline
UNet (Ronneberger et al., 2015) is a classical end-to-end fully 
convolutional network architecture that utilizes an encoder–decoder 
structure with skip connections to retain spatial information. Its U-
shaped design effectively integrates contextual and localization fea-
tures, making it well-suited for segmentation tasks. UNet++ (Zhou 
et al., 2018) is an improved version of UNet that introduces nested skip 
connections and a deep supervision mechanism to reduce the semantic 
gap between the encoder and decoder. This redesigned skip connection 
strategy enhances feature fusion efficiency, improving segmentation 
performance. Mask R-CNN (He et al., 2017) is an instance segmenta-
tion framework based on Faster R-CNN, incorporating an additional 
parallel mask prediction branch to enable precise instance-level seg-
mentation. Its RoIAlign layer addresses feature extraction quantization 
issues, significantly refining mask quality. Segmenter (Strudel et al., 
2021) is a semantic segmentation model based on a pure Transformer 
architecture, leveraging Vision Transformer to capture global context, 
overcoming the local receptive field limitations of traditional convo-
lutional networks. SegFormer (Xie et al., 2021) is a lightweight and 
efficient semantic segmentation framework that integrates a hierar-
chical Transformer encoder with a lightweight MLP decoder. Unlike 
traditional methods, it eliminates the need for positional encoding, 
preventing performance degradation when the test resolution differs 
from the training resolution. The MLP decoder effectively fuses local 
and global features while maintaining a compact parameter footprint 
and demonstrating strong generalization capabilities. FCOS-EAM (Jia 
et al., 2024) is a two-stage green fruit segmentation method based 
on FCOS, designed to handle overlapping fruits in complex orchard 
environments. It introduces a Boundary Attention Module (BAM) to 
refine boundary features for improved detection and an Edge Attention 
Module (EAM) to enhance edge feature extraction for more precise 
segmentation. Additionally, a non-local affinity parsing technique is 
employed to learn the shared visual characteristics of fruits. MViTv2 
combined with Cascade Mask R-CNN and an Image Slicing Frame-
work (El Akrouchi et al., 2025) leverages multi-scale Vision Trans-
former features alongside a cascaded mask R-CNN structure, further 
enhancing detection robustness across different fruit sizes. The inte-
gration of image slicing techniques improves performance, particularly 
for small and densely packed fruits. Mask R-CNN with Soft-NMS (Chen 
et al., 2023) replaces the traditional non-maximum suppression algo-
rithm with a linear weighting method, effectively mitigating issues 
related to occlusions in fruit counting and segmentation tasks. This 
approach enhances detection stability in scenarios with highly clustered 
objects.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Comparison with SOTA models in object detection
The purpose of this experiment is to compare the segmentation 
performance of different models, particularly in the context of passion 
fruit image recognition, and to evaluate the performance of various 
object detection models in terms of precision, recall, and accuracy. The 
experiment compares classic state-of-the-art (SOTA) object detection 
models such as UNet, Mask R-CNN, UNet++, Segmenter, Segformer, 
and the model proposed in this study, to analyze their advantages 
and limitations for this specific task. Through these comparisons, the 
characteristics, strengths, and weaknesses of each model are revealed, 
providing a theoretical basis for subsequent optimization.
As shown in Table 1, Figs. 9, 8 and 7, the proposed model out-
performs all other models across various metrics. The relatively lower 
precision of UNet and Mask R-CNN can be attributed to their weaker 
performance in handling small fruits and dense regions. UNet++ shows 
improvements in these aspects, indicating its advantage in processing 
complex images, particularly in handling target segmentation bound-
aries. Segmenter and Segformer gradually approach the performance 
of the proposed model, but still fall short in terms of precision and F1 
score. From a theoretical standpoint, the advantages of the proposed 
model lie primarily in its design characteristics, especially the combina-
tion of multi-scale feature fusion and the density-attention mechanism. 
UNet and its variants perform well when handling single-scale features, 
but in complex scenes, particularly when fruit distribution is highly 
dense, they struggle to adequately capture multi-scale information, 
leading to low detection accuracy in dense regions. The proposed 
model utilizes the density-attention mechanism to dynamically adjust 
attention weights, particularly optimizing for small fruits and dense 
regions, which allows the network to maintain high precision in dense 
areas. This is one of the main reasons for the relatively higher mAP@50 
and mAP@95 scores. Furthermore, the cross-multi-scale feature fu-
sion mechanism ensures that features are captured effectively across 
different scales, thereby improving the overall prediction accuracy.
3.2. Comparison with SOTA models in smart agriculture
The purpose of this experiment is to compare the performance of dif-
ferent smart agriculture SOTA models in passion fruit yield prediction 
tasks, with a focus on precision and robustness in image segmenta-
tion, object detection, and recognition. By comparing several advanced 
models, such as MViTv2 + Cascade Mask R-CNN + Image Slicing 
Framework, Mask R-CNN + Soft-NMS + Linear Weighting, and FCOS-
EAM, the aim is to analyze the strengths and weaknesses of each model 
and their application performance in complex agricultural environ-
ments. This experiment provides valuable insights for object detection 
and yield estimation in smart agriculture, particularly in precision 
farming, where challenges such as complex backgrounds, dense fruit 
distribution, and small fruit detection need to be addressed.
As shown in Table 2, Figs. 9 and 10, the proposed model outper-
forms all other models across various metrics, particularly in precision, 
recall, and accuracy. While MViTv2 + Cascade Mask R-CNN + Im-
age Slicing Framework has certain advantages in handling complex 
scenes, it exhibits weaker performance in detecting small fruits and 
dense regions, indicating a lack of precision in detail capture and 
high-accuracy detection. The precision of Mask R-CNN + Soft-NMS 
+ Linear Weighting shows improvement, suggesting that this model 
enhances detection accuracy through post-processing techniques such 
as Soft-NMS, but still faces limitations in handling high-density areas, 
particularly in small object detection. FCOS-EAM shows further im-
provement in precision and recall, particularly in detecting dense areas 
and small fruits. However, the proposed model performs the best across 
all tested metrics, demonstrating significant advantages in detecting 
complex backgrounds, small fruits, and dense regions. These results 
indicate that the proposed model can effectively address the challenges 
associated with passion fruit yield prediction in complex agricultural 
settings, providing more accurate results.

3.4. Analysis of density-attention mechanism
The density-attention mechanism in this study plays a pivotal role 
in identifying densely populated fruit regions and small fruit targets. 
Through heatmap visualization, as shown in Fig. 12, it becomes evi-
dent that the network allocates higher attention weights to areas with 
particularly dense fruit distributions. This approach not only effectively 
reduces missed detections among overlapping or adjacent fruits but 
also ensures more accurate recognition of small fruits. The heatmaps 
indicate that when the number of fruits increases and their distribution 
becomes uneven, the network adaptively enhances attention alloca-
tion to relevant pixels, thus maintaining high detection accuracy and 
robustness even in complex or heavily occluded environments. This 
density-focused dynamic approach enables the model to handle fruits of 
varying sizes and densities in real-world agricultural settings, thereby 
improving the overall accuracy of yield predictions.
3.5. Practical applicability and real-world feasibility
The proposed method is designed to address three key challenges 
that frequently occur in commercial passion fruit production: fruit 
occlusion by leaves and branches, the difficulty of detecting small 
fruits at early growth stages, and the risk of double counting in dense 
fruit clusters. These issues often lead to substantial errors in manual 
yield estimation, which in turn affect harvest planning and market 
supply scheduling. To overcome these challenges, the framework in-
tegrates a density-attention mechanism and a cross-multi-scale feature 
fusion strategy in a mutually reinforcing manner. The density-attention 
module adaptively increases the network’s focus on image regions 
with high fruit density, ensuring that small and partially occluded 
fruits receive sufficient representational weight during feature extrac-
tion. This targeted attention allocation reduces the risk of missed 
detections in complex backgrounds and minimizes false negatives in 
early-stage fruits that are visually similar to surrounding foliage. In 
parallel, the cross-multi-scale feature fusion module aggregates infor-
mation from multiple receptive fields, combining fine-grained local 
features from high-resolution layers with broader contextual cues from 
lower-resolution layers. This fusion allows the network to maintain 
sensitivity to small fruit boundaries while simultaneously capturing 
spatial relationships among densely distributed fruits. When combined, 
these two modules create a complementary effect: the density-attention 
mechanism ensures that the most challenging regions are prioritized 
for analysis, while the multi-scale fusion provides the necessary fea-
ture diversity to resolve ambiguities in those regions. This synergy 
enables the model not only to identify individual fruits with higher 
precision but also to avoid redundant detections in overlapping views, 
thereby improving the accuracy and stability of yield predictions under 
real-world orchard conditions. From a quantitative perspective, the 
proposed method achieves a precision of 0.88 and recall of 0.83 in 
complex orchard environments (Table 1), which translates into a sig-
nificant reduction in false detections compared to traditional detection 
models (e.g., +8% mAP@50 improvement over Segformer, +14% over 
UNet++). In difficult cases, such as small fruit detection and dense 
cluster counting (Table 3), the model achieves mAP@50–95 scores 
of 0.64 and 0.61, respectively—representing relative improvements of 
15%–20% over competing state-of-the-art methods. These gains are 
particularly important for automated harvesting scheduling and market 
supply planning, where underestimation or overestimation can directly 
impact economic returns.

4. Conclusions
A passion fruit yield prediction model based on camera features 
and semantic segmentation is proposed in this study, aiming to address 
the challenges of fruit recognition and yield estimation in agricul-
ture. Traditional object detection and recognition methods often face 
issues such as low accuracy and poor generalization capability in 
cases involving dense fruit, complex backgrounds, and varying fruit 
sizes. The innovations of this study primarily lie in three aspects: first, 
the proposed density-attention mechanism dynamically adjusts the at-
tention weights, particularly optimizing for dense regions and small 
fruits; second, through multi-scale feature fusion, the model effectively 
captures features at different scales, ensuring high recognition accu-
racy across fruit scenes with varying sizes and distribution densities; 
and third, by integrating camera features with semantic segmentation, 
the precise conversion from pixels to actual physical dimensions is 
achieved, providing a reliable basis for accurate yield estimation. The 
experimental results demonstrate that the proposed model exhibits 
significant advantages in passion fruit yield prediction tasks. In the 
object detection task, the model achieved a precision of 0.88, recall 
of 0.83, accuracy of 0.85, mAP@50 of 0.84, mAP@95 of 0.58, and an 
F1-score of 0.85, all of which significantly outperform existing models. 
Particularly in complex backgrounds, small fruit, and dense fruit sce-
narios, the proposed model, through the density-attention mechanism 
and multi-scale feature fusion, not only improves detection accuracy 
but also effectively addresses the issue of missed detections caused by 
fruit density or overlap. Compared to existing models such as MViTv2 
+ Cascade Mask R-CNN + Image Slicing Framework, Mask R-CNN +
Soft-NMS + Linear Weighting, and FCOS-EAM, the proposed model 
demonstrates higher precision and robustness across all test metrics.
