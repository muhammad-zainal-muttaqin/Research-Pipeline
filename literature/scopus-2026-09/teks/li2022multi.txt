Abstract—Locating and grasping occluded fruits are cru-
cial and challenging tasks in robotic harvest, suffering from
insufﬁcient ﬁlling rate and noise of stereo cameras. In this
paper, we propose a multi-vision-based method to locate and
estimate a grasping pose for an occluded fruit, based on a
deep learning multi-task network and a new frustum-based
method of point-cloud-processing. The multi-task deep learning
network is presented to detect the complete bounding box and
segment the visible part of occluded targets. Once the detection
and segmentation are ﬁnished, we introduce the concept of 3D
frustum for ease of estimating the centroid of the visible part and
then reconstruct the shape of the occluded fruits. Accordingly,
the estimation of the grasping approach is derived. Combining
the measurements from multiple stereo visions, we obtained a
new centroid of the fruit and an grasping pose of the occluded
fruit. To demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method,
the experiments in orchards were performed. All shown results
supported the research claims.
I. INTRODUCTION
The agricultural environment, which is with highly non-
structural properties, has always been a major challenge for
harvesting robotics research. Although the crop canopy is
effectively narrowed in standardized orchards, occlusions of
fruits are still common [1]. Statistically, around 26.4% of ki-
wifruit [2], 40% of strawberries [3], 50% of tomatoes [4], and
65% of citrus [5] are affected by different degrees of occlusion
in the period of harvest. In our previous investigation, we
counted the occlusions of 100 dwarf densely planted apples

in the harvest period and found that around 62% of apple
fruits were occluded to varying degrees. It can be seen that
the occlusion problem is a common problem faced by the
harvesting robots of fruits and vegetables, including tomato,
strawberry, citrus, and apple [6], which has attracted extensive
attention of researchers. Existing studies have shown that the
impacts of occlusion on the accuracy of fruit localization
are manifested in three aspects: a) the image fruit detection
process is easily affected by occlusion, resulting in the missing
and/or false detections [7]; b) it is difﬁcult to effectively extract
edge features in dark places [8]; c) the obstruction of the
occluded fruit easily causes the system to locate the obstacle
or the local area of the fruit during the measurement of stereo
vision, and the estimated position has a large deviation from
the actual position [9].
In order to improve the recognition and positioning accu-
racy of fruits under occlusion conditions, researchers used
morphological methods to ﬁnely extract fruit boundaries. [10]
used local shape matching and probabilistic Hough transform
methods to detect and aggregate fruit fragments under oc-
clusion conditions. The detection accuracy of spherical fruits
such as citrus, tomato, etc., can reach 78.3% and 84.8%. [11]
proposed an occluded apple target recognition method which
is based on the convex hull theory, using the convex hull
of the target edge to estimate the circle center and radius
parameters, the average positioning error is about 4.28%. Such
a method is intuitive, interpretable, and simple to implement.
However, because of the lack of color and spatial information,
it generally has the defects of insufﬁcient accuracy, high
misrecognition rate, and weak robustness, which limits the

applications in large-scale complex and changeable scenes.
Some researchers address the localization problem of occluded
fruits through image inpainting methods, such as diffusion
inpainting method [12], patch inpainting method [13]. In these
methods, researchers repair and ﬁll image texture and color
to restore fruit image features. But the limitation is that the
ability of generalization is weak when the missing part is large.
As deep learning techniques advance, the use of generative
adversarial networks for inpainting [14] and reconstruction
[15] of occluded objects has also received increasing attention.
[16] adopted the conditional adversarial generative network
Pix2Pix model to supplement the image information input and
improve the accuracy of semantic segmentation of occluded
apple branches.
The above morphological methods and inpainting methods
are aimed at the problem of pixel-level recognition, detec-
tion and localization in the two-dimensional image domain.
Limited by the information dimension, the pixel-level local-
ization method, however, is difﬁcult to make full use of the
three-dimensional morphological information of the targets.
In recent years, increasing investigators have been using 3D
stereo vision information to study the occlusion problem. [17]
proposed a 3D point cloud reconstruction method of fruit
based on the symmetry plane when studying the location of
occluded strawberry fruit in the greenhouse, taking advantage
of the symmetry of fruit shape. [18] used the multi-channel
information of color maps, depth maps, and near-infrared
images collected by time-of-ﬂight (ToF) stereo cameras to
construct an Unet-like network [19], replacing the adversarial
generative network to realize the localization and reconstruc-
tion of tomatoes in the greenhouse. [20] aimed at the robot
picking problem under the hybrid vision conﬁguration, using
two stereo cameras in the vision conﬁguration to obtain the
stereo vision information under different perspectives. By the
Euclidean distance analysis and the intersection of the three-
dimensional fruit bounding box, the information on locations
and sizes of occluded fruits were extracted. [21] studied the
accurate localization method of occluded fruits in the orchard
environment, using an improved instance segmentation net-
work model to extract the visible area of the occluded fruits
and the complete bounding box, respectively, with the point
cloud generated by the active binocular stereo camera. A
RANSAC-based method is employed to process the fruit point
cloud and to estimate the size and position parameters of apple
fruit. As a result, the intersection ratio and centroid positioning
errors are 0.83 and 5.69 mm, respectively.
The above research results show that the accurate iden-
tiﬁcation and localization of occluded fruits in unstructured
environments is a hot topic that attracts many researchers.
Positive progress has been made in the reconstruction of
fruit position and morphological parameters based on 2D and
3D image information. Despite these developments, there are
still some issues to be solved. Under natural illumination
conditions, affected by local highlights and occluded pixel
matching, the 3D point cloud information generated by stereo
camera generally has excessive edge smoothing and ﬂying
point noise, which makes it difﬁcult to ﬁt the spherical surface
and reconstruct the shape. The existing related research mostly
considers the occlusion positioning of crops in the greenhouse,
but the research on three-dimensional visual positioning of
occluded fruits suitable for orchard illumination environment
is insufﬁcient, and the positioning accuracy of occluded fruits
under natural light is insufﬁcient, which puzzles the further
improvement of robot picking success rate. Given this, the
contributions of this paper are as follows:
(a) A multi-task depth convolution neural network is pro-
posed. By extracting the depth feature of the RGB image
information of the occluded fruit, the complete bounding box
detection and visible part instance segmentation of the fruit
can be completed.
(b) Based on the proposed multi-task depth convolution
neural network, a multi-vision method for fruit location and
pose estimation is proposed. In this method, the morphological
information of occluded fruits is obtained from the point
clouds generated by multiple stereo cameras through the frus-
tum and three-dimensional point cloud processing technology,
for the ease of robotic grasping.
II. IMAGE FRUIT DETECTION AND INSTANCE
SEGMENTATION
In this section, we develop a multi-camera vision system
for harvesting robots. Based on the vision system, a method of
occluded fruit localization and pose estimation for harvesting
robots is investigated by a multi-task DCNN and a frustum-
based point-cloud-processing algorithm.
To achieve the tasks of fruit detection and visible part
segmentation altogether, a fruit perception single-shot network
network has been proposed, which contains one shared en-
coder and two subsequent decoders. There are no complex
and redundant shared blocks between the two decoders, which
makes the network be end-to-end trained easily.
Fig. 1: The architecture of the proposed multi-task network
The encoder of the multi-task network contains two net-
works, backbone and neck. The backbone network is for
extracting and understanding the abstract features of input
images. In order to solves the problem of gradient duplication
during optimization, we used CSPdarknet as the backbone,
which has achieved good performance on YOLOv4. At the

same time, it enables feature propagation and feature reuse to
reduce the number of parameters and calculations, which is
beneﬁcial to ensure the real-time performance of the network.
Then, we use the neck to fuse the different sacle and semantic
features output from the backbone. The neck network contains
two modules, Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPP)[22] and Feature
Pyramid Network (FPN) [23]. The generation and fusion of
the features at different scales are accomplished by SPP, and
the fusion of the features at different semantic levels are made
by FPN, which let multiple scales and multiple semantic level
information in the generated features.
Moreover, the proposed network contains two decoders for
detection task and segmentation task: the detect head and the
segment head.
1) Detect Head: Given that FPN can only passes down
high-level semantic features, which is a pattern of top-down,
a bottom-up network with feature pyramid called Path Ag-
gregation Network (PAN), is used to make it easier for the
feature information at the bottom to reach the top. We combine
the semantic features transferred by FPN and the positioning
features transferred by PAN to obtain a better feature fusion
effect before detection. Then, we use an anchor-free detector
to reduce the number of design parameters, which means there
is only one prediction for each grid of the multi-scale feature
map to predict four values directly. Additionally, the outputs of
the detector are decoupled. Namely, the categories, conﬁdence,
and location are independent.
2) Segment Head: The segmentation branch starts at the
bottom of the FPN, which has the size of (W/8, H/8, 256).
Through three up-sampling operations with the CSP layer, the
size of the output feature map has been restored to (W, H,
2). The two channels of the output represent the segmentation
result of the apple and the background. We set the background
as a separate category to avoid the threshold selection for a
single channel.
III. LOCALIZATION AND POSE ESTIMATION WITH
MULTIPLE-STEREO-CAMERA
In practice, defects and outliers exist in the point cloud,
causing debris, holes and distortion in the visible part, which is
caused by the characteristics of the sensor and the inaccuracy
of mask segmentation. Using the point clouds of the visible
part directly is error-prone to locate the whole position of a
fruit. To address the issue, we put forward a frustum-based
localization and pose estimation method in our previous work
[21], where a pipeline for occluded fruits was presented, as
shown in Figure 2, which can accomplish reconstruction of
an occluded fruit’s shape and position from a single view. In
this paper, to provide more information on occluded fruits,
we employed multiple cameras to cooperatively construct a
multi-view sensing framework, as shown in Figure 3. By
the proposed method, the position and pose of an occluded
fruit could be generated from every frame of RGBD images.
The combination of two perspectives has two advantages: a)
the positional relationship between an occluded fruit and its
shelter could be detected clearly; b) two sets of positioning

As shown in Figure 2, to train the segmentation network for
the proposed multi-task DCNN, two kinds of datasets were
collected: an open-source MinneApple dataset[24] containing
1000 images with over 41k labeled instances of apples, and the
dataset manually collected in two modern standard orchards
in Haidian District and Changping District, Beijing, China,
acquired by Realsense D435i RGBD camera, containing 1000
images with over 12k labeled instances and bounding boxes of
apples with different kinds of occlusion: non-, leaf-, branch-
and fruit-occlusion.
We trained the datasets on the graphic workstation with two
Xeon 12 core CPU, 128GB RM, and NVIDIA RTX2080Ti.
The training and validation sets are distributed into 4:1.
In the training stage, we employ a) photometric distortion
including random contrast, random saturation, random hue,
random brightness, b) random mirror operation, c) random
sample crop, d) random ﬂip, e) random expansion to augment
the dataset.
B. Hardware and software settings
The hardware of the robot includes a laptop for real-time
control, a mobile track platform, a stereo camera and a 7-
DOF robotic arm. The laptop has 8-core processor with the
frequency up to 4.2GHz, which can ensure the stable opera-
tions of the control system. The wheeled robotic platform has
motors with the power of 650W and the maximum working
speed of 5km/h. A stereo camera, realsense D435, collects
the color and depth images at the resolution of 1280x720 at
the same time, to generate high precision pointclouds for the
environmental sensing. The end effector is with a three-ﬁnger
gripper that has a structure with Fin Ray effect, to achieve a
damage-free fruits grasping.
The software system of the robotic control is implemented
on Robot Operation System (ROS) Melodic, based on Linux
Ubuntu 18.04. It is responsible for the hardware abstraction of
the robot, the control of the underlying devices (e.g. cameras
and robotic arm), and the realization of common functions
(e.g. motion planning and robot visualization). ROS enables
inter-process communication through message passing, which
make each function module work independently and improve
the stability and expansibility of the system. In addition, we
use pytorch with CUDA and CUDNN for network prediction,
Open3D and OpenCV for the processing of images and point
cloud, MoveIt! framework for the motion planning of the
robotic arm.
