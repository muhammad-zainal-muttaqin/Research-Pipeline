Abstract. The world is changing day by day through technical innovations. Due to urbanization, climatic changes, 
land degradation, water scarcity and also through industrial revolution, migration of people from rural areas to 
urban areas is drastically increasing, as a result, the availability of labor for agricultural practices are reducing.
Agricultural production must be increased to supply the food required to the growing population. Precision farming
finds a way to overcome these challenges by providing various economical techniques for effective utilization of 
land and available resources with less involvement of labor. This paper presents a simple and efficient machine
vision mango fruit segmentation algorithm using image processing techniques. The proposed algorithm utilizes color 
features to segment the mango fruit. The dataset is collected for 5 months temporally from December 2018 to May
2019 right from the flowering stage to the maturation stage of the fruit under daylight condition. The proposed 
algorithm of mango fruit recognition is tested on the collected dataset. Dominant features are extracted from color
features, to obtain more information on the image through different color space RGB image is converted into HSV 
color space and then de-noising methods are applied to remove the noise formed because of not recognizing the 
presence of leaves and branches properly. After de-noising the image thresholding method is applied to remove the 
background. Finally, fruit recognition is performed with the help of segmentation methods. The developed 
algorithm gives good results of fruit region segmentation.
Keywords: Image processing, Fruit recognition, Fruit Segmentation.
1. INTRODUCTION
World has remarkably changed in technology and global demographics. From two decades, population 
growth has reached beyond the limit. The people are becoming more civilized and the rate of agricultural
practices are depleting. Available resources and land area for cultivation has incredibly reduced. The biggest
challenge is to use the available land for cultivation and double the production with minimum utilization of 
available resource by following non- invasive and non-destructive farming practices. This paper mainly focuses
on developing automatic mango fruit yield estimation system using machine vision techniques. Mango farming
is one of the leading fruit crops farming in India. It is estimated that 50% of the world’s mango production is
from India and the productivity rate ranks first among all other countries. Further, 39% of the land is utilized 
for fruit crops wherein, 23% of land accounts for mango crop cultivation alone. It is also noted that over a 
thousand varieties of mangos are cultivated only in India.
To address these issues, precision farming technique can be adopted for efficient use of land and resources 
to get increased yields compared to traditional cultivation practices. Precision farming helps in preventing soil 
degradation, reduction of chemical application in crop production, efficient use of water resources and also 
improves socio- economic status of farmers. In consideration with agronomical, technical, environmental and 
economical perspective adoption of precision farming in agriculture practices is need of the hour. The demand 
for automatic mango farming technology leads to the problem taken up in this article.

2. MATERIALS
Mango fruit is considered for yield estimation, the materials and criteria used to build the mango fruit dataset 
is explained in the following section. The built dataset is used for mango fruit segmentation.
2.1 Field of Study
The dataset was built using a mango orchard located at Yelachahalli, Yelwala, Mysore, Karnataka, India. The 
area is spread over 200 acres. This particular area is selected for experimentation due to high mango productivity. 
Constant fertilizing and controlling schemes are adopted. The controlling schemes include scheduling and quantity
of manuring, watering, weeding, pest and disease control, harvesting, etc. The temperature throughout the study was 
noted to have 27oC approximately on average.
2.2 Dataset
The dataset consists of images of mango trees, obtained with the main camera of mobile phone (Oneplus A5000,
dual camera 16MP, f/1.7, 24mm wide, 20MP, f/2.6, 36mm standard) under natural daylight conditions with an 
image resolution is 1920*1080 pixels. Data was collected for 5 months temporally in the interval of 7 days for the 
first 3 months. Then the frequency of data collection is increased for the subsequent two months based on fruit 
maturity. 800 images for a day is captured which includes images of all the 5 trees. The images were captured in two
sessions namely morning and evening, which is aligned with the direction of the sunlight. Further, we have captured 
these trees from distances of 2 and 3 meters respectively (Figure.1).
The selected trees from the orchid vary from 15-20 years of age. The trees range between 7-8 feet high from the 
surface. The size of each acquired image ranges between 7-8 MB. The dataset accounts to 21000 images altogether. 
In a day, the dataset is collected in two sessions: session 1 was between 9:30 am and 10:00 am and session 2 between 
2:30 pm and 3:00 pm from north-east and south-west directions respectively (Figure. 2 and Figure. 3). Therefore, the
acquired images cover the contour of a tree. However, top and bottom views are exempted from the acquisition. The 
dataset was collected throughout a season for five months.

Towards the segmentation of fruits from mango trees, the primary approach is to segment the tree in the image. 
As the images of the tree include the ground, sky, other background areas, it is important to segment the tree to get
better accuracy rate. A simple threshold-based statistical approach is employed for the matured stage mango fruit 
images of the dataset for tree and fruit region segmentation.

algorithms are utilized to separate mango fruit images by choosing red (R), blue (B) and green (G) color pixels 
against the background region, subsequently eliminating background region district in the procured images.

3.1 Color Feature Analysis 
Color is important characteristic of digital images. The threshold based color segmentation techniques uses the 
color pixel values present in the image and similar pixel values are grouped for the segmentation[10].The acquired 
image is usually encoded in RGB color model. Other color models can be obtained by converting RGB model to other 
color models. 
Red, blue and green color channels makes all possible colors. Analysis of color feature of mango tree images is 
carried out in RGB color model. Image is expressed through histograms, the distribution of background and 
foreground pixels in the original image can be clearly observed in R, G, B color layers. After removing background, it 
is difficult to differentiate the mango fruit with RGB color space. The background removed RGB image is converted 
in to HSV color space and histogram is plotted for each layer (Figure.6). Through histograms, threshold values are 
selected for segmentation. Based on maximum R, G, B and S color space pixel values the (R-G) and S values are 
calculated for segmentation of background and fruit region.

4. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The automatic generation of these representations enables the use of the method of recognition and elucidation
for the number of visible fruits estimation, which helps to take up the first step for the evolution of the yield 
estimation system for mango fruit. It is important to separate the fruit region from the masked image by removing 
leaves and trunk region to precisely detect the fruits in the image. Based on color analysis, the HSV space, similar to 
the human visualization model, showed the most prominent separation of the mango fruit region from the 
background. Therefore, this study used the H, S and V color space to define the range of the fruit area. As a result 
the background region is removed and only the fruit region is extracted. Color feature analysis is implemented to the 
collected dataset and the segmentation of the fruit region from the background region can be observed in the results 
given. Two level color thresholding based segmentation method is used for the fruit region segmentation. In the first 
level of segmentation sky, soil, twig, tree trunk region is eliminated. In the second level of segmentation leaves and 
other than fruit region is removed. RGB and HSV color space gave better results for fruit region extraction.
Thresholding segmentation method gave better results of fruit region segmentation for the collected dataset. As the 
dataset was collected in daylight conditions, during experimentation illumination and occlusion were the main 
challenges to handle, texture and shape analysis  for fruit region segmentation did not give good results compared to 
thresholding method. The proposed fruit region segmentation lays background for mango yield estimation system.

CONCLUSION
Two step fruit recognition algorithms are developed for fruit region segmentation. Mature mango fruits, based on 
color features are segmented. Dataset of 21000 mango tree images was developed with a commercial digital camera 
from flowering to maturation stage of mango fruit trees under natural light. The two phases of fruit region 
segmentation based on color-based segmentation has provided 93.33% results and lays an improved background for 
yield estimation of mango fruits. The dataset used in this work has led to successful fruit recognition in a mango
orchard. This likewise empowers assurance of single tree yields at a beginning phase of mango fruit improvement, a 
while before gather. Early and exact yield estimation will have enormous advantages for farmers; mango producers 
will have better control of financial matters, creation, reap the board and capacity limit. Area explicit administration 
will likewise be better executed, if yield expectation data are accessible earlier. These outcomes show extraordinary 
capability of the models for segmentation of mango fruit.
