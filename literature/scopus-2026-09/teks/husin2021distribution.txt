Abstract—Oil palm tree has been the key to Malaysia’s
economic expansion, where it has become the most important,
national agricultural crop. Techniques of planting, assessment,
and detection are crucial to harvest a good quality of palm oil.
At present, the ripeness of oil palm fresh fruit bunch (FFB)
is determined using computer vision, eyesight, near-infrared
(NIR) spectroscopy, light detection and ranging (LiDAR), and
Hue, Saturation, and Intensity (HSI) techniques. This research
objective is to categorize the ripeness of oil palm FFB from data
received from the sensor and to create a distribution map. The
methodology chosen to develop this project is the waterfall model,
while the ﬁndings of this study will be the classiﬁed ripeness of
oil palm FFB are either under-ripe, ripe, or overripe, and the
map of distribution.
Index Terms—Oil palm fresh fruit bunch (FFB), ripeness
classiﬁcation, LiDAR sensor, intensity value
I. INTRODUCTION
Palm oil or the scientiﬁc name Elaeis guineensis is grown
in many tropical regions around the world. Palm oil is mainly
used as cooking ingredient because it does not contain trans
fats that can clog the arteries. Cooking oil, margarine and
shortening are some examples of products that used palm
oil. It is rich in anti-oxidants, vitamin A, and stable at high
heat. Palm oil also has high content of tocotrienols, a type
of vitamin E that is widely used in the cosmetics industry
[1]. The similarity of oil palm with properties of biodiesel
and petroleum-derived diesel has made oil palm as favorable
alternatives to a renewable and sustainable fuel [2].
Oil palm tree has been the key to Malaysia’s economic
expansion, where it has become the most important national
agricultural crop. According to data from Malaysia Palm Oil
Council (MPOC), Malaysia currently accounts for 28% of
world palm oil production and 33% of world exports [3].
Currently, oil palm cultivation in Malaysia covers 4.49 million
hectares of land; producing 17.73 million tonnes of palm oil
and 2.13 tonnes of palm kernel oil [4].
As there has been steady increase in demand for palm
oil, it is therefore critical to produce high quality palm oil
products. In oil palm production, quality identiﬁcation of
the oil palm mostly based on its shape, texture, and color.
These methods or techniques result in the inaccuracy and
inconsistencies observation because only observed by human’s
eyesight. Not to add that it is a time-wasting and tiresome task
[5]. Furthermore, observing color changes throughout ripening
and counting the FFBs fruit loose do not appear to be efﬁcient
technique, high labor cost, and lack of objectivity [6]. Various
research has been performed in determining the ripeness of oil
palm FFB by using computer vision. However, the limitation
of this method depends on the fruit colors that would affect
the result when the visual is captured under different light
intensities and at disparate times of day [7].
The purpose of this project is to categorize the maturity of
oil palm FFB into overripe, ripe, and under-ripe. The data is
obtained from LiDAR Lite V3, where the level of ripeness is
determined from the oil palm FFB intensity value that was
collected. The distribution map will be produced based on the
location (latitude-longitude) of the oil palm trees and the oil
palm FFB’s ripeness to identify the oil palm FFB that are
suitable for harvest.
II. RELATED WORK
In this section, we review the previous work that have been
performed in identifying oil palm FFB and we describe LiDar
technique that we apply in our work.
A. Techniques to classify oil palm fresh fruit bunch (FFB)
The most popular techniques to classify the FFB is imaging
techniques that are diverse in nature. Imaging techniques in
previous work include application of color vision [8], use
of RGB color model [6, 7, 9, 10, 11], and photogrammetric
grading technique [12].

Sulaiman et al. [9] applies different image processing pro-
cedures such as image binarization, morphological processing,
and colour feature extraction of oil palm fruit. The calculation
of mean colour intensity is based on RGB colour model and
they designed the graphical user interface (GUI) using Matlab
software. Another work used laser-based imaging system to
detect ripeness of FFB [6]. Ali et al. [11] combined computer
vision and laser-light backscattering imaging in determining
the oil content and color changes of oil palm FFB at different
maturity levels. Jaffar et al. [12] applies photogrammetric
grading technique that correlates the color of the palm oil
fruits to their ripeness.
Besides using RGB, there are other work that utilize the
HSI (hue, saturation, intensity) color model to determine the
relationship between FFB’s color between the underipe and
ripe category so that the grading system could be accurately
[5].
Albakri [13] assess the oil palm ripeness using a speciﬁc
wavelength of 680 nm as a light source and digital camera to
capture the image. The parameters to detect oil palm FFB ma-
turities are chlorophyll concentration and fruitlet detachment.
The 680 nm image contrast is used to detect the chlorophyll
due to it correlated with the chlorophyll absorbance .
Another study evaluates a multi-band portable, active optical
sensor system; comprising of four spectral bands, 570, 670,
750, and 870 nm, to detect oil palm FFB maturity [14].
Besides that, other techniques include leaf analysis [15], op-
tical sensing using four-band sensor[14] and portable VIS/NIR
spectrometer was employed to rapidly measure quality of oil
palm FFB on-site [16]
Another work classiﬁed oil palm FFB into the right maturity
by using Genetic Algorithm Neural Network (GANN) or
known as modern chemometrics computational method and
near-infrared (NIR) spectroscopy technology [17].
Most of the previous research use computer vision or imag-
ing techniques which the result might have some constraint
when the visual is captured under different light intensities and
at disparate times of day. Furthermore, in oil palm plantation
ﬁeld, NIR spectroscopy only acceptable for short distance
detection [7].

B. LiDar
Lidar Lite v3 Module is a low-cost distant sensor to measure
different responses depending on the distance between the
sensor and the object using the laser reﬂection technique.
LiDAR data have been used in different areas such as urban
planning [18], forest management [19, 20], soil mapping [21],
and coastal management [22].
LiDar is one of the remote sensing technique that can
provide accurate, timely and repetitive information about the
surface area. This can assist in decision making for an efﬁcient
oil palm plantation management [23].

been applied in oil palm plantation. LiDAR has the advantage

Based on the previous research mentioned, there is a gap on
efﬁcient system to conclude the data collected and to classify
the oil palm FFB ripeness. Majority of the previous work did
the testing by taking samples of oil palm FFB, but not in the
plantation ﬁeld. Therefore, in this study, we will produce the
distribution map based on the location (latitude-longitude) of
palm oil trees in the plantation ﬁeld. The impact of this project
on the community is to ease and boost planters’ productivity
by developing an efﬁcient system for them. They can know
when the right time is to harvest the oil palm FFB and the
exact location is without having to observe every palm oil
tree on sites.

IV. RESULTS AND FINDINGS
The data is obtained from LiDAR Lite V3, where the level
of ripeness is determined from the oil palm FFB intensity value
that was collected during the scanning. Before collecting the
real data, which is scanning the oil palm FFB, the LiDAR Lite
V3 has been initially calibrated so that the LiDAR’s laser can
reﬂect on any tested surface or object and return its intensity
value.
In analysis of data, descriptive statistics are used to describe
the classiﬁcation of the oil palm FFB ripeness by calculating
the mean of return intensity value. The intensity range for
every ripeness is determined from the veriﬁed oil palm FFB
ripeness which has been conﬁrmed by Siti Rahmah Abdul
Rahman, the oil palm tissue culturist at MPOB Bangi.

After analysing the mean intensity for every ripeness, in-
tensity range has been set such as the Table II, where the
intensity range for overripe is between 75 and 119, ripe is
between 119 and 121.5, and under-ripe is between 121.5
and 135. The intensity range is also been set and considered
based on theoretical explanation that ripe oil palm fruits have
higher absorbance rate as it has lower intensity range and the
reﬂectance rate is lower than under-ripe oil palm fruits [7].

Due to the higher NIR absorbance rate, ripe oil palm fruits
have lower reﬂectance as it has higher lipid content. These
ﬁndings correlate with [25], where his study stated that under-
ripe oil palm fruits only have 6% lipid with 80% of water
content and ripe oil palm fruits have 58.3% lipid content,
and via a new inductive concept for frequency technique in
identifying the oil palm fruits’ lipid content, this study has
been concluded.
V. CONCLUSION
The main purpose of this project is for the classiﬁcation
of oil palm FFB ripeness into overripe, ripe, and under-ripe
and this project is developed to ease and boost planters’
productivity by developing an efﬁcient system for them. They
can know when the right time is to harvest the oil palm
FFB and where the location is by not have to observe every
palm oil tree on sites. It is concluded that every objective
in developing this project is accomplished although there
are some difﬁculties throughout this project development and
application. For the data collection, it is considered insufﬁcient
to have a high accuracy system to classify the oil palm FFB as
the constraints we encounter due to the outbreak of COVID-19
and incapable to do the testing in the exact oil palm plantation
ﬁeld where loads of oil palm FFB can be tested.
