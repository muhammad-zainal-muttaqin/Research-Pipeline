FruitGaussian: 3D Gaussian Splatting for Automated Fruit
Counting in Natural Orchard
Xiangyu Hong, Linlin Qin, Chun Shi, Gang Wu
Department of Automation, University of Science and Technology of China, Jinzhai Road 96, Hefei 230027, P. R. China
E-mail: qinll@ustc.edu.cn;
Abstract: Automatic fruit counting in natural orchard environments remains challenging due to foliage occlusion, illumina-
tion variations, and inefﬁcient 3D reconstruction methods. To address these limitations, we propose FruitGaussian, a novel 3D
Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)-based framework for high-ﬁdelity orchard scene understanding and fruit counting. Our approach
introduces two key innovations: a hierarchical semantic embedding mechanism that fuses multimodal features (RGB, semantics,
depth) for precise fruit instance encoding, and a depth-aware regularization constraint that leverages monocular depth priors to
suppress boundary artifacts and ﬂoating noise. By integrating explicit 3D Gaussian representations with differentiable render-
ing, FruitGaussian achieves real-time high-ﬁdelity rendering and geometric ﬁdelity while enabling direct point cloud extraction
within seconds. Evaluations on real-world persimmon orchard datasets demonstrate a 96% counting accuracy, surpassing Neural
Radiance Fields (NeRF)-based methods by 16 %. The explicit Gaussian representation enables efﬁcient point cloud extraction
within seconds, signiﬁcantly advancing the practicality and robustness of vision-based yield estimation in precision agriculture.
Key Words: 3DGS, Fruit Counting, Depth Regularization, Precision Agriculture
1
Introduction
Automatic acquisition of orchard phenotypic data and
yield information constitutes a fundamental requirement for
smart orchard construction. With increasing global food de-
mand, agricultural resource optimization based on advanced
sensing technologies has emerged as a critical direction in
precision agriculture[1]. Fruit counting technology, which
quantiﬁes crop yield distribution patterns, effectively sup-
ports harvest planning and resource allocation, serving as a
vital technical pathway for sustainable agricultural develop-
ment.
Fruit counting in natural orchard environments faces mul-
tiple challenges: (1) Three-dimensional geometric charac-
teristics of fruits are susceptible to interference from foliage
occlusion, illumination variations, and fruit overlap; (2) Ro-
bust feature representation in complex scenes remains chal-
lenging, limiting the generalization capabilities of traditional
vision methods. Existing approaches primarily follow two
technical routes: 2D image processing and 3D reconstruc-
tion.
In 2D image processing methods, early studies employed
color space conversion and morphological operations[2],
while deep learning frameworks like YOLO and R-CNN sig-
niﬁcantly improved performance through end-to-end feature
learning[3]. Video-based methods utilize Kalman ﬁlters for
multi-object tracking[4], yet suffer from tracking drift under
occlusion due to inherent 2D projection information loss.
3D reconstruction methods enhance counting accu-
racy through multi-view geometric constraints, exempli-
ﬁed by Structure from Motion (SfM) and multi-view stereo
vision[5–7]. However, current approaches exhibit notable
limitations:
(1) Dependency on expensive sensors (e.g.,
stereo cameras, LiDAR) for depth acquisition; (2) The sparse
point clouds generated by SfM cannot adequately support
ﬁne-grained phenotypic analysis or the operation of picking
robots.
Recent advances in novel view synthesis using Neural Ra-
diance Fields (NeRF) [8] have opened new possibilities for
3D orchard scene reconstruction. As a pioneering work in
this domain, FruitNeRF [9] establishes an implicit neural
representation framework that integrates multi-view images
to construct semantic-aware NeRF models. Through uni-
form voxel sampling and subsequent clustering algorithms,
this approach achieves fruit counting with explicit point
cloud generation. Compared with traditional SfM methods
[10], This framework effectively mitigates error propagation
from 2D semantic segmentation. Nevertheless, two critical
limitations persist: The volume rendering paradigm of NeRF
requires dense ray sampling and global optimization, leading
to prohibitive computational costs that impede real-time de-
ployment. Furthermore, inherent limitations in unbounded
scene modeling tend to generate background artifacts and
ﬂoaters, while the current single semantic constraint frame-
work proves inadequate for suppressing such complex noise
interference.
In this work, we present FruitGaussian, a 3D Gaus-
sian Splatting (3DGS)-based framework for high-ﬁdelity or-
chard scene understanding and automated fruit counting.
The main contributions are as follows:
(1) We propose
the ﬁrst explicit orchard modeling framework using 3DGS,
enhanced by hierarchical semantic embedding mechanisms
that enable effective multi-modal feature fusion and encod-
ing fruit instances. (2) We develop a depth-aware regulariza-
tion constraint that introduces implicit geometric priors to
resolve boundaryless reconstruction challenges, effectively
suppressing ﬂoating artifacts and ghosting phenomena while
maintaining reconstruction ﬁdelity under complex illumina-
tion conditions. To validate the efﬁcacy of the FruitGaus-
sian framework, we conducted systematic experimental eval-
uations on the dataset collected from real orchard environ-
ments. The results demonstrate that our method outperforms
existing approaches across core metrics including photore-
alistic 3D rendering quality, point cloud geometric ﬁdelity,
and fruit counting accuracy.

Methodology
In this section, we present the framework and pipeline of
our proposed method, as illustrated in Fig. 1.
2.1
Multimodal Data Perception
The data collection was conducted in September, the opti-
mal harvest season for persimmon maturation, at a ’Taishuu’
persimmon orchard located in Feidong County, Hefei City,
Anhui Province, China (geographic coordinates: 31°59’ N,
117°51’ E). The target cultivar exhibits phenotypic complex-
ity with yellow-green pericarp spectrally overlapping foliage
(Fig. 2), presenting signiﬁcant detection challenges under
dense fruit distribution and severe occlusion.
Fig. 2: Orchard environment.Yellow-green fruits exhibit
overlap with foliage under conditions of dense occlusion and
variable ambient lighting.
We used a Nikon D750 camera (equipped with a 24mm
lens, set at f/8 aperture) to capture 400 RGB images (with a
resolution of 4000×6000 pixels) through multi-view acqui-
sition: 12 azimuthal positions within a 1-meter radius, each
with 3 vertical tiers (spaced 0.5 meters apart). COLMAP[10]
recovered camera poses (K ∈R3×3, [R|t] ∈SE(3)) and
sparse point clouds for 3DGS initialization.
Fig. 3: Multimodal data processing results.Visualization

Subsequently, we integrated the open-domain detection
capabilities of Grounding DINO[11] with the zero-shot seg-
mentation advantages of Segment Anything V2[12] to gener-
ate ﬁne-grained semantic masks. Concurrently, we utilized
Depth Anything V2[13] to extract monocular depth priors
and employed SfM point clouds for global depth alignment.
The resulting RGB images, semantic masks, and depth es-
timation maps are illustrated in Fig. 3. We determined the
actual total number of fruits by manually counting them.
2.2
Scene Reconstruction
Traditional 3D scene representations such as point clouds,
voxels, and shape descriptors (Signed Distance Function,
SDF) exhibit limited capabilities in capturing and express-
ing details of complex objects or scenes. In recent years, im-
plicit neural representations [8] have demonstrated remark-
able performance in novel view synthesis by representing 3D
scenes with coordinate-based neural networks. For instance,
FruitNeRF [9] employs two distinct multilayer perceptron

(MLP) branches to output color and fruit feature based on
NeRF’s feature ﬁeld reﬁnement, followed by volumetric
rendering to generate RGB images and high-dimensional
feature maps. However, due to NeRF’s implicit radiance

sampling during training and rendering, leading to substan-

cannot directly generate explicit point clouds, signiﬁcantly
limiting NeRF’s applicability in downstream screening and
editing tasks.
Our work aims to construct an expressive 3D scene rep-
resentation for natural orchard environments that achieves
high-ﬁdelity modeling of scene appearance and geometry
while effectively distinguishing fruit features to facilitate
downstream screening and counting tasks. We design our
approach based on the recent 3DGS technique [14], extend-
ing it from pure 3D reconstruction to ﬁne-grained scene un-
derstanding and fruit counting. The network architecture of
FruitGaussian is illustrated in Fig. 4.

2.2.2
FruitGaussian
This section delineates the core algorithmic design for or-
chard scene understanding and semantically-aware fruit ex-
traction. Building upon 3D Gaussian representations, we
augment each Gaussian with learnable semantic attributes
to enable viewpoint-invariant 3D classiﬁcation, inspired by
[17].
Speciﬁcally, we append a 16-dimensional semantic
embedding f ∈R16, as intrinsic properties to each primi-
tive, paralleling the treatment of spherical harmonics coef-
ﬁcients. To counteract geometric ambiguity in unbounded
regions (e.g., sky), we further implement depth-aware regu-
larization that constrains Gaussian distributions during opti-
mization. The complete pipeline is illustrated in Fig. 4.
Semantic Scene Representation: Our framework de-
composes natural scenes into three semantically disjoint cat-
egories: sky, fruit, and others. Each Gaussian learns prob-
abilistic class afﬁliations through gradient-based optimiza-
tion of its semantic embedding f. This design capitalizes on
the intrinsic viewpoint invariance of 3D semantic attributes,
which we formalize as DC components in spherical harmon-
ics space.
Differentiable Semantic Rendering: Following the ras-
terization pipeline of [14], we implement depth-ordered
composition with top-N overlapping primitives per pixel.

2.3
Point Cloud Extraction and Counting
Building upon the reconstructed 3D scene with seman-
tic annotations, we develop a semantic ﬁltering pipeline for
precise fruit-oriented point cloud extraction. The proposed
method directly adopts the converged Gaussian centroids
from FruitGaussian training as spatial coordinates, while de-
riving RGB color attributes by extracting the DC compo-
nents of spherical harmonics coefﬁcients. Concurrently, a
trained linear classiﬁer decodes the semantic embeddings to
assign per-point category labels. As visualized in Fig. 5, our
method extracts both photometrically accurate RGB point
clouds and semantically annotated counterparts.
Our fruit counting pipeline ﬁrst applies semantic-color
coding to annotated point clouds, linking chromatic features
to category labels. It then extracts fruit-instance clusters di-
rectly via semantic-guided RGB thresholding.Subsequently,
we employ Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applica-
tions with Noise (DBSCAN)[19] for unsupervised instance

Limitations and Future Directions
While FruitGaussian demonstrates promising perfor-
mance in orchard scene reconstruction and fruit counting,
several challenges persist alongside opportunities for future
exploration:
Dynamic Scene Modeling: The static scene assump-
tion struggles to handle wind-induced vegetation movement,
causing motion blur artifacts in foliage-dense regions. In-
corporating temporally-aware Gaussian representations[20]
could mitigate this through explicit motion modeling.
Feature-Deﬁcient Reconstruction:
Structural homo-
geneity and texture repetition in large-scale orchards chal-
lenge SfM with unordered images (only 30% success rate
in our ﬁeld tests).
A hybrid approach combining 3DGS

Conclusion
This paper presents FruitGaussian, the ﬁrst explicit
3DGS-based framework tailored for orchard scene recon-
struction and automated fruit counting.
By integrating
depth-guided geometric regularization and hierarchical se-
mantic embeddings, our method effectively addresses key
challenges such as foliage occlusion, illumination variabil-
ity, and computational inefﬁciency.
Experimental results
validate its superiority over existing approaches:
Fruit-
Gaussian achieves a 96% counting accuracy (16% higher
than NeRF baselines) and real-time point cloud generation
(within seconds), signiﬁcantly advancing the practicality of
agricultural yield estimation. The explicit 3DGS represen-
tation further enables seamless downstream tasks, including
semantic ﬁltering and instance clustering via DBSCAN.
Current limitations include handling dynamic vegetation
motion and texture repetition in large-scale orchards. Future
work will explore spatiotemporal Gaussian representations
and SLAM-3DGS fusion to enhance robustness. Our frame-
work highlights the potential of geometry-semantics fusion
for scalable precision agriculture.
