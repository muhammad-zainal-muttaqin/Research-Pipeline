a b s t r a c t
In precision viticulture, autonomous systems are increasingly deployed for inspection and yield estimation. How-
ever, mapping 3D vineyard environments and monitoring fruit distribution presents challenges due to fruit oc-
clusion, moving objects, and unstructured backgrounds. To address these issues, this study presents a semantic 
3D mapping and fruit localization system designed for yield estimation in dynamic environments. The proposed 
framework integrates the ORB-SLAM3 backend with a lightweight semantic segmentation model, named Edge 
Fusion Segmentation Network (EFSegNet). Derived from the PIDNet baseline, EFSegNet introduces a Gradient 
Feature Fusion Block (GFFM) and a Deformable Spatial Attention (DSA) module. These components are designed 
to reﬁne fruit boundaries and separate overlapping grape bunches, a requirement for individual counting. The 
generated semantic masks guide the removal of dynamic feature points to maintain map consistency, while en-
abling semantic mapping of fruit clusters. Additionally, a three-stage point cloud ﬁltering strategy is applied to 
reﬁne the global map, followed by DBSCAN clustering and PCA-based modeling to extract the 3D location and 
quantity of grape bunches. Validation experiments in a real-world dynamic vineyard indicate that the system 
operated at 23.1 FPS, with an average 3D localization error of 21.02 mm. The average counting error was 5.95%, 
demonstrating the system’s applicability for automated yield estimation.
1.  Introduction
In precision agriculture, obtaining accurate spatial data on fruit dis-
tribution and yield is fundamental for orchard management [1,2]. Au-
tonomous inspection robots equipped with 3D mapping and yield es-
timation capabilities assist in optimizing logistics, labor planning, and 
market forecasting [3,4]. Beyond detection, the 3D localization of fruit 
clusters supports tasks such as high-throughput phenotyping, maturity 
monitoring [5], and pest detection [6], providing data for vineyard op-
erations.
Although agricultural robots are utilized in structured settings like 
greenhouses [7] and standardized orchards [8], perception in unstruc-
tured ﬁeld conditions remains a challenge. Traditional fruit recogni-
tion methods, relying on image processing techniques such as color 
thresholding [9], are aﬀected by variable lighting and cluttered back-
grounds in real-world orchards. Deep learning has enhanced feature ex-
traction capabilities. Earlier works adopted object detection networks 
such as YOLO [10] and R-CNN [11]. To enable volume estimation 
through contour extraction, research has transitioned towards seman-
tic segmentation networks, including Mask R-CNN [12], DeepLabv3+

less, segmenting irregularly shaped, occluded, and touching grape

cision for speed, resulting in under-counting of clustered fruits. While 
Transformer-based architectures [19] oﬀer high accuracy, they impose 
a computational load that aﬀects real-time deployment on mobile plat-

To resolve the spatial ambiguity in 2D methods, research has fo-

ing RGB-D cameras, LiDAR, and structured light, have been inte-

tion. For instance, instance segmentation combined with geometric 
analysis has been used for 3D reconstruction of guava fruits [21]. 
Similarly, algorithms integrating obstacle perception have been pro-
posed for tomato selection [22]. Furthermore, point cloud processing 
methods have proven instrumental, as exempliﬁed by the combina-
tion of Mask R-CNN with PointNet for pomegranate modeling [23]. 
Slim-Banana integrates a lightweight YOLO-based detector with cali-
brated TOF depth to recover 3D coordinates from 2D detections [24].

A two-stage multimodal method further uses instance masks to regular-
ize depth completion before estimating 3D harvesting points via key-
point and pose inference [25]. Despite success in static scenes or of-
ﬂine processing, many systems face diﬃculties in dynamic real-world 
orchards populated by moving persons. This necessitates the fusion of 
perception with Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM). Ap-
proaches such as ORB-Livox [26] have fused LiDAR and visual SLAM 
for dynamic fruit detection. Semantic mapping solutions using depth 
cameras have also been explored in citrus orchards [27] and straw-
berry ﬁelds [28]. Monocular SLAM has been used for indoor UAV nav-
igation [29] and tree trait estimation [30]. Multi-sensor fusion strate-
gies [31] and 2D-to-3D conversion mechanisms [32] have also been 
proposed. However, inspection robots in dynamic vineyards still face 
issues: (a) map corruption and tracking drift caused by moving per-
sons; (b) computational constraints aﬀecting real-time monitoring; and 
(c) inaccurate yield counts due to coarse spatial aggregation in dense
canopies.
This study addresses these challenges by proposing a real-time se-
mantic 3D mapping and yield estimation system based on the ORB-
SLAM3 framework. We introduce a lightweight network, EFSegNet, to 
generate masks. These masks serve a dual purpose: ﬁltering out dynamic 
objects to stabilize SLAM tracking, and segmenting grape bunches with 
deﬁned boundaries to support counting. A spatial clustering strategy 
leveraging DBSCAN and PCA-based ﬁtting is then applied to determine 
the 3D position of each grape bunch.
The contributions of this paper are:
1. Development of a 3D mapping and yield estimation framework that 
integrates semantic perception with ORB-SLAM3, ensuring inspec-
tion capabilities in dynamic vineyards with human interference.
2. Proposal of the EFSegNet segmentation model by incorporating a 
Gradient Feature Fusion Block (GFFM) and Deformable Spatial At-
tention (DSA) into the PIDNet baseline, introducing a network de-
signed to separate overlapping fruit clusters and handle irregular 
boundaries for counting accuracy.
3. Implementation of a three-stage point cloud ﬁltering strategy that 
improves global map quality by sequentially eliminating noise and 
outliers via voxel grid ﬁltering, statistical outlier removal, and radius 
ﬁltering.
4. Deployment of a fruit localization and counting pipeline using DB-
SCAN clustering and PCA-based ellipsoid ﬁtting to extract the 3D 
coordinates and quantity of grape bunches for yield estimation.

2.1.  Hardware conﬁguration
The experimental setup consists of three primary subsystems: the 
chassis, the sensor suite, and the control unit. The overall architecture 
is depicted in Fig. 1.
The mobility platform is based on a diﬀerential drive chassis mea-
suring 0.776 m × 0.671 m × 0.319 m (L×W×H). It weighs 35.6 kg and can 
carry a payload of up to 45 kg. The platform is capable of speeds up to 
1.65 m∕s and can navigate slopes up to 25◦, with support for manual joy-
stick control. The perception module includes a ZED2 stereo camera and 
a Robosense Helios 32 LiDAR. The ZED2 functions as the main sensor for 
real-time fruit detection and 3D mapping. It is an active stereo system 
with an integrated IMU, oﬀering a depth range of 0.2 to 20 m and a res-
olution of 2208 × 1242 pixels at up to 100 FPS. The Robosense Helios 32 
LiDAR (360◦ FOV, ±1 cm accuracy) is used solely to acquire ground 
truth point clouds for accuracy validation and does not participate in 
the real-time mapping loop. A Lenovo Legion Y9000 laptop serves as 
the central processing unit, featuring an Intel i5-12500H CPU, 16 GB of 
RAM, and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060 GPU (8 GB VRAM), running 
Ubuntu 20.04. This unit handles all sensor data processing, semantic

2.2.  Data collection and site description
Field experiments were carried out at the Shangguo Pick-Your-
Own Orchard in Panyu District, Guangzhou, China (113◦26′25.688′′E, 
22◦54′41.602′′N), as shown in Fig. 2. Situated in the Pearl River Delta, 
this location oﬀers a warm, humid climate ideal for fruit cultivation. The 
vineyard represents a typical unstructured agricultural setting, charac-
terized by dense planting, complex intertwined foliage, and fruit occlu-
sion. These conditions provide a realistic testbed for grape detection and 
localization tasks.
Image sequences of green grapes were captured using the mobile 
robot as it navigated through the orchard rows. The ZED2 camera was 
conﬁgured to operate at a resolution of 640 × 360 pixels and a frame rate 
of 30 FPS. From the recorded sequences, 940 representative RGB images 
were selected randomly to create the dataset. These images were man-
ually annotated at the pixel level using the LabelMe tool, with pixels 
categorized into two semantic classes: ’fruit’ and ’pedestrian’. This re-
sulting dataset was subsequently used to train and evaluate the seman-
tic segmentation network performance. Sample images from the dataset 
are presented in Fig. 3.
2.3.  System framework
The proposed semantic mapping system, as shown in Fig. 4, inte-
grates four key modules to enable robust 3D mapping and fruit local-
ization: the EFSegNet semantic segmentation module, the ORB-SLAM3 
visual backend, the point cloud optimization module, and the fruit clus-
tering/localization module. First, the input stereo images are processed 
by EFSegNet. The resulting semantic masks are then utilized by the 
ORB-SLAM3 module to ﬁlter out feature points associated with dynamic 
regions during feature extraction, thereby improving pose estimation 
stability and map accuracy. Following dynamic point elimination, the 
system constructs a global 3D point cloud map using depth data from 
keyframes. This map is semantically enriched by projecting 2D masks 
onto their corresponding 3D points. To improve map quality, a three-
stage ﬁltering process is applied, utilizing voxel downsampling, statis-
tical outlier removal, and radius ﬁltering to remove noise and redun-
dant points. Finally, the reﬁned fruit point cloud is clustered using the

2.3.1.  Segmentation network
Reliable yield estimation depends on the ability to distinguish in-
dividual grape bunches, particularly when they are touching or over-
lapping. Standard real-time networks often struggle to deﬁne the subtle 
boundaries between adjacent clusters, resulting in under-counting. To 
solve this, we introduce EFSegNet, a real-time semantic segmentation 
network derived from the PIDNet backbone, speciﬁcally tuned for vine-
yard inspection.
As illustrated in Fig. 5, EFSegNet maintains a three-branch struc-
ture: spatial, context, and edge. To enhance edge feature extraction, we 
replace the standard convolutional modules in the edge branch with 
a Gradient Feature Fusion Block (GFFM). This module facilitates the 
fusion of spatial and semantic information by providing explicit edge 
guidance. Additionally, a Deformable Spatial Attention (DSA) mod-
ule is integrated into the spatial branch to improve the network’s 
sensitivity to anisotropic targets, such as irregularly shaped grape
clusters.
While PIDNet includes an edge-guided design, its original standard 
convolutions are insuﬃcient for capturing the complex edge gradients 
found in vineyards. Our proposed GFFM addresses this by integrating 
three types of diﬀerential convolutions to heighten sensitivity to edge 
gradient changes, as shown in Fig. 6. A lightweight residual path with 
a gated attention mechanism is also added to compensate for feature 
degradation.

2.3.2.  Dynamic feature point removal
To eliminate dynamic feature points in dynamic environments and 
enhance the robustness of the ORB-SLAM3 system in dynamic scenes, 
a semantic segmentation module is integrated into the original ORB-
SLAM3 framework. Speciﬁcally, an additional semantic segmentation 
thread is introduced. In this thread, each RGB frame is processed by EF-
SegNet, to produce a corresponding semantic mask. The semantic mask 
is subsequently synchronized and forwarded to the front-end tracking 
thread. As illustrated in Fig. 8, during the frame initialization stage, the 
system ﬁrst extracts ORB feature points from the input image. Subse-
quently, it obtains the spatial coordinates of these feature points and 
queries the corresponding semantic labels from the semantic mask. If 
a label indicates a dynamic object, the associated feature point is re-
moved; otherwise, it is retained. Therefore, by leveraging the semantic 
mask, all dynamic feature points within the regions of moving objects 
can be eliminated, while only the static feature points from the back-
ground are preserved for subsequent tracking and mapping. This process 
reduces matching errors and trajectory drift caused by dynamic object 
motion, thereby improving the robustness of the SLAM system in dy-
namic environments.

3.  Experiments and results
To quantitatively evaluate the contribution of each proposed mod-
ule, we conducted a series of experiments focusing on six key aspects: se-
mantic segmentation performance analysis (Section 3.1), dynamic elim-
ination performance analysis (Section 3.2), 3D reconstruction in a dy-
namic vineyard (Section 3.3), 3D point cloud map optimization (Sec-
tion 3.4), fruit localization and bunch counting (Section 3.5), and over-
all system real-time performance (Section 3.6).
3.1.  Semantic segmentation performance
3.1.1.  Semantic segmentation comparative experiments
The EFSegNet semantic segmentation network was trained on a sys-
tem with an Intel i5-12500H CPU and an NVIDIA RTX 3060 GPU, run-
ning the Ubuntu 20.04 operating system. The deep learning framework 
used was PyTorch 1.11. The dataset was partitioned into training, val-
idation, and test sets at an 8:1:1 ratio. The network was trained using 
the conﬁguration parameters detailed in Table 1.
To provide a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed EFSegNet 
network’s semantic segmentation performance, we conducted a system-
atic comparison against several state-of-the-art networks: DeepLabv3+, 
BiSeNetv2, SegFormer-B0, DDRNet-23-slim [35], and PIDNet. All exper-
iments were performed on the vineyard dataset. To ensure a fair and re-

3.2.  Dynamic elimination performance
To evaluate the eﬀectiveness of dynamic feature point removal, the 
semantic mask corresponding to each input image is integrated into the 
system. During the frame creation stage, dynamic feature points located 
within red pedestrian regions are removed based on the semantic mask, 
thereby reducing the negative impact of moving objects on subsequent 
tracking and localization accuracy. Three representative frames from 
the dataset constructed in this study are selected to demonstrate the ef-
fect of semantic mask–based dynamic point removal. Fig. 13(a) shows 
the original input image frames. Fig. 13(b) displays the feature points 
extracted by the original ORB-SLAM3 system. As no dynamic point ﬁlter-
ing is applied, feature points are also extracted from moving pedestrian 
regions, which degrades localization accuracy. Fig. 13(c) shows the re-
moval results using masks generated by the original PIDNet network. 
Most dynamic points are eﬀectively removed; however, due to the com-
plex background of the vineyard and pedestrian motion, the segmenta-
tion task becomes more challenging, resulting in incomplete removal of 
edge features associated with dynamic objects. Fig. 13(d) presents the 
results using masks generated by the proposed EFSegNet network. The 
system successfully removes dynamic feature points both within pedes-
trian regions and along their boundaries, signiﬁcantly reducing the in-
ﬂuence of moving objects on SLAM performance and improving the re-
liability of subsequent pose estimation and map construction. The eﬀec-
tiveness of dynamic feature point elimination is demonstrated through 
comparative analysis of diﬀerent approaches, as illustrated in Fig. 13. 
The results show that EFSegNet achieves superior performance in re-
moving both interior and boundary dynamic feature points compared 
to the original PIDNet and baseline ORB-SLAM3.

3.4.  Map optimization analysis
Although the system achieves clear point cloud reconstruction 
and fruit semantic enhancement in various orchard perspectives and
scenarios, a detailed analysis of local regions reveals some limitations. 
The resulting point cloud maps still contain a degree of noise, including 
outliers, non-uniform density, and fragmented structural edges. These 
abnormalities are mainly caused by light eﬀects, branch and leaf shad-
ing, and depth estimation errors in the orchard environment, which may 
have an impact on the subsequent fruit clustering and spatial modeling 
accuracy if not treated.

overall point cloud map converges signiﬁcantly, the local contours are

3.5.1.  Fruit localization accuracy
To obtain high-precision 3D ground truth positions of the fruit, a 
Robosense Helios 32 LiDAR was integrated into the experimental plat-
form. This LiDAR provides high spatial resolution and ranging accuracy 
(typically < 1 cm), enabling it to precisely capture the true geometry 
of grape bunches and the surrounding environment. During data ac-
quisition, the robotic platform moved slowly through the trellis rows 
to ensure complete scanning of each grape bunch. Subsequently, the 
ground truth data was generated through a meticulous manual anno-
tation process on the collected LiDAR point clouds. Each grape bunch 
was manually annotated in CloudCompare by selecting its correspond-
ing point cloud region. The geometric centroid of each annotated cluster 
was computed and recorded as the ground truth 3D position. To ensure

following Fu et al.’s two-stage framework (point-to-plane initialization 
and diﬀerentiable reﬁnement [40]). We validated the calibration stabil-
ity by performing a stereo baseline consistency check across 10 indepen-
dent trials. The reﬁned alignment achieved a mean translation residual 
of 2.91 mm (𝜎= 0.51 mm). For the ground-truth generation, the anno-
tated LiDAR centroids were transformed into the global SLAM frame

3.5.3.  Robustness to illumination variations
Agricultural environments are subject to drastic lighting changes 
which can signiﬁcantly aﬀect the performance of visual sensors. To eval-
uate system robustness, comparative experiments were performed under 
three typical lighting intensities including normal light, shadows, and 
backlight conditions, as illustrated in Fig. 21. The quantitative impact 
of these variations on fruit localization and counting is detailed in Ta-
bles 9 and 10. Under normal lighting, the system achieved the lowest 3D 
RMSE of 19.73 mm and a relative counting error of 5.4%. In shadowed 
conditions, the localization error increased slightly to 21.99 mm while 
the counting error was 5.6%. However, backlight scenarios caused the 
localization error to rise to 25.22 mm with a standard deviation of 7.6 
mm, and the counting error reached 7.3%. This degradation is primar-
ily attributed to the instability of stereo matching and increased depth 
noise caused by local overexposure and poor texture contrast in back-
light scenarios. Despite this increase, the overall performance remains 
within a reliable range for yield estimation tasks.

3.6.  System real-time performance
To evaluate the real-time processing capability of the system, the 
average processing time of each module of the proposed system was 
counted, as shown in Table 12. The system’s real-time performance is 
primarily determined by the processing times of the semantic segmen-
tation, track, and point cloud mapping modules. Speciﬁcally, the track 
module responsible for feature extraction and matching operates at an 
average of 14.37 ms, while the semantic segmentation module, EFSeg-
Net, processes at 7.57 ms on average. The point cloud mapping mod-
ule takes 21.4 ms per frame. In contrast, the fruit clustering and local-
ization module operates in parallel threads with an average processing 
time of 8.1 ms, and thus has a limited impact on overall real-time per-
formance. The parallel execution design ensures that all key modules 
remain within acceptable time bounds, supporting eﬃcient real-time 
operation, and the system achieves a real-time performance of 23.1 FPS.
4.  Discussion
This study introduces a comprehensive real-time 3D mapping and 
fruit localization system speciﬁcally designed for dynamic vineyard 
yield estimation. Experimental results demonstrate the system’s robust-
ness in addressing dynamic disturbances and the unstructured condi-
tions typical of orchard environments.
4.1.  Comparative analysis
Previous orchard SLAM studies, such as ORB-Livox[26], SLAM-
PYE[41], and SCTNet-B & ORB-SLAM2[42], typically evaluated their 
systems in controlled, static scenarios, as shown in Table 13. In con-
trast, our system integrates dynamic object ﬁltering through semantic 
segmentation, eﬀectively mitigating dynamic disturbances encountered 
in realistic vineyard environments, such as moving persons. Regarding 
real-time performance, the system achieves a throughput of 23.1 FPS 
while providing 3D mapping and fruit localization. Other systems re-
port diﬀerent frame rates–for example, ORB-Livox (>30 FPS), SLAM-
PYE (23 FPS), and SCTNet-B & ORB-SLAM2 (28.36 FPS)–yet SCTNet-B & 
ORB-SLAM2 lacks fruit localization, and SLAM-PYE omits 3D mapping. 
In terms of localization accuracy, our system achieves a mean RMSE of 
21.02 mm under dynamic outdoor conditions, substantially outperform-
ing prior methods such as ORB-Livox (26 mm RMSE) and SLAM-PYE 
(46.3 mm horizontal RMSE). In addition to SLAM-based systems, yield 
estimation is also widely studied using pure-vision detection-tracking-
counting (DTC) pipelines. [43] reported grape-cluster counting with 
pruned YOLOv5s and SORT, achieving 84.9% accuracy at 43.1–50.4 
FPS. [44] reported counting performance with an 𝑅2 of 0.85 and RMSE 
of 1.57, and [45] reported fruit-number prediction with an 𝑅2 of 0.945 
and yield-estimation performance of 𝑅2=0.902. DTC relies on 2D asso-
ciation for de-duplication and can fail under revisits. Our SLAM world-
frame anchoring reduces repeated counts and provides centimeter-level 
3D localization, achieving 21.02 mm RMSE at 23.1 FPS. This is cost-
eﬀective when yield estimation requires both counting and spatial lo-
calization.
4.2.  Limitations
Despite its demonstrated robustness, the proposed system faces spe-
ciﬁc limitations that aﬀect its overall performance.
Firstly, system accuracy heavily depends on depth estimation from 
binocular stereo vision. While semantic segmentation helps mitigate the 
negative impact of dynamic disturbances, it does not resolve inherent 
limitations of stereo matching algorithms in regions with weak texture 
or poor illumination. Such conditions increase depth measurement un-
certainty, resulting in gaps, structural distortions, and reduced localiza-
tion accuracy, especially during longitudinal vineyard traversal.
Secondly, while the three-stage ﬁltering strategy eﬀectively removes 
ghost points and noise, it inevitably incurs a loss of geometric details. 
The voxelization process, which substitutes point clusters with cen-
troids, coupled with aggressive outlier removal, tends to over-smooth

5.  Conclusion
This study presents a real-time semantic 3D mapping and fruit lo-
calization system designed to address the challenges of yield estima-
tion in dynamic and unstructured vineyard environments. The proposed 
system integrates the EFSegNet, enhanced with Gradient Feature Fu-
sion and Deformable Spatial Attention, into the ORB-SLAM3 frame-
work to eﬀectively eliminate dynamic feature points while ensuring 
high-quality semantic mapping. Experimental results demonstrate that 
EFSegNet achieves an mIoU of 89.45% and a Boundary F-measure of 
82.78%, signiﬁcantly reﬁning fruit boundaries and separating overlap-
ping clusters. This segmentation superiority translates into robust down-
stream performance, maintaining a low counting error of 6.23% even 
in heavily occluded scenarios and showing stability under varying il-
lumination conditions. Overall, the system realized an average 3D lo-
calization accuracy of 21.02 mm with a processing speed of 23.1 FPS, 
conﬁrming its eﬀectiveness for autonomous orchard inspection.
Future work will focus on three key directions to further improve sys-
tem adaptability. First, we plan to introduce multi-sensor fusion strate-
gies, incorporating LiDAR or IMU data, to compensate for the inherent 
limitations of visual depth estimation in low-texture or strong backlight 
regions. Second, we aim to develop adaptive ﬁltering and clustering al-
gorithms that automatically adjust parameters according to local point 
cloud density, enhancing generalizability across diﬀerent fruit varieties. 
Finally, future research will address environmental dynamism involving 
non-rigid deformations, speciﬁcally modeling foliage swaying caused by 
wind, to further ensure tracking stability in complex ﬁeld conditions.
