A B S T R A C T  
Oil palms are one of the essential crops in agricultural productivity for developing countries such as Malaysia and 
other tropical areas. For predicting the yield and production of palm oil, the counting process is often carried out. 
Manually counting oil palm trees is one of the solutions but it requires a massive labour force, and the result is 
often inaccurate. To overcome this problem, automated techniques for oil palm detection have been developed. 
However, the performance of existing automated techniques for oil palm detection deteriorates when the 
planting layout of the oil palm tree is not well organized. Deep learning applications for oil palm tree detection 
and counting offer a powerful solution to the challenges of precision agriculture, enabling plantations to increase 
productivity and sustainability while reducing costs and manual labour. Deep structured learning, more 
generally deep learning is one of the widely used computer vision technology, especially in agricultural engi­
neering. Deep learning method is an essential tool when it comes to monitoring the plantation. Different deep 
learning networks are utilized for classification tasks towards oil palm trees. In order to promote the use of deep 
learning in the oil palm industry, this paper main contribution is to provide an understanding of the utilisation of 
deep learning and its application in oil palm tree counting. The gaps and opportunities for research in oil palm 
plantations based on deep learning techniques will also be discussed.   
1. Introduction 
Along with the increasingly rising demand for solving complicated 
problems with the support of learnable machines, deep learning has 
developed over the past few years as a field of interest to researchers. 
Machine learning assures to minimize the efforts through self-learning of 
past experiences [1] practising three ways of learning especially, 
learning without supervised, semi-supervised and under supervised [1]. 
As the prerequisite, feature extraction is needed for the conventional 
machine learning techniques, and this requires a domain expert [2]. One 
of the challenging tasks is the selection of relevant features with the 
specific problem. Nonetheless, the deep learning method overcomes 
feature selection problems by not having a pre-selected feature but 
automatically extracts important features from the raw input for a 
problem in hand [3]. The deep learning model is the combination of 
processing layers that have the ability to learn through multiple levels of 
abstraction and this grant the network to learn distinct features [4]. 
Deep learning has become a way to achieve promising results in 
different applications such as image recognition [5], voice recognition 
[6], topic classification, sentiment analysis [7], language translation [8], 
natural language understanding, signal processing [9], face recognition 
[10], forgery detection [11], prediction of bioactivity of small molecules 
[12] and many others. Three common architectures are used to create 
deep learning which are Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), Deep 
Belief Network (DBN), and Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) [13]. DBN 
is widely used for general classification problems while RNN is used 
when data is more in the form of sequential. However, CNN is more 
popular when it comes to the classification of image, text, and sound 
[14]. 
Agricultural technology grew rapidly in the 20th century. The 
importance of technology to agriculture has been discovered and 
numerous amount of commercial technology has led to an incredible 
evolution of mechanical technology [15]. The introduction of the first 
successful gasoline tractor was in 1892. The invention of tractors not 
only changed the way the land was cultivated but also changed the crops 
of farmers [16]. With the support of technology, productivity is 
increased. Yet, the production still is not enough to support the world’s 
demand for the future. Hence, the idea of “precision agriculture” has 
been adopted [17]. Precision agriculture mostly focused on observing, 
monitoring, reacting to the variability of crops either from inter or

Internet of Things (IoT) [21] have been applied to the agriculture field 
which led to the idea of “smart farming” [22,23]. Smart farming is 
critical in addressing the problems of agricultural production in the 
aspects of food safety, environmental effects, yield, and sustainability 
[24]. In order to overcome these problems, it is important to examine 
and understand the agriculture ecosystems which require continuous

recent years, the growing popularity of deep learning method in agri­

In the year 2018, the agriculture sector occupied 7.3% of the total 
Gross Domestic Product (GDP) and oil palm has contributed 37.9% to 
the GDP of agriculture [28]. One of the main usages of oil palm trees is to 
produce palm oil. According to the Food and Agriculture Organization 
(FAO), palm oil accounts for around 33% of global vegetable oil pro­
duction, making it the most widely produced and traded vegetable oil in 
the world. The significant role of the oil palm industry in the GDP of 
many countries is due to its economic benefits. These benefits include 
employment opportunities, income generation, and foreign exchange 
earnings, which contribute to the overall economic growth of the 
country. The oil palm industry also plays a critical role in meeting the 
global demand for vegetable oil, which is essential for food, cosmetics, 
and other industries [29]. 
When it comes to managing plantations, counting and detection of 
trees is one of the important works. To monitor their growth, and 
planning for the planting layout, counting and detection of oil palm trees 
are required. In the past few years, automated detection and counting of 
trees using high-resolution remote sensing images is one of the most 
popular methods due to the rapid advancement of high-resolution sat­
ellites [30–32] and unmanned aerial vehicles (UAV) [33,34]. The pro­
cess of gathering spatial information from the Earth’s surface either 
using aircraft or satellite is known as remote sensing. The energy re­
flected from Earth is collected through remote sensors mounted on 
satellites or aircraft. This technology brings more opportunities to the 
process and extract of complementary information when implemented 
with advanced image processing techniques. Remote sensing is partic­
ularly useful in the application of oil palm detection and monitoring due 
to its function such as disease detection, plant health monitoring, tree 
counting, as well as for age estimation, yield estimation, and carbon 
production [35]. 
Since oil palm has become one of the persistent elements on the land 
for agriculture, it is an element of interest for remote sensing applica­
tions [35–37]. Oil palm management teams have started to realize the 
economic value of remote sensing for the monitor of land-use changes 
[38]. However, the remote sensing applications for oil palm detection 
are still under research and development level. Currently, most of the 
counting and monitoring processes still rely on traditional surveying or 
counting methods. Furthermore, the collaboration between academia 
and industry has not been working well [39], which is due to the lack of 
knowledge of the techniques and confidential details in the context of 
effective processing of palm oil production on the industrial side. It is 
crucial to narrow the barrier between academia and industry for effec­
tive use of remote sensing technology. Recently, deep learning models, 
such as CNN, have been commonly applied for identify images and 
recognizing objects [40]. Since 2013, deep learning-based have ach­
ieved a great performance when applied to remote sensing domain for 
land cover classification [41,42], scene classification [43], building 
extraction [44], and vehicle detections [45]. Moreover, the CNN-based 
method has performed excellently and achieved great detection results 
for the plantation with absolute oil palm area [46,47]. 
Deep learning applications are important for oil palm tree detection 
and counting to ensure accuracy, efficiency and cost effectiveness. Deep 
learning models can achieve high accuracy rates in detecting and 
counting oil palm trees. This accuracy is critical for precision agricul­
ture, where small errors in crop counts can have significant impacts on 
yield and profitability. Deep learning algorithms can process large 
amounts of image data quickly and efficiently. This enables oil palm 
plantations to analyse vast areas of land, identify tree locations, and 
count them faster than traditional manual methods. Using deep learning 
for oil palm tree detection and counting can be cost-effective, as it re­
duces the need for manual labour and allows for more targeted and 
efficient use of resources such as fertilizers and pesticides. 
The goals of this study are to: (1) present an overview of deep 
learning applications in agriculture - the oil palm sector; (2) describe the 
deep learning training procedure for counting oil palm trees; and (3) 
review the deep learning techniques for oil palm tree counting and 
finally 4) identify knowledge gaps and required research for efficient 
deep learning-based oil palm counting. The remaining article is orga­
nized as follows: the next section starts with the review process, appli­
cation of deep learning in the agriculture, training of deep learning 
network, deep learning techniques for oil palm tree counting and lastly, 
the gaps and future recommendations for future research on oil palm 
counting. 
2. Review topic selection 
To conduct this review, first of all, a domain-specific less intact 
research topic was carefully chosen after a deep study of existing liter­
ature related to latent advancement in oil palm using deep learning. Our 
search focus is mainly on oil palm tree counting using deep learning 
approaches, oil palm tree counting using drone and large-scale oil palm 
tree detection 
The basic searching was done by an automated search. The starting 
input for the search was “oil palm tree” AND “deep learning.” Articles 
were retrieved, and abstracts were read to find the synonyms of the 
keywords. The search input was used to obtain a broad view of the 
studies. From the basic search experiment, a more complex search string 
was built in order to avoid missing relevant studies. The final search 
string was as follows: (“machine AND learning” OR “deep AND learning” 
OR “artificial AND intelligence” OR “convolutional neural network”) 
AND (“oil AND palm”) AND (“remote AND sensing”) AND (“drone” OR 
“Unmanned AND automated AND vehicle”) 
After executing iterated combinations of defined search strings in 
five different databases Scopus, Science Direct, Springer Link, Web of 
Science, and Google Scholar. The initial search was performed based on 
the title, abstract, and keywords; however, the full text was considered 
for final selection, categorization, and information extraction. The 
article selection phase was inaugurated after streaming through the 
entire set of downloaded articles. To limit the literature search, a date 
filter was applied to extract articles within the 10 years period. 
To narrow down the selection, only articles that propose the appli­
cation of deep learning and machine learning to explore oil palm were 
considered. On the other hand, all the articles that did not apply deep 
learning/machine learning for agricultural oil palm as a special case 
were discarded. For instance, a study was not included if it investigated 
public opinion (consumer perception) on the impacts of palm oil using 
ML methods. Consequently, the number of remaining publications was 
reduced to 103, and we decided to analyse all the publications further 
regardless of the impact factor and the number of citations.

Deep learning has successfully shown it’s capability in various do­
mains and it has also recently entered the domain of agriculture. The 
promising performance of deep learning in classifying images [48,49] 
has slowly replaced the traditional machine learning in the area of image

Deep learning is a subset of machine learning that uses neural networks 
to analyse and learn from data. In agriculture, deep learning can be used 
for various applications such as crop yield prediction, plant disease 
detection, and weed identification. In the modern agriculture domain, 
deep learning focuses on image classification and object detection task 
to calculate the number of vegetation and distinguish between different 
vegetation. This is particularly important for plant managers to manage 
the plantation. Deep learning has the ability to capture the image fea­
tures automatically compared with traditional machinie learning 
method. This boosted the performance of the image recognition and 
object detection accuracy as the traditional machine learning method, 
required user to manually extract the image features during the training 
stage. Fig. 1 illustrates the machine learning and deep learning concept 
for agriculture. The process of deep learning for agriculture typically 
involves data collection, data preprocessing, model selection, model 
training, model evaluation to measure its accuracy and performance. 
Once the model is deemed accurate and reliable, it can be deployed to 
make predictions or detect anomalies in real-time. 
Deep learning is a process of machine learning that rely on artificial 
neural networks (ANN). Some of the deep learning architecture such as 
Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), Deep Neural Network (DNN) 
[53], Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN), Deep Belief Network (DBN), 
and Fully Convolutional Network (FCN) have achieved an excellent 
performance when applied to different research areas, including agri­
culture [54], face recognition [10], and natural language processing 
[55]. Furthermore, several of the datasets, architectures and frameworks 
are available free online for the building of the deep learning model. 
Datasets such as MNIST [56], ImageNet [57], VisualQA [58], CIFAR-10 
[59] are some of the open datasets for experimenting with deep 
learning. Apart from that, popular frameworks including TensorFlow, 
InceptionNet, PyTorch, Keras, and Open Neural Network Exchange 
(ONNX) are utilized for the building of deep learning models much more 
conveniently [60]. Some of the pre-trained networks such as AlexNet, 
CaffeNet, ResNet, Visual Geometry Group (VGG), and InceptionNet are 
widely used as a starting point for the training of deep learning networks 
for recognition of second tasks. These pre-trained networks are having 
the advantage of learning new tasks easily due to most of them are 
already trained with large amount of datasets [61]. Nonetheless, its 
disadvantages might be the necessity of powerful hardware spec and 
heavy time consumption for the operation of parallel programming 
while, traditional methods such as Scale-invariant feature transform 
(SIFT) or Support Vector Machine (SVM) [62–64] have simpler and 
shorter training process. 
The applications of deep learning in agriculture have spread over 
several areas mainly in the field of disease identification [65], land cover 
[34], plant recognition [66–68], weed identification [69], and tree 
counting [33,70]. amongst all the deep learning architectures, CNN has 
been the most commonly used architecture while their alternatives are 
based on FCN. FCN is constructed by replacing the fully connected layers 
from CNN to convolutional layers for image localization purposed using 
a sliding window algorithm [71]. Besides, Region-based Convolutional 
Neural Network (R-CNN) is a two-stage detection algorithm in which the 
first stage identifies the location of the object in an image and the second 
stage classifies it. R-CNN eliminates the problem of classifying the entire 
images, instead, only some selected regions are classified [72]. Addi­
tionally, the training datasets for the deep learning architecture are 
created in various ways such as manually interpretation of images, im­
ages from open datasets like MNIST, ImageNet, or UAV. The process of 
data augmentation is often applied to the dataset to improve the 
network results [73]. 
In deep learning, CNN [74,75], is a widely used deep neural network 
for image classification. One of its advantages is CNN is capable of direct 
extract of features from a new dataset without the requirement of 
manual feature extraction like traditional computer vision feature 
detection algorithms, such as Scale-invariant feature transformer (SIFT) 
and Local binary patterns (LBP) [76]. For computer vision, the auto­
mated approach of feature extraction is the most effective learning 
model for object detection, recognition, and classification. Generally, 
traditional machine learning requires manual feature extraction for 
training and this process is separate from classifying objects. However,

Technically, CNN is the combination of different layers that perform 
image classification tasks [77]. First, the input layer will receive the 
datasets and transferred them to the further layers for features extrac­
tion. Afterwards, the filter in the convolutional layer is applied to the 
dataset for the purpose of extracting features. Next, the Rectified linear 
unit (ReLU) replaced all the negative numbers from the convolution 
layer to zero results in less time and effective training. Further stage, the 
pooling layer captures reduce the image size to preserve the essential 
information and a fully connected layer translates the high-level filtered 
images into labels with categories. Then, the SoftMax layer classified the 
images depends on the decimal probabilities for each class [78].

4. Training of deep learning network 
4.1. Data preparation 
For training of the new network, data preparation is the first step and 
it has a great impact on the final result [79]. The size and format of the 
dataset utilized for the training of neural networks strongly affect the 
final performance of the trained network [80]. Recently, Zheng et al. 
[68] proposed a method for the detection of oil palm trees using 
QuickBird satellite imagery. For creating a training dataset, the image is 
then cropped into 14 regions with the size of 600 × 600 pixels. amongst 
these 14 regions, 6 regions are referred as the training area, 2 are 
referred as the validation area, and the rest are referred as the testing 
area. For the training area, bilinear interpolation is applied to resize the 
images to 2400 × 2400 pixels. After the training images are enlarged, 
the 5000 training samples are generated by randomly cropped the 
training images with the image size of 500 × 500 pixels. Besides, Abd 
Mubin et al. [44] prepare the dataset by exporting the RGB images ac­
quired from WorldView-3 to JPEG format. The converted images are 
divided into 24 tiles with a resolution of 991 × 115 pixels. Next, the 24 
tiles are further split into smaller sub-tile. The resolution sub-tile for the 
area that contains mature oil palms and young oil palms are different. 
The sub-tile of mature oil palm and young oil palm areas are 31 × 31 
pixels and 26 × 26 pixels respectively. 
Moreover, Li et al. [81] manually interpreted 9000 samples for the 
training of the neural network. From the 9000 samples, 5000 of them are 
distributed as the oil palm tree samples and 4000 of them are the 
background samples. These samples are then further divided into a 
training dataset and test dataset with an image resolution of 17 × 17 
pixels. Furthermore, Freudenberg et al. [33] created a dataset by 
marking the oil palm tree and non-oil palm object. The marking is done 
by manually digitized of the oil palm tree centre with a radius of 2 m. A 
total of 10,679 palms and 4600 non-palms points are marked. In addi­
tion, most of the researchers are creating their dataset for the detection 
of oil palm trees. The entire image acquired using remote sensing are 
divided into a smaller region as the learning material for deep neural 
network. 
4.2. Pre-trained network 
As its name suggests, a pre-trained network is a deep learning model 
that has already been trained with open datasets. There are a lot of pre- 
trained architectures that are available online for modifying to build a 
CNN model for detection or recognition of new objects. LeNet archi­
tecture is one of the top performed network architecture with an error 
rate of 28.2%. The LeNet architecture is constructed using two con­
volutional layers, ReLu, pooling layer, two fully connected layers and 
lastly a softmax layer [2]. Besides, AlexNet is the improved version of 
LeNet [82]. The ReLU of AlexNet is capable of performing non-linearity 
to the images as this speed up the recognition process. This network is 
more complex as it has five convolutional layers, and three 
fully-connected layers the error rate has improved from 28.2% to 16.4% 
compared with LeNet architecture. In addition, the Visual Geometry 
Group (VGG) contains VGG-16 and VGG-19. The main difference of this 
network is that the filter (kernels) from the convolutional layers are 
replaced by multiple 3 × 3 filters [83]. This improved the efficiency of 
extracting features from complex datasets. 
Cheang et al. [46] proposed LeNet based CNN architecture for 
counting and localizing palm trees. Different pre-trained networks have 
been evaluated such as SqueezeNet, AlexNet, and LeNet to find out the 
best networks for classifying oil palm trees. The validation accuracy of 
68.75%, 67.52%, and 94.5% are obtained from SqueezeNet, AlexNet, 
and LeNet respectively. The researchers presumed that a small dataset 
has caused the overfitting of AlexNet and SqueeNet. 
Besides, Li et al. [84] introduced LeNet based CNN architecture with 
the structure of two convolutional layers, pooling layers and one fully 
connected layer. Recently, the same author [85] has proposed another 
deep learning techniques that utilize of two CNN for detection of oil 
palm tree. According to their research, the first CNN is used for the land 
cover classification and the second CNN is for the classification of oil 
palm trees. Different pre-trained networks such as LeNet, AlexNet, and 
VGG-19 are randomly merged for evaluating their performance towards 
oil palm detection. 
Most of the pre-trained networks are utilized for the detection and 
counting of oil palm trees. The pre-trained networks are reused and 
trained for solving new problems through transfer learning process. The 
training of the new model requires heavy computational work and is 
time-consuming. With the pre-trained networks that are widely avail­
able online, the fine-tuning of the network can be carried out with the 
new dataset created. 
4.3. Optimization algorithms 
To achieve excellent performance in image recognition, optimizers 
are normally applied for changing the attributes of deep learning net­
works such as learning rate and weights for improving accuracy. 
Generally, optimization algorithms can be classified into two categories 
which are first-order optimization and second-order optimization algo­
rithms [86]. The first-order optimization algorithms operate using its 
gradient value for minimizing or maximizing the objective function. The 
commonly used first-order optimizer is the Gradient Descent. It de­
termines which weights must be altered so that the function can reach 
minima [87]. Through backpropagation, the loss is from one layer is 
transferred to another layer and the weights are changed based on the 
losses to minimize loss. Besides, the second-order optimization algo­
rithms carry out to minimize or maximize object function using 
second-order derivatives. The second-order derivatives identify the 
behaviour of first derivative which in turn shows the function’s curva­
ture. Adagrad is one of the second-order optimizers that is capable of 
changing the learning rate of the network [88]. The learning rate ‘n’ for 
each parameter is changed according to the time step ‘t’. The advantage 
of using this optimizer is that the second-order optimizer provides a 
huge update for less frequent parameters and a small step for frequent 
parameters. 
Based on a study from Ruder [87], stochastic gradient descent (SGD) 
is the most used optimization algorithm for the training of deep learning 
models. However, this optimizer has some limitations in terms of tuning 
of learning rate due to the high variance of model parameters [89]. 
Many optimization algorithms such as Adadelta [90], Adam [91], 
Adagrad, AdaMax [92], Nadam [92], and RMSprop [93] have been 
proposed as an adaptive variant of SGD. amongst all of the optimizers, 
Adam optimization algorithm has been widely used for the image clas­
sification for remote sensing images [94,95]. Apart from Adam opti­
mizer, there are several optimizers that have been used in image 
classification for remote sensing. In a paper published by Abd Mubin 
et al. [44], several optimizers have been selected for training of U-Net 
CNN model and their performance is evaluated. The authors have 
selected Adagrad as their optimizers due to the lower value of model 
loss. Furthermore, Li et al. [84] has selected mini-batch gradient descent 
as their optimizer for the training of LeNet CNN model. 5.0 Deep

In a paper by Li et al. [52], AlexNet architecture is selected for the 
training of CNN for oil palm detection. The label prediction of the image 
dataset is performed through the sliding window technique. After the 
process of label prediction, a post-processing method is introduced for 
merging the coordinates of the sample predicted if the coordinate 
nearby is less than the Euclidean spatial resolution threshold. The 
post-processing algorithm is proposed to reduce overcounting and 
improve the accuracy for the detection of oil palm. 
Moreover, Bonet [77] introduce a VGG-16 model CNN for the 
detection of oil palm tree. The transfer learning for feature extraction of 
VGG-16 architecture is carried out for fine-tune of the pre-trained 
network. Nonetheless, in this paper, the final layer of the VGG-16 is 
removed and replaced with a SoftMax layer under the fully connected 
layers. Similar to Li et al. [52], the author proposed the sliding window 
method for object detection for label prediction. 
Freudenberg et al. [97] proposed a deep learning method using 
U-Net type for detection of oil palm tree. The U-Net architecture is 
different from some pre-trained models specified for image recognition 
jobs. Normally, the input image to the pre-trained network is converted 
into a vector, while U-Net turns the image into multiple segments. 
Therefore, the proposed method is able to detect oil palm trees even 
under difficult conditions such as urban or shadows areas. However, the 
U-Net architecture has some limitations when classifying of oil palm 
tree. Some of the single oil palm tree is detected as multiple oil palm 
tree. To overcome this problem, the classifier window is set to be bigger 
for classifying multiple oil palm trees at once. 
Furthermore, Zheng et al. [76] have used Faster-RCNN for the 
detection of oil palm. Unlike CNN, Faster-RCNN has a built-in localizer 
for detection of the object without using a slide window algorithm. 
Instead, the region proposal network (RPN) is used for object detection 
in Faster-RCNN. Nonetheless, the authors have discovered two major 
drawbacks when using Faster-RCNN for detection of oil palm: (1) the 
size of training samples for oil palm tree is too small; (2) the other 
vegetation around the oil palm tree led to misclassify of oil palm tree. 
Hence, to overcome this problem, the authors tailored the RPN and 
proposed a post-processing algorithm to improve accuracy. Different 
RPN backbone structures are implemented and evaluated. 
Apart from the deep learning model using only a single CNN, Li et al. 
[72] and Zortea et al. [28] proposed two CNN models for detection of oil 
palm tree. However, the concepts proposed for both researchers are 
different. Li et al. use the first CNN model for land cover classification 
and the second CNN model for the classification of oil palm tree. 
Meanwhile, Zortea et al. let the first CNN model for learning of fine-scale 
details in the image and the second CNN focus on coarser details. Li et al. 
method will only start to detect the oil palm tree if the area is classified 
as a palm tree plantation area. This has reduced the running time of CNN 
model as the second CNN will only perform detection when the land 
cover type is identified by the first CNN mode. Nevertheless, Zortea et al. 
method can classify the oil palm tree better when different spatial res­
olution images are input into the deep learning model.

6. Gaps and recommendation for future research 
From the review of the publications on oil palm counting, not all 
applications address the issues of oil palm counting, while some deserve 
special highlights to disseminate broadly. In the next subsection, po­
tential research areas for future research and gaps of the current method 
will be discussed. 
6.1. Heavily rely on commercial remote sensing imagery sources 
On the prediction and counting of oil palm tree, image source plays a 
huge role in the training of deep learning models. Selecting a good im­
agery source results in the higher accuracy achieved during the vali­
dation step. As from the Table 1, Quickbird, Digital globe, WorldView, 
and UAV are often utilized for the training of deep learning models. 
However, the above-mentioned imagery source somehow requires a 
certain amount of payment to get access to it. Since the majority of the 
hard costs are spent on creating datasets, non-commercial remote 
sensing imagery sources can be considered as an option to replace 
commercial remote sensing imagery sources. Yet, commercial remote 
sensing images are selected as what they offered such as higher image 
resolution and spatial resolution. Additionally, some researchers are 
utilising non-commercial remote sensing images such as Google Earth 
and USGS. It heavily reduces the cost of building a deep learning model 
as well as achieving a reasonable counting accuracy. Besides, the 
blending of both image sources can also lower the project cost and 
eventually improve the deep learning model with more datasets. 
6.2. Number of training images 
In the future plantation management where operating costs are 
minimized and profit is maximized, the number of training images is one 
of the crucial aspects when comes to the training of the deep learning 
model. Technically, the more the datasets are prepared, the higher the 
accuracy of the trained deep learning model. Yet, in the actual envi­
ronment, deep learning network trained with a large training dataset 
might lead to overfitting or underfitting. Deep learning algorithms 
require large amounts of data to train effectively. For agriculture, this 
means collecting data from fields, weather stations, drones, satellites, 
and other sources. Hence, the trial and error method is often used to 
balance out the number of training datasets and the network accuracy. 
Nevertheless, from a business perspective, large training images simply 
means higher costs. The preparation of large datasets is time-consuming 
and involves a lot of computation works which sometimes lead to only a 
minor improvement for the network instead. From the papers reviewed, 
most of the researchers create their datasets with thousands of training 
images. Thus, future research featuring lower training images can be 
conducted to minimize the training cost of the deep learning model 
without affecting the accuracy that much. 
6.3. Multi-layered deep learning network 
Multi-layered deep learning stacking provides a different way of 
counting oil plant trees. For instance, the first layer can be used as a land 
field classification and the second layer will start work as a plant tree 
classification. This hugely improves the accuracy of the network trained 
as well as lowered the computation load when the counting process is 
performed. A multi-layered deep learning network provides enormous 
potential to the counting of the deep learning network. In a study con­
ducted by Li et al. [102], two CNN layered are used to test out the 
effectiveness of a multi-layered deep learning network towards oil palm 
plantations. The training process is somehow similar to the ordinary 
deep learning network in which each network is trained by its 
corresponding dataset. Nevertheless, in oil palm tree counting, the 
combination of multiple deep learning networks are not fully developed 
yet. The potential of using multiple combined deep learning networks 
for oil palm counting can be further research to compare the difference 
between a single-stage deep learning network. 
7. Conclusions 
Oil palm tree counting and detection are important practices for 
managing an oil palm plantation. Tree count management is crucial for 
the long-term monitoring of oil palm production. In addition, oil palm 
tree is one of the largest contributor to Malaysia agriculture sector. The 
oil palm industry creates nearly half a million employment and liveli­
hood to an estimated one million people. Traditionally, customary 
methods are often labour-intensive catalogue in the plantation field for 
counting of oil palm trees. Nonetheless, these methods are time- 
consuming, costly, and not practical for large-scale plantations. Hence, 
deep learning and remote sensing technologies are introduced as alter­
native methods for manual counting. Besides, there are numerous 
journals on the detection of oil palm trees published in the literature, 
and the number of those journals keeps growing recently. This paper had 
presented an overview of deep learning applications in the oil palm 
sector, explained the deep learning training procedure for counting oil 
palm trees and identified the knowledge gaps and required research for 
efficient deep learning-based oil palm counting. 
The introduction of deep learning to agriculture have changed how 
traditional plantation management works. As the information of earth 
surface can be easily obtained through remote sensing technology, the 
rise of popularity for managing plantation through modern technology 
such as image processing and deep learning has reduced the cost of 
managing plantation traditionally with the labour force. The application 
of deep learning helps companies to acquire valuable or important in­
formation that is further used for yield estimation and planning of 
planting layout. Lastly, to advance in mutual advantage, dissemination 
and knowledge transfer of techniques should be given more attention 
between the industry and academic experts. With the extra support from 
the industry, more joint research on future technology for oil palm 
management can be conducted smoothly.
