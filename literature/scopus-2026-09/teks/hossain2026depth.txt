Abstract. Fruit detection in orchards remains challenging due to irregular lighting,
dense leaf cover, and clustered fruit arrangements. Conventional RGB-based methods of-
ten fail to generalize under these conditions, limiting their practical use in precision agri-
culture. This study proposes a novel RGB-D framework that fuses YOLOv5 with Faster R-
CNN, exploiting complementary strengths for robust detection and segmentation. Depth
cues are incorporated to reﬁne proposal generation and improve non-maximum suppres-
sion, while YOLOv5 enhances boundary precision and segmentation eﬃciency. Faster
R-CNN contributes strong recall by capturing diﬃcult instances, and a late-fusion design
uniﬁes both outputs into reliable predictions. Evaluations on an orchard dataset demon-
strate competitive performance, achieving 97.3% accuracy, 84.0% recall, 91.0% mAP, and
91.9% F1-score. Compared to Mask R-CNN, Faster R-CNN, and improved YOLOv5s,
the proposed framework consistently yields higher robustness. These results highlight its
potential for real-time agricultural robotics and scalable fruit-harvesting systems.
Keywords: RGB-D perception, Fruit detection, Instance segmentation, Faster R-CNN,
YOLOv5, Depth-aware fusion, Agricultural robotics
1. Introduction. Precision agriculture increasingly depends on intelligent visual per-
ception systems to automate harvesting, yield estimation, and orchard management. A
fundamental requirement in this context is robust fruit detection and segmentation, which
provide the spatial cues necessary for robotic manipulators to interact with crops safely
and eﬃciently. However, orchards present diﬃcult conditions for vision systems due to
variable illumination, shadows, overlapping fruits, and dense foliage that cause frequent
occlusion [1]. Early approaches based on RGB images and convolutional detectors such
as Faster R-CNN and YOLO have demonstrated promising results. However, they often
fail in natural orchard environments where fruit color and texture are easily confused with
leaves and branches. For example, real-time detection in high-density apple orchards re-
mains challenging when only color information is available [2]. This limitation motivates
the integration of additional sensing modalities. Depth perception has recently emerged
as an eﬀective complement to RGB, since it encodes geometric structure that is invariant
to lighting and color variations. Multi-modal frameworks that combine RGB and depth
have shown improved robustness, enabling simultaneous fruit detection and size estima-
tion [3]. Fusion strategies such as neighborhood region depth fusion (NBR-DF) further
enhance accuracy in high-density orchards, while RGB-D guided pick-and-place systems

• A hybrid detection-segmentation framework that combines RGB and depth cues to

• Experimental validation showing state-of-the-art results on an orchard RGB-D data-

2. Related Work. Deep learning has transformed fruit detection and segmentation in
orchards, enabling progress well beyond traditional color thresholding and handcrafted
features. Yet, despite major advances, current approaches still face limitations when de-
ployed in real orchard environments, particularly under occlusion, illumination variation,
and dense foliage. Two-stage detectors such as Faster R-CNN have been widely adopted
for fruit detection, achieving high accuracy under controlled conditions [7]. However, they
often struggle when fruits are partially hidden by leaves or branches, leading to reduced
recall. Extensions such as Mask R-CNN provide instance-level segmentation [8], but their
performance is highly dependent on large, high-quality annotated datasets, which are
expensive to obtain in agriculture. One-stage detectors like YOLOv4 and YOLOv5 have
demonstrated strong real-time performance in orchards [9]. Nevertheless, they rely pri-
marily on RGB features, making them sensitive to lighting changes and fruit-leaf color
similarity. In practice, this leads to false positives when leaves share the same hue as
fruits and missed detections when fruits are shaded. Lightweight CNN models have been
introduced for embedded deployment but often at the cost of reduced accuracy [10]. To
address these challenges, depth information has been explored. RGB-D cameras provide
structural cues that are invariant to illumination and color, making them valuable for dis-
tinguishing fruits from background clutter [11]. Depth-guided approaches have improved
robustness under occlusion [12, 13], while stereo vision and LiDAR oﬀer alternatives for
large-scale orchard mapping [14]. Yet, depth data is often underutilized either incorpo-
rated only at the input stage or treated separately from RGB, without tight integration
into detection pipelines. Segmentation methods such as Mask R-CNN and DeepLabv3+
produce ﬁner fruit boundaries [15], but these models are computationally heavy and of-
ten impractical for real-time orchard robotics. Semantic segmentation of blossoms has
shown promise for yield prediction [16], though generalizing such methods to multi-fruit
detection in dynamic ﬁeld conditions remains diﬃcult. More recently, transformer-based
models and hybrid CNN transformer designs have been applied to fruit recognition [17].
These approaches improve detection of small and partially occluded fruits, but their high
computational cost and need for large datasets limit their adoption in resource constrained

agricultural robots. Multitask networks that unify detection, segmentation, and size esti-
mation have been validated for yield mapping [18], but integrating geometric depth cues
into such frameworks remains largely unexplored. Survey studies [19, 20] consistently
highlight that current fruit detection systems still fail to achieve robust precision-recall
trade-oﬀs in natural orchard conditions. These limitations highlight the need for meth-
ods that (i) exploit both RGB appearance and geometric depth cues in a tightly coupled
manner, (ii) balance accuracy with real-time eﬃciency for deployment in orchard robots,
and (iii) provide both detection and segmentation for precise fruit perception. Our pro-
posed framework addresses these needs by combining Faster R-CNN and YOLOv5 in a
depth-aware late-fusion design, where RGB and HHA-encoded depth features guide pro-
posal generation and segmentation masks are reﬁned through fusion. By leveraging both
detectors and depth cues, the method improves recall under occlusion, reduces false pos-
itives from leaf-fruit confusion, and maintains competitive inference speed suitable for
embedded agricultural platforms.
3. Methodology. The proposed study introduces a dual-stream learning framework that
integrates RGB-D sensing with deep neural networks to achieve accurate and reliable fruit
detection and segmentation. Unlike conventional single-input methods that depend only
on RGB color information, the design explicitly incorporates geometric depth cues to
improve robustness against occlusion, lighting variations, and visual similarities between
fruits and foliage. The workﬂow consists of four stages: (i) preparation of RGB-D data,
(ii) a depth-guided proposal generator based on Faster R-CNN, (iii) a real-time segmen-
tation branch using YOLOv5-Seg, and (iv) a fusion and optimization stage that uniﬁes
both outputs into precise predictions. The input to the framework is acquired using an
Intel RealSense D435 sensor, which produces both a color image and a per-pixel depth
map. Depth information provides essential three-dimensional structure but is often noisy
in its raw form. To make it more descriptive for learning, the depth map is converted into
a three-channel geometric encoding composed of disparity, elevation, and angular orienta-
tion. For a pixel location p = (px, py) with a depth measurement Z(p), the transformation

4. Experiment Results and Discussion. The experimental work was carried out on
a dedicated computing platform running Ubuntu 20.04.6 LTS (64-bit). The system was
conﬁgured with an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060 GPU containing 8 GB of VRAM. The
proposed RGB-D framework was trained for 200 epochs using the Adam optimizer, which
provided stable and consistent convergence across both the RGB and depth branches.
The initial learning rate was set to 0.001 and reduced using a multi-step decay sched-
ule, with learning rate drops at epochs 120 and 160. A batch size of 4 was selected in
accordance with the memory limitations of the NVIDIA RTX 4060 GPU used for all
experiments. All depth-related components, including HHA encoding and depth-guided
NMS, were trained jointly with the backbone network. The experiment result showed that
removing depth-guided NMS led to a notable reduction in recall under heavy occlusion,
while excluding the HHA encoding reduced the mAP due to the loss of geometric depth
cues. Similarly, turning oﬀthe fusion weighting decreased the F1-score by weakening the
complementarity between the two detection branches. The fusion weight β for combining
RGB and depth conﬁdence scores was determined through validation experiments and
ﬁxed at 0.55 for all evaluations. In addition, we evaluated the runtime performance of the

full RGB-D pipeline on the RTX 4060 platform, where the system achieved an inference
rate of approximately 18 FPS with an 8 GB memory footprint. Although it is not the
fastest model among the baselines, this speed remains suitable for slow-moving orchard

practical ﬁeld conditions. The apple dataset was divided into 2290 training images and
801 validation images. To maintain consistency and optimize performance, all images
were resized to 896 × 896 pixels during training. To develop and train the segmenta-
tion network for the proposed apple detection framework, two types of datasets were
utilized. The ﬁrst was the publicly available MinneApple dataset, which provides 1,000
annotated orchard images and has been widely adopted as a benchmark in fruit detection
research. The second was the Amodal dataset, consisting of 3,019 images, which oﬀers
a richer variety of apple appearances under diverse orchard conditions. The combination
of these datasets enabled robust training by capturing both standardized benchmark sce-

5. Conclusions. In this work, we developed an RGB-D fruit detection and segmenta-
tion framework that integrates Faster R-CNN and YOLOv5 in a depth-aware late-fusion
design. By combining appearance cues with geometric depth information, the method
achieved reliable detection and segmentation performance under challenging orchard con-
ditions, including illumination variation, foliage occlusion, and densely clustered fruit.
When evaluated across all four key metrics accuracy, recall, mAP, and F1-score, the pro-
posed framework demonstrated competitive overall performance compared with existing
baselines. Although certain models achieved higher results in individual metrics, none
provided a stronger balance across all measures. To provide a more comprehensive as-
sessment, additional ablation studies and runtime analysis were incorporated, oﬀering
clearer insight into the contribution of each module and the computational feasibility of
the system. These results highlight the eﬀectiveness of depth-guided fusion in improv-
ing robustness in natural orchard environments. Future work will explore extending the
framework to multi-fruit scenarios and integrating the full system into real robotic har-
vesting platforms.
