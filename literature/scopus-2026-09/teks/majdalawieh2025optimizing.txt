Abstract— Current approaches to automating fruit 
harvesting face challenges in accuracy, speed, and adaptability 
under varying field conditions. Traditional methods often suffer 
from limited precision, slow performance, and a lack of 
robustness, which can lead to suboptimal yield and increased 
labor costs. This study addresses these shortcomings by 
developing a real-time deep learning architecture that enhances 
fruit harvesting automation. Our proposed system comprises 
two primary phases: fruit detection and fruit counting. Utilizing 
the cutting-edge YOLO V7 for detection and integrating it with 
DeepSORT and an ROI line-based method for counting, the 
architecture achieves an unprecedented balance of speed and 
accuracy, particularly suited for the demands of agricultural 
sciences. The YOLO-V7 model demonstrated exceptional 
efficacy in fruit detection, achieving a mean average precision 
(mAP@0.50) of 97.5%. It also surpassed real-time processing 
speed expectations, maintaining an evaluation rate of 30 frames 
per second (FPS), which is beyond the real-time requirement of 
24 FPS. When compared to previous iterations like YOLO-V5 
and YOLO-V4, our model's overall precision improved by 1.9% 
and 3.8%, respectively, and exhibited greater recall by margins 
of 0.7% and 2.3%. Notably, YOLO V7's mAP was higher by 
0.8% and 3.2% compared to YOLO V5 and V4. Even with a 
high confidence threshold of 95%, YOLO V7 outperformed the 
earlier models by 3.6% and 8.7%, validating its superior 
capability for agricultural applications. 
Keywords — Artificial Intelligence, Agriculture, DeepSORT, 
Deep Learning, Farming, Machine Learning, YOLO. 
I. INTRODUCTION  
Modern agriculture requires advanced technologies to 
optimize crop management, reduce resource wastage, and 
improve efficiency. Currently, fruit harvesting primarily relies 
on manual picking methods, which are labor-intensive, costly, 
and prone to errors. Advances in artificial intelligence, 
especially in the domain of computer vision, provide 
promising solutions to automate this critical agricultural 
activity. Over recent years, image processing techniques have 
effectively automated agricultural tasks such as yield 
estimation, crop counting, weed detection, and fruit 
identification [1][2]. 
Among recent advancements, deep learning architectures 
such as RCNN, Faster RCNN, and YOLO (You Only Look 
Once) have demonstrated exceptional capabilities in detecting 
objects accurately and rapidly within complex agricultural 
environments [3][4]. Integrating these deep learning 
frameworks with robotic systems has led to the development 
of automated fruit harvesting solutions, which operate in two 
primary stages: fruit detection and robotic picking [5]. This 
combination significantly reduces manual labor, enhances 
productivity, and facilitates precise yield estimation for 
improved resource and market planning [6][7]. 
However, creating a robust computer vision system for 
fruit harvesting faces challenges due to environmental 
complexities, including varying illumination, occlusion, and 
non-uniform orchard conditions [8]. To address these 
challenges, this paper proposes an integrated pipeline 
leveraging YOLO-V7 for accurate and efficient fruit detection 
combined with the DeepSORT algorithm for real-time 
tracking and automated fruit counting. The proposed approach 
aims to achieve superior accuracy, speed, and robustness, 
providing a viable alternative to manual fruit harvesting while 
significantly 
enhancing 
productivity 
and 
resource 
management. 
II. RESEARCH MOTIVATION 
Manual fruit harvesting and yield estimation remain 
costly, labor-intensive, and prone to inaccuracies [10]. 
Growers typically estimate orchard yields by sampling limited 
areas and extrapolating results, leading to unreliable 
predictions [11][12]. Despite recent developments in 
automated detection, current methods still face challenges due 
to computational demands, slow inference speeds, and errors 
caused by occlusions and variable environmental conditions 
[13]. Moreover, most existing studies have primarily 
addressed fruit detection, with limited attention toward 
automated counting, a crucial factor for yield estimation 
[14][15]. 
To address these gaps, this study proposes a lightweight 
deep-learning framework combining YOLO-V7 for fruit 
detection and DeepSORT for real-time counting. The main 
objectives are: 
• 
Developing a lightweight model suitable for limited 
computational resources. 
• 
Achieving real-time performance (over 24 FPS). 
• 
Automating fruit counting via integration with 
DeepSORT and ROI-based counting methods. 
• 
Ensuring robust performance across diverse orchard 
conditions.

C. Object Detection And Tracking Framework 
 
To address the limitations identified in previous works, 
this study employs YOLO-V7 [3] integrated with DeepSORT 
[14][20][49] for effective fruit detection, tracking, and 
automated counting. YOLO frameworks have consistently 
demonstrated exceptional performance due to their real-time 
capabilities and high accuracy, making them widely adopted 
in various industrial and agricultural applications [4]. Among 
recent YOLO architectures, YOLO-V7 stands out as the 
fastest and most precise single-stage object detection model 
available, significantly outperforming traditional two-stage 
approaches in both accuracy and speed [3][4]. 
 
Complementing YOLO-V7’s detection capabilities, the 
DeepSORT algorithm is implemented for robust object 
tracking and real-time fruit counting. DeepSORT is an 
advanced variant of the SORT algorithm [50], incorporating 
both motion-based prediction (Kalman filtering) and 
appearance-based metrics generated from deep convolutional 
neural networks. This combination greatly enhances tracking 
accuracy, particularly in scenarios involving occlusion, 
variable viewpoints, and camera motion [14][51][52]. The 
integration of YOLO-V7 and DeepSORT thus delivers 
reliable real-time tracking and accurate fruit counting, 
essential for precise yield estimation and resource planning. 
 
Unlike previous studies primarily focusing on standalone 
object detection or limited integration of tracking methods, 
our proposed approach advances existing frameworks by 
combining the superior detection capabilities of YOLO-V7 
with DeepSORT's real-time tracking robustness. Earlier 
methods, such as YOLO-V4 and YOLO-V5, achieved real-
time detection but lacked comprehensive tracking and 
automated counting mechanisms suitable for realistic orchard 
conditions. Our pipeline introduces novel optimizations, 
including dynamic adaptive tracking to automatically adjust 
detection

counting methods, and custom data augmentation strategies 
tailored specifically for agricultural settings. Furthermore, 
the proposed system supports seamless integration with IoT

IV. MATERIAL AND METHODS 
A. System Requirements 
The experiments were performed using high-performance 
computing hardware equipped with NVIDIA GTX 1080 Ti 
GPUs to support the real-time processing requirements of our 
deep learning models. High-resolution cameras were 
strategically placed in orchard environments, ensuring 
comprehensive coverage under varying lighting and 
environmental conditions. The experimental setup was 
specifically optimized for accurate fruit detection at different 
times of day and under diverse weather conditions. The 
software utilized included Python 3.8 and TensorFlow 2.4, 
chosen due to their robust support for convolutional neural 
networks and extensive image processing libraries. Model 
training was executed over 400 epochs, employing a batch 
size of 32 and an Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 
0.001. 
These 
parameters 
were 
determined 
through 
preliminary testing to maximize accuracy and computational 
efficiency, ensuring reproducibility and reliability of our 
results in practical agricultural settings. 
B. Dataset Construction 
The dataset comprised apple images sourced from the 
DeepFruits dataset and mango images from the MangoYOLO 
dataset. Initially, 63 apple orchard images from DeepFruits 
were supplemented by an additional 500 apple images 
collected from Google to enhance dataset diversity. For 
mango images, 1000 samples were randomly selected from 
the 1730 available in MangoYOLO. After applying data 
augmentation techniques (detailed in the next section), the 
final combined dataset contained 2000 orchard images 
equally divided between apple and mango classes to mitigate 
class imbalance. The dataset was then partitioned into 
training (70%), validation (20%), and testing (10%) subsets 
for model development and evaluation. 
C. Data Augmentation 
Data augmentation techniques were applied to enrich the 
dataset, enhance model robustness, and minimize potential 
overfitting. Spatial-level transformations were selected due to 
their proven effectiveness in improving object detection 
performance [53]. The following transformations were 
applied to the dataset images: 
• 
Rotation: -25° and +25° 
• 
Horizontal and vertical flips 
• 
90-degree rotations (clockwise, counterclockwise, 
upside-down) 
Through augmentation, apple images increased from 563 
to 1000 and mango images from 800 to 1000. All images 
were resized to 416×416 pixels, consistent with YOLO model 
input standards.

E. The YOLO Architecture 
The YOLO (You Only Look Once) frameworks are state-
of-the-art object detection methods employing a single neural 
network for simultaneous object classification and bounding-
box prediction. YOLO-V7, the latest iteration, currently 
achieves the highest accuracy and inference speed within the 
YOLO family, reaching an AP of 56.8% across inference 
speeds ranging from 5 to 160 FPS. It significantly 
outperforms its predecessor YOLO-V5 by approximately 
120% in terms of speed and efficiency, surpassing even 
transformer-based detection architectures [54]. 
 
The YOLO-V7 architecture consists of three primary 
components: backbone, neck, and head (Fig. 1). Unlike 
previous YOLO models (e.g., YOLO-V3, YOLO-V4, 
YOLO-V5) that use Darknet as the backbone, YOLO-V7 
utilizes the Extended Efficient Layer Aggregation Network 
(E-ELAN). This backbone architecture employs techniques 
such as expand, shuffle, and merge cardinality, facilitating 
improved learning capabilities without compromising 
gradient flow. It also utilizes grouped convolutions to 
optimize computational efficiency [54][55]. YOLO-V7’s E-
ELAN inherently enhances multi-scale feature learning—
improving its ability to detect both large and small objects 
without further architectural changes. In this study, we relied 
on these built-in E-ELAN capabilities without applying 
additional small-object–specific modifications. While our 
results demonstrate high detection performance across fruit 
sizes, future work will explore targeted optimizations (e.g., 
anchor re-tuning or auxiliary detection heads) to further boost 
accuracy on very small or densely clustered fruits.

The neck of YOLO-V7 incorporates the Feature Pyramid 
Network (FPN) and Path Aggregation Network (PAN) 
structures to aggregate multi-scale feature maps effectively. 
A novel feature in YOLO-V7 is the optimized model scaling 
approach. Unlike conventional scaling methods that 
independently optimize resolution, depth, width, and stage 
parameters, YOLO-V7 coherently scales these factors, 
enhancing 
its 
adaptability 
across 
various 
hardware 
constraints [56][57]. 
 
Furthermore, 
YOLO-V7 
incorporates 
several 
optimization techniques known as trainable "bag-of-
freebies." Notably, the framework uses reparameterized 
convolution (RepConv) modules for better gradient variance 
across feature maps and improved robustness. Additionally, 
YOLO-V7 employs a novel multi-head detection approach 
guided by deep supervision, utilizing auxiliary heads 
supervised by the lead head’s predictions. Two label 
assignment strategies—lead-head-guided and coarse-to-fine 
hierarchical labeling—enhance the training efficiency and 
accuracy of the model [58][59].

F. Deep-Learning Based Real-Time Object Tracking And 
Counting 
For tracking and counting fruits in real-time, this study 
utilizes DeepSORT, an advanced object-tracking algorithm 
derived from the Simple Online Real-time Tracking (SORT) 
method [20][60]. Initially, SORT leveraged Kalman filtering 
for motion prediction, IOU matching for bounding box 
association, and the Hungarian algorithm [61][62] for 
efficient ID assignment. Despite SORT’s speed advantage, it 
struggled with issues such as occlusion and viewpoint

variations [63]. DeepSORT addresses these limitations by 
integrating deep appearance descriptors generated through 
convolutional 
neural 
networks 
(CNNs), 
significantly 
improving robustness and accuracy. These descriptors are 
represented as feature vectors, effectively distinguishing 
object appearances across different frames using cosine 
distance. This enhancement results in significantly fewer 
identity switches and robust tracking under challenging 
conditions, achieving real-time tracking performance at 
approximately 25–50 FPS on standard GPUs [20][60]. 
Synthetic occlusion masks overlay leaves and branches 
during training to teach the detector to recognize hidden 
fruits. During inference, DeepSORT smooths appearance 
descriptors, adjusts matching thresholds adaptively, and uses 
Kalman filter motion predictions to maintain identities 
through brief occlusions—reducing switches and improving 
counting. We rely on raw CNN embeddings with cosine-
distance matching and no extra L2 normalization.

For fruit counting, we implemented the Region of Interest 
(ROI) line-based counting technique combined with 
DeepSORT. As fruits cross a predefined ROI line, the system 
automatically increments the fruit count based on tracked 
object IDs, enabling precise, real-time fruit counting. This 
method is highly accurate, adaptable, and suitable for 
practical orchard monitoring scenarios involving moving 
cameras, making it effective for yield estimation and orchard 
management tasks. 
V. PROPOSED ARCHITECTURE 
While our framework leverages the proven strengths of 
YOLO-V7 and DeepSORT, its real innovation lies in the 
tailored enhancements and seamless integration we introduce 
specifically for agricultural environments. Unlike a 
straightforward concatenation, we implement (1) dynamic 
adaptive tracking, which automatically adjusts DeepSORT’s 
association parameters in response to orchard-specific 
challenges such as varying fruit density and movement; (2) 
an ROI-line counting module that translates tracked object 
crossings into robust, frame-accurate fruit counts; and (3) 
occlusion-aware data augmentation, where synthetic masks 
simulate leaf and branch overlaps during training to fortify 
detection under heavy foliage. Moreover, we optimize the 
entire pipeline for edge deployment—streamlining model 
complexity and enabling tight coupling with low-latency IoT 
platforms. These targeted modifications elevate our system 
beyond prior YOLO + SORT integrations, delivering a 
cohesive, field-ready solution for real-time fruit harvesting 
automation. 
The proposed model (Fig. 5) consists of two phases: 
Detection and Counting. In the Detection Phase, YOLO-V7 
is trained for 400 epochs (using pretrained weights) on a 
combined dataset of apple (DeepFruits) and mango 
(MangoYOLO) images, with over 100 hyperparameter 
configurations tested to select the best weights under varying 
lighting conditions. In the Counting Phase, DeepSORT 
assigns persistent IDs to each detected fruit across frames, 
while an ROI-line at 50% video height automatically 
increments the count whenever a fruit crosses it—providing 
reliable, real-time counting.

VIII. LIMITATIONS 
      This study acknowledges several limitations influencing 
the practical applicability and broader generalizability of the 
proposed model. First, the performance of the model heavily 
depends on the quality and diversity of training data, which 
might not encompass all real-world variations, such as 
additional fruit types, varying fruit sizes, or significantly 
different orchard layouts and environmental conditions. 
Consequently, this may restrict the model’s adaptability 
across diverse agricultural contexts, potentially leading to 
reduced accuracy and robustness when encountering 
previously 
unseen 
scenarios. 
Second, 
the 
current 
implementation 
requires 
high-performance 
computing 
resources and specialized camera setups, potentially limiting 
its scalability and accessibility for smaller farms or resource-
constrained environments. Finally, the neural network 
complexity introduces potential risks of overfitting, despite 
using data augmentation and regularization techniques. 
Addressing these limitations through future work—such as 
incorporating more varied datasets, validating performance 
across diverse fruit varieties and orchard conditions, 
optimizing the model for resource efficiency, and simplifying 
network architecture—can significantly enhance the model's 
versatility and practicality in agricultural automation.
