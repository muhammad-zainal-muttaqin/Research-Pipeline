Occlusion-Aware Apple Detection in Complex Orchard Canopies

Abstract
Accurate perception of occluded apples in tree canopies is important for precision orchard
management and robotic harvesting, but RGB-only vision is sensitive to leaf occlusion, fruit
overlap, and unstable illumination. This study presents OAFBC-Net, a lightweight RGB-
depth network for joint localization and occlusion-level recognition of apples classified as
non-occluded (NO), soft-occluded (SO), or hard-occluded (HO). OAFBC-Net integrates
three components: a Fourier amplitude-phase enhancement fusion module (FAPEF) for
cross-modal frequency-domain fusion, a weighted bidirectional feature pyramid (BiFPN)
for multi-scale interaction, and a Triple Context Guided Block (TCG) for local-to-global
semantic compensation. Using an orchard RGB-depth dataset with 3000 image pairs and
134,999 annotated instances acquired with an Intel RealSense D455 camera, OAFBC-Net
achieves AP50 values of 72.1%, 87.3%, and 57.9% for SO, NO, and HO, respectively. Under
the same split, it outperforms the self-implemented RGB-depth baselines. With FAPEF,
BiFPN, and TCG enabled, the macro-average mAP50 and mAP50–95 reach 72.4% and
55.2%, respectively. The model has 3.6 M parameters. On a single image, the total latency
is 11.3 ms, corresponding to an inference speed of 88.5 FPS. Three random seeds and
five-fold cross-validation with a fixed independent test set show consistent gains over the
dual-modal baseline. These findings support OAFBC-Net as a promising visual perception
method for occluded-fruit detection in orchards, while cross-site and field-robot validation
remain necessary for deployment-oriented claims.
Keywords: occluded apple detection; RGB-depth fusion; orchard canopy; frequency-domain
fusion; context modeling; precision horticulture; robotic harvesting
1. Introduction
Apples play an important role in the global fresh-fruit supply chain and orchard
economy [1]. As large-scale cultivation and intensive management continue to advance,
higher requirements are imposed on fruit quality, yield, and harvesting efficiency [2]. Field
operations still rely heavily on manual labor [3], while rising labor costs and structural labor
shortages constrain the long-term competitiveness and sustainable operation of the apple
industry [4]. At the same time, the increasing maturity of sensing technology, machine
vision, machine learning, and robot control in complex environments greatly accelerates the
transition of orchard robots and intelligent operation systems from experimental prototypes
to field validation and demonstration applications [5,6].

In applications such as mechanized harvesting, grading equipment, and picking robots,
stable and accurate perception of apples on trees is a key prerequisite for path planning
and operational decision-making [7]. Canopy occlusion and fruit overlap restrict the visible
regions of fruits and lead to incomplete boundaries, which reduces localization reliability.
These factors further affect the approach trajectory planning, grasping posture selection,
and obstacle-avoidance safety of end effectors, and they also weaken the consistency of fruit
counting and yield estimation [8]. However, in real orchard scenes, drastic illumination
changes and overlapping branches and leaves make the background highly complex.
In addition, the similarity between fruits and leaves in color and texture makes robust
detection and stable localization under occlusion still highly challenging [9].
Object detection simultaneously performs localization and category discrimination,
and it is a fundamental step in orchard fruit perception and robotic operation. Existing
methods can be divided into two-stage frameworks, which first generate candidate regions
and then perform classification and regression, and one-stage frameworks, which directly
conduct end-to-end regression. The latter offers higher inference efficiency and easier
deployment, with the YOLO series being a representative example [10,11]. Many studies
on agricultural fruit detection build datasets and models based on visible-light RGB images,
and they continuously optimize one-stage detection frameworks for complex orchard back-
grounds, scale variations, and edge deployment constraints. Qiu et al. [12] use YOLOv11n
as the baseline for orchard maturity recognition. While maintaining efficient end-to-end
inference, they introduce BiFPN to strengthen multi-scale feature fusion, adopt GhostConv
and pruning to compress the model size, and combine an improved IoU-based loss to en-
hance regression accuracy, thereby achieving lightweight deployment for apple localization
and binary maturity classification. Based on YOLOv8, Z. Liu et al. [13] propose Faster-
YOLO-AP for single-class apple detection. They address redundancy by constructing a
smaller-scale backbone and designing an efficient convolution module, PDWConv, together
with a replacement regression loss. Their method achieves a favorable accuracy-efficiency
balance in canopy scenes with low parameter and computational costs, confirming the
adaptability of lightweight one-stage models to orchard edge devices. To alleviate missed
detections caused by occlusion and overlap, Fu et al. [14] introduce a new feature scale and
a structural enhancement module into the YOLOv10 framework. By combining attention
mechanisms and localization loss improvement, they significantly improve the detection
performance for occluded apples while maintaining real-time inference on embedded
platforms. H. Yang et al. [15] focus on the difficulties of occlusion, overlap, and small-object
detection in high-density orchards. They systematically improve YOLOv7 from data pre-
processing, lightweight backbone design, and neck fusion structure to the detection head
and loss function, which significantly improves recognition performance under complex
illumination and occlusion conditions. Wang and He [16] address young fruit detection
before thinning by introducing channel pruning and transfer learning into YOLOv5s. Their
method greatly reduces model size and parameter count while maintaining high accuracy,
confirming the potential of RGB-based one-stage models for fast deployment on portable
terminals. Ma et al. [17] study apple detection across multiple growth stages and combine
YOLOv8n with ShuffleNetv2, Ghost modules, SE attention, and an improved regression
loss. Their method jointly improves accuracy, speed, and lightweight performance, and
it shows good real-time capability on edge devices. Han et al. [18] further integrate CNN
and GCN within the YOLOv8n framework. By jointly modeling local textures and global
relationships, they enhance robustness in severe weather and occlusion-overlap scenes
while preserving inference efficiency. Overall, existing studies show that RGB-based one-
stage detection frameworks represented by YOLO have high practical value for apple
target recognition. They also form a clear optimization path, namely improving detec-

tion accuracy and edge deployment efficiency in complex orchard environments through
multi-scale feature enhancement, lightweight structural reconstruction, attention or relation
modeling, and regression loss improvement. Nevertheless, in real scenes where canopy
occlusion, fruit overlap, and strong illumination fluctuations coexist, RGB perception still
mainly relies on appearance information. It is easily disturbed by shadows, highlights, and
fruit-leaf similarity, which leads to missed detections, false detections, and localization jitter.
Its limited ability to represent depth order and three-dimensional geometric relationships
also further affects the stability of end-effector approach, grasping, and obstacle-avoidance
decisions in picking robots [19]. Therefore, relying only on RGB is insufficient to continu-
ously satisfy the need for highly robust perception under complex canopy conditions, and
it is necessary to introduce multimodal schemes that can supplement spatial geometric
information [20].
To improve perception robustness in complex orchard environments, agricultural
vision research gradually shifts from single-sensor schemes to multi-sensor collaboration.
Existing studies show that multimodal information fusion can improve image representa-
tion quality, feature extraction capability, and detection accuracy to some extent. RGB-depth
can provide geometric structure cues by capturing distance and surface undulation infor-
mation. Tang et al. [21] construct a recognition-localization framework based on RGB-D
and improved SOLOv2. They use RGB images to extract semantic appearance features,
use depth to constrain spatial structure, and combine camera intrinsic parameters to solve
three-dimensional coordinates. Their method outperforms the original baseline in both
segmentation and localization metrics. Liu and Hao [22] propose YOLOfuse based on
YOLOv5s. Through dual-branch feature extraction, weighted fusion, coordinate attention,
and Soft-NMS, their method improves detection performance in dense overlapping scenes
and outperforms single-RGB input while maintaining real-time speed. Beyond RGB-D,
RGB-T enhances the separability between targets and backgrounds through temperature
differences. Cao et al. [23] combine RGB, depth, and thermal imaging information in
mango detection, improving target separation under illumination fluctuation and occlusion.
Lin et al. [24] adopt cold-stimulation thermal imaging for apple bruise detection, which
creates an observable temperature difference between bruised regions and healthy tissues
and significantly improves recall and detection accuracy. RGB-NIR and spectral fusion
supplement visible-light representation through narrow-band reflectance information.
Ünal et al. [25] find that NIR provides more stable representation of subcutaneous damage
under low-light and low-contrast conditions. Luo et al. [26] fuse RGB and NIR information
for citrus defect detection and achieve higher detection accuracy and recall in complex back-
grounds, with more evident improvement for small targets. In general, different modalities
have their own advantages and constraints in acquisition cost, environmental sensitivity,
and information complementarity. Thermal infrared and near-infrared or spectral modali-
ties are valuable in low-illumination or low-contrast tasks, but their performance is strongly
affected by imaging conditions and task scenarios. In contrast, the depth modality directly
provides spatial structure and relative distance information, making it more suitable for
alleviating geometric ambiguity under canopy occlusion, fruit overlap, and appearance
similarity.
Multimodal fusion is commonly divided into early, mid-level, and late fusion [27,28].
Early fusion completes cross-modal alignment and concatenation at the input layer, which
helps preserve fine-grained appearance and geometric details. However, it is more sensitive
to registration errors and sensor noise, and it may increase computational and bandwidth
costs [29]. Mid-level fusion conducts cross-modal feature interaction in the backbone or
neck network and is more likely to form joint representations for multimodal recognition
tasks [30]. It is therefore widely adopted in multimodal learning tasks, but its actual

effect depends heavily on feature alignment and the design of interaction modules [31].
Wang et al. [32] systematically compare pixel-level, feature-level, and decision-level strate-
gies for visible-infrared object recognition. They point out that feature-level fusion can
retain classification-related discriminative information while controlling computational
cost. They further use deep mutual learning to enable cross-modal feature interaction
and combine it with weighted decision-level fusion to produce final outputs.
Their
experiments show that this strategy improves recognition performance under visible-
light and infrared conditions. For honey origin traceability and component prediction,
Z. Liu et al. [33] compare data-level and feature-level fusion. Their results show that,
based on spectral preprocessing and task-oriented feature extraction such as partial least
squares, feature-level fusion achieves better classification and regression performance.
These studies jointly indicate that the benefits of mid-level fusion depend not only on
the fusion position, but also on cross-modal alignment, task-oriented feature extraction
and interaction mechanisms, and the matching between subsequent classifiers or fusion
strategies. If alignment is inconsistent or the interaction design is insufficient to describe
the complementarity of heterogeneous modalities, the fusion advantage becomes clearly
limited. Late fusion integrates the outputs after independent inference from different
modal branches. It is flexible in engineering implementation and has a low cost for module
replacement, but it is difficult to exploit fine-grained complementary information from
intermediate layers. Yang et al. [34] adopt a CNN-LSTM-MLP structure for harvest-stage
prediction of greenhouse melons. They separately model the RGB image branch, which
is based on YOLOv8n detection and morphological estimation, and the environmental or
irrigation time-series branch, and then complete fused prediction through an MLP after
high-level feature concatenation. The CPROS method proposed by Li et al. [35] trains and
infers visible-light and infrared branches separately, and fuses class probabilities and detec-
tion boxes at the output layer. This design reduces dependence on strict pixel alignment,
feature alignment, and large-scale paired data, and it facilitates combination across different
detection backbones. The authors also note that, in some small-sample cases or scenarios
with high requirements for fine-grained localization, its performance may be inferior to
methods that rely on intermediate feature interaction. In summary, for orchard scenes
with severe occlusion and illumination changes, the fusion strategy must balance accuracy,
robustness, and real-time performance to satisfy the requirement of robotic operations for
stable perception [36].
Building on the above discussion, this study targets robust joint localization and
harvesting-oriented occlusion-level recognition of apples (NO, SO, and HO) in complex
orchard canopies through mid-level RGB-depth fusion.
The aim of this research is to develop a lightweight RGB-depth detector, OAFBC-
Net, that improves occlusion-aware apple perception for orchard monitoring and robotic
harvesting while maintaining practical real-time inference.
To achieve this aim, the specific objectives are as follows: (1) To develop a lightweight
RGB-depth detection framework for joint apple localization and harvesting-oriented
occlusion-level classification in natural orchard scenes. (2) To investigate mid-level cross-
modal fusion and context-aware multi-scale representation strategies that improve per-
ception of partially occluded apples. (3) To validate the proposed approach on an orchard
RGB-depth dataset through comparisons with unimodal and dual-modal baselines, abla-
tion and cross-validation analyses, and supplementary assessments of runtime efficiency
and depth-sensor effects under varying illumination.
Based on these objectives, the main contributions are as follows: (1) OAFBC-Net, an
integrated RGB-depth detection framework for joint localization and NO/SO/HO classifi-
cation in complex orchard canopies. (2) FAPEF, a Fourier amplitude-phase enhancement

2. Materials and Methods
2.1. Overall Framework
To improve occlusion-aware apple detection in complex orchard scenes, this study pro-
poses OAFBC-Net (Figure 1). The network detects apple instances and assigns each detection
to one of three harvesting-oriented occlusion levels: non-occluded (NO), soft-occluded (SO),
or hard-occluded (HO). Frequency-domain fusion (FAPEF), contextual modeling (TCG), and
weighted multi-scale fusion (BiFPN) are integrated to improve robustness under branch and
leaf occlusion, fruit overlap, weak boundaries, and illumination variation.
 
Figure 1. Overview of OAFBC-Net.
First, the network introduces the Fourier amplitude-phase enhancement fusion
(FAPEF) module at the P4 and P5 feature maps. Each modal stream is first enhanced
and spatially stabilized by DyT; FAPEF then re-transforms the stabilized features into the
frequency domain, adaptively fuses the amplitude and principal-value phase spectra of
RGB and depth, reconstructs the complex spectrum, and maps the fused representation
back to the spatial domain through an orthonormal inverse frequency transform followed
by DyT.
Second, the Triple Context Guided Block (TCG Block) is applied to the FAPEF out-
puts at P4 and P5 to strengthen the semantic association between local visible regions
and surrounding contextual information. In occlusion scenes, visible apple regions are
often incomplete; the TCG Block supplements missing semantic cues by modeling spatial
relationships among apples, branches, leaves, adjacent fruits, and background regions,
thereby improving discrimination among NO, SO, and HO categories.
Third, the network adopts a weighted bidirectional multi-scale fusion structure, BiFPN,
in the neck to integrate P3, P4, and P5 features from different levels. BiFPN measures the
importance of features at different scales through learnable weights and builds both top-
https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy16141318

6 of 37
down and bottom-up information transfer paths, enabling sufficient fusion between shallow
detail features and deep semantic features.
Overall, OAFBC-Net integrates FAPEF, the TCG Block, and BiFPN into a unified
framework, enabling joint optimization of frequency-domain fusion, contextual semantic
modeling, and multi-scale feature interaction. The network finally outputs the spatial
location and occlusion level of each apple, providing a visual perception basis for target
selection, approach-direction planning, and picking-strategy formulation in picking robots.

2.3. BiFPN
To enhance the multi-scale detection ability for occluded apples in complex orchards,
this paper adopts the Bidirectional feature pyramid network, BiFPN, in the Neck of OAFBC-
Net [39]. BiFPN takes the RGB-depth fused P3, P4, and P5 features as inputs, where
P3 represents shallow fused features, P4 represents middle-level fused features, and P5
represents high-level fused features. The structural comparison is shown in Figure 4.
Compared with the traditional FPN-PAN network, which transfers information layer by
layer along adjacent pyramid levels and usually adopts simple addition or concatenation
for feature fusion, BiFPN introduces a bidirectional multi-path fusion mechanism. This
mechanism supports cross-layer skip connections and repeated stacking, allowing low-level
detail features and high-level semantic features to interact over multiple rounds. At the
same time, BiFPN adopts learnable weighted feature fusion, which automatically adjusts
the importance of features from different levels according to the task. This design makes
fuller use of multi-scale information and significantly improves the model’s perception
ability for small apples, overlapping apples, and apples with different occlusion levels.

2.4. Triple Context Guided Block (TCG Block)
In multimodal object detection, targets are often difficult to distinguish from their
surrounding environment because of scale variation, occlusion, and background texture
interference. Standard convolution with only a local receptive field is insufficient to capture
both fine-grained details and scene-level semantics. To address this issue, this paper
proposes the Triple Context Guided Block (TCG Block), inspired by CGNet [40]. The
core idea is to explicitly fuse three complementary types of context within the same basic
operator, namely local context, surrounding context captured by dilated convolution, and
global context captured by channel attention. These contextual cues are further integrated
into the CSP structure through a global residual form, so that the block preserves efficient
gradient flow in the backbone while capturing fine-grained edge information, spatially
related environmental cues, and channel-level semantic recalibration.
Specifically, as shown in Figure 5, the TCG Block serves as the core operator for
three-level context modeling. Given an input feature X ∈RB×C×H×W, a 1 × 1 convolution
is first used to compress the channel dimension and obtain X′. Two parallel depthwise
separable convolution branches are then applied to the compressed feature. The local
context branch floc(·) uses a standard 3 × 3 depthwise convolution to capture fine-grained
details within each pixel’s 3 × 3 neighborhood (the central pixel and its eight adjacent
pixels). The surrounding context branch fsur(·) uses a 3 × 3 depthwise dilated convolution
with dilation rate rd = 2, which expands the equivalent receptive field to 5 × 5 and models
a larger neighborhood spatially related to the target. The outputs of the two branches are
concatenated along the channel dimension and then fused by BatchNorm and the activation
function σ(·), which denotes the Sigmoid activation function, to obtain the joint feature, as
defined in Equation (20).

At the network level, the TCG Block uses the CSP topology of C3k2 as its outer
framework. The input feature map is first projected by a 1 × 1 convolution and split into
two branches along the channel dimension. One branch sequentially passes through n three-
context core operators described above. All original and intermediate outputs are then
concatenated along the channel dimension and fused by another 1 × 1 convolution, which
balances parameter efficiency and cross-layer gradient flow. Considering that shallow
features mainly contain fine-grained textures and have weaker demand for large receptive
fields and channel attention, while deep features have higher semantic abstraction and
require broader context to distinguish occluded targets or targets similar to the background,
this paper keeps the original C3k2 unit at the shallow P3 level and replaces it with the
TCG Block only at the deeper P4 and P5 levels, where C3k-style nesting is enabled. In this
way, the network fully aggregates local, surrounding, and global contexts at deeper layers
with lower resolution and larger channel dimensions, thereby strengthening pixel-level
discrimination in complex scenes while avoiding the extra computational cost caused by
redundant dilated convolution at shallow layers.

different models, the proposed OAFBC-Net and all comparison models are trained with

comparisons under RGB and depth input conditions, and ablation experiments on FAPEF,

3.1. Dataset
The Apple dataset is an orchard RGB-depth image dataset collected at the First Station
of the Taihang Mountain Road of Hebei Agricultural University in Shunping County,
Baoding City, Hebei Province, China. Images are acquired using an Intel RealSense D455
camera (Intel Corporation, Santa Clara, CA, USA), which captures paired RGB images and
depth maps at a resolution of 1280 × 720 pixels. Figure 7 shows representative visible-light
and depth images from the dataset.

This study follows the three-level occlusion classification standard for apple targets
used in previous research, which is designed for harvesting passability and grasping feasi-
bility. Apple targets are divided into three categories: No Occlusion (NO), Soft Occlusion
(SO), and Hard Occlusion (HO) [41]. As shown in Figure 8, NO indicates that the apple
target has a clear contour and a complete visible region, with little or no occlusion from
branches, leaves, or adjacent fruits. Such targets can usually be directly used for visual
localization and harvesting decisions. SO indicates that the apple is occluded by leaves,
but its main body remains relatively distinguishable, and the end effector still has a high
probability of successful picking after a slight push. HO indicates that the apple is severely
occluded by branches, orchard structures, or adjacent fruits, which increases the difficulty
of target localization and grasping decisions. Unlike conventional detection tasks that focus
only on fruit localization, this classification standard further extends detection to a pass-
ability assessment task for motion planning and grasping decisions in harvesting robots.
Therefore, the proposed model outputs not only the position coordinates of apple targets,
but also their corresponding occlusion and rigidity-related category information, namely
NO, SO, and HO, providing more task-oriented visual input for subsequent manipulator
path planning, grasp pose selection, and obstacle avoidance decisions. The annotated
instance categories are shown in Table 1.

3.4. Comparison Experiment
The comparison models are grouped into three categories. The first category includes
lightweight YOLO-series detectors, namely YOLOv5, YOLOv8, YOLOv10, and YOLOv11.
The second category includes classical detectors, including SSD, RetinaNet, and Faster
R-CNN. The third category includes multimodal YOLO-based baselines. To ensure fair
comparison under dual-modal input conditions, this study constructs RGB-depth fusion
versions of YOLOv5, YOLOv8, YOLOv10, and YOLOv11, denoted as YOLOv5-RGBD,
YOLOv8-RGBD, YOLOv10-RGBD, and YOLOv11-RGBD, respectively. These models are
self-implemented RGB-depth baselines based on the corresponding YOLO architectures,
not official YOLO variants. OAFBC-Net is evaluated as the proposed RGB-depth detection
method. Experiments are conducted under three input settings: RGB-only, depth-only, and
RGB + depth fusion. All models are compared under the same experimental conditions.
As shown in Table 4, the detection performance of different models varies clearly across
input modalities. For single-modal detection methods, the Only-RGB models consistently
outperformed the depth-only models. This is mainly because RGB images contain rich color,
texture, and edge information, which more effectively represents the appearance of apple
targets. In contrast, depth images provide spatial structural cues, but their texture details
and semantic representation are relatively limited when used alone, leading to a clear
decline in detection performance. Under the Only-RGB setting, Faster R-CNN achieved

3.6. Reproducibility and Cross-Validation Analysis
Although the preceding comparative experiments and ablation experiments are con-
ducted under fixed data splits and a fixed random seed, the training results of deep learning
detection models may still be affected by random initialization, the randomness of data
augmentation, and the partitioning strategy for the training and validation sets. To further
verify the reliability of the performance improvement achieved by the proposed model,
this paper supplements the main comparative experiments and ablation experiments
with multi-seed repeated experiments and five-fold cross-validation experiments. The
multi-seed experiments are used to analyze the effect of different random initializations
on model performance under unchanged data splits, while the five-fold cross-validation
experiments are used to analyze the effect of different training and validation splits on
model performance under a fixed random seed.
Considering that repeated training of all models would significantly increase the
experimental cost, this paper selects two representative models for stability validation,
namely YOLOv11-RGBD and OAFBC-Net. YOLOv11-RGBD is the RGB-D dual-modal
fusion baseline model constructed in this paper based on the YOLOv11 architecture, and
OAFBC-Net is the proposed occluded apple detection model. Through repeated exper-
iments on these two models, the influence of RGB-D dual-modal information and the
proposed feature enhancement modules on the stability of detection performance can be
further analyzed.
To verify whether the performance improvement of OAFBC-Net is caused by the
accidental effect of a specific random seed, this paper uses multiple random seeds to
conduct repeated model training. In this experiment, the training, validation, and test
splits remain unchanged, and only the random seed in the training process is changed.
Specifically, three independent training runs are conducted with seed = 0, seed = 42, and
seed = 2026.
During each training run, the random seeds of Python random, NumPy, and PyTorch
are set to the same value. The experimental conditions, including input image size, number
of training epochs, optimizer, learning rate strategy, batch size, data augmentation method,
and evaluation metrics, are kept consistent. In this way, the differences among experimental
results can be attributed mainly to random initialization and training randomness, rather
than differences in experimental configurations.

This paper records the Precision, Recall, mAP50, and mAP50-95 of the two models
under different random seeds, and further calculates the mean and standard deviation of
the results obtained from the three random seeds. The mean reflects the overall detection
performance of the model under different random initialization conditions, while the
standard deviation measures the fluctuation of the training results. If a model maintains
high detection accuracy with a small standard deviation under multiple random seeds, its
performance improvement can be considered reproducible rather than an accidental result
caused by a specific random seed.
As shown in Table 6, OAFBC-Net exhibits good stability in Precision, Recall, mAP50,
and mAP50-95 under three different random seeds, with average values of 64.8 ± 0.5%,
70.9 ± 0.7%, 72.1 ± 0.5%, and 55.0 ± 0.3%, respectively. Compared with YOLOv11-RGBD,
OAFBC-Net improves the average Precision, Recall, mAP50, and mAP50-95, among which
mAP50 and mAP50-95 increase by 6.6 and 7.0 percentage points, respectively. This indicates
that the proposed method achieves better detection performance under different random
initialization conditions. In terms of standard deviation, all metrics of OAFBC-Net show
small fluctuations. In particular, the standard deviations of mAP50 and mAP50-95 are 0.5%
and 0.3%, respectively, indicating that the model has good consistency in overall detection
accuracy and localization performance under multiple IoU thresholds. By contrast, the
mAP50 and mAP50-95 of YOLOv11-RGBD are 65.5 ± 0.6% and 48.0 ± 0.1%, respectively,
suggesting that although YOLOv11-RGBD also shows a certain degree of stability, its overall
detection performance is clearly lower than that of OAFBC-Net. Notably, while improving
detection accuracy, OAFBC-Net still maintains low standard deviations in Precision, Recall,
and mAP50, which indicates that the proposed RGB-D feature fusion and occlusion feature
enhancement strategies do not increase the sensitivity of the model to random initialization.
Overall, the multi-seed experimental results show that the performance improvement of
OAFBC-Net is not accidental, but is stable, reproducible, and consistently advantageous
under different random seeds.
Considering that the test set should remain as independent as possible, this paper
keeps the original independent test set unchanged during five-fold cross-validation and
only repartitions the development dataset formed by the original training and validation
sets. Specifically, the original training set and validation set are first merged to form
the development dataset, which is then divided into five non-overlapping subsets. In
each experimental round, four subsets are selected as the training set, and the remaining
subset is used as the validation set. After model training is completed, the model weights
with the best performance on the current validation set are selected and evaluated on the
independent test set, which remains unchanged throughout the process. This procedure is
repeated five times until each subset is used once as the validation set.
To analyze the influence of data partitioning on model performance as independently
as possible, seed = 0 is uniformly adopted in the five-fold cross-validation experiments. In
addition, the input image size, number of training epochs, optimizer, learning rate strategy,
batch size, data augmentation method, and evaluation metrics are kept consistent with
those in the main comparative experiments for all folds.
As shown in Table 7, OAFBC-Net outperforms YOLOv11-RGBD in Precision, Recall,
mAP50, and mAP50-95 in the five-fold cross-validation experiments. The average gains
are 5.5, 1.8, 5.4, and 5.2 percentage points, respectively. OAFBC-Net also shows smaller
standard deviations than YOLOv11-RGBD across all metrics. In particular, the standard
deviations of Recall, mAP50, and mAP50-95 are 0.2%, 0.8%, and 0.7%, respectively, which
are lower than the corresponding values of 0.9%, 2.0%, and 1.3% for YOLOv11-RGBD.
These results indicate lower sensitivity to data partitioning and better reproducibility
within the tested dataset and protocol.

3.7. Inference Efficiency Analysis
Parameter count and GFLOPs alone cannot fully support operational viability claims
for OAFBC-Net, because FAPEF performs multiple two-dimensional fast Fourier transform
(FFT) and inverse fast Fourier transform (IFFT) operations inside deep feature tensor channels.
Depending on the deployment platform, such frequency-domain operations may introduce
computational bottlenecks or memory-transfer latency. To address this concern, this section
reports measured inference efficiency for all eight ablation configurations in Table 5 under an
identical runtime protocol. The goal is to verify whether FAPEF prevents real-time inference
and to provide empirical support for the lightweight design of OAFBC-Net.
All latency tests were conducted on the experimental platform described in Section 3.2
and Table 2, using an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4070 Laptop GPU (8 GB), Intel Core i9-
14900HX CPU, Windows 11, Python 3.10.19, PyTorch 2.8.0, and CUDA 12.6. At present,
edge hardware such as NVIDIA Jetson devices are not available in this study. Therefore,
the reported results should be interpreted as desktop/laptop GPU measurements rather
than as direct evidence of embedded orchard deployment. Validation on edge platforms,
including Jetson-based field perception systems, will be conducted in future work.
The eight ablation models in Table 5 were selected because they share the same RGB-
depth input setting, training protocol, and overall detector framework, which enables
controlled analysis of the runtime impact of FAPEF, BiFPN, and the TCG Block. Model 1
serves as the dual-modal baseline without any proposed module. Models 2–4 introduce
FAPEF, BiFPN, and the TCG Block individually. Models 5–7 represent pairwise combi-
nations, and Model 8 is the complete OAFBC-Net. This design allows direct comparison
between Model 8 and Model 7 to isolate the runtime effect of FAPEF, while Model 1 and
Model 2 further quantify the overhead of frequency-domain fusion alone.
For each model, inference efficiency was measured with paired RGB-depth inputs
at a resolution of 640 × 640 pixels and batch size = 1. Each model was warmed up for
50 iterations and then evaluated over 1000 consecutive runs. The reported preprocessing
time, inference time, and postprocessing time denote the mean per-image latency. Total
latency was computed as the sum of these three terms, and FPS was calculated as 1000/Total
latency. All tests used the same trained weights as in the ablation experiments in Section 3.5.
Table 8 summarizes the measured runtime results. Although FAPEF introduces FFT/IFFT
operations at the P4 and P5 stages, these transforms are applied only to lower-resolution se-
mantic feature maps rather than to the highest-resolution P3 layer, which limits the additional
computational and memory-transfer cost of the frequency-domain branch.

As shown in Table 8, the complete OAFBC-Net, Model 8, achieves the highest inference
efficiency among all ablation configurations on the RTX 4070 Laptop GPU. Its total latency
is 11.3 ms per image, corresponding to 88.5 FPS. Model 8 also uses the fewest parameters,

comparison, the baseline Model 1 requires 13.4 ms per image and reaches 74.6 FPS, while
using 5.0 M parameters and 11.5 GFLOPs. Relative to Model 1, the full model reduces
total latency by 15.7% and increases throughput by 18.6%, while simultaneously improving
detection accuracy across all occlusion levels reported in Table 5. These results indicate
that the proposed modules do not prevent real-time inference on the tested desktop GPU.
Instead, the final architecture achieves a better balance among detection accuracy, model
size, and runtime performance than the baseline dual-modal detector.
The results also directly address the concern that FFT/IFFT operations in FAPEF
may become a computational bottleneck. When FAPEF is introduced alone, Model 2
reduces total latency from 13.4 ms for Model 1 to 12.4 ms, and increases throughput from
74.6 FPS to 80.6 FPS, despite the addition of frequency-domain fusion. More importantly,
Model 8 and Model 7 share BiFPN and the TCG Block and differ only in the presence of
FAPEF. Under the tested setting, the full model with FAPEF requires 11.3 ms per image
and achieves 88.5 FPS; whereas, the variant without FAPEF requires 12.0 ms per image
and achieves 83.3 FPS. Therefore, the FFT/IFFT operations in FAPEF do not dominate
end-to-end latency and do not create a measurable runtime bottleneck relative to the closest
ablated counterpart. This finding is consistent with the efficiency-oriented placement of
FAPEF at the lower-resolution P4 and P5 stages described in Section 2.2.
The ablation results further reveal module-specific runtime behaviors. When the TCG
Block is introduced alone, Model 4 achieves a total latency of 11.4 ms per image and a
throughput of 87.7 FPS, which is the highest among the single-module variants. When
FAPEF is combined with the TCG Block, Model 6 maintains a competitive total latency of
11.8 ms per image and a throughput of 84.8 FPS. By contrast, BiFPN increases inference cost
more noticeably when combined with FAPEF. Model 5, which integrates FAPEF and BiFPN
without the TCG Block, requires 13.5 ms per image and reaches 74.1 FPS, making it the
slowest configuration in Table 8. However, when BiFPN is integrated together with FAPEF
and the TCG Block in the complete OAFBC-Net, Model 8 reduces total latency to 11.3 ms
per image and achieves 88.5 FPS. This suggests that the three modules are jointly optimized
in OAFBC-Net and that runtime performance should be evaluated at the complete-system
level rather than from an isolated FAPEF implementation alone.
In summary, the inference efficiency experiments support the operational viability
of OAFBC-Net on the current RTX 4070 Laptop GPU platform. The complete model
achieves 88.5 FPS with 3.6 M parameters and 10.2 GFLOPs, and the measured latencies
do not indicate that FAPEF-related FFT/IFFT operations become the dominant runtime
bottleneck in the tested dual-branch topology. Nevertheless, these conclusions are limited
to the desktop/laptop GPU used in this study. Because edge devices such as NVIDIA
Jetson platforms were not available during this revision, future work will extend the same
inference protocol to embedded hardware and further evaluate TensorRT or quantization-
based deployment for orchard edge perception.

As shown in Figure 14, the visible-light single-modal YOLOv11n has good recognition
ability for the No Occlusion category, with a correct classification ratio of 0.75. However,
the correct classification ratios for Soft Occlusion and Hard Occlusion are 0.61 and 0.47, re-

of 0.26, 0.71, and 0.14 for the three apple categories, respectively, showing weaker overall
performance. This suggests that depth noise, holes, and boundary discontinuity make it
difficult to distinguish occlusion levels when depth images are used alone. In contrast,
OAFBC-Net achieves correct classification ratios of 0.69, 0.77, and 0.57 for Soft Occlusion,
No Occlusion, and Hard Occlusion, respectively. For Soft Occlusion and Hard Occlusion,
OAFBC-Net improves over the visible-light model by 0.08 and 0.10, respectively, indicating
that RGB-depth fusion improves occlusion-level discrimination.
Overall, the confusion matrices in Figure 14 show that OAFBC-Net achieves higher
classification accuracy and lower category confusion for non-occluded, soft-occluded, and
hard-occluded apples. The advantage is most evident for Hard Occlusion. These results
suggest that the multimodal fusion strategy and feature enhancement modules improve
robustness in complex occlusion scenes and may provide useful visual perception results for
target localization, occlusion assessment, and grasping decisions in orchard picking robots.
3.9. Analysis of Depth Sensor Effects Under Direct Natural Illumination
The Intel RealSense D455 utilized in this study is an active stereo depth camera that
estimates depth through infrared pattern projection and stereo matching. This category
of sensors is highly sensitive to two factors in outdoor orchard environments, which are
sunlight degradation, where direct sunlight saturates the infrared receiver and invalidates
projected patterns to reduce the valid pixel ratio in depth maps, and organic leaf structural
voids, where thin organic structures such as leaves often manifest as fragmented, miss-
ing, and discontinuous boundaries rather than intact continuous surfaces due to surface
reflection, translucency, and complex microstructures. Since OAFBC-Net relies on the
cross-modal fusion of RGB and depth data, such hardware-level depth invalidation can
directly affect the detection performance of the Hard Occlusion category.
On the full test set, OAFBC-Net achieves AP50 scores of 87.3%, 72.1%, and 57.9% for
the No Occlusion, Soft Occlusion, and Hard Occlusion categories, respectively, as presented
in Table 4. Evidently, the performance for the Hard Occlusion category is substantially
lower than that for the other two categories, revealing a significant performance gap. It is
necessary to further differentiate the extent to which this gap stems from insufficient model
learning of severely occluded targets versus depth observation failure of the D455 under
https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy16141318

Agronomy 2026, 16, 1318
31 of 37
direct light and organic leaf structural voids. To address this, this section selects 300 Direct
Sunlight images and 300 Non-direct or Diffuse images from the independent test set of the
complete dataset based on illumination conditions to construct an illumination-stratified
subset. Statistical analysis is then conducted at both the depth quality and detection
performance levels to quantify the contribution of sensor limitations to the Hard Occlusion
performance gap.
Depth quality metrics are computed on the raw depth maps to reflect the hardware
observation failure of the RealSense D455. The image-level Valid Depth Ratio, denoted as
VDR, is defined as the proportion of valid pixels in the raw depth map relative to the full
image. The bounding-box Invalid Depth Ratio, denoted as IDR, is defined as the proportion
of invalid pixels within each ground-truth bounding box relative to the total box pixels,
with mean values calculated separately for the No Occlusion, Soft Occlusion, and Hard
Occlusion categories. Invalid pixels are identified as those with a depth value of 0 in the
depth map.
Table 9 presents the depth quality statistics for the two groups. The mean VDR of the
Direct Sunlight group is 47.86%, which is lower than the 52.12% of the Non-direct or Diffuse
group, indicating a decline in full-image depth validity under direct light conditions. At the
target level, the mean IDR within Hard Occlusion bounding boxes increases from 39.41%
under Non-direct or Diffuse conditions to 54.73% under Direct Sunlight, representing
an increase of 15.32 percentage points. The increases in IDR within Soft Occlusion and
No Occlusion bounding boxes are 6.78 and 4.86 percentage points, respectively. Both
groups satisfy the condition IDRNO < IDRSO < IDRHO, which demonstrates that depth
invalidation is most severe in Hard Occlusion target regions and that this effect is further
amplified under Direct Sunlight conditions. This implies that the sensitivity of the D455
to sunlight degradation and organic leaf structural voids is most pronounced in the Hard
Occlusion category.

is favorable. In addition, compared to the RGB-only baseline, OAFBC-Net achieves a Hard
Occlusion performance gain of 8.3 percentage points under Direct Sunlight and a larger
gain of 13.7 percentage points under Non-direct or Diffuse conditions. This indicates that
the model derives greater benefits from cross-modal fusion as depth quality improves.
By synthesizing the data from Tables 9 and 10, the underperformance of the Hard
Occlusion category relative to the No Occlusion and Soft Occlusion categories can be
attributed to three interconnected factors. The first factor is the intrinsic difficulty of the
task. Even under favorable Non-direct or Diffuse conditions, the Hard Occlusion AP50
of OAFBC-Net remains at 61.8%, which is considerably lower than the 88.7% achieved
for the No Occlusion category. This suggests that severe occlusion inherently constitutes
a detection bottleneck that is not exclusively caused by sensor or model limitations. The
second factor involves the hardware constraints of the RealSense D455 camera. Under
Direct Sunlight, the Valid Depth Ratio decreases and the Invalid Depth Ratio within Hard
Occlusion bounding boxes increases significantly, which corresponds to a 7.3 percentage
point drop in the Hard Occlusion AP50 for OAFBC-Net. In contrast, the RGB-only model
remains largely unaffected. This contrast proves that a substantial portion of the Hard
Occlusion performance gap is related to depth invalidation caused by direct light and
organic leaf structural voids rather than purely inadequate network design. The third
factor is the fusion capability of the model. OAFBC-Net consistently outperforms the
RGB-only model in Hard Occlusion detection under both illumination conditions, with
a more pronounced fusion gain under Non-direct or Diffuse lighting. This confirms that
modules such as FAPEF can successfully extract useful geometric information when depth
data is partially valid. However, the model cannot completely compensate for hardware
observation deficits when the depth information within a bounding box is extensively
invalid. Consequently, the overall Hard Occlusion AP50 of 57.9% on the complete test set,
which is significantly lower than that of the other two categories, should be interpreted
as the combined result of intrinsic occlusion difficulty, outdoor depth observation failures
of the D455 camera, and the upper bounds of network fusion capabilities. It should
not be attributed solely to insufficient model capacity. The statistical results obtained
from the illumination-stratified subsets provide a firm quantitative foundation for this
comprehensive attribution.
4. Discussion
OAFBC-Net is developed for occlusion-aware apple detection in semi-structured
orchard canopies, where RGB-only methods often miss or mislocalize partially hidden
fruit. On the single-site RGB-depth dataset, integrating FAPEF, BiFPN, and TCG improves
https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy16141318

Agronomy 2026, 16, 1318
33 of 37
AP50 for hard-occluded apples compared with concatenation-based RGB-depth baselines,
suggesting that frequency-domain cross-modal fusion and context modeling help disam-
biguate canopy clutter. These conclusions are limited to the reported orchard location,
imaging setup, and annotation protocol. Cross-orchard, cross-cultivar, and field-robot trials
are still required before operational readiness for autonomous harvesting can be claimed.
Compared with other object detection algorithms, OAFBC-Net achieves better overall
performance in the occluded apple detection task. In the comparative experiments among
RGB-only, depth-only, and RGB + depth detection methods, OAFBC-Net achieves AP50 val-
ues of 72.1%, 87.3%, and 57.9% for soft-occluded, non-occluded, and hard-occluded apples,
respectively. In particular, for hard-occluded apple detection, OAFBC-Net shows a clear
advantage over the self-implemented multimodal YOLO baselines. These results indicate
that frequency-domain enhancement, context modeling, and multi-scale fusion improve the
ability of the model to recognize occluded targets in this dataset. Meanwhile, OAFBC-Net
has only 3.6 M parameters, indicating a lightweight structure with competitive accuracy.
Model stability is further examined through multi-seed experiments and five-fold
cross-validation. The multi-seed experiments show that OAFBC-Net maintains stable
detection performance under different random initialization conditions, indicating that
its performance gain is not tied to a single random seed. The five-fold cross-validation
results show consistent performance under different training and validation splits. These
results provide additional evidence for reproducibility and robustness to data partitioning,
while remaining limited to the collected dataset and fixed test set. They also indicate
that FAPEF, BiFPN, and TCG improve detection accuracy without obvious increases in
training-result instability.
Although the proposed method performs well on the apple RGB-depth multimodal
occlusion detection dataset, it still has several limitations. First, the data are mainly collected
from specific orchard environments. Although the dataset contains different illumination
conditions, occlusion types, and complex backgrounds, the generalization ability of the
model across different regions, apple varieties, tree structures, and growth stages still
requires validation. Second, depth images are affected by sensor noise, holes, strong light
interference, and acquisition-distance changes. When the spatial depth difference between
fruits and branches or leaves is small, the model may still produce missed or false detections.
Section 3.9 further shows that direct sunlight increases Invalid Depth Ratios in Hard
Occlusion bounding boxes and corresponds to a measurable Hard Occlusion AP50 drop of
7.3 percentage points for OAFBC-Net, indicating that part of the HO performance gap is
associated with RealSense D455 depth invalidation under outdoor active stereo sensing
rather than with network design alone. In addition, although OAFBC-Net is lightweight
in parameter size, Section 3.7 shows that the complete model achieves a total latency of
11.3 ms per image and 88.5 FPS on the NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4070 Laptop GPU under batch
size = 1 and input size 640 × 640 pixels. These results support real-time inference on the
tested desktop GPU platform. However, deployment efficiency and stability on low-power
embedded platforms, mobile terminals, and real picking robot systems, including NVIDIA
Jetson devices, still require further validation. In more complex natural environments, such
as rain, fog, strong backlight, nighttime operation, severe branch and leaf occlusion, and
dense fruit clusters, model robustness still needs improvement.
Future work will explore pruning, knowledge distillation, model quantization, and
inference acceleration to reduce the computational complexity and storage cost of OAFBC-
Net and improve its real-time detection ability on embedded devices and orchard picking
robot platforms. To improve generalization in complex orchard environments, the RGB-
depth apple occlusion detection dataset will be expanded with samples from different
orchards, varieties, weather conditions, illumination conditions, and growth stages. Future
https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy16141318

research will also consider additional modalities, such as near-infrared, thermal infrared,
or multispectral information, fused with RGB and depth data to improve target detection
under low illumination, severe occlusion, and complex backgrounds. Finally, OAFBC-
Net will be integrated with 3D localization, picking-point estimation, robotic-arm path
planning, and obstacle-avoidance control to evaluate its practical value in autonomous
orchard picking tasks.
5. Conclusions
This paper proposes OAFBC-Net, an RGB-depth multimodal detection network for
occluded apple detection and occlusion-level recognition in complex orchard environments.
The method aims to improve the detection accuracy and robustness of non-occluded,
soft-occluded, and hard-occluded apple targets in natural orchards. To address missed
detections, false detections, and unstable localization that commonly occur in RGB-only
detection under illumination variation, branch and leaf occlusion, fruit overlap, and back-
ground similarity, this paper introduces depth images as a supplement to spatial geometric
information and enhances the perception of occluded apples through cross-modal fea-
ture fusion.
In the feature fusion stage, this paper designs the FAPEF Fourier amplitude-phase
enhancement fusion module. FAPEF maps RGB and depth features into the frequency
domain and uses amplitude and phase information to enhance fruit edge, texture, con-
tour, and spatial-structure representations. In the multi-scale feature fusion stage, BiFPN
achieves adaptive fusion among features at different scales through learnable weights
and strengthens the interaction between shallow detail information and deep semantic
information. TCG jointly models local, surrounding, and global contexts to supplement
missing semantic cues under occlusion and improve the discrimination of soft-occluded
and hard-occluded apples.
The experimental results show that OAFBC-Net achieves strong performance on the
apple RGB-depth occlusion detection dataset. Compared with the YOLO series, SSD,
RetinaNet, Faster R-CNN, and several multimodal YOLO baselines under the tested set-
tings, OAFBC-Net achieves AP50 values of 72.1%, 87.3%, and 57.9% for soft-occluded,
non-occluded, and hard-occluded apples, respectively. Ablation experiments show that
FAPEF, BiFPN, and TCG each contribute to model performance. When the three modules
are used together, Precision, Recall, mAP50, and mAP50-95 reach 64.8%, 71.7%, 72.4%,
and 55.2%, respectively, while the parameter count is reduced to 3.6 M compared with
the baseline. The visualization results also show that OAFBC-Net focuses more accurately
on apple target regions and reduces some missed and false detections under complex
occlusion conditions.
The multi-seed experiments and five-fold cross-validation results show that OAFBC-
Net maintains stable performance under different random initialization and data partition-
ing conditions. This indicates that the performance improvement has good reproducibility
within the tested dataset and protocol. The inference efficiency experiments in Section 3.7
further show that the complete model achieves 88.5 FPS with 3.6 M parameters on the
RTX 4070 Laptop GPU, indicating that FAPEF-related FFT/IFFT operations do not prevent
real-time inference on the tested desktop platform. The illumination-stratified analysis in
Section 3.9 further indicates that Hard Occlusion performance degradation is associated
with D455 depth invalidation under direct sunlight, in addition to task difficulty and fusion
limitations. These results further support the effectiveness of RGB-depth cross-modal
fusion and occlusion feature enhancement in complex orchard scenes.
In summary, OAFBC-Net provides an effective RGB-depth fusion method for occluded
apple detection in complex orchard environments and offers a technical reference for high-
https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy16141318

Agronomy 2026, 16, 1318
35 of 37
precision visual perception in orchard picking robots. The method improves occluded-
target detection while considering lightweight model design, and the stability, runtime, and
illumination-stratified attribution experiments in Sections 3.7 and 3.9 support its application
potential on the tested desktop GPU and clarify the respective roles of task difficulty, D455
depth invalidation, and network fusion in Hard Occlusion detection. Future work should
extend the same inference protocol to embedded hardware such as NVIDIA Jetson platforms,
and combine the method with model compression, multimodal expansion, and robotic closed-
loop control to support more stable and efficient automated orchard picking.
