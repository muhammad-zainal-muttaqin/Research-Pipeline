Abstract—The challenge of accurately ripeness level 
classifying from fresh oil palm fruit, this research investigated 
the problems and methods that were used in ripeness level 
classification. The goal of this research is to present a method 
for classifying the ripeness levels of oil palm fruits using deep 
learning techniques applied to image data. This research applies 
a deep learning model for classifying the ripeness level of fresh 
oil palm fruit and aiming for real-time application or mobile 
usage. The proposed model, Yolov9n (nano), adapts principles 
from convolutional neural networks that was tuned the learning 
parameters consist of the Adam optimizer, a learning rate of 
0.001, and a batch size of 32, result in the most accurate ripeness 
level classification model. The research demonstrates the 
Yolov9n (nano) improved model with optimized parameters to 
achieve the highest accuracy. Training and validation datasets 
represent precision of 99.4%, recall of 99.6%, mean average 
precision at 50% (mAP50) of 99.5%, and mAP50-95 of 92.6%. 
These findings confirm the model's effectiveness in accurately 
classifying the ripeness level of oil palm fruit from images. The 
study provides guidance for the practical deep learning 
implementation of ripeness level classification on fresh oil palm 
imaging for efficient harvesting by farmers cultivating oil palm. 
Keywords—Fresh oil palm, ripeness classification, deep 
learning, convolutional neural network, YOLO 
I. INTRODUCTION  
Oil palm was introduced to Thailand in 1929, with early 
cultivation in Songkhla and Chanthaburi provinces. The first 
commercial plantation was established in Prik Subdistrict, 
Sadao District, Songkhla Province [1]. Currently, Thailand's 
oil palm plantations are concentrated in the southern region, 
accounting for 87.69% of the total cultivation area. Surat 
Thani, Krabi, and Chumphon provinces collectively represent 
nearly 58% of this area. This expansion aligns with Thailand’s 
Renewable Energy and Alternative Energy Plan, driving a 
1.99% increase in cultivated land from 6.03 million rai in 2021 
to 6.15 million rai in 2022 [2]. Commercial oil palm varieties 
are classified into three types: (1) Virescens, with green 
immature fruits that turn orange when ripe; (2) Nigrescens, 
with immature fruits exhibiting a black apex and ivory base 
that ripen to red; and (3) Albescens, a rare variety with pale 
yellow skin when ripe. Despite these classifications, ripeness 
determination is traditionally performed via visual inspection, 
a method susceptible to errors due to factors such as operator 
experience, lighting, and the height of palm trees [4].

Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have significantly 
improved the accuracy of oil palm ripeness classification from 
object detection in images. Object detection models, a subset 
of CNNs, have proven highly effective in automating ripeness 
classification through image-based analysis. [5-7]. These 
models are broadly divided into two categories: two-stage    
and single-stage detectors. Two-stage models, including         
R-CNN, Fast R-CNN, and Faster R-CNN, achieve high 
accuracy by generating regions of interest (ROIs) before 
classification. However, these methods are computationally 
intensive. In contrast, Single-stage models, including YOLO 
(You Only Look Once) and SSD, combine ROI generation 
and classification into a single step, offering faster 
performance suitable for real-time applications [8].   
This research will focus on the ability of YOLO models 
that has revolutionized the field of object detection with its 
unique approach, contrasting with traditional methods that are 
complex and involve multiple steps. This model operates in a 
single forward pass, dividing the image into a grid and 
simultaneously predicting both bounding boxes and object 
categories within each grid cell. This results in faster 
processing and higher accuracy. Since its inception in 2015, 
the YOLO framework has undergone extensive evolution, 
with significant milestones from YOLOv1 to YOLOv10, each 
introducing innovative features [9-10]. The initial version of 
YOLO employed a groundbreaking single-stage architecture 
that simultaneously predicted bounding boxes and class 
probabilities, significantly enhancing inference speed for real-
time applications. This approach significantly improved 
inference speed, enabling real-time object detection, though it 
traded off some accuracy for efficiency. And then, this model 
improved on its predecessor by introducing Batch 
Normalization, anchor boxes, and the Darknet-19 backbone, 
enhancing both speed and accuracy. This version also 
supported higher-resolution input for detecting smaller 
objects. In addition, this model focused on balancing speed 
and accuracy, integrating the CSPDarknet53 backbone, spatial 
pyramid pooling, and advanced techniques such as Mosaic

II. METHODOLOGY 
This research aims to achieve precise and dependable 
classification of the ripeness levels of fresh oil palm fruits. 
This will help minimize errors in the harvesting process and 
the sorting of fruit at palm oil extraction plants. The research 
utilizes an algorithm designed for real-time use, or one that 
can be implemented on mobile devices, to ensure its practical 
applicability in future field conditions. The researchers have 
selected YOLO model for its efficiency and effectiveness in 
image classification tasks. The research methodology is 
structured into four main phases as outlined below. 
A. Data Acquisition 
The data used in this research comprises images of fresh 
oil palm fruit, obtained from the Science Data Bank [11]. This 
dataset was created and stored for use by researchers and 
developers interested for building the deep learning models. 
The goal is to aid in the accurate and reliable classification of 
ripeness levels of fresh oil palm fruits. The original dataset 
contains six categories of ripeness, namely: Unripe, Under-
ripe, Ripe, Over-ripe, Empty Bunch, and Abnormal FFB 
(Fresh Fruit Bunch). 
This dataset consists of sequential images extracted from 
video footage of fresh oil palm bunches, totaling 4,160 images 
with a resolution of 416x416 pixels. These images have been 
labeled with their respective ripeness categories. The dataset 
includes two types of images that consist of the images of 
fresh oil palm bunches with a single uniform ripeness level 
and the images containing fresh oil palm bunches with varying 
ripeness levels. The detail image dataset is detailed in Table 1.  
B. Data Preparation 
The researchers utilized a dataset of fresh oil palm bunch 
images that had been properly labeled, which served as the 
original image data for the study. Data preparation was 
conducted using the Roboflow application, a platform 
designed to manage and enhance image data for training 
computer vision models. This program offers various features 
that facilitate the efficient preparation of image data for model 
training. The process involved the following steps: 
1) Data Preprocessing

the researchers employed the Isolate Objects method, which 
segments the oil palm bunches into individual images, each 
representing a single bunch. This process was applied to the 
original dataset, which consisted of images containing 
multiple oil palm bunches in a single frame, as shown in Fig. 
1. Additionally, the researchers modified the classification 
scheme, selecting only three ripeness categories out of the six 
available: Unripe, Ripe, and Over-ripe. These categories were 
chosen because they are more  easily  distinguishable  by  the

2) Data Augmentations 
Data augmentation is a technique used to generate diverse 
and increased amounts of image data. In this research, the 
researchers employed various augmentation methods, 
including image flipping, cropping, rotation, brightness 
adjustment, and the introduction of noise. These methods 
were used to amplify the diversity of the training data, thereby 
strengthening the model’s robustness and generalization 
power. 
3) Split Data 
For the data preparation process, the dataset used for this 
research consists of a total of 14,477 images. The dataset is 
divided into three subsets: the training dataset, which includes 
11,900 images; the validation dataset, consisting of 1,782 
images; and the testing dataset, which contains 975 images, as 
detailed in Table 2.

D. Model Evaluation 
The performance of object classification can be evaluated 
using various metrics, as follows: 
a) Confusion Matrix 
 
    The confusion matrix is a tool for evaluating model 
predictions made by the trained model. It displays how closely 
the predicted outcomes align with the true values, by 
comparing them to the actual labels. It includes: True Positive 
(TP), where the model correctly predicts data as Positive; 
False Positive (FP), where the model incorrectly predicts data 
as Positive; True Negative (TN), where the model correctly 
predicts data as Negative; and False Negative (FN), where the 
model incorrectly predicts data as Negative. 
b) Precision 
    Precision is a performance metric used to evaluate the 
effectiveness of an object classification model. It measures the 
accuracy of predictions when an object is classified as 
belonging to the positive class. Precision is computed by 
dividing the number of True Positives (TP) by the sum of True 
Positives (TP) and False Positives (FP). The value of precision 
ranges from 0 to 1, with higher values indicating more 
accurate predictions for the positive class.

III. EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS 
This research was conducted using Python and the 
Ultralytics library on a Google Colab environment equipped 
with a T4 GPU. The researcher selected the initial weights of 
each YOLOv9 by using pretrained weights that were pre-
trained on the COCO test-dev 2017 dataset. Several 
hyperparameters were meticulously tuned during the 
experiments to enhance the optimization of the model's 
performance performance. 
A. Optimization and Customization of the YOLOv9n Model 
In this research, the model was fine-tuned by adjusting 
various hyperparameters to analyze and select the most 
efficient YOLOv9n configuration for classifying the ripeness 
levels of fresh oil palm fruits. The optimizer is an algorithm 
used in the training process of a model. The primary goal of 
the optimizer is to calibrate the model’s parameters to ensure 
the minimization of the loss function, thereby improving the 
accuracy of predictions. Additionally, the optimizer impacts 
the speed and efficiency of model training. In this research, 
two different optimizers were employed for comparison 
purposes, as follows: SGD (Stochastic Gradient Descent) and 
Adam (Adaptive Moment Estimation to adjust the 
experimental result. 
B. Learning rate 
The learning rate is a critical parameter that determines the 
magnitude of updates applied to the model’s weights during 
each training iteration. It serves as a measure of how quickly 
the model adapts to the learned data. The primary function of 
the learning rate is to control the magnitude of weight 
adjustments based on the feedback received from the loss 
function A high learning rate facilitates faster convergence, 
although it carries the risk of bypassing the global minimum. 
In contrast, a low learning rate leads to slower convergence, 
demands more training time, and may cause the model to get 
trapped in suboptimal solutions (local minimum).In this 
research, different learning rates were tested, namely 0.01, 
0.001, and 0.0001, to evaluate their impact on model 
performance. 
C. Batch Size 
Batch size indicates the number of training samples 
(images) utilized by the model in each iteration for parameter 
updates. The batch size is a crucial hyperparameter that

IV. CONCLUSION 
The ripeness level of oil palm fruits directly impacts the 
quality of palm oil, as optimally ripe fruits yield high-quality 
palm oil upon extraction. Therefore, errors in the classification 
of oil palm ripeness and improper harvesting can lead to 
significant losses and reduced income for farmers. The 
proposed method in this research employs the YOLOv9n 
convolutional neural network to classify the ripeness levels     
of fresh oil palm fruits. The original dataset utilized in this 
research was obtained from the Science Data Bank website. 
The ripeness levels were categorized into three classes: 
Unripe, Ripe, and Over-ripe. During the data preparation 
phase, the original images were preprocessed using Roboflow, 
which involved object isolation and data augmentation 
techniques to improve the model's training accuracy and 
overall performance. The model parameters, including the 
optimizer, learning rate, and batch size, were fine-tuned            
to optimize performance. The results indicated that the 
YOLOv9n model achieved the highest accuracy for 
classifying the ripeness levels of fresh oil palm fruits with the 
following parameters: Adam Optimizer, learning rate 0.001, 
and batch size 32. The evaluation metrics demonstrated high 
performance, with Precision of 0.994, recall of 0.996, mAP50 
of 0.995, and mAP50-95 of 0.926. Therefore, this research 
proposed the YOLOv9n to identify and categorize the ripeness 
levels of fresh oil palm fruits in images. Although the 
developed model demonstrates high accuracy in well-lit 
environments, it still has limitations when applied to images 
captured in low-light conditions or challenging environments, 
such as during the evening or at night. However, to achieve 
optimal results, it is recommended to ensure well-lit 
conditions to clearly visualize the color of the oil palm fruits. 
The findings of this research can be extended to improve and 
apply the ripeness classification model for other crops, such 
as durian or coconuts, to enhance harvesting efficiency.
