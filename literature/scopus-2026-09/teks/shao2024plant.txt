 
 
 
 
Plant fruit counting using context aware feature and point to point 
pairing

ABSTRACT   
Plant fruit count of can predict the yield of the whole orchard, which has the key guiding effect to agricultural production 
process. In this work, we propose a context feature aggregated convolutional neural network for plant fruit counting. The 
feature extracted by VGG network is pooled to get the context information, which is concatenated with the original 
feature for classification and regression. The output of the network is point, which is matched to the ground truth point to 
define the loss. Experimental results show that compared with existing network structure for fruit counting, our method 
achieves better counting performance on public available fruit dataset. The improvement of accuracy indicators shows 
that the proposed method has a good effect on plant images with different scales, illumination, contrast and occlusion.   
Keywords: fruit counting, deep learning, feature fusion, point to point pairing 
 
1. INTRODUCTION  
Plant phenotyping refers to quantitative description of the physical and chemical properties of a plant. In the process of 
agricultural production, the yields of plant species can feedback a lot of information. Specifically, the yield of fruit 
reflects the real-time growth, and the yield count of plants can predict the yield of the whole orchard. This has the key 
guiding effect to agricultural production process. Other plant phenotyping counting tasks include counting flowers for 
guiding the process of pruning and thinning that impacts fruit yield and quality, and counting spikes or heads in cereal 
crops for estimating grain yield. However, farmers often collect the output of plants manually, which is often time-
consuming and inaccurate [1]. Therefore, automatic counting method is desirable to estimate plant yield and reduce 
manual work.  
Convolutional neural networks (CNN) have provided the state-of-the-art performance for object counting tasks in 
various applications, especially in crowd counting. These methods have been developed and applied for plant counting, 
including segmentation based [2], detection based [3], direct regression and density estimation methods. Most of the 
early objects counting methods are the branch of image detection. The method is realized by summing up the detected 
object instances. With the increase of counting density, a lot of counting accuracy will be lost when using detection-
based method. Besides, object detection requires high specificity for the ground-truth annotation, which is time-
consuming. The annotations may be inaccurate when the targets are partially overlapped.  
The direct regression method that uses light weight image labels learns the features of the image and maps them to a 
scalar count for the input image. CNN can learn the appropriate characteristics of the object for counting with the help of 
explicit localization information provided by the annotated points, which is not utilizes in the direct regression method. 
Thus suboptimal result may be obtained with this method. In contrast, density estimation method takes use of the spatial 
information provided by the annotated points and estimates a density for an image whose integral matches the object 
counts of the image. This method has been widely used in crowd counting [4-5]. For the application in plant phenotyping, 
a CNN model with local counts as the regression target was used in [6], where dot annotation of object is required. A 
scalable, high-throughput approach to automatically count and segment panicles from aerial sorghum crop imagery was 
proposed in [7]. Representative detection and density estimation based methods were compared in [8] for counting fruit 
and grains. The method based on density estimation includes the congested scene recognition network (CSRNet) [4] that 
uses dilated convolutional neural networks and the context-aware network (CAN) [5], both of which were initially 
designed for crowd counting.

The density estimation based algorithm supervises the network by marking the density map generated by Gaussian blur 
after target points. The performance of the method is affected by the accuracy of Gaussian kernel function, which will 
lead to the loss of counting points. In order to solve such problem, point to point method (P2PNet) [9] takes point 
annotation of images as the learning target of the networked, rather than traditional bounding boxes or density maps 
created from dotted labels. Using point labeling of counting target to directly supervise network training avoids the 
problem of loss of counting accuracy caused by difference of Gaussian kernel function in traditional counting methods.  
The network structure of P2PNET was designed to focus on crowd targets, where all objects are of the same category 
with no disturbance from objects similar to the background. In the case of plant fruits counting, the images of plant fruits 
are scattered and concentrated, which is different from the dense and dense crowd images, due to the light and growth 
conditions of plants. The background of plant fruit images is complex. In this case, the density map generated by 
Gaussian kernel method often has the problem of fuzzy and large error.  
In order to solve this problem, a method for counting fruit objects in plant image was proposed, which could count fruits 
under different light and shade conditions. The proposed method is based on P2PNet [9], adopting the same backbone for 
feature extraction and point pairing strategy as in P2PNet.  However, before going into the regression and classification 
networks, the feature is concatenated with context information, which is obtained by pooling on the original feature. The 
results on fruit dataset show that the proposed method has improved performance for plant images growing in different 
environments.  
2. METHODS 
The density estimation counting method uses point annotation to generate Gaussian kernel density map to supervise 
network training. However, the accuracy of the generated Gaussian density map depends on the accuracy of the artificial 
Gaussian kernel function. As shown in Figure 1, for labeling data of plant fruit points with different densities, the fuzzy 
processing caused by Gaussian kernel may lead to the loss of partial counting targets, thus affecting counting accuracy 
and effect.

P2PNet uses point matching instead of Gaussian kernel density map to supervise the neural network training. The target 
points are directly input into the network as learning objects. The Hungarian algorithm is used for one-to-one matching 
supervision of the counting target points. We modify this structure by adding context information and attention fusion 
module for plant fruit counting. The feature pyramid structure in P2PNet is removed to prevent counting accuracy 
decreasing caused by extracting more irrelevant spatial information from multiple layers. The overall structure of the 
network is shown in Figure 2.  
The main steps of the proposed method are as follows: firstly, the front part of the network uses the first 13 convolution 
layers and 4 maximum pooling layers of VGG-16, removing the full connection layers of the back two layers, to extract 
feature F. Average pooling with different scales groups the feature followed by a 1×1 convolutional layer to form the 
context feature. They are then up-sampled back to the original feature size and subtracted from the original feature to 
form the contrast features Fcontrast.  With the obtained contrast features as weight coefficient, weighted sum of the context 
feature of multi-scale produces the last context feature Fcontext, as shown in Figure 2.   
Secondly, the original and scale-aware context features, F and Fcontext, feed into a multi-scale channel attention module 
(MS-CAM) [10] to aggregate the feature. In this process, a local and a global attention are calculated based on the sum 
of F and Fcontext. The sum of the two attentions is denoted as A. Then, the aggregated feature FA is computed as bellow,

After the fruit image is input into the network, the front-end network is responsible for extracting the feature information 
from the plant image. The convolution layer transforms the image from its original size of H × W to 1/8 of H × W. 
Different from the traditional density map method with Gaussian kernel function required to mark Gaussian blur for 
points, the point matching method can directly input the point labeling of the counting target into the network as the 
learning target. The feature information output from the front end of the network can output the predicted target point set 
after the regression and classification layer, which can be directly compared with the real point labeling points and 
calculate the loss to ensure the accuracy of the counting accuracy. At the same time, the loss function is designed as the 
Euclidian distance gap between the predicted points and the real points, so as to ensure that the network can converge to 
the appropriate parameters in the training process, so as to minimize the difference between the predicted points and the 
points marked. 
3. RESULTS 
We tested our method on public available fruit dataset and compared the results with other counting methods. These 
methods include density estimation method CSRNet [4] and CAN [5], and point pairing method P2PNet [9]. Like 
P2PNet, the proposed method extracts the relevant feature information with the VGG16 network, and directly generates 
the prediction of counting target points through regression and classification, which reduces the interference brought by 
irrelevant environmental information. It can be applied to plant images in many environments.

3.1 Dataset 
The counting experiment was carried out on the public fruit dataset [11]. The data set includes 1120 images of growing 
to mature apple fruits taken from different angles. The annotation points are given in the form of point annotation, and 
the marking target is the coordinates of the central point of the apple fruit. Based on the requirements of model training, 
these images are divided into three parts: training set, verification set and test set. The training set includes 896 images, 
while the verification set and the test set include 112 images respectively, which are used for the supervision of network 
training process and the detection of counting effect.

3.3 Results 
Table 1 lists the test results of apple dataset with several counting methods.  The lowest MAE and RMSE are obtained 
with the proposed method among all the methods. Compared with CSRNet [4] network structure and CAN [5] network 
structure, our method achieves better counting performance on the apple dataset. The MAE index of our method reaches 
0.67, indicating that this method has better effect on counting accuracy and feature information extraction. The RMSE 
index reaches 0.98, which indicates that the deviation of the method to the sample data is not large. The values of these 
two indicators show that proposed method has a good effect on plant images with different scales, illumination, contrast

3.4 Discussion and conclusion 
Our proposed method is based on the crowd counting method P2PNET which uses a pairing algorithm based on the 
relationship between points. In counting plant fruits, the images of plant fruits are scattered and concentrated, which is 
different from the dense and dense crowd images, due to the light and growth conditions of plants. In this case, the 
density map generated by Gaussian kernel method often has the problem of fuzzy and large error. In order to solve this 
problem, we choose to match and capture the plant counting target based on point, so as to obtain higher counting 
accuracy. Compared with the traditional counting network which relies on the generated Gaussian density graph as the 
supervision during training, our method is better in accuracy and stability. In plant counting, the accuracy of density map 
is not enough and the counting accuracy is lost due to the influence of space occlusion and illumination inconsistency. 
The experimental results proved the feasibility of this method on plant fruit.
