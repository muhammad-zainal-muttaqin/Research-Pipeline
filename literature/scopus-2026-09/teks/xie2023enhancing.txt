Abstract—Accurate fruit localization is a crucial and challeng-
ing task for achieving automated fruit harvesting in orchards.
The depth information output by the stereo camera suffers from
the problems of insufﬁcient ﬁlling rate and noise, which will
affect the accuracy of fruit localization. In this paper, we propose
a stereo matching network based on attention mechanism to
improve the quality of depth information. The network ﬁrst
extracts multi-scale features from left and right views, then uses
the attention mechanism to generate the disparity cost, and ﬁnally
the reﬁnement disparity map is output from the network. Based
on the disparity, we obtain the depth information and estimate
the centroid of the fruit using the 3D frustum method. We
collected orchard data and trained the network model to verify
the effectiveness of the stereo matching network on the orchard
dataset. Finally, fruit localization experiments were conducted
based on the network, and the results show that the accuracy of
fruit localization has been improved.

I. INTRODUCTION
It is highly challenging to operate in the highly unstructured
agricultural environment, and fruit harvesting is one of the
challenging tasks[1], [2]. Fruit localization is a crucial step for
robots to complete harvesting, and improving its localization
accuracy is of signiﬁcant importance. The studies have shown
that the impact of fruit localization accuracy is manifested in
two aspects: fruit detection and depth measurement. The fruit
detection process in the image is susceptible to the effects of
illumination and occlusion[3], resulting in missing features,
missed detection or offset localization[4]. At the same time,
in the measurement process of stereo vision, local regions of
positioning obstacles or fruits may cause signiﬁcant deviation
between the estimated position and the actual position[5].
To improve the accuracy of fruit localization, researchers
have conducted extensive studies. In terms of traditional image
detection, [6] proposed an occluded apple detection method
based on convex hull theory using morphological methods.
The center and radius were estimated using the convex hull
of the target edge, with an average positioning error of about

color ﬁlling [7] to restore the image features of fruits to
obtain more precise image positions. However, these methods
generally suffer from drawbacks such as insufﬁcient accuracy,
high misidentiﬁcation rates, and poor generalization ability. As
the development of deep learning technology, fruit detection
accuracy and robustness have been improved. [8] proposed
an improved one-stage fully convolutional network algorithm,
which improved the average accuracy of apple detection to
96.3%. Furthermore, pixel-level object segmentation[9] can
more accurately describe the position of fruits in the image.
[10] used a conditional adversarial generative network model
to supplement the input of image information and improve
the accuracy of semantic segmentation of obstructed apple
branches.
On the other hand, fruit positioning requires the fruit
point-cloud generated by combining depth information with
pixel positions, which means fruit depth measurement also
has an important impact on fruit localization. Point-based
3D shape ﬁtting [11] can tolerate noise with non-fruit point
cloud to a certain extent, which is currently the mainstream
fruit localization method, such as least squares [12], random
consensus sampling and Hough transform [13]. These methods
have been used to obtain the three-dimensional coordinates
of fruits. However, the above ﬁtting methods have a large
positioning error for obstructed fruits. To address this issue,
[14] proposed a fruit localization method based on the frustum
method, which has good robustness for locating obstructed
fruits and improves fruit localization accuracy.
Although these studies have made some progress, there
are still some unresolved issues. In natural environments, the
3D point cloud information generated by stereo cameras is
prone to deformation due to local highlights. Additionally,
extracting information from overexposed and dark areas is
challenging, resulting in missing point cloud information.
Usually, the quality of the point-cloud is closely related to the
depth information of the stereo camera output. Stereo cameras
are mainly used to obtain depth information through three
methods: structured light, time-of-ﬂight (ToF), and disparity
matching. Among them, disparity matching has been proven

II. STEREO MATCHING AND FRUIT LOCATION
In this section, we ﬁrst introduce the overall structure of
the stereo matching network, focusing on the generation of
the matching cost. Then we describe how to obtain the three-
dimensional position of the fruit through the disparity map and
object detection.
A. Stereo matching network
Most current researches on stereo matching networks are
based on the cost volume. Recent studies have shown that
using the parallax attention mechanism (PAM) [19] to generate
matching costs can achieve higher accuracy and lower memory
overhead than traditional methods. Therefore, we adopt this
stereo matching network, as shown in Figure 1. The overall
structure of the stereo matching network is shown in Figure
1a, which is mainly divided into three parts. The ﬁrst part
is feature extraction, which uses a feature pyramid network
shown in Figure 1b to perform multi-scale feature extraction
on the stereo images and obtain richer features. The direction
of the red arrow in Figure 1b indicates the process of feature
extraction based on the FPN module. The grayscale image
from the infrared camera is input, and features are output in
three scales.
The second part is the calculation of disparity matching
cost. In the network, a visual attention mechanism is used to
replace the traditional method of 3D cost volume convolution,
reducing computational costs. As shown in Figure 1c, the core
of the mechanism is to obtain the matching cost by multiplying
the tensors A and B. A represents the dimension transforma-
tion of the features from the left view, converting the default
shape C × H × W into H × W × C, while features from the
right view is transformed into H × C × W, denoted as B. By
performing tensor multiplication between A and B,a tensor
with shape H × W × W is obtained. Since the stereo infrared
cameras are already aligned vertically, matching only needs
to be performed on the same horizontal line. For two images
with W points each on the same vertical coordinate, each point
has a C dimension of features. A cross-correlation operation is
performed on each point, which is a matrix multiplication. The
multiplication result is a W ×W matrix, which represents the
matching cost between the element at coordinate j in the left
view image and the element at coordinate k in the right view
image at a certain vertical coordinate, i.e., the matching cost.
Multiplying by the number of points in the vertical dimension,
H, yields the matching cost extended to the entire image. This
method does not need to consider the interference brought
by features on different vertical coordinates compared to 3D
convolution on the cost volume, resulting in more accurate
matching cost and lower memory overhead.

III. EXPERIMENTS
A. Stereo network training
We collected a dataset of 1000 images in the orchards in
Haidian and Changping, Beijing, China, acquired by a Intel
RealSense D435, under different lighting and distances. Each
includes synchronized left and right infrared images and color
images. As shown in Figure 2, networks detection, segmenta-
tion and stereo are need to train. The training of detection and
segmentation networks has been completed in our previous
work [14]. In this work, the detection and segmentation
networks are combined to a multi-task network, where the
backbone, neck and detection head are all copied completely
from YOLOX and the segmentation head is a sequence of
convolutions from the bottom of the FPN in YOLOX. The
conﬁguration for training the stereo network is as follows: an
r5 2600 CPU, 16GB of memory, an RTX3060 graphics card,
running Ubuntu 20.04 with the software packages of CUDA
11.6 and PyTorch 1.13. The dataset was randomly cropped and
preprocessed to improve the model’s generalization ability, and
the training-to-testing ratio was set at 9:1. The whole dataset
was trained for 500 epoches.

IV. CONCLUSION
This paper considers the impact of depth information output
by stereo cameras on the accuracy of fruit localization in
orchards, and introduces a stereo matching network to improve
the quality of depth information. We ﬁrst collected data and
trained the model to validate its effectiveness on the orchard
dataset. Then, to further verify the effect of the stereo matching
network on fruit localization, we compared this model with the
stereo matching algorithm of the stereo camera. The results
show that the point-cloud generated by the stereo matching
network has higher quality and can effectively improve the
accuracy of fruit localization.
