A B S T R A C T
Citrus fruit instance segmentation is an important prerequisite for citrus fruit detection, recognition, and yield 
estimation in complex environments of citrus orchards. In order to address the challenges of leaf shading, fruit 
overlapping, and light variations in citrus fruit detection, identification, and yield estimation, this study proposes 
the YOLO11n-LMPS based on the improved You Only Look Once-11n-Segmentation (YOLO11n-Seg) for citrus 
instance segmentation and yield estimation. The YOLO11n-LMPS proposes a Parallel Multi-scale Channel 
Enhancement (PMCE) module for enhancing different sizes of fruit feature extraction and suppressing back­
ground noise. An improved Lightweight Separated and Enhancement Attention Module (LSEAM) is used in the 
Cross Stage Partial with Pyramid Squeeze Attention (C2PSA) to enhance attention and masking robustness and 
reduce computational complexity. The Multi-Scale Dilated Attention (MSDA) is introduced to optimise detail and 
global semantic information capture and extend the sensory field. The experimental results in Section 3.2 show 
that the mAPmask 50 of the YOLO11n-LMPS reaches 91.6%, and the mAPmask 50∶95 reaches 71.1%. The 
mAPbox 50 of the YOLO11n-LMPS reaches 92.1%, and the mAPbox 50∶95 reaches 74.1%. mAPmask 50, 
mAPmask 50∶95, mAPbox 50and mAPbox 50∶95 are higher than other mainstream models. Under the complex 
orchard environment, YOLO11n-LMPS improves citrus fruit detection and segmentation accuracy in complex 
orchard scenarios, offering a reliable solution for yield estimation.

1. Introduction
The conventional citrus harvesting process is predominantly labor- 
intensive, necessitating the engagement of substantial human re­
sources for completion, with the fruit-picking phase constituting 
approximately 40% of the total operational workload in citrus produc­
tion systems, which is not only inefficient but also has a high labour cost, 
which seriously affects the efficiency of citrus picking and citrus revenue 
(Harrell et al., 2009; Sanders, 2005). This necessitates the imple­
mentation of picking robots to pick citrus intelligently, and the study of a 
visual recognition system with a high recognition rate of citrus fruits 
within dynamic orchard environments is a pivotal technical prerequisite 
for establishing the automated picking of citrus. Vision-based fruit 
recognition and localization are essential not only for accurate target 
perception, but also for subsequent grasp planning, motion execution, 
and yield monitoring.
Research on orchard fruit perception can generally be categorized 
into two major groups: target recognition methods based on traditional 
RGB image processing (Ferrari et al., 2009; Gevers, 1999), and visual 
recognition methods based on deep learning (Jiao et al., 2019; Neupane 
et al., 2019). Traditional RGB image-based target recognition methods 
mainly rely on texture (Chaivivatrakul and Dailey, 2014), color (Luo and 
Crandall, 2006), geometric shape (Wu and Wang, 1993), and their 
combined features for fruit detection, and have been applied to crops 
such as grape (P´erez et al., 2017), tomato (Zhao et al., 2016), banana 
(Sadgrove et al., 2018), apple (Linker, 2018), and strawberry (Oo and 
Aung, 2018). For citrus perception, existing studies on traditional RGB 
image-based target recognition have employed strategies such as local 
texture description (Lu et al., 2018), elliptical boundary fitting (Liu 
et al., 2018), color-space modeling (Dorj et al., 2017), and template 
matching (Li et al., 2016) to achieve fruit recognition under natural 
illumination conditions. Although traditional RGB image-based target 
recognition methods exhibit a certain degree of feasibility under 
controlled 
conditions,

branch-and-leaf occlusion, fruit adhesion, dense distribution of small 
targets, and drastic illumination fluctuations can substantially weaken 
the discriminative capability of handcrafted features, thereby limiting 
recognition accuracy and generalization performance.
With the continued development of deep learning in agricultural 
vision, fruit detection methods based on convolutional neural networks 
and end-to-end learning frameworks have demonstrated stronger ad­
vantages in complex background modeling, multiscale feature extrac­
tion, and target discrimination (Hua et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2021; Wu 
et al., 2020). At present, one-stage and two-stage detection methods 
have been widely applied in various fruit detection and recognition 
scenarios, thereby promoting simultaneous improvements in detection 
efficiency and accuracy (Badgujar et al., 2024; Inbar et al., 2023; Wan 
et al., 2020). In citrus fruit detection and recognition research, improved 
YOLO-based models have achieved promising results in real-time 
detection (Xu et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024), lightweight deploy­
ment (Han et al., 2025), and small-object recognition (Zhang et al., 
2022; 2025). Chen et al. (2020) improved YOLOv4 to enhance citrus 
detection performance for fruits at different growth stages in complex 
natural scenes. Huang et al. (2021) combined attention mechanisms, 
adaptive feature fusion, and pruning strategies to achieve real-time 
citrus detection on edge devices. Chen et al. (2025) further integrated 
lightweight detection with a deployment-oriented application, 
achieving real-time, high-precision citrus detection and accurate 
counting. Nevertheless, most existing studies remain at the level of 
determining object presence and coarse localization via bounding boxes. 
Their primary emphasis is on detection and counting, while insufficient 
attention has been paid to individual boundary recovery, instance sep­
aration, and pixel-level representation under complex occlusion 
conditions.
Although bounding-box detection can provide the category and 
location of fruits, it cannot accurately characterize the true contours, 
individual boundaries, or contact regions among fruits. This limitation 
becomes more pronounced in natural orchard environments featuring 
intertwined branches and leaves, overlapping fruits, and substantial 
scale variation, thereby increasing the risks of missed detections, false 
detections, and duplicate counting. In contrast, instance segmentation 
can explicitly separate each fruit at the pixel level, thereby preserving 
edge, shape, and size information and making it more suitable for 
individual-level recognition of overlapping fruits. Kang et al. (2020)
demonstrated that, for overlapping fruits in orchard environments, the 
instance-level contour information provided by instance segmentation is 
of substantial importance for geometric attribute extraction, precise 
localization, and subsequent harvesting operations.
In recent years, segmentation methods based on frameworks such as 
Transformer, U-Net, and YOLO-seg have been increasingly introduced 
into agricultural automation research, demonstrating considerable po­
tential in fruit boundary recovery, occluded target recognition, and 
complex-background suppression (Luo et al., 2024). Han et al. (2025)
proposed a lightweight instance segmentation model based on 
YOLOv8n-seg and achieved a favorable balance between segmentation 
accuracy and inference efficiency in orchard scenarios. Lu et al. (2025)
developed a general fruit segmentation model based on a U-shaped 
encoder–decoder architecture and a large-kernel convolutional 
network, further confirming the extensibility of segmentation tech­
niques in agricultural vision. Nevertheless, several high-accuracy seg­
mentation models still depend heavily on computational resources, 
which restricts their direct deployment on resource-constrained orchard 
mobile platforms and harvesting robots. Therefore, maintaining infer­
ence efficiency while preserving high segmentation accuracy remains a 
critical issue in orchard visual perception research.
Vision-based orchard yield prediction has gradually shifted from 
empirical sampling and manual counting toward automated perception- 
driven quantitative estimation. Existing studies can generally be divided 
into two methodological paradigms: indirect yield prediction based on 
canopy structure, vegetation indices, phenological information, envi­
ronmental variables, or other multi-source features, and direct yield 
estimation based on fruit detection, localization, counting, and subse­
quent estimation modeling. He et al. (2022) systematically reviewed 
orchard yield prediction and estimation methods and summarized in­
direct feature-based prediction and direct fruit-counting-based estima­
tion as two major technical routes in orchard yield assessment. In 
addition, Maheswari et al. (2021) further reviewed intelligent fruit yield 
estimation using deep learning-based semantic segmentation and indi­
cated that fruit detection, localization, counting, and yield estimation 
constitute a continuous workflow for intelligent orchard yield assess­
ment. These studies suggest that segmentation and yield prediction are 
not isolated tasks; rather, accurate pixel-level fruit localization provides 
a more reliable spatial basis for fruit counting, geometric measurement, 
and yield inference.

CNN-based yield estimation using UAV imagery, suggesting that seg­
mentation, counting, and yield prediction are becoming an inter­
connected technical chain in intelligent citrus orchard perception.
Although existing citrus yield estimation studies have demonstrated 
the feasibility of detection- and counting-based approaches, most of 
them primarily rely on bounding-box-level fruit counts (Zeng et al., 
2025), tree-level canopy information (Xie et al., 2026), multi-view tree 
(Xu et al., 2025) images (Zhai et al., 2025), or UAV-derived structural 
and vegetation features. Relatively limited attention has been given to 
instance-level yield inference in which pixel-wise fruit masks are used to 
derive geometric attributes such as projected area, equivalent radius, 
and single-fruit mass. In complex orchard environments, occlusion, fruit 
overlap, scale variation, and illumination heterogeneity can simulta­
neously affect fruit counting accuracy and mask-derived geometric 
measurements. Therefore, the instance segmentation framework 
emphasized in this study is intended not only to improve citrus locali­
zation and counting under complex scenes, but also to establish a more 
interpretable geometric representation for visible-fruit mass estimation 
and subsequent yield inference.
In summary, citrus instance segmentation and yield prediction in 
complex natural orchards still face several key challenges: 
(1) Harvesting platforms operating over extended periods are 
continuously affected by natural illumination fluctuations, 
resulting in unstable image brightness distributions and weak­
ened representation of fruit texture and boundaries.
(2) Citrus fruits commonly suffer from branch-and-leaf occlusion, 
fruit overlap, and local shadow coverage, which can lead to 
incomplete target contours and ambiguous instance boundaries.
(3) Different imaging distances and viewpoints cause substantial 
fruit scale variation, increasing the risk of missing small targets 
and weakening multiscale target representation.
(4) Existing detection frameworks are insufficient for simultaneously 
satisfying the requirements of pixel-level instance separation, 
stable counting, and geometric attribute extraction, thereby 
limiting their application potential in visible-fruit mass estima­
tion and yield inference.
In response to the above issues, this study proposes a citrus instance 
segmentation and yield prediction method for complex orchard envi­
ronments. Built upon YOLO11n-Seg, the proposed framework, termed 
YOLO11n-LMPS, takes instance-level pixel representation as its core and 
improves the Identification of individual citrus fruits under occlusion, 
multiscale variation, and complex backgrounds, thereby providing a 
more reliable visual basis for visible fruit statistics and yield inference. 
Specifically, a Parallel Multiscale Channel Enhancement (PMCE) mod­
ule is proposed to strengthen the modeling of salient information from 
fruits of different scales while suppressing background noise interfer­
ence. An improved Lightweight Separable Enhancement Attention 
Module (LSEAM) is embedded into C2PSA to enhance the model’s 
attention allocation to occluded targets and weakly salient regions. In 
addition, a Multi-scale Dilated Attention (MSDA) module is incorpo­
rated to enlarge the receptive field and strengthen the collaborative 
representation of local details and global semantics. On this basis, an 
instance-aware yield prediction strategy is further constructed by 
adaptively screening segmentation masks and bounding-box results, 
discriminating overlaps, and integrating instance-level information, 
thereby improving the stability of visible fruit statistics and providing a 
more reliable quantitative basis for orchard yield estimation. Overall, 
YOLO11n-LMPS aims to achieve a balance between computational cost 
and high-accuracy instance segmentation, and to provide theoretical 
support for visual perception and yield evaluation in citrus harvesting 
robots operating in complex natural environments.
2. Materials and methods
2.1. Image acquisition
To study a high-precision instance segmentation and yield prediction 
method for citrus under orchard operation scenarios involving changes 
in lighting, occlusion, and overlap, the dataset used in this study was 
acquired on November 12, 2024, at Lianghu Farm, Jiangxia District, 
Wuhan City, Hubei Province, under various scene conditions including 
backlighting, front lighting, single-target, multi-target, branch and leaf 
occlusion, and cluster overlap. Image acquisition was performed using a 
Canon camera, with shooting distances randomly selected between 0.5 
m and 1.5 m During image acquisition, the Canon camera used both 
backlighting and front-lighting shooting directions. Images were 
collected from multiple angles under open-air conditions to simulate the 
picking environment of a harvesting robot. The images included back­
lighting, facing-lighting, single-target, multi-target, and occlusion by 
branches and leaves, but not occluded by branches, leaves, or heavy 
fruits. The acquired citrus image data is shown in Fig. 1. To enhance data 
diversity and improve the model's robustness to changes in view area 
and local visibility, the original 4320×2880 image was cropped into six 
sub-images and subjected to quality screening, resulting in 1519 images. 
To avoid data leakage caused by cropping from the same source, all 
cropped sub-images generated from the same original image were 
assigned to the same data subset.

2.3. Dataset production
The LabelMe software is used to annotate citrus image datasets, 
saving them as JSON files, and using data format conversion code to 
convert the JSON dataset into YOLO format text files. Because citrus 
trees have dense foliage and overlapping shadows between fruits, the 
background becomes blurry when the camera locks onto the target, and 
the captured image may have shadows due to the shooting angle and 
direction. If the citrus area is too large to be obscured, it becomes 
difficult to manually judge the condition of the fruit. Alternatively, the 
citrus fruit may be too blurry in the background, or the shadows may 
make it impossible to identify the citrus area and its outline. Therefore, 
to ensure the accuracy and robustness of the dataset, the following 
principles were followed when labeling the dataset: 
(1) Target instances that occupy a very small proportion of the image 
after preprocessing and are difficult to form stable and identifi­
able features were not included in the labeling set to avoid 
introducing weakly supervised samples with high noise.
(2) When the degree of occlusion is high, and the target boundary 
cannot be reliably determined, an ignore strategy was adopted, 
and no instance labeling was provided; when the degree of

2.4. Base model
Khanam and Hussain (2024) proposed the architecturally innovative 
You Only Look Once-11 (YOLOv 11) target detection model that in­
troduces Faster Implementation of Cross Stage Partial (CSP) Bottleneck 
with Two Convolutions (C3k2) blocks, Spatial Pyramid Pooling Fast 
(SPPF) and Cross Stage Partial with Pyramid Squeeze Attention (C2PSA) 
components to meet the needs of a wide range of applications from edge 
devices to high-performance computing environments. As the latest 
model for single-stage target detection, YOLO11 can accurately and 
efficiently complete the target detection task with excellent stability and 
robustness.
The orchard environment is complex, and citrus fruits are influenced 
by variable factors such as changes in lighting and shading by lush 
branches and other overlapping citrus fruits, which in turn leads to 
increased difficulty in detecting citrus fruits, making the model prone to 
leakage when identifying fruits. At the same time, the shooting angle 
and direction cause the collected images to have shadows, which pose 
challenges to the model’s performance in precisely recognizing citrus 
fruits and estimating the yield. Therefore, to solve the above-mentioned 
difficult problems of citrus fruit detection in the natural environment, 
YOLO11n-Seg is selected as the detection method for citrus in this study.
2.5. Introduction to the YOLO11n-LMPS model
To address the key challenges of citrus instance segmentation in 
complex orchard environments, such as multi-scale fruit appearance 
differences, background interference from branches and leaves, and 
feature weakening caused by occlusion, we built YOLO11n-LMPS based 
on YOLO11n-Seg. The overall structure of the model is shown in Fig. 2. 
First, we designed a Parallel Multi-scale Channel Enhancement (PMCE) 
module to enhance multi-scale feature modeling capabilities. Second, 
we introduced an optimized C2PSA-LSEAM module into the Backbone to 
improve attention focus and mask prediction robustness in occluded 
scenarios. Finally, we introduced a Multi-Scale Dilated Attention 
(MSDA) module into the Neck to alleviate the problems of insufficient 
semantic meaning of low-resolution features and limited context 
modeling.

2.5.2. Multi-scale dilated attention module
To address the problem that the feature map is insufficient to provide 
accurate semantic prediction due to low-resolution feature maps, the 
MSDA (Jiao et al., 2023) module is introduced, which leverages the 
sparse properties of the self-attention mechanism across multiple scales 
to capture multilevel semantic content, broadening the model's recep­
tive field, so that the model can efficiently identify and understand both 
detailed and global information in a complex scene.
The Multi-Scale Dilated Attention (MSDA) module performs multi- 
scale semantic modeling through different attention heads, while 
assigning distinct dilation rates to individual heads so that each head 
focuses on feature responses within a specific receptive-field range, 
thereby enhancing multi-scale representation and semantic aggregation 
in complex scenes. Meanwhile, by exploiting the sparse modeling 
property of self-attention, MSDA enlarges the effective receptive field 
and improves feature expressiveness while reducing computational 
redundancy, thus achieving a favorable balance between representa­
tional capacity and computational complexity. Its architecture is shown 
in Fig. 4.
The MSDA module receives input from feature maps X∈RB×C×H×W, 
generates Query, Key, and Value through linear projection, and divides 
the channels into multiple attention branches. It performs Sliding Win­
dow Dilated Attention (SWDA) operations at different dilation rates r =
1, 2, 3, 4. Under a uniform 3 × 3 convolution kernel setting, each 
attention head corresponds to an effective receptive field of 3 × 3, 5 × 5, 
7 × 7, and 9 × 9, respectively. It performs weighted aggregation of 
sparsely sampled key-value features within a local window centered on 
the query block, thereby achieving adaptive modeling of multi-scale 
semantic information and enhancing the model's ability to represent 
multi-scale targets and complex scenes.

model's computational efficiency and feature capture capability. 
Considering the requirements of the detection task for local texture 
representation, spatial context awareness, and computational efficiency, 
this paper selects 3 × 3 as the basic sliding window. Compared to 5 × 5 
and 7 × 7 windows, 3 × 3 only needs to process 9 neighboring pixels, 
with a complexity of O(9HW), which can significantly reduce compu­
tational overhead. At the same time, combined with the multi-dilation 
rate design, its effective receptive field can be extended to 9 × 9, 
which is sufficient to cover the spatial context of small and medium- 
sized targets such as citrus fruits. In contrast, although an excessively 
large window can introduce more information, it will significantly in­
crease complexity and bring more background interference; an exces­
sively small window is difficult to effectively model boundary and 
texture information. Therefore, the combination of a 3 × 3 sliding 
window and a multi-dilation rate strategy achieves a better balance 
between feature representation capability, noise suppression capability, 
and computational affordability. To verify the above reasoning, exper­
iments were conducted on the YOLO11n-LMPS model with different 
sliding window sizes ω.
The experimental results of Table 2 for different sliding-window sizes 
indicate that, under complex orchard backgrounds characterized by 
foliage occlusion and densely distributed fruits, the model must 
strengthen local-region modeling to improve discrimination between 
targets and background. Compared with other configurations, a 3 × 3 
window combined with a multi-dilation strategy can jointly encode local 
fruit attributes, such as shape and color, as well as neighboring bound­
ary cues between fruits and branches, making it more suitable for citrus 
orchard scenes.
Furthermore, the SWDA mechanism performs sparse selection and 
weighted aggregation of key-value features around the central query, 
thereby satisfying locality constraints while efficiently modeling long- 
range dependencies. The resulting feature maps are then concatenated 
and linearly transformed for deeper integration, yielding a more 
discriminative optimized feature representation. Based on the process­

2.5.3. C2PSA-LSEAM module
The citrus instance segmentation dataset, after data preprocessing, 
suffers from occlusion in most of the citrus images due to external factors 
such as lush foliage and other overlapping citrus, resulting in the 
response of the features that may be significantly affected, thus affecting 
the recognition accuracy. To solve the above problems and improve the 
network model's ability to pay attention to and capture the occluded part 
of the features, the Separated and Enhancement Attention Module 
(SEAM) is introduced into the C2PSA module (Yu et al., 2024). To 
optimize the model’s computational performance and boost the feature 
representation capacity, the SEAM is improved by lightweight. The 
module after the improvement is named the Lightweight Separated and 
Enhancement Attention Module (LSEAM), and the structure of 
C2PSA-LSEAM and LSEAM is shown in Fig. 5.
This paper constructs an improved C2PSA-LSEAM module, 
enhancing its comprehensive representation capabilities and robustness. 
The module first performs channel adjustment and compression on the 
input features using 1 × 1 convolutions to reduce computational 
complexity and complete initial feature mapping. Then, it divides the 
features into two branches along the channel dimension: one branch is 
retained for subsequent fusion, while the other is fed into a multi-layer 
Pyramid Split Attention Block (PSABlock) for depth enhancement. Each 
PSABlock, combined with LSEAM, achieves collaborative modeling of 
channel and spatial attention. Depth-separable convolutions and group 
convolutions are introduced to reduce parameter count and computa­
tional redundancy. Simultaneously, global average pooling and fully 
connected mapping are used to generate dynamic weights, strength­
ening key feature responses and suppressing redundant information. 
After multi-layer enhancement, the depth branch and the retained 
branch are concatenated along the channel dimension and restored to 
the original channel size through an additional 1 × 1 convolution. This 
significantly improves feature representation quality and environmental 
robustness while maintaining the input spatial resolution and number of

Identification, detection, and yield prediction of citrus fruits in nat­

practical application capability. To evaluate the performance of the 
model for citrus fruit detection and yield estimation, Precision (P), 
Recall (R), and Mean Average Precision (mAP) were selected to evaluate 
the effectiveness of the model proposed in this paper. mAP0.5, and 
mAP0.5:0.95 were used to evaluate the effectiveness of the model pro­
posed in this paper by taking the values of Precision and Recall at 
different confidence values. The mAP0.5 and mAP0.5:0.95 are used to 
combine the values of Precision and Recall at different confidence values 
to draw a P-R curve, and the area under the curve is the Average Pre­
cision (AP) of a category.

3.4. Analysis of the results of the citrus production estimates
To verify the feasibility and effectiveness of the citrus yield estima­
tion method proposed in Section 2.6, this section further conducts 
single-fruit mass prediction experiments together with visual validation 
under complex environmental conditions. Based on the masks and 
contour information generated by YOLO11n-LMPS, and by combining 
geometric fitting, volume estimation, and mass conversion, this study 
predicts both individual citrus fruit mass and overall yield, and the 
experimental results verify the effectiveness and applicability of the 
method described in Section 2.6.
To further reveal the intrinsic relationship among counting accuracy, 
segmentation accuracy, and final yield estimation, a correlation analysis 
was conducted using the predicted fruit count, actual fruit count, mask 
segmentation accuracy, and single-fruit absolute mass error. For the 
correlation analysis between predicted and actual fruit counts, 20 
experimental samples were randomly selected. In addition, 35 experi­
mental samples were randomly selected to analyze the relationship be­
tween mask segmentation accuracy and single-fruit absolute mass error. 
The mask segmentation accuracy was defined as As = ST/SA, where ST 
denotes the overlapping area between the predicted mask and the 
ground-truth citrus mask, and SAdenotes the predicted mask area.
As shown in Fig. 10(a), the predicted fruit count exhibited a high 
degree of linear agreement with the actual fruit count, with a coefficient 
of determination of R2 = 0.995. This result indicates that the proposed 
method can stably estimate the number of visible citrus fruits in complex 
orchard images. Since the quantity term in final yield estimation is 
directly determined by fruit counting, this result demonstrates that the 
influence of counting error on final yield estimation was maintained at a 
relatively low level. As shown in Fig. 10(b), mask segmentation accuracy 
exhibited a significant negative correlation with single-fruit absolute

4. Conclusions
This study addresses the detection and yield prediction of citrus fruit 
in complex environments. By innovatively employing instance seg­
mentation, we propose a Parallel Multi-scale Channel Enhancement 
module (PMCE), introduce a Multi-scale Dilated Attention module 
(MSDA), and a Lightweight Separation Enhancement Attention Module 
(LSEAM), combining them with C2PSA to form the C2PSA-LSEAM 
module. Building on the foundational YOLO11n-Seg, we construct the

YOLO11n-LMPS model for citrus fruit detection and yield prediction in 
complex environments. Aiming for the practical needs of agricultural 
robot visual perception, we propose a multi-scale dynamic fusion and 
channel-space collaborative enhancement strategy. Using depthwise 
separable convolution (DSConv) to compress computation while 
retaining multi-scale feature extraction capabilities (e.g., 1 × 1, 3 × 3, 5 
× 5, and 7 × 7 convolution kernels), we effectively address illumination 
variations, background noise, and foliage occlusion. The PMCE module 
optimizes computational efficiency through feature stream segmenta­
tion and FCEM within the CSP architecture. The EPSF module integrates 
multi-scale features to improve segmentation accuracy. The MSDA le­
verages the sparsity of the self-attention mechanism to capture multi- 
level semantic content and expand the receptive field. The Light­
weight Separation Enhanced Attention Module (LSEAM) is combined 
with the C2PSA to form the C2PSA-LSEAM module, enhancing feature 
response to occlusion. To address the semantic prediction deficiencies of 
low-resolution feature maps and the weakening of feature response due 
to external factors, each of these modules is specifically designed and 
modified, providing a highly adaptable solution for citrus fruit detection 
and yield prediction in complex environments.
Compared to mainstream models, this study improves segmentation 
accuracy by organically integrating multi-scale fusion, attention mech­
anisms, and regularization strategies. It provides a theoretical basis for 
addressing the complexities of agricultural scenarios (such as over­
lapping fruit and leaf occlusion) and offers a novel approach for efficient 
robotic recognition. This approach demonstrates originality and prac­
tical value for citrus fruit detection and yield prediction in complex 
environments, far exceeding simple network adjustments.
Based on this study's innovations in citrus fruit detection and yield 
prediction, future research can be expanded in the following directions 
to further improve the model's robustness, practicality, and generaliza­
tion capabilities: 
(1) Future work could explore integrating RGB images with multi- 
spectral, LiDAR, or environmental sensor data via multi-modal 
deep learning frameworks to enhance robustness against light­
ing variations and occlusions, enabling more accurate yield 
prediction.
(2) To meet the practical needs of agricultural robots, optimize the 
YOLO11n-LMPS model for edge computing and smartphone 
deployment using techniques like knowledge distillation and 
quantization to reduce latency while preserving multi-scale 
feature extraction capabilities.
(3) Extend the model to other fruit crops (e.g., apples, grapes) using 
transfer learning and cross-species knowledge transfer strategies, 
combined with advanced data augmentation (e.g., GAN- 
generated synthetic occlusion scenes) to improve generalization 
and reduce crop-specific dependencies.
These directions aim to address remaining challenges in complex 
agricultural scenarios, such as dynamic environmental adaptation and 
multi-source data fusion, while maintaining the core advantage of 
lightweight design.
