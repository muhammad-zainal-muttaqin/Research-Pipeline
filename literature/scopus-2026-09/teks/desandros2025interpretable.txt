Abstract— Determining the optimal harvest time for oil palm 
fruit is essential in the palm oil industry, as the quality of the 
yield is primarily influenced by the ripeness level of the fruit 
bunch. However, manual ripeness assessment by farmers often 
results in a subjective and inefficient process, potentially leading 
to suboptimal product quality. This research is motivated by the 
growing demand for automated ripeness classification, which is 
often challenged by limited data availability. The focus of this 
pilot study is to identify which visual features contribute most 
significantly to classification, laying the groundwork for future 
deep learning models that require larger datasets. A range of 
feature extraction techniques was applied to a small-sized image 
dataset, followed by dimensionality reduction and statistical 
learning methods for feature selection and classification. The 
experiments were conducted using a 5-fold cross-validation 
scheme to ensure fair evaluation. The results show that the PLS-
LR algorithm outperformed the more complex PLS-RBF_SVC 
in the evaluation, by scoring mean accuracy of 0.8393 ± 0.0253, 
mean precision of 0.8391 ± 0.0276, mean recall of 0.8186 ± 
0.0191, and mean F1-score of 0.8233 ± 0.0185. Moreover, 
features from multi-color histograms, color indices, and texture-
based LBP groups emerged as key contributors based on the 
PLS feature importance analysis. These insights can serve as 
prior knowledge for advancing modeling strategies and 
supporting sustainable practices in the oil palm industry 
through automation technologies.

I. INTRODUCTION  
Oil palm (Elaeis guineensis) is a species of tree that 
produce palm oil, a key agricultural commodity that plays 
crucial role in modern food industry, due to its versatility and 
nutritional value  [1]. In addition to these industrial benefits, 
this commodity has played a significant role in driving 
economic growth in several developing countries, notably 
Indonesia and Malaysia [2] by generating substantial 
employment opportunities, enhancing quality of life in rural 
area, and reducing poverty in countries where the trees is 
cultivated.  
Due to the critical of nature of the industry, numerous 
studies have addressed challenges in the oil palm sector 
through technological innovations, such as evaluating the 
impact of soil variability on productivity [3], [4], identifying 
nutrient deficiencies in seedlings using UAV-based 
multispectral imaging [5], and applying trunk injection 
techniques to enhance the weight of the harvested fruits [6]. 
One of the most major challenges in the business is to 
determine the most appropriate time to harvest according to 
the ripeness level of the fruits. Failure in assessing the oil 
palm ripeness will lead to major economic loss, as harvesting 
unripe or overripe fruit can significantly degrade the quality 
of crude palm oil (CPO) [7]. Traditionally, this assessment 
relies on manual inspection by harvesters or field experts, 
which known to be a time-consuming process and prone to 
subjective bias. 
Recent advances in computer vision and deep learning 
have supported in automating several stages in oil palm 
industry, as shown in multiple studies such as utilization of 
self-attention-based classifier models [8], [9], [10] and a more 
advanced object detection framework [11] to classify the 
fruit’s ripeness level, as well as estimation of nitrogen content 
in oil palm leaves through Dark Green Color Index (DGCI) 
measurement [12]. However, deep learning approaches often 
face limitations in small dataset availability and suffer from a 
lack of interpretability. A hasty implementation of deep 
learning model can lead to emergence of unwanted problems 
in the real-world application since the model operates in 
black-box manner [13], creating difficulties in unravelling 
the model’s decision making process. 
To avoid aimless implementation of deep learning model, 
this proposed study was performed to understand the visual 
features that determines the oil palm fruit ripeness level by 
adopting a manual feature extraction from the image to form 
a tabular dataset, prior to the utilization of traditional 
statistical learning methods to reduce the feature spaces and 
classify the label. Furthermore, this study utilized a relatively 
new, publicly available and high-quality dataset, to support 
reliable feature importance analysis. This strategy is not only 
expected to improve model robustness in limited data 
conditions but also allows for interpretable analysis through 
feature importance estimation [14]. Moreover, compressing 
feature spaces to a certain degree will also help to streamline 
the industrial implementation which may require lightweight 
models to operate in a real-time manner. 
The technical aspect of this study will be clearly discussed 
on the next following sections: (1) Methodology that describe 
the detailed procedures and computation techniques used to 
perform the research, (2) Results and Discussion that presents 
the experiment result and explanation regarding the gathered 
insights, and (3) Conclusion that serve as closing remarks to 
conclude the whole study.

II. METHODOLOGY 
This research used several stages of computational 
methods to extract features from the images, perform 
dimensionality reduction, and feature selection. The resulting 
tabular dataset from feature extraction was divided into 80% 
for training and 20% for testing. Then, standardization was 
performed on both sets, with the testing set transformed using 
the scaler fitted previously on the training set to prevent data 
leakage. As an additional strategy to avoid data leakage, a 5-
fold cross-validation method was employed during the 
experimentation on the training set. The complete 
methodological workflow of this research is presented on Fig. 
1. 
A. Dataset 
The proposed study was conducted using a publicly 
available oil palm images dataset that can be accessed 
through https://zenodo.org/records/11114885 [15]. This 
dataset was acquired by professionals from University of 
Technology Malaysia in 2024, containing high quality of 160 
ripe, 60 overripe, and 130 unripe fruits. Most of the images 
contain a single, non-overlapping fruit bunch with proper 
lighting condition, which makes the dataset well-suited for 
learning the key features associated with oil palm fruit 
ripeness classification. The data was split into 80/20 
proportions during the cross-validation process. After 
selecting the required subset of data, the experiment was 
conducted through a series of computations which 
categorized into three main parts such as image features 
extraction, dimensionality reduction, and feature selection. 
The data processing procedures are described in detail 
through the following subsections. 
B. Image Features Extraction 
In processing image data, traditional ML methods require 
manual feature extraction to obtain necessary predictors. 
Therefore, several computer vision methods are applied to 
each image to extract histogram, color-related (indexes and 
statistics) features, local binary pattern (LBP), gray level co-
occurrence matrix (GLCM), and Haralick texture features.

Haralick features are derived from GLCM [21], which 
calculates the spatial relationships between pixels in grayscale 
images. In this study,  these features are obtained from

the image. The final feature vector H ∈ℝ is an averaged 
value of the 13 features across all four directions. 
C. Dimensionality Reduction 
The previously described feature extraction process 
produces 354 features per image, whereas the entire dataset 
consists of only 350 images. This so called “large-p, small-n” 
problem, where the number of features exceeds the number of 
observations [22], poses challenges for effective model 
training and increases the risk of overfitting. This discrepancy 
can be handled through implementation of dimensionality 
reduction to reduce the feature space to a more manageable 
and statistically appropriate size [23]. 
To perform dimensionality reduction approach, Partial 
Least Square (PLS) is applied to the standardized dataset to 
project the original high-dimensional input data X ∈ℝO×P 
into a lower-dimensional latent feature space T ∈ℝO×R where 
k << p.  PLS works by identifying the directions in the 
feature space that both capture variance in the independent 
variables X and are maximally correlated with the response 
variable y, so that the regression can be performed in lower 
feature space: *V ! WX where q is a matrix containing the 
regression coefficients in the latent space. This technique will 
help to minimize multicollinearity issues that might present in 
the high-dimensional dataset.   
D. Feature Selection 
Feature selection approach is performed following the 
implementation of PLS-based dimensionality reduction. 
Since PLS is originally created to solve regression problems, 
we customized this process to make the evaluation of the 
selected features calculated based on the performance of 
classifier models. In this study, Logistic Regression (LR) [24] 
and Super Vector Classification with Radial Basis Function 
kernel (RBF_SVC) [25] are utilized to generate the 
probability of the prediction using the input from PLS-
dimensionality reduced data. LR is selected to represent 
linear solution while RBF-SVC is selected to represent non-
linear solution to solve the classification task. Through this 
approach, we can get the insights on how the generated 
features correlated with the ripeness level of the oil palm fruit 
images. To promote fairness in the evaluation, the whole 
feature selection experiments were performed using stratified 
5-fold cross-validation strategy. Given a training dataset Y !

The feature extraction process resulted in a total of 354 
features that can be grouped into six distinct categories: multi-
color histogram, color-related (indexes and statistics) features, 
RGB moments, Gray- GLCM, LBP, and Haralick texture 
features. A comparative visualization of these features across 
different ripeness levels is presented in Fig. 2. From this 
figure, certain features show distinguishable separation 
between the ripeness classes, for instance, several features of 
the histogram group and several RGB moment values. Despite 
these preliminary insights, the high dimensionality of the 
feature space makes it challenging to rely only on the average 
value of each feature to identify the most informative 
predictors of oil palm fruit ripeness. Therefore, a more 
scientific approach, such as statistical feature selection, is 
required to systematically determine which features contribute 
the most to the fruit's ripeness level. 
Feature selection was independently carried out using two 
variants of classifier following the PLS for dimensionality 
reduction, namely PLS combined with Logistic Regression 
(PLS-LR) and PLS combined with Support Vector 
Classification using the Radial Basis Function kernel (PLS-
RBF_SVC). As mentioned on the methodology section, both 
experiments was performed within a 5-fold cross-validation 
framework and the evaluation was performed independently 
on each cross validation set. The number of latent components 
for the PLS projection was limited to a maximum of 20 to 
avoid overfitting and maintain model interpretability.  
For the PLS-LR algorithm, the logistic regression 
classifier was configured with max_iter = 1000 to ensure 
convergence during the optimization process. This parameter 
specifies the maximum number of iterations allowed for the 
solver to find the optimal weights. In case of high-dimensional 
feature spaces, setting a higher maximum iteration helps 
prevent early termination before reaching a stable solution. 
The experiment using PLS-LR approach resulted in 14 most 
optimal PLS component that yields to the highest mean 
accuracy, precision, recall, and F-1 score across all 5 test set 
within the 5-fold cross validation, as can be seen in Fig. 3 (a). 
Meanwhile, the PLS-RBF_SVC feature selection was 
conducted using default parameters. In contrast to the prior 
experiment, the PLS-RBF_SVC model resulted in a slightly 
larger number of PLS components, which slightly varied

To interpret the result of dimensionality reduction 
reflected on the PLS components, PLS regression coefficients 
were used to estimate the magnitude of feature importance. It 
is important to note that these coefficients was extracted 
directly from the PLS, rather than from the subsequent 
statistical models (LR and SVC). Table IV lists the top 10 
features with the highest absolute importance values derived 
from the PLS model. It can be seen that some features from 
multi-color histograms (particularly from HSV and YCrCb 
color spaces), LBP, and color statistics (index and moments) 
are present on the list. 
The contribution of HSV and YCrCb histogram-related 
features indicates the ripeness of oil palm fruit will be more 
clearly captured within HSV and YCrCb color space. A 
similar insight also inferred by Zulkhoiri et al. [27] who found 
that transforming RGB images to HSV and YCrCb color

statistical color descriptors also appeared as important 
predictors. This aligns with the known characteristic of oil 
palm fruit ripening, where fruit color shifts gradually from 
dark purple to a brighter reddish color [11]. The 
coloridx_exr_std, which ranked as the third most important 
feature, explains the spatial variability of the Excess Red 
(ExR) index across the This makes sense visually since 
ripening in oil palm bunches often doesn’t happen uniformly, 
with red patches appearing in certain regions while others 
remain dark. In contrast, unripe bunch tend to have a more 
consistent dark purple color across the fruits. As such, the 
spatial variation in ExR act as a meaningful feature for 
determining the ripeness stages.  
Meanwhile, the inclusion of 2 LBP features (lbp_3  and 
lbp_21) on the list is less straightforward to explain. These 
features might describe a wide range of subtle texture changes 
such as micro-structure variations, shadow patterns, 
reflectance distribution, all of which can be influenced by the 
fruit ripening process. One plausible explanation is that 
overripe fruits tend to develop longer and more dominant 
spines that partially cover the fruit surface, creating a 
distinguishable texture characteristic. However, this part 
would require further investigation to precisely understand the 
effect of certain visual appearance to the value of LBP-related 
features. 
C. Limitation and Future Research Directions 
Although the proposed framework can provide consistent 
results and interpretability the stratified 5-fold cross validation 
setting, the model might find difficulties in generalizing 
overlapping, noisy, low-light, and damaged fruit bunch image, 
as the dataset only contains hundreds of high-quality images. 
Based on these limitations future research could be continued 
towards two major direction: (1) a more in depth investigation 
to the feature study, by performing ablation study and 
sensitivity analysis under varying lighting, resolution, or 
partial feature removal, (2) scale the usability of the process 
by training a deep learning image classifier or object detectors 
using multimodal data fusion strategy, by combining images 
from multiple color space with the local texture features to 
achieve higher model’s performance, and (3) test the model 
on the non-ideal or damaged fruit bunch images to examine its 
performance on real world application. However, while the 
second option holds promise for usable product development 
in agriculture industry, it would require a much larger and 
more diverse dataset to ensure model convergence and 
generalization. Therefore, major expansion of the image 
collection, potentially into the range of several thousand 
labelled samples, would be necessary.
