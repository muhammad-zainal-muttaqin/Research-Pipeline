Abstract— This research aims to recognize and count the 
number of cocoa pods on trees using an Unmanned Aerial 
Vehicle (UAV). The main challenge in this research is to 
estimate the yield of stacked cocoa fruits and count the number 
of fruits on the trees. Therefore, this research proposes an 
algorithm that accurately estimates cocoa fruit yield. Image 
processing is done through many stages: image segmentation, 
boundary determination, shape analysis, and overlap analysis. 
The proposed algorithm can recognize and calculate the 
number of cocoa pods on a tree accurately through overlap 
analysis between the pods found. The results show that the 
detection accuracy after applying overlap analysis reaches 
94.5%. 
Keywords— Fruit counting, Cocoa, Image segmentation, 
Shape analysis, Size estimation, Oversegmentation identification. 
I.INTRODUCTION 
Estimating the amount and timing of cocoa harvest is 
essential for farmers to know the condition of harvested 
products in the plantation industry. In large plantations, yield 
estimation requires a lot of resources, time, and substantial 
costs. However, recent technological advancements and 
research have led to efficient automation solutions to 
improve accuracy and reduce costs in the yield estimation 
process. 
Queuing at supermarket checkouts can be frustrating. 
This topic is one of the challenging fields where Machine 
Vision Technology is used in various research fields, 
including agriculture. As the initial stage in crop yield 
estimation. Crop yields estimation on plantations, using 
object recognition and image processing methods for 
detection and counting the number of fruits [1-2]. 
Agricultural drones help farmers to patrol the fields, 
supervise fruit growth, monitor crop quality, etc. Farming 
activities have become more effective and efficient with 
drones and image-processing technology [3-4]. 
In facing the challenge of detecting the number of cacao 
pods on a tree, object recognition methods can be a solution. 
Using simple and complex algorithms can separate 
overlapping areas based on differences in the colour and 
shape of cocoa pods. Object recognition techniques can help 
accurately identify and count how many cacao pods are, 
using visual characteristics such as the colour and shape of 
the pods. In this case, selecting an appropriate algorithm will 
largely depend on the complexity of the overlapping cocoa 
pods, the variation in colour and shape, and the available 
computational resources. Thus, object recognition methods 
can effectively address the problem of counting cocoa pods 
on a tree in the cocoa farming industry [5-6].  
Several studies on fruit detection and counting trees have 
obtained good results using several methods to overcome 
factors such as occlusion, fruit position to the camera, and 
light. Despite the development of various methods, there are 
still other challenges in fruit detection, such as shape analysis, 
size estimation, and identification of overlapping objects.

distances. Eighty-five images of cocoa pods were taken with

Adriyono et. al, (2017) propose a method to detect and 
identify the number of mango fruits in closed conditions

fruits were detected. 
Shreya et. al, (2017) use colour features to automatically 
detect and classify apples' ripeness level with image 
processing techniques for extracting colour components (R, 
G, B) and using artificial neural networks to classify and 
calculate ripe and immature apples based on colour 
components. The accuracy is 98.1% when the three attributes 
are used as input.

Lin et. al, (2018) proposed a strawberry flower detection 
method based on the current depth-level-based region visual 
representation architecture using Faster R-CNN. The

conditions, such as lighting, size, and location changes, even 
if the strawberry flowers are obstructed by leaves or

it can use multiple convolution layers in RPN without

development carried out for fruit yield prediction. The 
method consists of several steps, including edge detection, 
labelling of connected regions, and detection using the

design an automatic monitoring system for plant health in the 
future. The segmentation results achieved more than 98%

Hough transform (CHT) and colour threshold iteratively as 
image processing methods to design an accurate monitoring 
system for apple fruits on trees with precise detection and 
calculation. Apple fruits identified with clearly visible CHT 
are more favourable for early harvest than partially visible 
fruits. The proposed methodology can detect and count fully 
and partially visible apple fruits. The advantage of Circular 
Hough Transform is its ability to detect and count 
overlapping fruits. Evaluation of the results using the 
proposed algorithm showed an accuracy of 97.70%.  
Mokrane et. al, (2019) present an offline flight planning 
for UAVs (Unmanned Aerial Vehicles) that aims to generate

automatic calculation. The approach involves three main 
steps: photogrammetry using UAV, derivation of ROI 
(Region of Interest) map, and generation of coverage path 
planning using a Rotating Caliper Algorithm. The proposed 
approach allows the UAV system to generate coverage paths 
involving take-off and landing points. The generated path can 
be sent to the UAV operator to carry out area coverage in 
fruit counting.

UAV (Unmanned Aerial Vehicle) as data input. Data 
collection was carried out in clear weather conditions. The 
drone flew between rows of cocoa trees with an average 
distance between each tree of 3 meters. Data collection was 
carried out with the scenario in Figure 1.

1) Input Image 
This process is the first step in the automatic fruit 
counting system. In this process, images containing fruits are 
fed into the system. The image can be a photo, video, or 
visual image of the fruits to be counted.  
2) Image noise removal 
 In this process, input images containing noise, such as 
noise or other visual disturbances, are removed or mitigated. 
The aim is to improve the image quality so that the next 
process, which is segmentation, can be performed accurately.  
3) Segmentation 
The segmentation process separates fruits from the 
background or other objects in the image. Usually, the 
segmentation methods used are object detection or 
segmentation methods based on colour, texture, or other 
visual features. The goal is to identify and separate each 
detected fruit. 
4) Shape analysis 
This process analyses their shapes or contours after the 
fruits are segmented. The method used can be shape-based 
feature extraction, such as geometric feature extraction or 
statistical feature extraction from the contours of the fruits. 
The aim is to recognize or identify fruits based on their shape 
or geometric characteristics.  
5) Size estimation 
 After the shape analysis process, the following process is 
to estimate the size of the fruits. The method used can be 
either measuring the dimensions of the fruits in the image or 
using a mathematical or statistical model to estimate the size 
of the fruits. The goal is to calculate the size or dimensions 
of the fruits accurately. 
6) Over-segmentation 
The over-segmentation process is a process to address 
segmentation issues that may result in over-segmentation or

A.  Acquisition Processing And Image De-Noising 
Image acquisition, processing, and noise reduction are 
essential steps in image processing. The acquisition involves 
taking an image from its sources, such as a camera or scanner. 
Once the image is acquired, noise or disturbances in the 
image can reduce its quality. 
1) Image acquisition  
The importance of image processing as input in image 
processing must be addressed. One of the crucial aspects is 
to evaluate and improve the results of measuring the size of 
cocoa pods measured at various distances and make 
improvements or adjustments, if necessary, to improve the 
consistency of cocoa pod size. Taking the images under 
similar lighting conditions is also essential, as the light source 
can affect the image segmentation results. All recorded 
images of cocoa pods were taken from the community 
plantation but from different positions, regular distances, and 
light sources. This research should have an appropriate data 
diversity management method to avoid plagiarism. 
2) Removal of image noise 
In the analysis of image acquisition methods, some 
aspects that can be analyzed include image resolution, sensor 
type, exposure method, acquisition time, pre-processing, and 
hardware and software settings. This analysis can help 
understand the performance, image quality, and influence of 
various factors in image acquisition methods. So we use a 
smoothing filter to remove the effects and reduce interference 
in the image. After testing, a 3x3 Gaussian filter is selected 
to remove noise in the input image. 
B. Image segmentation 
This process is done to separate objects or regions of 
interest in an image into separate parts, so that they can be 
identified, analyzed, or further processed separately. 
Selection of the right segmentation method, good image 
acquisition, proper image processing, including noise 
removal, are very important in producing accurate 
segmentation results. In this study, the image segmentation 
method used is clustering based on RGB color space and 
thresholding algorithm. These are two important steps in the 
image segmentation process.

3) Finding Contours 
After successfully separating the cocoa pods from the 
background, the next step is identifying the object region and 
marking the object boundary using the contours method [11]. 
This method generates a curve that follows the contours of 
the binary object and stores it as a point vector on the 
boundary. After performing the image segmentation stage, 
the resulting binary image will be filtered using a median 
filter with a size of 5x5 to remove noise still in the image. 
Then we use the findContours() function using the open cv 
library to find the object's outline in the image.

area and boundary of each identified part [12].

E. Identify Oversegmentation 
A problem occurs in Otsu's algorithm when over-
segmentation occurs in the image when the image object is 
segmented into more overlapping or redundant regions. 
Therefore, we must separate the overlapping regions using 
adaptive segmentation to overcome the over segmentation 
problem.

2) Adaptive Segmentation 
The hybrid algorithm developed in this study aims to 
reduce overlapping areas in the image segmentation process 
to reduce over segmentation. This algorithm is called 
adaptive segmentation, which uses the number of edges of 
the overlapping region to create an adaptive threshold in the 
binary threshold, as shown in Figure 8. 
 
Fig. 9. Oversegmentation detection diagram 
 
This process is iterated according to the overlapping 
region, as seen in Figure 10; the basic steps are explained as 
follows: 
• Performing overlap detection between segmented 
objects, such as by comparing the corresponding 
objects' position, size, or shape. 
• Using specific metrics or criteria to determine the 
overlap between objects, such as overlap area, 
minimum distance between objects, or how many 
pixels coincide between objects. 
• Decides which objects are considered overlapping 
objects based on a pre-defined threshold or value. 
• Performs the process of merging or separating 
overlapping objects by merging objects with similar 
attributes or separating objects with a particular 
overlap area. 
• Determine the number of objects covered in one 
region based on the contour size. 
• Repeating the above process if necessary, go to step 
3; otherwise, go to step 7. 
• Re-evaluate or verify the overlapping object detection 
results to ensure the quality and accuracy of the image 
analysis results obtained. 
• After the recognition step, the method synthesizes 
those regions to produce the final result. 
After the identification process, this method is utilized to 
synthesize these regions to produce the outcome.

IV. CONCLUSION 
In this study, we used image processing methods to 
identify, detect, and estimate the production of cocoa pods in 
plantations. The most crucial thing in this research is to 
divide cocoa pods into three categories, namely single, 
partially covered, and overlapping, to identify each other. 
This method has proven effective in large areas and can retain 
information in smaller areas. 
This research can be used in agricultural supervision and 
efficiently managing cocoa fruit yields. It can serve as a basis 
for developing more advanced technology in counting fruit 
on trees using drones. The results of this research are 
expected to contribute significantly to developing high-
accuracy tree fruit-counting technology in agriculture and 
plantations, especially in cocoa cultivation.
