Abstract. Indonesia lacks skilled workers capable of determining the ripeness of oil
palm fruits, which can aﬀect oil palm production. In this evolving era, Artiﬁcial Intel-
ligence (AI) has signiﬁcantly improved ripeness detection systems, fostering innovation
and competitiveness in agriculture. This paper presents an analysis and comparison of
methods to determine Fresh Fruit Bunch (FFB) ripeness using YOLOv8s and YOLOv10s.
Data were collected during a visit to an oil palm plantation operated by PTPN 4 in Medan,
North Sumatra, Indonesia. 250 images were collected and manually classiﬁed into fold-
ers: “ripe”, “rotten” and “unripe”. Both models successfully identiﬁed the ripeness level
of palm fruits, with YOLOv8 exhibiting better bounding box placement and standard con-
ﬁdence scores. YOLOv8’s eﬀectiveness reached an mAP50 value of 0.995 after 25 and 50
training periods. Similarly, YOLOv10s reached values of 0.985 and 0.977 after 50 and
25 training periods. However overall, YOLOv8s has achieved better results in comparison
for its accuracy.
Keywords: YOLO, Ripeness detection, Deep learning, Oil palm fruit, Artiﬁcial intelli-
gence
1. Introduction. Oil palm is the primary source of vegetable oil worldwide, due to its
high productivity compared to other oil-producing plants. With over 50% of the world’s
palm oil production coming from Indonesia [1], it plays a crucial role in both the Indone-
sian and global economies. In 2021, export volume increased by 0.6% with export value
reaching US$35 billion – 52% higher than the export value in 2020 of US$22.9 billion
[2]. Over a two-year period, industrial plantations expanded by 116,000 hectares, mark-
ing a 54% increase over the previous year. Regionally, the rise in deforestation linked to
palm oil expansion was the most prominent in Kalimantan and Papua, while Sumatra
experienced a somewhat smaller increase [3]. In 2021, export volume increased by 0.6%
with export value reached US$35 billion or 52% higher than the export value in 2020 at
US$22.9 billion [2]. Industrial plantations expanded by 116,000 hectares over two years,
representing a 54% increase over the year before. Upon regional analysis, the increase in
deforestation due to palm oil expansion was most signiﬁcant in Kalimantan and Papua,
with a slightly lower rise observed in Sumatra [3].
However, despite the high productivity, oil palm encounters diﬃculties in its processing,
speciﬁcally in determining the classiﬁcation of ripeness. The fruits on a single oil palm tree
or plantation tend to diﬀer in their stages of ripeness, complicating the sorting process
because only ripe fruits should be harvested to maximize oil quality and productivity.
Additionally, sorting oil palm fruits requires a large labor force. Identifying and separating
ripe fruits from unripe ones requires skill and precision, and in some circumstances, a lack

workers with expertise or skilled at determining the ripeness of oil palm fruits is still
limited, which can hinder palm oil productivity.
Artiﬁcial intelligence technologies are increasingly being used in the agricultural sector
due to their popularity. The shift towards AI-driven solutions, especially in the palm oil
sector, is expected to improve eﬃciency and precision while meeting the rising need for
advanced agricultural practices. Recent innovations in artiﬁcial intelligence, as mentioned
in [4], have contributed signiﬁcantly to improving the accuracy and scalability of ripeness
detection systems, thereby fostering innovation and competitiveness in the agricultural
industry.
This paper also presents an analysis and comparison of the diﬀerent approaches and
methods for analyzing the ripeness of FFBs (Fresh Fruit Bunches) by the previous re-
searchers. The methods explored by previous researchers include R-CNNs (Regional-based
Convolutional Neural Networks) [5], CNNs (Convolutional Neural Networks) [4,6,7], KNN
(K-Nearest Neighbor) [8,9], ResNet50 [9], YOLO (You Only Look Once) [9-14], Eﬃcient-
Det4 [10], DenseNet [15,16,22], and AlexNet [16]. These algorithms provide signiﬁcant
advances in accuracy and eﬃciency. R-CNN and CNN models frequently require exten-
sive computer resources and long training times [5]. With large data sets, KNN may be
memory-consuming and ineﬃcient for prediction [8]. ResNet50, while powerful, is prone
to overﬁtting and requires careful tuning [4]. Although YOLO is fast, it may struggle with
small objects and precise localization [9]. EﬃcientDet4 and DenseNet can be diﬃcult to
implement and optimize (with a slightly lower accuracy), which results in increasing costs
[10]. AlexNet, as an older architecture, may not perform as well as newer versions in terms
of accuracy and eﬃciency [6].
Due to YOLO’s popularity as the algorithm for object detection, the purpose of this
research is to compare the detection accuracy of palm oil fruit ripeness using YOLOv8s
and a newly developed algorithm, YOLOv10s, which is based on YOLOv8s. The deci-
sion is based on previous research comparing YOLOv7 with YOLOv8, which claims that
YOLOv8 has improved capabilities, especially in identifying small objects [17]. Although
in the study of [18], which still uses YOLOv7 to detect objects in real time, it can still
achieve quite high accuracy.
This paper is structured into several chapters. The introduction presents an overview
of the core idea of this research. The literature review summarizes existing research on
palm oil fruit ripeness detection, comparing various deep learning models, especially those
using the YOLO algorithm. The methodology describes the datasets, research setup,
and deep learning algorithms employed in this study, including data preprocessing and
model training procedures. The results and discussion present the ﬁndings of the research,
analyzing the performance of diﬀerent models in terms of accuracy, precision, recall,
and other relevant metrics. The results will then be discussed, suggesting directions for
improvement. This paper concludes with a summary of key ﬁndings of the study and
suggests directions for future research.
2. Literature Review. In the past few years, palm oil ripeness detection has been
developed through the integration of various learning techniques, such as those presented
in [19] relying on the K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN) algorithm and achieving an average
accuracy of 65% on a limited dataset. Although these methods have great potential for
machine learning, more advanced techniques are still required.
Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have been frequently used in recent research.
For instance, [4] applied CNNs to larger datasets and achieved a signiﬁcant accuracy
improvement of 76% compared to traditional methods. Another recent advance was doc-
umented in [6,7], which achieved accuracies of 95.6% and 98%, by using CNNs on datasets

In another study [5], Faster R-CNN achieved 80% accuracy with a smaller dataset of

v3 and ResNet50 on a dataset of 299 images, Faster R-CNN achieved an accuracy of

time object detection tasks. Comprehensive research by [10] compared YOLOv8 and Eﬃ-
cientDet4 using a large dataset comprising 8299 images. YOLOv8 has achieved an impres-
sive accuracy of 97.6%, underscoring the eﬃciency and advancements of YOLO models,
EﬃcientDet4 closely followed with 94%. The research by [16] compared DenseNet with
AlexNet on a dataset of 400 images, achieving accuracies of 86% and 77%.
Furthermore, the research by [15] achieved 92.5% accuracy by using DenseNet on a
high-resolution dataset of 160 images, categorized into raw, under-ripe, and ripe stages,
sized at 3264 × 2448 pixels and stored in JPEG format. This further validated the eﬀec-
tiveness of DenseNet architectures in image classiﬁcation tasks. YOLOv4 has been used
in other research, such as those by [11] and [19], and has achieved accuracies of up to 99%,
indicating YOLOv4’s potential in real-time applications. Overall, this research represents
signiﬁcant progress in the use of deep learning algorithms for palm oil fruit ripeness de-
tection, which has been studied on numerous occasions. The YOLO method achieved the
highest accuracy and shows great potential for practical applications in improving palm

3. Methodology. In order to gather and process the data from the visit to one of PTPN
4’s oil palm plantations in Medan, North Sumatra, Indonesia, multiple methods were
used in this study. To get the best outcomes, these actions were necessary. The procedure
comprised gathering datasets, pre- and post-processing images, and training and testing
algorithms. The ‘s’ models of YOLOv8 and YOLOv10 algorithms were employed in this
research. The phases of putting research into practice are shown in Figure 1.
The data collection process involved capturing images of harvested palm fruits using
an iPhone 12 camera. There were two diﬀerent kinds of photos that were captured: close-
ups of individual fruits and group photos of mature fruit clusters by visiting the palm
plantation harvesting site to acquire these photos. For the training data, every fruit and
every cluster of ripe fruits were photographed from several perspectives.

During the dataset collection process, 250 images were collected and manually cate-
gorized into three folders: ripe, rotten, and unripe. While this categorization was not
strictly necessary, it facilitated the upload and annotation process. The dataset was then
uploaded to Roboﬂow, one folder at a time, with images annotated according to their
pre-assigned categories. Out of the 250 images collected, we selected 80 of them based
on their superior quality. These selected images underwent several augmentation post-
processing steps, including ﬂipping and rotating (15 and 90 degrees), resulting in a total
of 185 images.
A popular real-time object recognition technique in computer vision and machine learn-
ing called YOLO (You Only Look Once) is used as the main algorithm for detection in
this research, as it is known that YOLO is suitable for faster object detection and its
accuracy [21]. YOLOv8s was built by Ultralytics while YOLOv10s was developed by the
researchers at the Tsinghua University using YOLOv8s as the main base for the algorithm.
YOLOv8s and YOLOv10s were the primary algorithms employed for comparison in this
study. The previous researcher of YOLOv10s [20], claims that YOLOv10s was built using
YOLOv8s as the base model, but with increased capability and eﬃciency. In comparison
to the most recent YOLOv9-C version, it also has 25% fewer parameters (see Figure 2)
[20].

5. Conclusions. This research focused on utilizing YOLOv8s and YOLOv10s algorithms
to enhance the detection and classiﬁcation accuracy of palm oil fruits. We gathered a
dataset from an oil palm plantation in Medan, North Sumatra, Indonesia, consisting of
250 images. The images were categorized based on ripeness levels: ripe, rotten, and unripe.
The images were augmented through techniques such as ﬂipping and rotating to enhance
the model’s reliability.

YOLOv8s achieved an mAP50 score of 0.995 when trained for both 25 and 50 epochs.
Similarly, YOLOv10s scored 0.985 after 50 epochs and 0.977 after 25 epochs. Notably,
YOLOv10s exhibited greater eﬃciency with 24.5 GFLOPS compared to 28.4 GFLOPS
for faster processing. Each detection results successfully identify ripeness classiﬁcation of
the palm fruit with a minimal diﬀerence, but YOLOv8s shows more accurate bounding
box placements and higher conﬁdence scores.
In conclusion, this research compared YOLOv8s with its improved version, YOLOv10s,
to improve eﬃciency in oil palm production. Although the result showed no signiﬁcant
improvement in precision in ripeness detection, YOLOv10s require 25% fewer parameters.
This research also promotes sustainable practices in palm oil production. Future studies
could expand on these ﬁndings to investigate further enhancements and applications in
agricultural ﬁelds.
