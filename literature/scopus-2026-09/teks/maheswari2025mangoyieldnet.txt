MangoYieldNet: Fruit yield estimation for mango orchards 
using DeepLabv3 + with ResNet18 architecture

Abstract
Most yield estimation in orchards is performed manually, with the vital limitations of 
requiring experts, making subjective decisions and incurring elevated charges. This 
problem has been gradually overcome through modern farming techniques by develop-
ing an intelligent model for fruit yield estimation using deep learning, one of the emerg-
ing areas due to its high precision results. This study focuses on estimating yield through 
MangoYieldNet, which detects and counts mangoes from tree images collected from the 
orchards using DeepLabv3+ (a DL-based semantic segmentation architecture). After the 
sampling process, a total of 556 images (from 278 trees) were captured and annotated. 
Using image augmentation techniques (reflection, rotation and translation) a dataset 
of 1152 images was developed; it was split in an 80:20 ratio for training and validation, 
respectively. DeepLabv3+ with ResNet18 consists of 100 deep layers including convolu-
tional layers, ReLU activation function, batch normalization, pooling, fully connected and 
softmax layers by which feature extraction can be done smoothly without missing any 
important feature that corresponds to fruits even in most of the challenging conditions of 
orchards. The accuracy values of 99.96% and 99.47% were obtained in the training and val-
idation phases, respectively. The proposed method outperformed other DL-based seman-
tic segmentation architectures in terms of accurate object delineation and shorter execu-
tion time. The architecture was tested with 30 unobserved images and revealed a mean 
accuracy and a mean intersection over union of 96.5% and 95.97%, respectively. Finally, 
the segmented fruits were counted using the circle Hough transform method. The mango 
count was compared with the manual count obtained from the farmers and provided the 
regression coefficient value of 0.98. Then, the final yield in kilograms was estimated for the 
sampled trees. The error percentage between actual and predicted weight is 4.99%. A user-
friendly graphical user interface was developed for estimating mango yield.
Keywords  Intelligent system · Mango yield estimation · Deep learning · DeepLabv3+ with 
ResNet18 · GUI for yield mapping

1  Introduction
In the year 2021–22, 909.64Mmt of fruits were produced globally, including almost all 
the leading varieties of fruits [1]. India is the world’s leading producer of mangoes, with 
over 1000 varieties being cultivated. Mango is rich in all nutrients and is a potent pro-
tector against heart disease, cholesterol reduction and cancer. During the year 2021–2022, 
fresh mangoes were exported from India to all over the world; their total value was about ₹ 
327.45 crores / $ 44.05 million [2].
One factor influencing the budgetary value of any produce is the accurate estimation 
of fruit yield in the orchards. The yield of any crop can be predicted by considering the 
various environmental factors such as soil type, climatic conditions, variety of crop and so 
on. Nowadays precision agriculture is widely used in fruit yield estimation by employing 
Artificial Intelligence (AI) techniques. Srinivasu et al. [3] deployed an explainable AI tech-
nique to classify suitable crops based on the environmental conditions and soil type. Prior 
to harvesting, the fruit yield is usually estimated manually by randomly selecting the small 
areas in the orchard. Then the whole orchard estimation was done based on the results of 
the sampled area. Hence, errors are very high due to the differences between individual 
fruit trees and variations in many orchard parameters. Accurate estimation of fruit yield is 
influenced by the factors like differences in cultivation patterns of various fruits and sparse 
or dense planted fruit trees [4]. Usually, experts estimate fruit yield by visual inspection, an 
approach which has the drawbacks of expertise requirement, elevated charges for consulta-
tion and subjective decisions. These problems need to be addressed for better production 
management and marketing [5, 6]. In this regard, several image processing and machine 
learning methods have been studied for detecting and localizing fruits in the orchards based 
on colour, texture, shape and size. In the above methods, the features are extracted from the 
source objects constituting input to the algorithms. It is a tedious process and if the features 
are not extracted well, the fruit could not be identified properly and further counting of 
fruits does not provide the correct results. To overcome these issues, AI based intelligent 
techniques have been deployed. In these techniques, the deep neural network architecture 
itself extracts the features from the source objects. Hence, better detection and localization 
can be obtained. In addition, the availability of large amounts of open-source data and high 
computational resources motivated the researchers to develop an intelligent system for fruit 
yield estimation. The literatures related to fruit yield estimation using deep neural networks 
were explained in the next section.
1.1  Literature survey
Mango detection was done [7] in which the mangoes were differentiated from the back-
ground using color processing (to filter the unrelated colors from the object), followed by 
grayscale filtering and then binary filtering to eliminate the unwanted information from 
the objects. In addition, they employed the Circle Hough Transform (CHT) to measure the 
size of the mangoes and achieved a detection accuracy of 60%. The method for counting 
mangoes was explored [8] using colour (RGB and YCbCr space) and texture segmenta-
tion methods, with the results being compared to the manual count. The Regression Coef-
ficient ­(R2) value of 0.74 was obtained for an overall dataset of 555 images. A model was 
developed [9] for mango detection using a support vector machine classifier by extract-
ing the features from the mango images using the histogram of gradients feature extractor.

The mean Intersection over Union (IoU) and F-score values of 79.38% and 89.38% were 
obtained. The work showed better detection of mangoes in varying lighting conditions, and

illumination variations, partial occlusion and overlapping conditions.
Recent studies have explored machine-vision based systems that use deep neural net-
works [11–15]. To estimate fruit yield, the region of interest needs to be detected and local-
ized for further evaluation which is usually achieved by the segmentation of objects. In 
this regard, semantic segmentation plays a vital role as it classifies each pixel into a par-
ticular class rather than classifying a whole image into a specific class. A few studies have 
been performed on detecting and counting fruit using DL-based semantic segmentation 
architectures recently. A vision-based fruit detection and localization system was proposed 
[16] using Dual attentive fully convolutional Siamese Networks Version2 (DaSNet-V2) to 
guide a robotic harvesting system. A LightWeight model LW-net was used as the back-
bone network for DaSNet-V2 in order to train the RGB depth images obtained from an 
apple orchard. DaSNet-V2 with LW-net obtained the mean IoU of 85.8% for instance seg-
mentation of fruits and 79.5% for semantic segmentation of branches. DaSNet-V2 achieved 
robust and efficient performance among all the architectures analyzed. Further, the work 
can be improved to guide the automatic harvesting robots.
In [17], a system named Central Roundish Object Painter (CROP) and CROP-Shallow 
using U-Net architecture [18] that could recognize the fruits present at the center of the 
RGB image. CROP is deeper than normal U-Net architecture. This work divided the data-
set into three groups namely Data_Fruits, Data_Pears1 and Data_Pears2. The developed 
CROP architecture was initially trained with the Data_Fruits dataset, which comprises 
images of various fruits collected from the Internet. The trained CROP was fine-tuned and 
re-trained using the Data_Pears2 dataset which consists images of pears collected from 
farms. The Data_Pears1 was used as the test dataset for evaluating the CROP architecture 
before and after fine-tuning. The IoU values of 88.2% and 91.7% for the test dataset before 
and after fine-tuning were obtained, respectively. The work dealt with a smaller number of 
training data; accuracy and generalization can be improved with more training data.
A MangoYOLO model was proposed [19] for estimating the mango yield using DL, 
Kalman filtering and Hungarian algorithm by capturing 10fps video frames. Multiple count 
problems were addressed by Kalman filtering which predicts the correct position of the 
fruit and correlation between adjacent frames was achieved using the Hungarian algorithm. 
The proposed method revealed the bias-corrected Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) of 
18fruit/tree tested with 1162 video frames. The actual mango count recorded manually was 
3286 and the architecture using a video tracking system, the mango count was 2050 and 
using the dual-view method (in which the images are captured from both sides) 1322 man-
goes with RMSE of 21.7fruit/tree. The proposed MangoYOLO provided better detection 
than the dual-view method. The work proposed by Hari Chandana et al. [20] detected the 
mango pixels by employing MangoYOLO5 which is the modified architecture of YOLOv5. 
The feedback layer along with two more convolutional layers were removed in the modi-
fied architecture to reduce the overall weight of the network. This will reduce the train-
ing time significantly (60%) when compared with original YOLOv5. Table 1 summarizes 
the reviewed studies of mango yield estimation using DL-based semantic segmentation 
architectures.

The architectures explored in previous studies need to be improved to provide increased 
accuracy, precision, recall and F1-score. To do so, the fruit detection and localization sys-
tems using deep neural network architectures must be explored further. Among various 
DL-based semantic segmentation architectures, DeepLabv3+ is one of the advanced archi-
tectures which uses DeepLabv3 as the encoder module for excellent feature extraction and 
a simple decoder module for exact object boundary delineation [24]. The proposed work 
detects, localizes and counts the number of mangoes present in the tree in an orchard using 
DeepLabv3+ architecture to develop a better yield estimation system.
The contributions of the proposed method are:
•	 Creation of mango dataset for developing a fruit yield estimation model.
•	 Precise mango yield estimation using advanced DeepLabv3+ , a DL-based semantic 
segmentation architecture.
•	 Accurate fruit detection and boundary delineation by fine-tuning the hyperparameters 
of the architecture.
The paper is organized as follows: Sect. 2 describes the proposed methodology namely 
sampling of trees, data collection, annotation, augmentation and implementation using 
DeepLabv3+ architecture. The results are discussed in Sect. 3. Further, mango counting 
along with the total yield of the orchard, size and weight estimation of fruits are described 
in Sect. 4 and Graphical User Interface (GUI) for mango yield estimation is presented in 
Sect. 5. The conclusions with the future scope are presented in Sect. 6.

The proposed intelligent yield estimation system for mango orchard consists of the follow-
ing steps.

2.2  Data collection
After obtaining the sampling size, 278 mango trees were chosen from the total population 
of 1000 trees. The sample included approximately equal numbers of trees having dense, 
sparse and mildly dense structures, to ensure an effective training process. Images of these 
chosen trees were captured using a handheld Digital Single-Lens Reflex (DSLR) camera 
EOS 80D having the specifications of 18–135 mm lens, 24.2MP resolution, Complemen-
tary Metal Oxide Semiconductor (CMOS) sensor and minimum focal length of 18 mm. 
For counting the fruits in each tree, images were captured on both sides of the trees, in the 
daytime with bright sunlight, 5m away from the tree; at this distance, the fruit in an image 
occupies approximately 5-30pixels. In total, 556 original images were obtained from the 
orchard during the mango season (in May). The captured images are 3980×2650 pixels in 
size. In the pre-processing stage, these images are resized into 720×960 pixels for training 
using DeepLabv3+ architecture.
2.3  Data annotation
The original and its corresponding ground-truth images are required to train the Deep-
Labv3+ architecture. For this purpose, the collected 556 images were manually anno-
tated using open access Computer Vision Annotation Tool (CVAT), (https://​www.​CVAT.​
ai). Pixel-wise labeling was conducted on the original images. Two labels were used: the 
mango regions were labeled as mango and the remaining regions (leaves, branches, stem, 
trunk, sky and ground) as background. The original and its annotated ground-truth images 
(as shown in Fig.  1) are the inputs to the DeepLabv3+ architecture. The annotation of 
images plays a vital role in DL-based semantic segmentation tasks since it is used to meas-
ure the performance of the architecture by calculating the mean IoU between predicted and 
annotated images. Images are annotated with more attention to accurately label the fruit 
and background pixels. But a very minimal amount of human error is unavoidable in highly 
occluded trees.

2.5  Mango detection and localization using DeepLabv3+ architecture
DeepLabv3+ module gives excellent performance for object detection tasks. Among 
the various semantic segmentation architectures, DeepLabv3+ network combines the 
Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP) and encoder-decoder module. It uses the Deep-
Labv3 [28] as an encoder along with the atrous separable convolution for better feature 
extraction at multiple scales. Further, depth-wise convolution was used to reduce the 
computational complexity. On the decoder side, a simple as well as worthwhile struc-
ture recovers the location of the object by recovering the spatial information with exact 
object delineation. An increased field of view is needed for the applications (involving 
a complete understanding of a particular scene) in semantic segmentation, where every 
pixel is labeled [29]. In our work, the mangoes were localized by increasing the field of 
view. The spatial resolution was obtained by employing atrous or dilated convolution 
by including a parameter called dilation rate (d) without increasing the number of com-
putations. According to the requirement, d can be varied. A dilated convolution for the

3  Results and discussion
3.1  Training and validation
The program was written in MATLAB (2021a) and run on a computer having a 64-bit 
Intel® Core™ i7-8700 CPU @ 3.20  GHz processor and 32  GB of RAM. The single 
Graphics Processing Unit (GPU) of NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1080 was used to process the 
image data with the Compute Unified Device Architecture (CUDA) cores of 2560, a stand-
ard memory configuration of 8 GB GDDR5X with a memory speed of 10Gbps. Of the 
1152 images, 80% (922) were used for training and 20% (230) for validation. The architec-
ture was trained using the original and annotated images with the tuned training parameters 
(stated in Table 2). The accuracy values of 99.96% and 99.47% and loss values of 0.19% 
and 0.54% were obtained for the training and validation phases, respectively at the final 
iteration. Figure 7 shows the training and validation progress plot for every epoch.
3.2  Testing
For testing the MangoYieldNet with unknown data, 30 test images (using dual view 
method i.e., captured from both sides of 15 trees from an orchard) were chosen. These 
images were fed without annotation to the trained architecture to evaluate the generaliza-
tion capability of the network.
The normalized confusion matrix for the test images is shown in Fig. 8 in which the actual 
class and architecture predicted class are compared for both mango and background pixels. 
The case is True Positive (TP), if the actual and predicted are mango pixels (96.5%), True 
Negative (TN) is denoted as actual and predicted are background pixels (100%), False Positive 
(FP) is the class in which actual is mango pixel but predicted as background pixel (3.5%), False 
Negative (FN) is the class in which actual is background pixel but predicted as mango pixel 
(0%). The mango pixels were wrongly predicted as background especially in the occluded fruit 
condition. A sample test image and its corresponding prediction is shown in Fig. 9.
For semantic segmentation-based tasks, mean accuracy and mean IoU are the essential 
performance metrics calculated since normal pixel accuracy provided inaccurate results in 
the case of class imbalance. Hence, the performance was compared in the proposed method 
by comparing the mean accuracy and mean IoU values. To avoid overfitting issues, the 
architecture was trained with various tree images collected from the mango orchard. The 
testing phase provided better results in unobserved data. In the testing phase, the mean 
accuracy of 96.5% and mean IoU of 95.97% were obtained.

6  Conclusion
Sustainable growth of the nation can be obtained by employing the techniques of pre-
cision agriculture. Before harvesting, one of the vital steps in agriculture is the precise 
yield estimation. This objective can be achieved by the proposed MangoYieldNet which 
can localize, count and estimate the yield of mango fruits from the selected orchard. For 
the testing phase, detection mean accuracy and mean IoU of 96.5% and 95.97% were 
obtained, respectively. The proposed DeepLabv3+ with ResNet18 detected majority of the 
fruits even in challenging conditions like partially occluded fruits and mango bunches in 
overlapped conditions by performing pixel-wise prediction and then applying ASPP. The 
architecture provided better prediction with faster computation than U-Net and SegNet, by 
employing depth-wise convolution. The fruit counting was performed by using CHT. The 
final yield mapping for the actual and predicted mango count was done by linear regres-
sion model and obtained the ­R2 value of 0.98. The encoder-decoder module of DL-based 
semantic segmentation architecture helps the stakeholders for better decision making prior 
to harvesting, profit or loss estimations and decisions for logistics. Future work will focus 
on deploying this proposed method into the comprehensive robotic fruit harvesting system 
thereby developing a complete artificial intelligent product for fruit picking.
