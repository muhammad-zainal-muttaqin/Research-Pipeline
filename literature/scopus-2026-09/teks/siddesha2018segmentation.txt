Abstract—Segmentation is one of the challenging step in image
analysis. It is very cumbersome process for the region with
complex background. Segmentation techniques can be either
of supervised or unsupervised in nature. In this paper, we
explored both supervised and unsupervised techniques for the
segmentation of oil palm crop bunch from the tree image. This
work has a vital importance in Harvesting which is one of the
important stages in crop management. Right time and proper
crop harvesting will enhance the quality. Here we explored Hill
climbing, Growcut, Random Walker and MSRM algorithm as a
part of supervised techniques and K-Means, Fuzzy-C-Means as
un-supervised techniques. The performance of these algorithms
are demonstrated by conducting experiment with dataset of 100
images.
Keywords—Oil palm, Segmentation,K-Means, Fuzzy C Means,
Hill climbing, Grow cut, Random walker, Maximal Similarity
based Region Merging.
I. INTRODUCTION
Precision agriculture is picking up very rapidly in today´s
world in order to provide the rising demand in supplying
crops. Lot of technologies are employed to arrive at best
and quality food products. Machine or Computer vision is
playing a crucial role in managing the voluminous data for
decision making in the areas like harvesting, grading and
disease detection. This helps farmers to lead a comfortable life.
The automation of different activities in agriculture provides
a quality output by removing ambiguity.
Harvesting is one of the crucial phases in crop management,
the different parameters like maturity, time and way of har-
vesting crops plays a vital role in improving the quality of the
crop. To automate this task ﬁrst and foremost step is to detect
the crop bunch and extract them from its background. Here
we explored some techniques to segment the oil palm crop
from its background. This task is tedious as the quality of the
image is based on proper lighting while image capturing and
the difference between crop bunch and their background little
variation in their color, texture etc.
With voluminous and bulk data, extraction of oil palm crop
bunch is very much required in harvesting. Attempts have
been made in implementing the machine vision techniques in
precision agriculture for various applications. The works have
been for harvesting, disease detection in crops like Mango
[19], Potato [26], Tea [6], Tobacco [3], Coffee [13], Tomato
[5],[7] etc.
From the literature we can see the major work is done on oil
palm crop bunch grading and few attempts have been reported
for segmentation. A method proposed [1] to treat color of Oil
palm in HSI (Hue, Saturation and Intensity) color space and
applied multivariate discriminant analysis. Depending upon
the quality feature evaluated improper classiﬁcation by the
vision system varied from 5 to 12%. Also attempts have
been made [20] to automate the grading system for Oil palm
bunches using RGB color model. An intelligent real-time
system developed [11] for automatic weeding identiﬁcation for
Oil palm plantation using image processing. Three techniques
of image processing, involving statistical approach Gray Level
Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM) and structural approach Fast
Fourier Transform (FFT) and Scale Invariant Feature Transfor-
mation (SIFT), have been used successfully for weed recogni-
tion for classiﬁcation. [24] analyzed the outer surface colors of
Oil palm fresh fruit bunches (FFB) to automatically grade the
fruits into unripe, ripe and over ripe. A model [35] proposed
for automated grading system for Oil palm fruit is developed
using the RGB color model and artiﬁcial fuzzy logic. A model
was proposed[23] for ripeness classiﬁcation of Oil palm fresh
fruit bunch (FFB) using Artiﬁcial Neural Network (ANN).
The color features were extracted from the images and fed to
ANN for learning. The performance of ANN was measured
by testing the network with independent test data. A work
carried out for crop bunch segmentation of arecanut[27] and
Coconut[28] crops.
Oil Palm belongs to Arecaceae family and is a native of
West Africa. India is the largest consumer of palm oil in the
world, utilizing around 17 percent of total world consumption.
Indonesia and Malaysia are the leading oil palm producers
contributing 40.8 per cent and 40.3 per cent to the world
production respectively. From the above literature survey it
is clear that only a few work have been reported related to oil
palm bunch grading and there is no information available on
the work on oil palm crop bunch extraction.
Harvesting is the process of crop extraction from the plant.
Selection of matured crop at right time improves the quality.
The detection of crop bunch is a very important decision
making process. An efﬁcient machine vision model is required
for the automation of this process. Selecting and segmenting
the oil palm crop bunches plant can be carried out using
image segmentation techniques. This process of segmentation
is highly challenging due to complex background. The back-

II. PROPOSED METHODS
In this work, different segmentation techniques like, Color
based K-Means Clustering, Fuzzy C Means, Hill climbing,
Random walker, Grow-cut and Maximum Similarity based
Region Merging (MSRM) have been explored for oil palm
crop bunch extraction.
A. K-Means Clustering
Is one of the familiar and well known non-deterministic,
numerical method of unsupervised nature used for low level
image segmentation. On applying this on the image, it creates
a speciﬁc number of disjoint (non-hierarchical) partitions or
clusters. This well suits to the condition in the clusters are
globally generated. Because of its unsupervised nature this
algorithm does not require any human intervention at the
process of separating the fore ground from back ground
of the given image. The main aim of this algorithm is in
optimizing and joining the partitioning choices focuses around
the set of clusters initially deﬁned by user[22],[29],[21]. The
applications of this clustering algorithm is for division on the
images having complex color texture, suffers with different
types of issues. First and the major issue is initialization of
the initial cluster and the second is issue is because of the
region which is missing and connected along with the space
partitioning procedure [18],[33],[9].
B. Fuzzy C-Means Clustering
The working procedure of this algorithm done with the
assignment of the membership of each data points to each
corresponding cluster center based on the distance of the data
point with the center of the cluster. If the data is nearer to
the cluster center means its membership is more towards the
particular cluster center. The summation of the membership
of each data point is equal to one. This best suits to the
overlapped data sets and is better in comparison with K-means
algorithm [2],[12].
C. Hill climbing
The hill climbing technique has two segmentation steps;
(1) it identiﬁes cluster with local maxima from a global three-
dimensional color histogram of an image; (2) it relates the
image pixels with the identiﬁed local maxima. Consequently
it generates several regions. Its working procedure starts from
computing image’s one dimensional color histogram, with the
hue values against the existing each color’s pixels. Then starts
with color histogram’s non-zero bin and moves upwards till
reaches the peak [30],[25].
D. Random walker
This is an interactive segmentation algorithm, As a ﬁrst
step user marks labels on the small number of pixels on
back ground and foreground of the image known as seeds.
The pixels which are not labeled are considered to release a
random walker and the probability is computed in such a way
that random walker of each pixel meets at the seed having
each label. If user creates N seeds having different label, then
for each pixel the probability need to be computed, whenever
a random walker arrives ﬁrst at each seed after leaving that
pixel. The computation is done analytically solving the system
of linear equations. Upon computation of the probabilities of
each pixel, the labels which is assigned to that pixel send a
random walker. The given image is framed as a graph, where
each pixel of the corresponding node is connected by edges
to their neighbor pixels, the similarity between the pixels are
highlighted by the weights of the edges [14].
E. Grow cut
Is a region growing based interactive segmentation algo-
rithm. This works by creating a label which is of same size
of the input image. This label used to capture the points
for selecting the background and foreground of the image
known as scribbles which in turn creates a map label acts as
markers. Cellular automata [31] concept is used for labeling
the pixel. This works iteratively and gives feedback to the user
immediately after computing the segmentation. This works
for images with ’N’ dimensions by performing multi label
segmentation and well suits to hard segmentation tasks.
F. Maximal Similarity Based Region Merging (MSRM)
Segmentation of area of interest (ROI) is a cumbersome
process in case of natural color images due to their complex
background which has similar color, texture when compared to
the foreground and the variations between them are very less.
Most of the supervised segmentation algorithms suffers with
either under segmentation or over segmentation issues. There
are low level image segmentation methods like mean shift
[34],[32],[16] ,watershed [15] etc, which divides the image
into small regions but these have same issues of under or
over segmentation. In order to over come with these issues
there are several interactive or supervised hybrid segmentation
algorithms are reported. Among these algorithms, MSRM is
one of the technique which works on the output of lower
segmentation methods and then applies the region merging
technique[8]. In our work the low or initial segmentation is
done with mean shift [4]. There will be an intermediate stage
after mean shift, for providing information about background
and foreground (object) regions of the image. This information
is provided by drawing the strokes for marking. The similarity
of different regions computed based on the background and
foreground information provided by the markers and the
similar regions are merged based on maximal similarity rule.
After merging, the region of interest or object is completely
segmented from the background of the given image. [10].

III. DATASET AND EXPERIMENTATION
We have created our own data set of around 100 oil palm
tree images with crop bunches. The images were captured
from oil palm farms around Mysuru district. The images
were captured from the ground by focusing the crop bunch
of the tree using NIKON COOLPIX L810 Digital camera
with 26X Zoom. In order to compare the performance of
the segmentation techniques, ground truth images have been
created by manually segmenting the images.
Experimentation conducted on dataset of 100 oil palm tree
images. The segmentation techniques explored here have some
drawbacks. The K Means have ambiguities in selection of
desired number of clusters based on needs, as it has to
be set earlier. Fuzzy C-means method raises the ambiguity
among the choice of features for better results for the given
image. In hill climbing algorithm, ﬁnding the appropriate local
maxima is a problem. The other two algorithms, Random
walker which works on the paths created by the user and
the Grow-cut method works on cellular automata concept,
the marking is must. This reduces the time complexity of the
algorithm.Maximal Similarity based Region Merging (MSRM)
is also a semi automatic and user has to mark the background
and the object; and makes computationally slow. But it is
intolerant to noise and moderately detects multiple objects and
provides better result.
The performance of the different segmentation techniques
discussed is evaluated using Correlation, Jaccard coefﬁcient,
Dice coefﬁcient, Root mean square error (RMSE), Rand Index,
Global Consistency Error and Variation of information . The

Correlation is one of the effective methods used in pattern
matching and recognition.Correlation analysis can be used to
ﬁnd the relationship between two variables (or images). This
value between +1 and -1 is calculated for representing the
linear relationship between two variables. The values which
are approaching to +1 means the segmentation quality is
good and approaching to -1 means the quality is poor. The
Jaccard similarity coefﬁcient, measures the cover of two sets.
The Jaccard coefﬁcient is zero if the two sets are disjoint,
and is one if they are identical. Mean Square Error (MSE)
is the measurement of quality of the image. The quality of
image is poor if its MSE is large. The Root MSE (RMSE)
is calculated using the formula, RMSE =
√
MSE. The
Dice co-efﬁcient is popular quality index for evaluating the
results of image segmentation, . Its range is again from 0 to
1 (1 for perfect match with ground truth).Rand index (RI)
is a quality index originally developed for comparing the
clustering. They are based on counting pairs of objects which
two clustering agree or disagree on. Global Consistency Error
(GCE) measures the extent to which one segmentation can
be viewed as a reﬁnement of the other. If one segment is a
proper subset of the other, then the pixel lies in an area of
reﬁnement, and the error should be zero. If there is no subset
relationship, then the two regions overlap in an inconsistent
manner. Variation of Information (VOI) deﬁnes the distance
between two segmentation as the average conditional entropy
of one segmentation given the other, and thus measures the
amount of randomness in one segmentation which cannot be
explained by the other[17].

images is shown in Table.2, From both the tables it is evident
that MSRM provides better segmentation result.
IV. DISCUSSION
In this work we explored six different segmentation methods
for the extraction of oil palm crop bunches from the tree
images. In K-means, selection of ’K’ values are manually or
randomly or heuristically. It guarantees to converge but not
return the optimal solution. The segmentation quality mainly
depends on the value of K and initial clusters. Choosing an
appropriate ’K’ value and early selection of desired number
of clusters decides the quality is ambiguous. Fuzzy C-means
method raises the ambiguity among the choice of features for
better results for the given image. Hill climbing algorithm
creates ambiguity in ﬁnding the local maxima, which need
not be the efﬁcient one. The Random walker works on the
paths created by the pixels which marked by the user as
fore ground and back ground, here the path will be created
randomly by considering the un marker pixels too, this reduces
the performance. Grow-cut algorithm is popular due to its
key features such as handling of ’N’ dimension images, seg-
ments multi labels support. This starts initially by considering
manually drawn seed and background pixels and propagates
with these as input labels using cellular automata principles
for classiﬁcation of all pixels. All pixels are visited in each
iteration, this quickly leads computational complexity with
increase in image size. MSRM is a semi automatic method
normally works with pre-segmented image (using mean-shift
or watershed method). Later needs with human intervention for
marking the background and the object manually, this affects
the computation. It is noise intolerant and moderately works
for detection of multiple objects.
V. CONCLUSION
After conducting experimentation using above mentioned
methods. From ﬁgure-1 to ﬁgure-6 we can see that K-
means has some background remains after segmentation, FCM
has some over segmentation areas, Hill climbers has some
over lapping, The Random walker and Grow-cut are fast in
computation compared to MSRM, it suffers with little under
segmentation.The MSRM method is partially segments the
foreground, it is better in comparison with other methods.
Further there is need of new segmentation method which
can automatically and completely segments the object with
complex background.
