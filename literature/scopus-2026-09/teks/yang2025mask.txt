A B S T R A C T
Segmentation and counting of grape berries provide crucial reference data for estimating grape yield, thinning, 
and ripeness detection in grape production tasks, particularly in the context of precision viticulture. However, 
conducting such analyses in field environments presents challenges due to the small size and large quantity 
of grape berries, forcing researchers to make trade-offs between experimental conditions and algorithmic 
approaches. Additionally, the absence of a publicly available benchmark for grape berry segmentation in field 
conditions further compounds the challenge.
To address these issues, we propose a novel mask Gaussian kernel representation for grape berries, as well 
as an innovative solution for in-field grape berry segmentation and counting named Mask-GK. This method 
trains neural networks with probability maps generated using berry mask Gaussian kernels and segments grape 
berries by applying the watershed algorithm to probability maps predicted by the neural network. Furthermore, 
we manually annotated the Sichuan Grape Berry Instance Segmentation and Counting Dataset (GBISC dataset). 
This dataset comprises 150 images captured in field environments, featuring grape varieties such as Kyoho, 
Shine Muscat, and Summer Black, including 50,718 labeled grape berry instances in total. In our experiments, 
our proposed method demonstrates reliable performance in grape berry segmentation and counting, achieving 
a MAE of 9.32 for grape berry counting and an AP of 0.665 (IoU threshold: 0.5 to 0.95) for grape berry 
instance segmentation, comparison with previous instance segmentation methods proves its reliability in grape 
berry segmentation and counting. Our source code and the GBISC dataset will be publicly available at
https://github.com/volcanoYcc/Mask-GK.
1. Introduction
Precision viticulture (Mohimont et al., 2022; Farjon et al., 2023), 
also known as precision farming or smart agriculture, employs ad-
vanced technologies, data analysis methods, and scientific expertise to 
monitor grape features and fine-tune grape cultivation practices, thus 
improve grape yield, fruit quality, and wine production. As artificial 
intelligence (AI) continues to advance, direct and automated extraction 
of visual features from grape fruit has become feasible (Saleem et al., 
2021). For instance, the study by Nuske et al. (2014) introduced 
an automated visual yield estimation system that accurately predicts 
grape orchard yields with high resolution. Several popular object de-
tection models, including Faster R-CNN, Mask R-CNN, and multiple 
YOLO models, have been tested for the task of grape detection and 
counting (Santos et al., 2020; Deng et al., 2020). In summary, AI

Due to the clustered nature of grapes, automated extraction of

In order to obtain a comprehensive understanding of grape berry 
characteristics, it is crucial to accomplish instance segmentation and 
counting of grape berries in natural field environment. This task can 
provide richer feature information than mere counting, including the 
size, shape, maturity, and color of each grape berry. However, com-
pared to grape clusters, grape berries are smaller, more numerous, 
and densely distributed, which poses significant challenges for instance 
segmentation and counting. Several studies have investigated the in-
stance segmentation or counting of grape berries. For instance, Shen 
et al. (2022), Deng et al. (2020) and Buayai et al. (2020) employ a 
two-stage approach: first, they detect or segment grape clusters, and 
then they perform berry counting within the detected clusters. Coviello 
et al. (2020) utilizes a crowd counting approach based on density 
maps, where regions with higher density correspond to the positions of 
the berries, achieving accurate grape berry counting. However, these 
methods cannot be extended to obtain grape berry instance segmenta-
tion results. Zabawa et al. (2020) introduced an innovative approach 
for berry counting and segmentation in a laboratory environment. 
They employed a semantic segmentation network that detects and 
isolates individual berry objects using an ‘edge’ class. However, this 
edge segmentation approach may introduce uncertainties when han-
dling outdoor conditions, particularly in intricate and densely packed 
vineyard settings. In summary, existing grape detection methods lack 
research on simultaneous instance segmentation and counting of grape 
berries in practical vineyard scenarios.
Segmenting and counting grape berries in natural field environ-
ments is an extremely challenging task due to the following reasons: 
(1) Compared to laboratory settings, vineyard environments are more 
complex, with grape berries often exhibiting colors and features similar 
to the background, making detection more difficult; (2) The small size 
and high quantity of grape berries limit the applicability of instance 
segmentation methods designed for typical scenarios, such as Mask-
RCNN. This is because most instance segmentation methods require 
identifying the position of each target before segmenting them individ-
ually, and the dense distribution of berry targets in vineyard scenarios 
results in reduced detection accuracy and heavy computational burden 
for such serial segmentation process (Nellithimaru and Kantor, 2019); 
(3) Due to the high labor costs of annotation, the lack of a publicly 
available benchmark for grape berry instance segmentation in field 
settings is also a crucial factor. All these challenges have hindered 
the progress of research on grape berry instance segmentation and 
counting.
Compared to traditional instance segmentation methods, probabil-
ity map-based counting methods can detect all objects in an image 
in parallel, utilizing global features for detecting each target. These 
methods are more suitable for counting tasks in dense scenarios (Cheng 
et al., 2021), and have also achieved excellent performance in grape 
berry counting tasks in vineyard environments (Yang et al., 2024). 
Traditional probability maps, representing targets by square or rectan-
gular Gaussian kernels, cannot represent the segmentation information 
of targets when used as ground truth for neural network training. 
Therefore, they are only suitable for object counting and localization 
tasks, but cannot be applied to instance segmentation tasks. We propose 
a method to represent the instance segmentation information of targets 
using mask Gaussian kernels to generate mask-based probability maps. 
Using these probability maps as ground truth, we propose a novel 
solution for in-field grape berry segmentation and counting named 
Mask-GK, and segment grape berries by applying the watershed al-
gorithm to probability maps predicted by the neural network. This 
approach maintains compatibility with conventional probability map-
based methods for detecting densely clustered grape berries in complex 
field environments. Also, by implementing parallelized segmentation 
of all berries, this approach effectively mitigates the prevalent issues 
of redundant segmentation and mask overlap inherent to traditional 
instance segmentation methods in high-density scenarios. Therefore, it 
allows for accurate grape berry counting and instance segmentation si-
multaneously, as shown in Fig. 1. Additionally, we manually annotated 
a Sichuan Grape Berry Instance Segmentation and Counting Dataset 
(GBISC dataset). This dataset comprises a total of 150 images in field 
environments, including grape varieties of Kyoho, Shine Muscat, and 
Summer Black, with a combined total of 50,718 grape berry instance 
segmentation annotations.
The main contributions of this paper are as follows:
1. We propose a mask Gaussian kernel method to represent the in-
stance segmentation information of targets, and generate mask-
based probability maps using these Gaussian kernels.

2. Related works
(1) Grape berry counting
Detecting the number of grape berries provides crucial reference 
data for grape production tasks such as yield estimation and controlling 
the number of berries within each cluster (Mohimont et al., 2022). 
Numerous studies have emerged to predict grape berry quantities, uti-
lizing both traditional machine vision and deep learning methods. For 
instance, Nuske et al. (2014) introduced an automated visual system for 
yield estimation in vineyards. This system mounts cameras and lighting 
equipment on vehicles, employing three main visual cues – texture, 
color, and shape – to detect grape berry positions through a robust 
classifier and examines three different methods for measuring grape 
cluster size. Although this system uses handcrafted features in machine 
learning and can detect visible berries, it suffers from significant count-
ing errors. Research on grape berry counting based on deep learning has 
advanced more rapidly in recent years compared to traditional machine 
learning methods. For example, in Li et al. (2023), a semi-supervised 
grape berry counting method, CDMENet, based on density mutual 
exclusion, is proposed. This method utilizes VGG-16 as the backbone 
network for extracting image features and introduces auxiliary tasks 
based on density mutual exclusion. These tasks leverage the spatial 
distribution patterns of grape berries, fully utilizing unlabeled data, and 
design a density difference loss function to enhance feature represen-
tation by amplifying differences between features of different density 
levels. Another study, San Woo et al. (2023), proposed a lightweight 
berry counting system based on YOLOv5, capable of running on the 
Android operating system and automating the counting of grape berries 
in grape clusters. This application identifies work clusters and visible 
and partially occluded berries through a single 2D image. Addition-
ally, Yang et al. (2024) innovatively integrates grape cluster detection, 
grape berry counting, and berry counting within each cluster. This 
study proposes a probability map-based grape detection and counting 
framework, PMGDC. This framework predicts probability maps of grape 
clusters and grape berries through an U-Net (Ronneberger et al., 2015) 
architecture neural network and uses three post-processing stages to 
extract detection results from the probability maps, thus completing 
three different grape detection tasks simultaneously. In summary, there 
are now many mature solutions for the task of grape berry counting, 
and they have achieved good results in actual cultivation scenarios.
(2) Grape berry instance segmentation
Grape berry instance segmentation is a more complex detection 
task compared to grape berry counting. This task provides richer fea-
ture information, including the size, shape, maturity, and color of 
each grape berry, making it more valuable for research than grape 
berry counting. Several studies have been conducted on grape berry 
instance segmentation. For instance, Chen et al. (2023) introduced a 
deep learning-based method for grape berry instance segmentation and 
counting. This method constructs a Red Globe grape sample dataset and 
employs an improved Mask R-CNN model to effectively address missed 
detections caused by occlusion. The improved Mask R-CNN model also 
demonstrates good generalization performance on segmentation tasks 
of new grape varieties that were not part of the training set. In Buayai 
et al. (2023), a DNN-based instance segmentation technology is used 
to identify berries within grape clusters. This method transforms the 
problem of thinning single grape clusters into an image classification 
problem through attention-enhanced imaging technology (Ma et al., 
2022, 2024). However, the detection objects in the above studies are 
grape berries within single clusters, and these methods cannot be 
applied to instance segmentation tasks over larger areas in cultivation 
scenarios. Some other research teams have focused on a unique detec-
tion paradigm for grape berry instance segmentation (Zabawa et al., 
2020; Palacios et al., 2022; Zabawa et al., 2022), detecting the edges of 
grape berries through a semantic segmentation network to distinguish 
individual berry instances. This method avoids the high computa-
tional complexity of traditional instance segmentation algorithms in 
dense grape berry detection scenarios, achieving faster inference speeds 
and better results compared to Mask R-CNN. However, the stabil-
ity of semantic segmentation for instance edges is relatively poor, 
requiring multiple manual filtering steps to remove noise such as non-
round berries and berries not fully enclosed by edges. Additionally, the 
contour detection method cannot accurately provide size and shape in-
formation for extremely small and dense grape berries, as the contours 
occupy the space of the berries. Therefore, although these methods 
focus on the detection of grape berries over larger field areas, they 
are limited by the stability of the methods. They require specialized 
machinery to remove background interference or detect targets under 
artificial lighting at night, thus limiting their application scenarios.
Overall, compared to grape berry counting methods, existing grape 
berry instance segmentation methods are fewer in number and have 
limited usage scenarios, making them inflexible for application in 
grape berry segmentation tasks in cultivation environments. The slow 
development of grape berry instance segmentation is influenced by 
several factors. Due to the complexity of foreground and background 
information in detection scenes, the small size, dense distribution, and 
similarity of grape berries to background features, traditional instance 
segmentation methods often perform poorly in field grape berry in-
stance segmentation tasks. Additionally, the current publicly available 
grape datasets lack berry instance segmentation information (Coviello 
et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2020; Santos et al., 2020), making it difficult to 
meet the training needs of instance segmentation methods. To address 
these issues, this paper for the first time publicly releases a field 
grape berry instance segmentation and counting dataset and proposes 
a method suitable for grape berry instance segmentation and counting 
tasks in complex field scenarios, filling a gap in this field.

mask Gaussian kernel to represent grape berry instance segmentation

truth data for neural network training. The remainder of this section 
will provide a detailed explanation of Gaussian kernels and the process 
of generating mask Gaussian kernels.

3.2.2. Obtain instance segmentation result from predicted probability maps
Previous methods generated Gaussian kernels based on object point 
and bounding box annotations, and probability maps composed of those 
Gaussian kernels were only used as ground truth data in model training 
for counting and localization purposes. After training neural networks 
with these probability maps, the probability maps predicted by neural 
networks can only be used to localize objects. That is, the central peaks 
of the Gaussian kernels are object centers. However, the correlation 
between the Gaussian kernel regions and the object regions is not 
significant.
In contrast, the approach proposed in this paper generates Gaussian 
kernels using object mask annotations directly. Therefore, when using 
probability maps composed of these Gaussian kernels as ground truth to 
train the neural network, the Gaussian kernels predicted by the network 
also include shape information about the objects. Consequently, we 
can reverse this process and use the probability maps predicted by 
the network to generate instance segmentation results for all detected 
objects.
In Mask-GK, we employ the watershed algorithm to post-process the 
probability maps predicted by the neural network. First, we identify 
all Gaussian kernels in the probability map whose central peak values 
exceed a given threshold with non-maxima suppression. These peaks 
correspond to the predicted berry object centers, and the number of 
Gaussian kernels represents the berry count. Next, we treat all the 
central points of the Gaussian kernels as the starting points for the 
watershed algorithm, applying the watershed algorithm to partition the 
image into regions corresponding to each Gaussian kernel without rep-
etition. Finally, we consider all points within Gaussian kernel regions 
with values greater than 9e−3 as the instance segmentation results for 
grape berries.
3.2.3. Detection pipeline
In our proposed Mask-GK method, we employ an U-Net (Ron-
neberger et al., 2015) architecture neural network with ResNet as the 
backbone. This network is used to predict the probability maps of 
grape berries, utilizing the mask-based probability maps introduced in 
Section 3.2.1 as ground truth data during model training. The detection 
pipeline of our method is shown in Fig. 6. First, the neural network ex-
tracts features from input images with five convolutional modules from 
ResNet, then restores the high-dimensional features to the same size 
as input images using five upsampling modules, while concatenating 
the up-features and down-features together. Next, a head convolutional 
module is applied to obtain the predicted grape berry probability 
maps. Finally, after neural network prediction, we use non-maxima 
suppression and the watershed algorithm on predicted probability maps 
to get the grape berry counting and instance segmentation results. The 
detection process of Mask-GK can be formulated as 𝑝= 𝑊(𝐻(𝐷(𝐵(𝑖)))), 
where 𝑖 denotes the input image, 𝐵 and 𝐷 represent the backbone and 
decoder, respectively. 𝐻 signifies the head convolutional module, 𝑊 is 
the watershed algorithm, and 𝑝 represents the grape berry segmentation 
and counting results.

4. Experiments and results
4.1. Experimental settings
In our paper, all the experiments were conducted on a consistent 
server with the following specifications: an Intel(R) Xeon(R) Gold 6330 
2.00 GHz CPU, a RTX 3090(24 GB) GPU, running the ubuntu 20.04 
operating system, and utilizing Cuda 11.3. The codebase was compiled 
using PyTorch 1.10.0 and Python 3.8.
To validate the grape berry counting performance of our proposed 
Mask-GK method, we evaluate the berry counting performance of the 
same neural network trained with probability maps generated using 
berry point, bounding box, and mask annotations, with backbones 
including ResNet-18, ResNet-34, ResNet-50, and ResNet-101. To assess 
the instance segmentation performance of Mask-GK, we compare the 
grape berry instance segmentation accuracy of the model using dif-
ferent types of backbones, while consistently training the model with 
mask-based probability maps.
In each training process, our neural network is trained for 100 
epochs. The data loader batch size is set to 1, and the number of 
workers is set to 4. We adopt random cropping, flipping, scaling, and 
HSV enhancement to augment the training data. AdamW is utilized as 
the optimizer, and the learning rate is dynamically adjusted using the 
cosine annealing strategy with the initial learning rate of 1e−4 and the 
end learning rate of 1e−5. MSE loss (Ma et al., 2020) is employed to 
quantify the deviation between the ground truth and the probability 
maps predicted by the neural network.
After each training epoch on the training set, we evaluate the 
model’s grape berry counting performance on the validation set. The 
model weights with the best berry counting performance across all the 
training epochs are saved as the final model weights. After training, 
the models are tested on the test set to obtain their final grape berry 
segmentation and counting performance. It should be noted that, the 
berry counting results should be obtained from probability maps us-
ing non-maxima suppression with a fixed threshold score. During our 
experiments, we observed that the optimal threshold scores of models 
trained with different ground truth data types were not consistent. 
Consequently, for models trained with different types of probability 
map, we select the threshold scores obtaining the best berry counting 
performance on the validation set as their threshold scores on the test 
set.
In addition to comparing the performance of Mask-GK using dif-
ferent ground truth data and backbones, we also compared the grape 
berry counting and segmentation performance, along with the learn-
able parameters and detection speed of Mask-GK, to methods pre-
viously designed for general scene instance segmentation tasks. The 
compared methods include Mask R-CNN (He et al., 2017), Cascade 
Mask R-CNN (Cai and Vasconcelos, 2019), InstaBoost (Fang et al., 
2019), PointRend (Kirillov et al., 2020), CondInst (Tian et al., 2020), 
YOLOv8 (Jocher et al., 2023), YOLOv9 (Wang et al., 2024), and 
YOLOv11 (Jocher et al., 2023). All three YOLO methods adopted 
implementations from ultralytics, while other compared methods were 
reproduced by mmdetection with ResNet-50 as the backbone. We 
increased the maximum predicted object count in each image from 100 
to 1000 for all compared methods, and set the number of regions of 
interest generated by the RPN layer to 2000 for non-YOLO methods. 
Besides these modifications, the training and testing parameters for all 
methods remained the same as the original parameters provided by 
mmdetection and ultralytics.

4.3. Experimental results
In this section, we first evaluate the grape berry segmentation 
and counting performance of Mask-GK when different ground truth 
probability maps and backbones are used. Then, we compare the 
performance of Mask-GK with previous instance segmentation methods 
designed for general detection scenes. Finally, we test the capability of 
Mask-GK in cross-dataset and open-world scenarios.
4.3.1. Ablation experiments
Here, we evaluate the grape berry counting and instance segmenta-
tion performance of our proposed Mask-GK method trained with prob-
ability maps generated using berry point, bounding box, and mask an-
notations, when backbones including ResNet-18, ResNet-34, ResNet-50, 
and ResNet-101 are used in the network.
(1) Grape berry counting
According to the experimental results presented in Table 2, it is 
evident that the grape berry counting performance of Mask-GK exhibits 
no significant difference across most backbone models when trained 
with mask-based probability maps and bounding box-based probability 
maps. However, when trained with point-based probability maps, the 
neural network consistently performs relatively poorly in grape berry 
counting. These experimental results prove the reliability of using the 
proposed mask-based probability maps as ground truth data in grape 
berry counting tasks. Additionally, the performance difference of the 
network trained with point-based ground truth data and the other two 
types of ground truth data emphasizes the necessity of incorporating 
more berry shape features into ground truth labels when addressing 
grape berry counting problems with neural networks.
Although the mask-based probability maps provide richer grape 
berry features compared to the bounding box-based probability maps 
and theoretically should achieve better counting performance, the ex-
perimental results showed that the counting performance of models 
trained with these two types of probability maps was not significantly 
different. This may be because the features provided by the bounding 
box-based probability maps are already sufficient for the model to 
make highly accurate counting predictions, with the main prediction 
errors being concentrated on a small number of difficult-to-detect grape 
berries. As a result, replacing the training ground truth with mask-based 
probability maps offers limited improvements in the model’s counting 
performance. Further parameter optimization of the mask Gaussian 
kernels as training ground truth may enhance its counting performance.
(2) Grape berry instance segmentation
Visualized grape berry instance segmentation results of Mask-GK are

in a distinct color. It can be seen that Mask-GK consistently achieves 
accurate instance segmentation prediction of grape berries, even for 
occluded and small berries. The experimental results, detailed in Table 
3, also demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach in segmenting 
grape berries across different neural network backbones.

Here, we compare the grape berry counting and instance segmen-
tation performance of Mask-GK with several previous instance seg-
mentation methods. It should be noted that grape berry counting and 
instance segmentation in grapevine scenes pose unique challenges due 
to the dense targets and small object sizes, which result in many 
instance segmentation methods designed for general detection scenes 
being unable to produce any prediction results, or only producing 
completely incorrect results. Therefore, we only present the detection 
performance of methods capable of completing grape berry instance 
segmentation and counting tasks on the GBISC dataset. Actually, we 
also tested some other instance segmentation methods designed for 
general scenes, including SOLO (Wang et al., 2020), YOLACT (Bolya 
et al., 2019), SparseInst (Cheng et al., 2022), BoxInst (Tian et al., 2021), 
and ConvNeXt-V2 (Woo et al., 2023). However, we observed that these 
methods were unable to successfully complete the segmentation and 
counting task for grape berries on the GBISC dataset.

Mask-GK with other instance segmentation methods on the test set of 
the GBISC dataset. Clearly, the grape berry counting performance of 
our proposed method outperforms all other methods, achieving the best 
counting results with an MAE of 9.40 and an RMSE of 12.28. Even when 
compared to CondInst, which is the best-performing method able to 
simultaneously segment and count grape berries, Mask-GK still exhibits 
a significant counting performance advantage. This experiment demon-
strates that our proposed method not only successfully accomplishes 
grape berry segmentation but also provides reliable counting results.

(3) Model size and detection efficiency
In Table 6, we additionally compare the number of learnable pa-
rameters and the average detection time on the GBISC dataset test set 
between Mask-GK and other instance segmentation methods. Mask-GK 
not only surpasses other methods in grape berry instance segmentation 
and counting performance across all backbones, but also has fewer 
learnable parameters when using ResNet-18. Furthermore, Mask-GK 
exhibits faster detection speeds than other instance segmentation meth-
ods, with the exception of three YOLO-based approaches. It should be 
noted that while these YOLO methods achieve exceptionally fast detec-
tion efficiency through state-of-the-art model optimization techniques 
and reduced input image sizes, their intricate architectural designs 
result in higher GPU memory consumption. This inherent constraint 
limits the permissible sizes of both input images and predicted object 
masks, ultimately leading to inferior segmentation performance on 
small and densely distributed grape berries in the GBISC dataset even 
when compared to early instance segmentation methods.
Mask-GK’s advantages in model size and detection efficiency can 
be attributed to its pure CNN architecture, which entirely avoids the 
use of fully connected layers. In contrast, fully connected layers are 
widely adopted in traditional instance segmentation methods, often 
resulting in a significant increase in additional parameters and detec-
tion time. Additionally, since Mask-GK can simultaneously accomplish 
the instance segmentation of all grape berries in an image, it avoids 
the repetitive detection process of segmenting each berry individually 
based on detected bounding boxes. This confers a significant efficiency 
advantage in densely packed vineyard scenarios.

5. Dicussion
In this paper, we experimentally verified that adding more berry 
shape information to the Gaussian kernels in probability maps can 
significantly improve the counting accuracy of neural networks trained 
Computers and Electronics in Agriculture 234 (2025) 110286 
11 

C. Yang et al.
with these probability maps for grape berry counting tasks. Experi-
mental results also demonstrate that the proposed Mask-GK method 
achieves high detection accuracy in both grape berry instance segmen-
tation and counting tasks. Notably, our method successfully addresses 
the challenging problem of dense target instance segmentation, which 
traditional methods struggle with. Compared to traditional instance 
segmentation methods, our proposed approach not only achieves su-
perior grape berry instance segmentation and counting performance 
on the GBISC dataset but also reduces instances of missed and false 
detections. Additionally, the predicted segmentation masks of Mask-
GK are more precise and can successfully address the issue of mask 
overlap that is challenging for traditional methods. Therefore, com-
pared to general-purpose instance segmentation methods, our proposed 
approach is better suited for grape berry segmentation and counting 
tasks.
However, the work presented in this paper still has certain limita-
tions. For smaller objects like grape berries, where each segmentation 
mask can be treated as a complete whole, the Mask-GK method, which 
uses mask annotations to generate Gaussian kernels, can effectively 
accomplish both instance segmentation and counting tasks. However, 
in more common scenes with larger objects, many object segmentation 
masks are not continuous or complete, making them less effectively 
addressed by the proposed method. Additionally, the dataset collected 
and annotated in this study only includes grape images captured dur-
ing well lighting periods, excluding images taken under poor lighting 
conditions such as dusk or nighttime. Finally, training the grape berry 
segmentation and counting method proposed in this paper requires 
expensive manual labor for instance segmentation annotation, which 
presents certain challenges for further research and extension.
6. Conclusion
The segmentation and counting of grape berries provide crucial ref-
erence data for estimating grape yield, thinning, and maturity detection 
in grape production tasks. This holds significant importance for digi-
tized precision viticulture. In our paper, we propose a mask Gaussian 
kernel method to represent the instance segmentation information of 
grape berries and generate mask-based probability maps using these 
Gaussian kernels. We also introduce a novel solution named Mask-GK 
for in-field grape berry segmentation and counting. This solution trains 
neural networks with mask-based grape berry probability maps and 
achieves grape berry instance segmentation by applying the watershed 
algorithm to probability maps predicted by the neural network. We 
publicly release a dataset named GBISC specifically designed for grape 
berry instance segmentation and counting tasks, which is the first of 
its kind in this field. Our experiments compare different methods of 
instance segmentation, revealing that our proposed method not only 
solves grape berry segmentation and counting tasks simultaneously 
but also outperforms previous methods on the GBISC dataset. Moving 
forward, we aim to expand the GBISC dataset by including additional 
grape varieties and establish a benchmark for grape berry segmentation 
and counting. Furthermore, we intend to design lightweight network 
models for deployment on edge computing devices, applying our work 
in real-world grape field environments.
