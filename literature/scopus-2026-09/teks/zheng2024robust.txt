A B S T R A C T  
CONTEXT: Accurately detecting and counting fruits is essential for orchard yield estimation and smart man­
agement. However, two drawbacks are inherent in the current citrus counting algorithms: First, the robustness 
needs to be improved, particularly illumination changes and dense occlusion. Secondly, the actual operating 
efficiency of the system is low. 
OBJECTIVE: To tackle the above issues, this paper proposed a robust and efficient fruit-counting pipeline based 
on Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV). 
METHODS: First, to obtain UAV video streaming data online, a live broadcast platform and a flight control 
Application called FlyCounter were developed. Secondly, the Illumination-Adaptive-Transformer network is used 
to enhance the low-illumination citrus image in real-time. Then, for the specific challenging scenario, a novel 
model named Fruit-YOLO is designed to accurately detect citrus in data streams. Finally, the DeepSORT is 
adopted to track and count fruits in video sequences. 
RESULTS AND CONCLUSIONS: The results indicate that for detection performance, the P, R and mAP of the Fruit- 
YOLO of input enhanced image are 0.898, 0.854 and 0.929 respectively, which are 1.7%, 4.6% and 3.5% higher 
than the original image respectively. Regarding counting performance, for the daytime and evening scenes, the

1. Introduction 
In recent years, fruit yield estimation, pest detection, and harvest 
robots have become a focus in the agricultural field (Xu et al., 2023; Ye 
et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023). China plays an important role in the 
production and consumption of fruits in the world, with citrus planting 
area and output ranking first all year round. (Zheng et al., 2022; Meng 
et al., 2023). Accurately obtaining the number of citrus is a key pre­
requisite for intelligent orchard yield estimation. However, the task of 
counting fruits in orchards is a crucial yet demanding endeavor, pri­
marily because it involves addressing various challenges such as severe 
occlusion, illumination changes, and fruits of varying sizes. The bottle­
neck problems of existing methods are: (1) The counting algorithm has 
poor robustness and low accuracy under dense occlusion and light 
changes. (2) In actual orchard operations, the overall system process is 
complex and inefficient. Therefore, developing a robust and efficient 
approach for fruit counting holds immense importance in advancing the 
realm of intelligent orchard yield estimation and ultimately enhancing 
the profitability of the industry. 
With the advancement of the sensor, computer, and artificial intel­
ligence technologies, some researchers have proposed various methods 
for estimating crop yields based on the quantity of fruits (Apolo-Apolo 
et al., 2020a, 2020b). Obtaining fruit quantity is the key to achieving 
intelligent orchard yield estimation, which involves the following three 
difficulties:  
(1) Work platform 
In existing fruit-counting research, images are mostly collected 
through sensors held by people or mounted on vehicles (Ye et al., 2023; 
Shen et al., 2023; Vasconez et al., 2020). This means that this methods of 
operation remain less efficient and may cause soil damage problems. In 
comparison, Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) with low cost, rich 
perspective information and high flexibility can better meet the opera­
tion requirements. Some scholars have effectively implemented the 
statistics of crops and animals by using UAVs (Zhang et al., 2023; Ara­
kawa et al., 2023; Apolo-Apolo et al., 2020a, 2020b; M´endez et al., 
2019; Rivas et al., 2018). Utilizing an aerial perspective for fruit 
counting represents a highly promising approach. However, due to the 
poor robustness of the algorithm in scenarios such as illumination 
changes, occlusion overlap, and small-sized fruits, it is difficult to apply 
to large-scale orchards. 
Furthermore, the majority of previous studies obtain fruit counting 
results through offline processing of pre-recorded UAV videos. Such 
methods usually have the disadvantages of complex operational pro­
cesses and lack of real-time execution capability. More importantly, 
previous research primarily concentrates on the development of yield 
estimation algorithms, with data collection being separated and offline, 
thus failing to achieve an efficient and end-to-end system. These 
methods cannot be adjusted in time to obtain the best estimation ac­
curacy, which may ultimately result in the inability to estimate yields on 
a large scale in orchards within the specified time (Zheng et al., 2022).  
(2) Fruit detection 
At present, significant advancements have been made by many re­
searchers in the visual detection of citrus (Tang et al., 2023). Chen et al. 
(2021) suggested a detection algorithm for citrus in different growth 
stages based on the improvement of YOLOv4. Zheng et al. (2021) pro­
posed a method called YOLO BP for detecting green citrus. The method 
had an average accuracy of 86.00% in 6 complex scenarios. Huang et al. 
(2022) proposed a method for detecting green citrus by incorporating 
the use of CBAM in YOLOv5 and utilizing α-IoU as the loss function. 
Their method achieved an average accuracy of 91.3% and a detection 
time of 16.7 ms. Chen et al. (2022) introduced a network for citrus 
detection using improved YOLOv4, but the real-time performance was 
poor. In summary, the above studies demonstrate the effectiveness of 
detecting citrus based on CNNs. However, there are currently few deep- 
learning methods for detecting citrus in UAV images. The detection 
performance of existing methods for immature green citrus in complex 
orchard scenes still cannot meet the requirements. 
Most of the existing citrus detection works are conducted in ideal 
conditions with high-quality images. However, a variety of harsh 
lighting conditions such as backlighting, low illumination, and exposure 
often exist in unstructured orchards. The reduction of image quality is 
one of the important factors that significantly affect the performance of 
the detector (Zhou et al., 2022). In recent years, some researchers have 
conducted research on fruit detection under different lighting conditions 
in orchards. Zheng et al. (2021) introduced a network named YOLO BP 
for the detection of green citrus, achieving an accuracy of 92.50% in 
challenging backlighting conditions. Wang and He (2022) proposed an 
apple detection method called MS-ADS, which has a recall rate of 97.2% 
and 98.1% for apples with shadows on the surface and uneven lighting, 
respectively. Xu et al. (2023) implemented a visual recognition method 
for tomato picking robots using the YOLOv3‑tiny network. In direct 
light, backlight and side light scenarios, the recall rates of this method 
are 91.52%, 83.37% and 96.11% respectively, with the worst perfor­
mance under backlight conditions. Zhou et al. (2022) used the histogram 
equalization algorithm to process fruit images with uneven brightness 
and implemented the detection of Camellia oleifera based on YOLOv7. 
The above study discussed the impact of different lighting conditions on 
algorithm performance in daytime orchards. Compared with structured 
environmental conditions, the algorithm is less robust under complex 
lighting conditions in orchards, especially in scenes with insufficient 
light such as backlight and low illumination. In addition, to address this 
problem, existing methods often need to construct data sets of different 
lighting scenes to improve model performance, which is difficult to 
annotate and time-consuming. 
Lighting changes in orchards during the day affect the accuracy of 
the algorithm. In contrast, the effects of environmental factors such as 
light changes and shadows can be effectively avoided in low-light en­
vironments (Xiong et al., 2020). In recent years, several researchers have 
conducted studies on fruit detection in low-illumination environments. 
Xiong et al. (2020) a network based on the improved YOLO v3 to detect 
citrus at night. Liang et al. (2020) used YOLO v3 and UNET to detect 
lychees and fruit stems in a nighttime environment. Fu et al. (2021) 
proposed a kiwi fruit detection algorithm named DY3TNet, which ach­
ieved the highest performance of 0.9005 under night-time LED lighting. 
Experimental results show that using image enhancement methods or 
simple lighting equipment can improve the performance of fruit detec­
tion in orchards. Zhang et al. (2023) implemented cherry detection 
during the day and night using the YOLOv4-Tiny network. The mAP in 
the nighttime test sets was 94.72%, which was higher than the 90.78% 
in the daytime test set. The above research shows that fruit detection in

Therefore, fruit detection and counting in low-illumination scenes in

(3) Fruit counting 
Accurate fruit counting is a key prerequisite for smart orchard yield 
estimation. In recent years, a lot of scholars have conducted studies 
estimating individual trees or entire orchards. Gao et al. (2021) used 
YOLOv4-Tiny combined with the improved Hungarian algorithm to 
implement apple counting. However, this method can only process 
image sequences within 30 s. Zhang et al., (2022a) suggested a citrus 
counting pipeline based on OrangeYolo and OrangeSort, which achieved 
a mAP of 0.957. He et al. (2022) counted fruits based on a multi-feature 
matching algorithm and adopted a more reasonable MOTA evaluation 
index. However, the method only achieves an AP of 78.40, which has 
poor detection performance. Based on RGB–D, Rong et al., (2023) 
proposed an improved YOLOv5-4D combined with the ByteTrack to 
achieve tomato detection and counting. 
Some researchers are exploring the integration of three-dimensional 
information from objects to improve the accuracy of fruit counting tasks. 
Nellithimaru and Kantor (2019) introduced the use of neural network 
models and SLAM to count grapes. Apolo-Apolo et al. (2020a, 2020b) 
used images to reconstruct and count fruits, and the method error was 
6.59%. This method has good accuracy but requires more expensive 
software to process the image data pre-captured by the UAV. H¨ani et al. 
(2020) were the first to apply a combination of the Gaussian Mixed 
Model and the Structure-from-Motion (SFM) to accomplish fruit count­
ing in orchards. Gen´e-Mola et al. (2020) and others proposed using SFM 
to reconstruct fruit trees and segment and count fruits based on Mask R- 
CNN. The above methods achieve good accuracy by combining three- 
dimensional information, but usually require expensive equipment 
and complex processing procedures. In addition, the specific steps of 
such methods require several hours or even more than ten hours to 
reconstruct the fruit, thus affecting the subsequent work process. 
Therefore, the above methods may not adequately fulfill the demands 
for efficient operation in the context of future smart orchards. 
To tackle the aforementioned challenges, this study proposes a novel 
fruit-counting methodology approach utilizing UAV, facilitating accu­
rate and efficient real-time counting of fruits in orchards. The main 
contributions of this paper can be summarized as follows:  
(1) To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to realize an 
online and efficient counting pipeline for orchards in low-light 
scenarios based on UAVs. By introducing the IAT network, the 
problem of poor algorithm robustness under daytime backlight 
and evening low-light conditions is effectively addressed. More­
over, this method effectively addresses the issue of labeling low- 
light images, thereby providing valuable support for the imple­
mentation of intelligent operations involving various other fruits 
in low-light environments.  
(2) A UAV data transmission platform for orchards was established, 
and a flight control application called FlyCounter was developed 
to realize intelligent flight of UAVs and online acquisition of 
video streams.  
(3) A fruit detection network called Fruit-YOLO was proposed, which 
effectively tackled challenges related to the missed detection of 
small-sized fruits and overlapping occlusions. 
(4) An end-to-end counting system is implemented, which acceler­
ates the acquisition of accurate results. This not only expedites 
the development, iterative improvement, and application of 
counting systems for developers but also furnishes farmers with 
timely and valuable information for their decision-making pro­
cesses in the same year. 
2. Materials 
2.1. Image acquisition 
This study uses a data set from an orchard in Baiyun District, 
Guangzhou City, Guangdong, China. The fruit variety is orange, and the 
skin color is yellow-green and green. The image acquisition experiment 
was conducted in April and June of 2023. Images were collected using 
PHANTOM 4 UAV (DJI Technology Co., Ltd., Shenzhen, China) with a 
resolution of 5472 × 3078 pixels and a shooting distance of 1.5 m. The 
video collection method is shown in Fig. 1 in the Supplementary Material. 
The experiment collected citrus images at different angles, under 
different lighting conditions and at different times of the day. As shown 
in Fig. 2 in the Supplementary Material, there are complex situations such 
as overlapping occlusion, backlight, and low illumination in the eve­
ning. At the same time, the skin of some immature fruits is green. 
2.2. Data pre-processing 
In this study, a collection of 23 UAV videos was amassed, with each 
video encompassing 7 to 17 trees. Each of these trees bore a range of 80 
to 100 fruits, and the video durations spanned from 4 to 8 min. The 
videos were randomly split into two sets: one with 13 videos for training 
and validation, and the other with 10 videos for testing. Since the 
training of the fruit recognition model requires feeding images, the 
frame extraction method (that is, extracting several frames from the 
video at certain intervals) was used to process the 13 training videos, 
and a total of 3954 training images were obtained. Finally, the dataset 
was divided into a training set and a validation set with a ratio of 7:3. 
The details of the division of citrus image data in each scene are shown 
in Table 1 in the Supplementary Material. 
To reduce the cost of manpower and time, DarkLabel software is used 
(https://github.com/darkpgmr/DarkLabel) to annotate images. The 
annotation categories of ripe citrus and unripe citrus are “mature” and 
“immature” respectively. The XML file contains the category of each 
fruit and its normalized coordinates in the image. 
3. Methodology 
As shown in Fig. 1, the UAV-based fruit counting methodology 
consists of three components: hardware equipment system and flight 
control system, vision system. When carrying out the task, high-quality 
video streams are first collected by the UAV system and transmitted to 
the Android terminal device through the OcuSync protocol. Then, the 
flight control system pushes streaming media data to Nginx through the 
RTMP protocol. Finally, the visual counting system performs image 
enhancement, fruit detection and counting on the video stream. 
3.1. Phase 1: UAV data transmission platform 
The UAV control and transmission system is an important prereq­
uisite for providing high-quality video streams online for subsequent 
vision systems. In previous experiments, we found that using a remote 
control handle has the following shortcomings: (1) The UAV’s intelligent 
system prohibits flight when there are obstacles nearby, resulting in the 
UAV being unable to move in a narrow orchard. (2) The flight speed of

UAVs under human control is difficult to maintain stable, which affects

To address the above problems, as shown in Fig. 2, an application 
called FlyCounter was developed to replace the previous manual control 
and realize the autonomous flight of UAVs. The “Enable/Close Virtual 
Stick” button provides the function of turning on or off the virtual stick, 
which is used for manual intervention to control the UAV in emergen­
cies. The “Start/Stop Streaming” button provides the function of starting 
or stopping the UAV video stream. The “Take Off”, “Hover” and “Land” 
buttons provide the three basic control functions of takeoff, hovering 
and landing respectively. The “Accelerate/Decelerate” and “Time In­
crease/Decrease” buttons provide UAV flight speed and flight time 
control functions respectively. The obstacle avoidance system was 
turned off after the UAV took off to allow it to fly normally in the narrow 
environment of the orchard. Farmers can personalize the flight altitude, 
movement direction, flight speed and flight time on FlyCounter. Fly­
Counter controls the UAV to fly at a constant speed and photograph the 
orchard according to the set parameters, achieving intelligent flight of 
the UAV and real-time transmission of video streams. 
With the continuous advancement of information and communica­
tions technologies, online live broadcasts have become popular. In this 
paper, a UAV video streaming online transmission platform for orchards 
is built based on the mainstream Nginx-RTMP-Module architecture. As 
illustrated in Fig. 3, the platform is composed of three components: push 
terminal, streaming media server, and pull terminal. These modules are 
sequentially interconnected via the RTMP protocol, with data trans­
mission occurring between the streaming media end and the control end 
through LightBridge image transmission technology. In this study, the 
streaming terminals are UAV and FlyCounter applications. The light­
weight Nginx server is adopted as the streaming media server. This 
server has the advantages of simple configuration and good scalability 
and is suitable for orchard field scenarios. The streaming end is a high- 
performance computing platform. 
First, before the UAV takes off, the high-performance processor and 
mobile phone are connected to the same intranet through Wi-Fi. Then, 
set the focus mode of the UAV camera to automatic in FlyCounter to 
ensure the stability and clarity of the video stream. Then, the program 
re-encodes the video stream into a binary stream and transmits it to the 
port of the specified host according to the input streaming URL. Finally, 
the computing platform accesses the Nginx server through the RTMP 
protocol to achieve online acquisition of video streams. 
3.2. Phase 2: adaptive image enhancement 
Although contemporary object detection models have excelled on 
various benchmark datasets, they encounter substantial difficulties 
when operating in low-light scenarios. In the complex environment of an 
orchard, there are many harsh lighting conditions, such as exposure, 
backlight, and insufficient light, which lead to a decrease in image 
quality and thus affect the performance of the detector. The 
Illumination-Adaptive-Transformer (IAT) network was proposed by Cui 
et al. (2022) to explore real-time dark light enhancement and visual 
tasks in a series of poor lighting scenarios. Compared with MAXIM (Tu 
et al., 2022) and Restormer (Zamir et al., 2021), the IAT network 
parameter amount is only 90 k+, which is an ultra-lightweight network 
that works well for counting orchard fruits in real-time. Therefore, the 
IAT model is used to enhance UAV images to address the problem of 
poor performance under backlight and low-illumination conditions. 
Fig. 4 shows the IAT network structure diagram. The input citrus 
images will be processed through the Local branch and the Global 
branch to complete the low-light enhancement task. Among them, each 
local branch consists of three PEM (Pixel-wise Enhancement Module) 
modules. To ensure lightweight design and input at any resolution, a 
Transformer structure composed of depth-wise convolution is used. In 
the global branch, the Attention module is used to obtain global infor­
mation to generate color matrices and gamma values. Enter the 
randomly initialized query into the module and work together with the 
key and value generated by the citrus image itself. Ten parameters are 
finally output, representing a 3 × 3 color matrix and a 1-dimensional 
gamma value respectively. During model training, as the epoch in­
creases, the network can adaptively adjust the color matrix and gamma 
value. 
3.3. Phase 3: detection with deep learning 
The accurate detection of citrus in the UAV video stream is a critical 
step in achieving orchard counting tasks. YOLOv5 (Ultralytics, 2021) 
has good real-time performance and accuracy, as well as fast fitting 
during training, making it suitable for the rapid development and 
deployment of systems for orchards. Nevertheless, during the orchard

Common designs in existing CNN architectures (i.e., across con­

degradation of fine-grained information and less efficient feature rep­

YOLOv5 and proposed a citrus detection network called Fruit-YOLO 
(Fig. 5). The improvements are mainly in Backbone and Neck, using 
the SPD-Conv module to replace the original stride-2 convolution layer. 
The structure diagram of the SPD-Conv module is shown in Fig. 3 in the 
Supplementary Material. There are 7 such replacement instances, spe­
cifically in Backbone using 5 stride-2 convolutional layers to down­
sample the feature map by a factor of 25, and in Neck using 2 stride-2 
convolutional layers. Enhancing these aspects will contribute to an 
improved detection performance, particularly for small-sized objects 
within the model. At the same time, the shortcomings of occlusion, 
missing overlapping foreground objects and background object detec­
tion errors in the original YOLOv5 are effectively addressed. 
3.4. Phase 4: fruit tracking and counting 
After fruit pixel coordinate information is obtained by Fruit-YOLO, 
the DeepSORT algorithm is adopted to track and count fruits. 
Different from counting of a single frame image, the function of this part 
is to obtain the cross-frame correlation between the entire image 
sequence and ascertain the total count of fruits in the entire orchard. The 
Overview of Fruit tracking and counting is shown in Fig. 4 in the Sup­
plementary Material.

4. Experiments 
4.1. Experimental environment 
To fairly compare the performance of each algorithm, the training 
platform and hyperparameter settings are the same. The platform con­
ditions are as follows: CPU is Intel Xeon Gold 6256, clocked at 3.60 GHz 
with 48 physical cores, 24 threads, and 1024 GB memory. The GPU is 
NVIDIA RTX A6000 with 48 GB display RAM. The system is Ubuntu 
18.04, and the software includes CUDA 11.8.130, CUDNN 8.6.0, NVIDIA 
driver 535.104, Opencv 4.8.0 and training framework Pytorch 2.0.1.

4.3. Image enhancement experiment 
4.3.1. Dataset construction and model training 
Usually, the construction of this type of data set requires the use of 
cameras to collect fruit trees at fixed positions and heights under normal 
light and low illumination conditions, respectively. However, this re­
quires a lot of equipment, labor and time costs. At the same time, it is 
difficult to manually annotate images collected under low-light condi­
tions. In addition, it was found in actual tests that this method is not 
feasible due to the difficulty in maintaining the absolute position of the 
UAV in two different periods when flying. Therefore, this study con­
structs three gradients of fruit low-light data sets T1, T2 and T3. The 
gradient coefficients were set to 0.6, 0.35 and 0.1 respectively to 
simulate the actual light conditions in the orchard. 
After IAT feeds the three data sets T1, T2 and T3 for training, three

iterating to 30 generations, the network is fitted. Judging from the 
parameter convergence, the network training results are ideal. The MT2 
model performs best on the validation set, with PSNR and SSIM values 
approaching 40 and 1 respectively. The details of datasets and com­

4.3.4. Failure cases 
The accuracy of the counting task depends heavily on the perfor­
mance of the detector. However, in the unstructured environment of an 
orchard, the detector is easily affected by multiple factors, such as oc­
clusion, light changes, and fine-grained feature changes (He et al., 
2022). Although the problems of missed detection of small-sized citrus, 
overlapping occlusion and low-light fruits are well addressed by Fruit- 
YOLO and IAT, it also bring some problems. 
As shown in Fig. 6 in the Supplementary Material, fruits in the red 
circle failed to be detected in the enhanced image. The reason is that 
under sufficient light conditions during the day, the high-brightness area 
at the edge of the image may be exposed due to the enhancement of the 
IAT network, resulting in missed detection of the Fruit-YOLO network. 
In addition, leaves may be mistakenly detected as positive samples by 
the Fruit-YOLO. Therefore, the recall rate of the Fruit-YOLO network has 
increased by 4.6%, while the precision rate has only increased by 1.7%. 
The development of image area brightness evaluation and adaptive 
enhancement algorithms can be effective in addressing this problem.

4.4.3. Multi-UAV field experiment 
Most previous methods often use indirect metrics to indicate effi­
ciency, such as FLOPs or parameters. However, these metrics are sub- 
optimal and not suitable for deployment in real-world operational sce­
narios. As stated in a lot of works, existing evaluation metrics for 
designing neural networks often do not truly reflect direct measure­
ments (Xia et al., 2022). In practice, we are more concerned with the FPS 
or latency of an algorithm besides performance. 
Multiple UAVs can help improve the efficiency and promotion value 
of the counting pipeline. To further improve system efficiency, this 
paper explores the feasibility of parallel operations with different 
numbers of UAVs. The UAVs are implemented by multi-threaded con­
current execution. The experimental objects are the detection, 
enhancement and tracking of the counting pipeline. The content of the 
experiment is to analyze the time and resources consumed by the 
pipeline when operating with different UAV numbers. The former 
mainly analyzes the time-consuming of three parts, while the latter 
mainly analyzes the VRAM requirements of three parts. 
Experimental results show that as the number of drones increases, 
the total time consumption of the pipeline increases, as shown in Fig. 7 
(a). When one, two and three UAVs operate simultaneously, it takes 
approximately 0.18 s, 0.33 s and 0.44 s for an image to be processed by 
the pipeline respectively. Fig. 7(b), (c) and (d) respectively show the 
time-consuming situation of each part of the system under multi-UAV. 
When three UAVs work simultaneously, the detection time increases 
from 0.06 s to 0.14 s, the enhancement time increases from 0.05 s to 
0.09 s, and the tracking time increases from 0.03 s to 0.15 s. The results 
show that when multiple drones work simultaneously, the pipeline still 
maintains high real-time performance. 
As shown in Fig. 8, the VRAM consumed by the pipeline increases as 
the number of UAVs increases. When operating with a single, two, and 
three UAVs, the VRAM consumed by the pipeline is approximately 4.95 
g, 9.90 g, and 14.85 g respectively. When a UAV is added for operation, 
the increase in VRAM for the detection counting part and enhancement 
part is approximately 1.17 g and 3.78 g respectively. Experimental re­
sults show that if hardware resources are sufficient, the proposed pipe­
line can carry out multi-UAV operations for efficient operations. 
4.4.4. Failure cases 
During the counting process, an obvious phenomenon appeared that 
is very likely to be the cause of the increase in errors: During the 
transmission of UAV images, when the network condition is poor, image 
distortion or image data packet loss often occurs. The actual positions of 
some fruits are inconsistent with the positions predicted by the tracker. 
The fundamental reason is that WIFI is used to transmit data in this 
study, which has the disadvantage of network instability, resulting in 
data packet loss. The retransmission mechanism retransmits data 
packets when they are lost, thereby increasing latency. This is also the 
reason why the online counting method has a delay of 7 s ~ 10s. Using 
high-quality communication equipment can effectively improve the 
quality and stability of communication signals. Adaptive bitrate control 
technology can also be used to dynamically adjust the data transmission 
rate based on current network conditions to ensure a stable connection 
in unstable network environments.

5. Discussion 
This paper proposes a novel solution for fruit counting about UAV 
applications in dark light scenes, especially the UAV live broadcast 
platform, low-light image enhancement, and detection and counting of 
fruits.

Some previous studies have suggested that UAVs can be very effec­
tive in monitoring and counting tasks in the agricultural sector (Ara­
kawa et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023; Apolo-Apolo et al., 2020a, 2020b; 
M´endez et al., 2019). Compared with the work of Arakawa et al. (2023) 
and Zhang et al. (2023), our study also built a UAV data transmission 
platform for orchards and developed an APP called FlyCounter to 
implement an end-to-end counting pipeline. Apolo-Apolo et al., 2020a, 
2020b and Gen´e-Mola et al. (2020) obtained higher counting accuracy 
by reconstructing orchards. This study achieved low-cost fruit counting 
based on tracking-by-detection while ensuring accuracy. Our method 
does not require step-by-step procedures and is simple to operate, which 
can significantly reduce the processing time required to obtain accurate 
results. Pipelines facilitate the rapid development, iterative optimization 
and application of systems to help farmers develop optimal strategies as 
early as possible. 
In the fruit detection task, compared with Zhang et al. (2022a, 
2022b) and Chen et al. (2022), the improved Fruit-YOLO can effectively 
improve the detection performance. In particular, in response to the 
problem of poor robustness in low-illumination scenes in existing works 
such as Xu et al. (2023), Zhou et al. (2022) and Wang and He, 2022, this 
study innovatively introduces the IAT network to optimize detector, 
which further improves the accuracy metric of the system. Similar to the 
idea of using an image equalization algorithm (Zhou et al., 2022) and 
artificial light source equipment (Liang et al., 2020; Fu et al., 2021; 
Zhang et al., 2023), our system has better robustness and practical sig­
nificance. In addition, the exploration of low illumination can help 
extend the system’s operating time and can be extended to all-weather 
yield estimation and picking of other fruits in the orchard. 
In the fruit counting task, compared with the existing state-of-the-art 
citrus counting work (Zhang et al., 2022a, 2022b), this paper uses more 
precise evaluation indicators (MOTA and MAPE) to evaluate the local 
performance and overall performance of the system respectively. 
Evaluate. The proposed method outperforms the method proposed by 
Zhang et al., 2022a with lower ID Switch and MAPE. In addition, our 
system supports parallel operations of multiple UAVs, which has the 
advantage of efficient operations and promotion value. 
Nevertheless, the proposed system still has the following limitations: 
(1) The UAV transmission platform cannot work properly when the 
network condition is poor, resulting in fruit tracking failure. Improving 
the communication quality and stability of the platform is a problem 
that needs to be addressed in the future. (2) After using the IAT network, 
the Fruit-YOLO network will miss or misdetect fruits. Further improving 
the IAT network can effectively address this problem, such as developing 
an image low-light area evaluation algorithm. (3) The pipeline only 
counts from one side of the fruit tree, which has problems with ID 
switching and missed detection. Research on cross-camera fruit count­
ing based on multi-UAV collaborative operations can be considered in 
the future. 
6. Conclusions 
This paper proposes a new fruit-counting pipeline based on UAV, 
which can efficiently and robustly count fruits in large-scale orchards. 
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to realize an online 
and efficient counting pipeline for orchards in low-light scenarios based 
on UAVs. We can draw the following conclusions:  
1. A UAV data transmission platform for orchards was built to obtain 
video streams online. Correspondingly, a flight control APP called 
FlyCounter was developed to achieve stable flight of UAVs. 
2. The SPD-Conv is used in Fruit-YOLO’s Backbone and Neck. Experi­
mental results show that Fruit-YOLO is superior to YOLOv5 in terms 
of detection accuracy, and demonstrates remarkable performance
