Abstract. This study presents an automated pineapple fruit detec-
tion and counting system designed for precision agriculture using high-
resolution UAV orthomosaic imagery. The proposed approach integrates 
dataset expansion, active learning, super-resolution preprocessing, and 
an ensemble of YOLOv8 models to improve detection performance, par-
ticularly for early-stage fruits. Experimental results demonstrate that 
the system achieves high detection accuracy (mAP@0.5 above 92%), sig-
niﬁcantly reduces false negatives, and increases detected fruit counts by 
128% compared to baseline models. Additionally, the system maintains 
practical c omputational performance on both GPU and CPU platforms, 
enabling scalable deployment in real-world agricultural scenarios. The 
ﬁndings highlight the importance of data-centric strategies and ensemble 
learning in addressing challenges such as occlusion, illumination variabil-
ity, and small object detection.

Introduction 
Automatic fruit detection in agricultural environments has signiﬁcantly 
advanced due to recent developments in deep learning and the increased avail-
ability of highresolution aerial imagery. Traditional computer vision approaches 
relied heavily on handcrafted features, such as color, shape, and texture descrip-
tors; however, these methods struggled in real ﬁeld conditions where illumina-
tion, occlusion, and plant v ariability introduce additional complexity. The intro-
duction of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) enabled more robust feature 
extraction, allowing o bject detection architectures to address these challenges
eﬀectively.
Despite recent advances in deep learning-based object detection, existing 
pineapple detection systems still face signiﬁcant limitations w hen applied to

Related Work 
2.1
UAV-Based Pineapple Detection 
One of the earliest comprehensive studies on pineapple detection from UAV 
imagery was presented by Wang et al. [17]. Their work applied Faster R-CNN 
and Mask R-CNN to identify pineapple plants and fruits in high-resolution drone 
images. The study demonstrated that deep learning methods could produce reli-
able detection and segmentation under varying illumination and ﬁeld conditions. 
Mask R-CNN, in particular, provided improved object delineation due to its

instance segmentation capabilities, although at the cost of increased computa-
tional complexity. The authors reported competitive values of mean Average 
Precision (mAP), while highlighting limitations regarding inference time, mak-
ing these models less s uitable for large-scale, real-time agricultural pipelines. 
This research established a foundational benchmark f or subsequent studies using
UAV-based pineapple detection.
Early UAV-based pineapple monitoring has demonstrated that deep learn-
ing can support yield-related workﬂows, but performance depends strongly on 
growth stage, occlusion, and imaging setup. Shiu et al. [14] used high-resolution 
UAV imagery (centimeter-level GSD) and applied Faster R-CNN to locate and 
count mature fruits (including fruits covered by protective materials), while Mask 
R-CNN was used to delineate net-covered areas. Although the study reported 
strong precision on mature targets, it explicitly notes that detecting other growth 
stages is substantially more challenging and suggests super-resolution as a poten-
tial strategy to improve interpretability in diﬃcult stages [14]. 
2.2
YOLO-Based Detection in Agriculture 
More recently, state-of-the-art approaches have shifted toward one-stage detec-
tors such as the YOLO family, which oﬀer faster inference while maintaining high 
accuracy. A n otable advancement was introduced by Li et al. through the DPD-
YOLO detector [9]. Built upon YOLOv8, the proposed architecture incorporates 
attention mechanisms and enhanced feature aggregation modules designed to 
handle dense pineapple arrangements and heavy occlusions commonly found in 
commercial plantations. Their experiments demonstrated substantial improve-
ments in precision, recall, and mAP compared to the baseline YOLOv8 model. 
Additionally, the authors reported improved robustness when detecting fruits 
partially covered by foliage, addressing one of the primary limitations of earlier 
detectors such as R-CNN variants. These innovations position DPD-YOLO as a
modern and eﬃcient solution for fruit detection tasks using aerial imagery.
In ﬁeld-oriented real-time applications, lightweight pineapple detectors have 
been proposed to enable deployment on agricultural machinery. For example, 
ESLC-YOLOv8 introduces lightweight architectural modules and reports high 
accuracy with a small model size, targeting real-time pineapple r ecognition in
complex environments [13]. Complementary systems also extend beyond detec-
tion into maturity classiﬁcation and downstream e stimation tasks; Langpoklak-
pam  et al. [8] combine YOLOv8-based detection with ripeness classiﬁcation and 
size/weight prediction from drone imagery, illustrating h ow detection perfor-
mance impacts complete pineapple monitoring pipelines.
Finally, recent studies have demonstrated the eﬀectiveness of single-stage 
object detectors for agricultural applications. In particular, Garcia-Downing e t
al. [6] proposed an automated system for coﬀee plant detection and counting

2.3
Dataset Limitations and Active Learning 
A recurring limitation in agricultural detection studies is that dataset creation 
and annotation are expensive and often insuﬃciently diverse, which can reduce 
generalization under conditions [2]. 
To reduce annotation burden and eﬃciently grow training sets, active learn-
ing for object detection selects i nformative samples for human review under a
limited budget [18]. 
Data quality plays a fundamental role in machine learning (ML) performance. 
The Garbage In, Garbage Out (GIGO) principle highlights that biased datasets 
inevitably produce unreliable predictions [11]. 
In agricultural detection and counting tasks, limited dataset diversity and 
incomplete labeling are repeatedly identiﬁed as major causes of p oor generaliza-
tion, especially under occlusion and varying phenological stages [2]. 
A structured division of the dataset into training, validation, and testing 
subsets is essential for fair evaluation and preventing overﬁtting [4]. 
2.4
Small Object Detection and Super-Resolution 
In addition, super-resolution has been studied as a means to improve small-
object detection in aerial and remote sensing imagery by enhancing spatial detail 
before detection [1]. In this work, we apply Real-ESRGAN [16] as a practical 
blind super-resolution method for real-world image degradations.
2.5
Ensemble Learning and Detection Fusion 
For large orthomosaics, detection is commonly performed on image tiles, which 
introduces duplicate detections near tile boundaries and across overlapping 
regions. Ensemble strategies can improve robustness but require principled fusion 
of overlapping boxes. Weighted Boxes Fusion (WBF) is a widely u sed method 
to fuse detections from multiple models by computing conﬁdence-weighted box 
averages rather than suppressing boxes via NMS alone [15]. 
Ensemble methods combine multiple models to increase robustness and 
reduce variance [5]. In agricultural applications, ensembles help mitigate the 
challenges of illumination variability, occlusion, and fruit size changes. YOLO-
based ensembles can aggregate predictions from models trained under diﬀerent 
hyperparameters or augmentations, improving small-object detection reliability.

2.6
Fundamentals of Object Detection 
Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) represent the core architecture behind 
modern detection systems due to their ability t o extract hierarchical visual fea-
tures from raw pixels [3,7]. 
Object detection integrate both localization and classiﬁcation of multiple 
objects within an image. Early approaches included region-based pipelines such 
as R-CNN and Faster R-CNN, which divided detection into prop osal and classi-
ﬁcation stages. Although accurate, these approaches are computationally expen-
sive and unsuitable for processing large orthomosaics.
Single-shot detectors like SSD [10] improved speed by performing detection 
in a single forward pass; however, small-object detection remains challenging in 
aerial and remote sensing imagery due to limited pixel footprint, background 
clutter, and scale variation.
The YOLO algorithm is object detection framework that performs object 
localization and classiﬁcation, simultaneously. Unlike designs, such as Faster R-
CNN, which generate region proposals prior to classiﬁcation, YOLO formulates 
object detection as a direct regression problem [12]. 
YOLO processes the entire input image in a single forward pass through a 
convolutional neural network (CNN). The image is divided into an S × S grid, 
where each grid cell is responsible for predicting bounding boxes and class prob-
abilities for objects whose centers fall within that cell. Each predicted bounding 
box is deﬁned by ﬁve parameters: the center coordinates ( x, y), width w, height
h, and conﬁdence score.
YOLO predicts bounding boxes, conﬁdence scores, and class probabilities 
in a uniﬁed stage [12]. This enables real-time inference and makes YOLO well 
suited f or aereal imagery and ﬁeld deplo yment.
The conﬁdence score reﬂects the probability that an object exists within the 
bounding box and the accuracy of t he predicted localization. Class probabilities 
are estimated as P(class | object) [12]. The ﬁnal detection score for a given class 
is computed, as shown in Eq. (1): 
Score = P (object) × P (class|object)

System Design 
The design for the Pineapple Fruit Detection and Counting System follows a 
pipeline, shown in Fig. 2. Each stage is as follo ws:
1. Image Fragmentation into 1280×1280 Tiles: To accommodate the com-
putational constraints, the original orthomosaic is partitioned into ﬁxed-size 
tiles o f 1280x1280 pixels.
2. Inference and Coordinate Recording: Each image tile is independently 
processed by the detection model, which produces a set of predictions regard-
ing bounding boxes, class lab els and conﬁdence scores. The system stores the 
predictions along with their tile-relative coordinates.
3. Spatial Alignment with Original Orthomosaic: Following the inference 
across all tiles, the systems projects the tile-based detections into the coordi-
nate system of the original orthomosaic.

Conclusion 
The proposed pineapple fruit detection system demonstrates that combining 
dataset enhancement, active learning, and YOLOv8 ensemble modeling signif-
icantly improves detection robustness in real agricultural environments. While 
the overall mAP@0.5 remains stable, the substantial increase in precision and 
the 128% growth in detected fruits highlight the eﬀectiveness of the data-centric 
approach in reducing false negatives, particularly for early-stage fruits.
A key contribution of this work is the integration of an ensemble strategy 
that balances precision and recall across diﬀerent ﬁeld conditions. 
Although the global mAP@0.5 remains similar, the increase in precision and 
the 128% improvement in detected fruit counts c onﬁrm the eﬀectiveness of the 
dataset reﬁnement and training strategy . 
From a practical perspective, the system shows strong scalability. The GPU 
acceleration reduces processing time by up to 80%, enabling near real-time anal-
ysis of large orthomosaics, while CPU-only execution remains viable for l ow-
budget or ﬁeld-deploy ed scenarios. 
Compared with existing state-of-the-art methods, including DPD-YOLO and 
Faster/Mask R-CNN pipelines, the proposed YOLOv8 ensemble achieves a more 
balanced trade-oﬀ between accuracy, recall, and computational eﬃciency.

These improvements will further strengthen the applicability of the system for
