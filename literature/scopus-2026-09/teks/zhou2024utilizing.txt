Abstract—Accurate fruit counting is essential for effective 
management in orchards and plantations. Traditionally, 
quantifying fruits like citrus on trees involves labor-intensive 
manual counting. Recently, computer vision-based automated 
methods have emerged as a viable alternative. However, these 
methods often struggle with issues like occlusion by leaves and 
duplicate counts due to multiple appearances in the image 
frame. This study proposes the integration of 2D detection 
techniques with 3D reconstruction technologies, utilizing 
Rays in Neural Radiance Fields(NeRF) to overcome these 
challenges and enhance the precision of fruit counting on 
individual trees. The implementation involves targeted 
training of the YOLOv8 model, combined with NVIDIA's 
instant-ngp for swift NeRF reconstruction, and further 
refined by data clustering via the DBSCAN algorithm. This 
approach markedly increases the detection accuracy 
especially for a single fruit tree, resulting in a mean absolute 
error (MAE) of 1.5 oranges with a prediction accuracy of 
90.625%, effectively refining fruit counting evaluations for a 
single fruit tree. 
Keywords-Fruit Counting; YOLOv8; Neural Radiance Fields. 
I. 
INTRODUCTION 
A. Background and Significance 
With the rapid advancements in artificial intelligence 
and computer vision technologies, smart agriculture has 
seen explosive growth, particularly in the sector of fruit 
cultivation. Despite the widespread use of traditional 
manual counting for citrus and other fruits, these methods 
suffer from low efficiency, high costs, and susceptibility to 
errors, especially under conditions of occlusion and 
environmental complexity. Consequently, this study 
combines 2D detection with 3D reconstruction techniques 
to propose a novel automated fruit counting method. Our 
method's physical significance lies in its ability to precisely 
map the spatial distribution of fruits on individual trees, 
enhancing the precision of orchard yield estimation, 
optimizing resource allocation, and reducing labor costs, 
thereby supporting high-quality agricultural development. 
B. Traditional Methods and Limitations 
1) Two-dimensional vision technologies 
Recent advancements by researchers like Sa et al.[1], 
Wang et al.[2], Zhuang et al.[3], and Z. Wang et al.[4] have 
significantly enhanced two-dimensional vision systems for 
fruit count statistics, especially for fruit detection and 
tracking. However, 2D methods still struggle with multiple 
fruits on a single tree under occluded conditions due to their 
inability to accurately discern spatial relationships between 
fruits, often resulting in duplicate counts.  
Conversely, three-dimensional vision models provide 
separate 3D coordinates for each target, enabling clear 
differentiation and accurate counting on individual trees, 
effectively addressing the shortcomings of 2D approaches. 
2) Three-dimensional vison technologies 
Three-dimensional vision technologies like radar-
assisted positioning and 3D reconstruction effectively 
address two-dimensional vision limitations. Techniques 
such as LiRaFusion and V-DETR have excelled in direct 3D 
object detection within spatial environments. Notably, 
methods based on Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF)[6] offer 
substantial advantages by generating high-quality 3D 
reconstructions and renderings, making them ideal for 
scenarios with complex lighting and severe occlusions. 
Challenges in collecting and processing extensive 3D 
datasets in agriculture scenarios still exists, as seen in the 
NeRF-RPN study by Hu et al.[7], highlight the need for 
integration with technologies like Region Proposal 
Networks (RPN) to enhance object localization and 
recognition. Despite its precision, NeRF-RPN demands 
significant computational resources, especially with high-
dimensional natural orchard data. 
Similarly, NeRF-Det by Xu et al.[8] refines 3D object 
detection 
using 
deep 
learning, 
optimizing 
spatial 
localization but also facing challenges in data collection and 
annotation for practical agricultural applications. To bridge 
the gaps of solely using 3D technologies, researchers like 
Liu et al.[9] are merging 2D and 3D approaches. They 
developed a fruit counting system using a Faster R-CNN

C. Research Focus 
Our study targets citrus counting for a single fruit tree, 
investigating how the combination of 2D and 3D vision 
techniques can enhance fruit counting accuracy. The 
primary research focus and innovations are detailed below: 
1) Utilization of NeRF and NeRF-Rays: 
We use instant-ngp[14] for swift NeRF training and 
continuous view synthesis. Then we developed functions 
including spatial cropping, viewpoints rearrangement and 
spatial perception based on the instant-ngp system. With 
Rays in NeRF, we successfully mapped 2D pixel 
information with spatial information in NeRF space. 
2) Integration of 2D and 3D Vision: 
To gather spatial information for each citrus, we 
integrated 
two-dimensional 
detection 
with 
three-
dimensional reconstruction from various perspectives. This 
hybrid approach leverages surface feature information 
from rendered 2D viewpoints and 3D data with rays to 
confirm the spatial position and independence of fruits, 
enhancing the fruit detection and counting workflow, data 
accuracy, and system efficiency. This not only boosts 
detection accuracy but also enhances counting reliability, 
making fruit count statistics more precise and practical. 
3) Advanced Processing and Analysis of 3D Data: 
Our approach includes the use of cluster analysis for 
data deduplication. Spatial data is processed and analyzed 
further to facilitate the visualization of fruits in three-
dimensional space, effectively generating spatial maps. This 
not only improves the accuracy of fruit count statistics but 
also provides a novel method for intuitively understanding 
the spatial arrangement and distribution of fruits. It 
addresses common challenges such as fruit occlusion and 
duplicate counts in traditional counting methods.

2) YOLO  
The YOLO (You Only Look Once) series of algorithms 
have been widely applied in various visual recognition tasks 
due to their cutting-edge 2D object detection capabilities. 
With continuous advancements, the YOLO series has 
undergone several iterations and updates. The version we 
are utilizing, YOLOv8, further optimizes the model 
structure and training process, enhancing the algorithm's 
generalization capability and detection precision. The 
choice to use YOLOv8 is primarily based on three points:  
a) High Precision and Efficiency: YOLOv8 performs 
excellently with complex backgrounds and varying lighting 
conditions, accurately identifying and locating fruits. 
b) Anchor-Free Detection: This feature allows the 
model to flexibly adapt to fruits of varying sizes and shapes, 
improving detection speed and accuracy.  
c) Optimized Network: YOLOv8 introduces a new 
convolution layer and C2f modules, enhancing feature 
extraction and the model's robustness and adaptability.

B. Implementation 
Our method is presented in a systematic way, consisting 
of four key modules: 3D Vision Reconstruction, 2D Vision 
Recognition, 3D Spatial Mapping and Data Processing.  
1) Three-dimensional Vision Reconstruction: 
We employ instant-ngp for NeRF reconstruction to 
provide precise spatial information essential for subsequent 
processes, with Colmap utilized for preprocessing the 
captured 
video 
data. 
Colmap, 
a 
comprehensive 
photogrammetric vision software, is primarily used to 
obtain initial camera parameters. Initially, Colmap conducts 
feature extraction and matching to generate camera 
parameters, which are then fed into instant-ngp to construct 
the NeRF space. After completing the 3D reconstruction, 
the system employs instant-ngp for automated cropping, 
retaining only the area of interest with the fruit trees. The 
result of cropping is illustrated in Figure 2, which 
automatically trims based on the size of the unit cube (the 
central object in view). This provides a clear visual 
foundation for further data collection and detailed analysis.

2) Two-dimensional Vison Recognition: 
The 2D vison recognition module involves targeted 
training of the YOLOv8 model as well as adjustments and 
cleaning of the dataset. The "2D Recognition Dataset" used 
will be detailed in the subsequent experimental section. This 
module is responsible for collecting screen information 
from the current perspective and using the model to 
calculate two-dimensional positional coordinates of all 
citrus fruits within the view. As shown in Figure 3, the 
system will traverse all re-planned perspectives, observing 
fruit trees from different angles and collecting 2D 
information. By training YOLOv8 on the specific dataset, 
the model focuses on citrus recognition on individual fruit 
trees and effectively distinguishes fruits from leaves.  
Under common complex backgrounds and variable 
lighting conditions, YOLOv8 can directly predict the 
bounding boxes of oranges from images. Additionally, by 
adopting a multi-scale training strategy, the detection 
capability for fruits of different sizes is enhanced, and the 
processing of fruit tree images taken from various distances 
is optimized, providing a solid data foundation for 
subsequent 3D spatial mapping steps. 
3) Three-dimensional Spatial Mapping: 
While the 2D localization module gather pixel 
coordinates in different viewpoints, the 3D spatial 
perception module collects spatial information through a 
three-step implementation using rays to accurately map the 
fruits on a single agricultural tree. The specific 
implementation steps are as follows: 
a) Rays toward Citrus: 
Initially, the system utilizes the fruit bounding boxes 
detected by the 2D vision recognition module to obtain the 
corresponding 2D pixel positions. This step involves 
invoking the "pixel_to_ray_pinhole" function in instant-ngp 
to simulate the pinhole camera model, transforming 2D 
image coordinates into 3D space rays. The pinhole camera 
model simplifies the imaging principles by simulating the 
process of light passing through a small aperture (pinhole) 
and forming an image on the imaging surface behind the 
camera. In this model, it is assumed that all light rays 
coming from the external world pass through this pinhole 
and form an inverted image on the camera's imaging surface. 
The pixel shift relative to the image center is identified, 
providing the basis for calculating the ray's direction, as all 
rays in the pinhole camera model are assumed to originate 
from the image center (the location of the pinhole). The 
fundamental formula is described as:

IV. 
CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK 
A. Conclusion 
This study successfully developed a spatial localization 
method that combines NeRF with two-dimensional vision 
recognition technologies for high-precision detection and 
counting of fruits on individual trees. The system effectively 
resolves issues of fruit occlusion and duplicate counting 
prevalent in traditional methods. Experimental results 
demonstrate that the system exhibits good adaptability and 
accuracy on fruit trees with varying fruit distributions. 
B. Future Work 
Future research will broaden the capabilities of this 
system. By switching the YOLOv8 model weights, the 
system will better adapt to various fruits like apples, pears, 
and apricots, ensuring accurate counts across different tree 
species. Moreover, we plan to incorporate advanced 3D 
reconstruction technologies such as dynamic NeRF for 
tracking moving or growing fruits, thus enhancing the 
model's dynamic capturing capabilities and real-time 
processing efficiency. This technology's application is also 
expected to extend into urban IoT, enhancing numerous 
smart cities localizing applications.  
In conclusion, the successful implementation of this 
study introduces a new dimension to future agricultural 
technologies, illustrating the vast potential of merging deep 
learning and NeRF modeling in both agriculture and smart 
city contexts. With ongoing technological enhancements 
and application expansions, we anticipate a broader 
implementation of agricultural automation and intelligence.
