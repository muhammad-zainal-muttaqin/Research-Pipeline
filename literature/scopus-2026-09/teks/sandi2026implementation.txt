Implementation and Optimization of a Machine
Learning Model for Pineapple Fruit Counting in
Aerial Imagery

1Instituto Tecnol´ogico de Costa Rica

Abstract—The pineapple industry is a cornerstone of Costa
Rica’s agricultural economy, but faces significant challenges
in optimizing monitoring processes, which rely heavily on
costly and imprecise manual counting methods. This study
focuses on the development and optimization of an automated
system for counting pineapple fruits from georeferenced
aerial imagery, with the goal of high accuracy with a
reduced dependence on specialized hardware. The imple-
mented methodology integrates image super-resolution tech-
niques with deep learning. We utilized the Real-ESRGAN+
model to improve image quality, processing each tile at
an effective resolution of 1280 x 1280px, which proved
crucial for identifying small fruits. A robust data set of
1,640 images was constructed through an iterative active
learning process. The YOLOv8s model was trained and
optimized using this dataset. The final system generates a
vector layer in Shapefile format (.shp), integrating the results
with Geographic Information Systems (GIS). Quantitative
results demonstrate high model effectiveness, achieving a
mean Average Precision mAP@0.5 of 0.965 on the test set.
The evaluation of complete orthomosaics showed strong
counting reliability, with percentage errors below 10% in
three of the four case studies, including images unseen during
training. The super-resolution stage was identified as the
main computational bottleneck, accounting for more than
96% of the total processing time. The proposed approach,
combining super-resolution, iterative dataset refinement, and
the YOLOv8s architecture, offers a robust and precise
solution for automating pineapple fruit counting, providing
an effective tool for precision agriculture.

I. INTRODUCTION
The pineapple industry has historically been a critical
economic driver in Costa Rica, representing one of the
main agricultural exports of the country [1]. Costa Rica
has established itself as a global leader in pineapple pro-
duction, contributing significantly to socioeconomic devel-
opment. However, the industry currently faces challenges
related to improving production efficiency and ensuring
sustainability in the face of market fluctuations and climate
change [2].
The monitoring of pineapple cultivation is essential,
particularly for organizations such as the United Nations
Development Program (PNUD), the Ministry of Environ-
ment and Energy (MINAE) and the National Center for
Geoenvironmental Information (CENIGA), which moni-
tor the expansion dynamics of pineapples due to both
economic impact and environmental repercussions [3].
Although satellite imagery and remote sensing techniques
are used, fruit counting and supervision methods remain
predominantly manual, leading to high costs and potential
inaccuracies.
The adoption of advanced technologies, specifically Ar-
tificial Intelligence (AI) and Deep Learning (DL), offers a
viable alternative to automate and optimize these processes
[4]. This implementation can significantly improve crop
management and improve productivity sustainably [5].
Furthermore, methodologies incorporating active learning
and image super-resolution, such as Real-ESRGAN, have
shown positive results by increasing sample quality and
reducing the need for specialized hardware during infer-
ence.
This project aims to implement and optimize a Machine
Learning model, based on YOLOv8, for the automated
counting of pineapple fruits in high-resolution aerial im-
agery. The workflow addresses the challenges posed by
large-sized TIFF images by fragmenting them into smaller
sections and applying super-resolution techniques (Real-
ESRGAN+) to enhance detail. This approach seeks to
improve accuracy and lower hardware requirements, facil-
itating wider adoption in resource-limited environments.
II. BACKGROUND AND RELATED WORK
A. Object Detection and Deep Learning
Deep Learning (DL), a subdiscipline of Machine Learn-
ing (ML), uses deep neural network architectures to pro-
cess complex data. Artificial neural networks, organized
into layers, transform information, allowing for complex
data processing via nonlinear activation functions and
optimization algorithms [6]. Deep neural networks, with
multiple hidden layers, are capable of learning complex
patterns directly from raw data, reducing the need for
manual feature engineering [7].
Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have revolu-
tionized computer vision due to their ability to detect local
patterns like edges and textures in grid-structured data,
such as images. CNN architectures typically consist of
convolutional layers, pooling layers, and fully connected
layers, which synergistically extract relevant features for
classification and prediction [6].
Object detection involves identifying the presence of
an object and its exact location using bounding boxes

[8]. Early methods based on low-level features have been
largely replaced by CNN-based algorithms such as R-
CNN, Fast R-CNN, and YOLO, which significantly im-
proved detection speed and accuracy [9], [6], [8].
B. YOLO and YOLOv8 Architecture
The YOLO algorithm (You Only Look Once) addresses
object detection as a direct regression problem, simulta-
neously predicting bounding boxes and class probabilities
in a single network pass, resulting in significantly faster
detection [8]. YOLOv8, released by Ultralytics in 2023,
represents a notable advancement in terms of precision and
efficiency, particularly for detecting small objects [10].
The YOLOv8 architecture is divided into the backbone
and the head. The backbone, a modified CSPDarknet53,
uses 53 convolutional layers to extract essential features
[11]. The head translates these features into concrete pre-
dictions, including location and category [12]. YOLOv8
incorporates a self-attention mechanism and a Feature
Pyramid Network (FPN) for multiscale detection, enabling
the identification of objects of various sizes.
While several object detection models such as Faster
R-CNN, EfficientDet, and YOLO-NAS offer competitive
accuracy, YOLOv8 was selected for this study due to its
favorable trade-off between detection precision, inference
speed, and hardware efficiency. Unlike two-stage detectors
like Faster R-CNN, which tend to have higher latency,
YOLOv8’s one-stage architecture enables real-time in-
ference, which is essential for large-scale aerial image
processing. Furthermore, compared to EfficientDet, which
often requires complex scaling and tuning, YOLOv8 pro-
vides a more streamlined deployment with state-of-the-
art performance, particularly for small object detection
tasks. YOLOv8 also demonstrated better compatibility
with custom training pipelines, active learning cycles, and
integration with GIS-ready output formats, making it an
optimal choice for the constraints and objectives of this
project.
C. Image Processing and Geospatial Data
Precision Agriculture (PA) leverages technologies such
as Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs or drones) for de-
tailed crop monitoring [13]. Aerial imagery, often provided
as orthomosaics, is commonly stored in GeoTIFF format.
GeoTIFF extends the TIFF standard by embedding spatial
information, including the Coordinate Reference System
(CRS) and Geotransformation data, allowing the image to
function as a georeferenced raster map [14].
Geographic Information Systems (GIS) are used to
manage and analyze spatially linked data. Georeferencing
is the crucial process for assigning real-world coordinates
to data [15]. Vector data, represented by points, lines, or
polygons, is frequently used for analysis. The Shapefile
(.shp) format is a standard for exchanging geospatial
vector data , and is used in this project to represent
detected fruits as georeferenced points [16].
D. Super-resolution and Data Augmentation
The quality of the input data is paramount for the
performance of deep learning models. Data Augmentation
increases the diversity of the training set by applying trans-
formations (geometric, photometric, or generative meth-
ods such as MixUp/Mosaic) while preserving semantic
meaning [17]. These techniques regularize the model and
prevent overfitting [18].
Real-ESRGAN (Realistic Enhanced Super-Resolution
Generative Adversarial Network) is a super-resolution
technique based on GANs that enhances image resolution,
recovering fine details. Real-ESRGAN+ incorporates high-
order degradation modeling, using purely synthetic data
to simulate realistic sensor artifacts and digital editing
effects. This approach improves the quality of images,
which is essential when dealing with low-resolution Aerial
Imagery [19].
III. METHODOLOGY
The automated pineapple fruit counting system uses
an agile and iterative methodology that encompasses the
research, analysis, design, implementation, and evaluation
phases. The system processes high-resolution orthomo-
saics, generating a report with automated fruit counts,
detection coordinates, and marked images.
A. Development Environment and Tools
The project was implemented in Python, using a local
workstation environment optimized for intensive Linux
training (Ubuntu 24.04 LTS). The hardware included an
Intel Core i7-11700 CPU and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX
3060 12 GB GPU. Key software libraries included Py-
Torch, Ultralytics YOLOv8, Real-ESRGAN, Rasterio, and
GeoPandas. Roboflow was used for data management,
augmentation, and collaborative labeling.
B. System Design and Workflow
The system workflow consists of several stages (Fig. 1):
input image selection, fragmentation, super-resolution,
prediction, alignment, and optimization.
Fig. 1. Second-level system diagram
1) Fragmentation
and
Super-Resolution:
High-
resolution orthomosaics are fragmented into manageable
tiles, initially 640 x 640 px, to optimize GPU memory
usage and inference time. Before inference, each tile
undergoes super-resolution using Real-ESRGAN+. This
process scales the fragments, enhancing details that
are often lost in low-resolution aerial imagery. The
enhanced images are then resized to 1280 x 1280 px for
compatibility with the YOLOv8 model.
2) Prediction and Alignment: The enhanced tiles are
processed by the YOLOv8 model, which performs object
detection and predicts bounding boxes with associated
confidence scores. Detections are filtered by confidence
thresholds.
Subsequently, the detections are aligned with the origi-
nal orthomosaic coordinate system. This requires a double

adjustment: (i) rescaling, using a scale factor s=0.5 to
translate from 1280 x 1280 px back to the physical size
of the original block, and (ii) displacement, by adding
the coordinates of the upper left corner of the block.
This procedure ensures spatial coherence and accurate
localization within the global context of the image.
3) Geospatial Output Generation: The final step in-
volves transforming the pixel coordinates of the detected
bounding boxes into real-world geographic coordinates.
This is achieved by extracting georeferencing metadata
from the original GeoTIFF file using libraries such as
Rasterio [20] and applying affine transformation equations.
The resulting data, representing each detected pineapple
fruit as a georeferenced point, is saved as a Shapefile
(.shp) [16]. This output facilitates direct visualization and
analysis within GIS software such as QGIS.
C. Dataset Preparation and Evolution
The data set was subjected to an iterative refinement
process.
1) Initial Dataset (640px): Started with 49 images,
augmented to 117 images using data augmentation
(flips and rotations). This data set revealed limita-
tions in the detection of small fruits due to detail
loss after Real-ESRGAN+ processing and resizing
to 640 x 640 px.
2) Improved Dataset (1280px): A second version
used 84 images, subjected to Real-ESRGAN+ (x4
scaling) and resized to 1280 x 1280 px. This res-
olution maintained optimal visual quality and fine
detail. Augmentation transformations were applied
via Roboflow [21], expanding the dataset to 616
images.
3) Final Dataset (1280px with Active Learning):
An active learning cycle was implemented to refine
the model further by identifying and manually re-
labeling 150 challenging images (e.g., low confi-
dence detections). Applying the same augmentation
pipeline expanded the final dataset to 1640 images.
The final data set contains 74,628 annotations for the
’pineapple’ class. Notably, 408 images (24.9% of the total)
contained no annotations (negative examples), a strategy
employed to improve the model’s discrimination capability
and reduce false positives.
The impact of super-resolution on labeling quality was
demonstrated through an experiment comparing autola-
beling with and without Real-ESRGAN+. The super-
resolution increased the number of annotations proposed
by 123% for a representative image, confirming that Real-
ESRGAN+ enhances the visibility of small fruits and fine
textures, leading to a higher quality data set for the training
of the detection model.

effective convergence. Both Train Box Loss and Train

C. Prediction in Individual Images (Final Model)
This study quantifies the performance of two models,
Model v1 (trained on 616 images) and Model v2 (trained
on 1640 images through active learning), for pineapple
fruit counting. Their detections were compared with man-
ual counts in four orthomosaics (Table II). Performance
was measured using absolute and percentage errors relative
to manual count.
The results consistently demonstrate the superiority of
Model v2, which significantly reduces the counting error.
For orthomosaics seen during training (WENDY006 and
FRUTA B12), Model v2 achieves a notable reduction in
percentage error, from 45.70% to 3.19% and from 52.96%
to 6.62%, respectively. Similarly, on unseen orthomosaics
(Lote Cerro and Lote Planta), which serve as a more
rigorous test of generalization, Model v2 outperforms
Model v1. The percentage error for Lote Cerro drops from
41.08% to 8.78%. Although the Lote Planta orthomosaic
presented a greater challenge, Model v2 still achieved a
substantial improvement, reducing the error from 75.00%
to 38.54% despite intrinsic complexities such as high weed
density.

D. Orthomosaic Prediction and Counting Accuracy
To validate the system’s robustness in real-world scenar-
ios, four complete orthomosaics were processed, including
two seen (WENDY006, FRUTA B12) and two unseen
(Lote Cerro, Lote Planta) during training.
The final model (Model v2, trained on 1640 images)
was compared with a previous model (Model v1, trained
on 616 images). The results, summarized in Table 12 (in
source document) , consistently demonstrate the superior-
ity of Model v2 and the effectiveness of the active learning
approach.
For WENDY006, Model v2 reduced the counting error
from 45.70% (Model v1) to 3.19%. For FRUTA B12,
the error decreased from 52.96% to 6.62%. Crucially, for
unseen orthomosaics, Model v2 significantly outperformed
Model v1. The error for Lote Cerro dropped from 41.08%
to 8.78%. Although Lote Planta presented the highest
challenge due to unique characteristics (e.g., dense weed
coverage), Model v2 reduced the error from 75.00% to
38.54%.
In general, Model v2 demonstrated high reliability,
achieving counting errors below 10% in three of the four
case studies.

V. CONCLUSIONS
The implemented solution, which combines Real-
ESRGAN+ super-resolution with the YOLOv8s architec-
ture, is an effective approach for automated pineapple
fruit counting in large-scale aerial imagery. By processing
images at an effective resolution of 1280 x 1280 px
and expanding the data set through active learning, the
final model achieved a mAP@0.5 of 0.965, validating the
hypothesis that effective resolution and data set quality
significantly improve the detection of small objects.
The system demonstrated high reliability in practical
applications, achieving counting errors below 10% in three
of the four test cases, including unseen orthomosaics.
However, the super-resolution stage remains the main
computational bottleneck, consuming more than 96% of
the total processing time. Automation of workflow and
generation of georeferenced Shapefile output provide a
valuable and practical tool for crop management in preci-
sion agriculture.
VI. RECOMMENDATIONS AND FUTURE WORK
To further enhance the solution’s practical utility, we
recommend:
1) Implementing Parallelism in Super-resolution:
Explore and implement parallelism techniques using
distributed processing or CUDA Streams to sig-
nificantly reduce the execution time of the super-
resolution phase.
2) Expanding Dataset Diversity and Optimizing
Image Acquisition: Incorporate orthomosaics from
various farms and different environmental conditions
to improve the resilience and generalizability of
the model. Although Real-ESRGAN+ is valuable,
acquiring images with the highest possible native
resolution is recommended for future work to reduce
dependence on intensive preprocessing.
3) Extensive
Agronomic
Validation:
Conduct
a
broader validation in collaboration with experts in
the pineapple industry, comparing system counts
with traditional estimation methodologies to robustly
quantify the impact of technology.
These enhancements would ensure more efficient pro-
cessing and consistent performance, paving the way for
advanced applications such as real-time monitoring during
drone flights.
