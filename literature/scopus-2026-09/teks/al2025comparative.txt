Comparative study of citrus fruits (Citrus reticulata Blanco cv. Batu 55) 
detection and counting with single and double labels based on 
convolutional neural network using YOLOv7

A B S T R A C T
Fruits detection and counting is an important task for yield prediction that could be achieved by computer vision. 
The ability to locate and count the fruits could also help the harvesting robot to do a picking task. YOLO is one of 
the deep learning models which is popular and widely used for object detection and has good performance in 
detection speed and precision. In the citrus counting task, the label could be set as a single label or multi-label 
which shows different citrus maturity. The performance of the deep learning model could be different with a 
different number of labels. Furthermore, there are several types of YOLOv7 models with different sizes and 
purposes which could also have different performances to do a similar task. This study aims to compare the 
performance of different kinds of YOLOv7-based deep learning models for citrus fruit detection and counting. 
Case study on the citrus cv. Batu 55 trees have been carried out. The results show that the original YOLOv7 
achieved the best performance both on single and double labels compared to the tiny and X versions of YOLOv7. 
The YOLOv7 could reach mAP50 of 0.906, a precision of 0.85, a sensitivity of 0.825, and an F1-score of 0.837, 
while for the counting task, the model has a good performance with R2 of 0.966.
1. Introduction
Citrus is the third-largest fruit plantation in Indonesia. The total 
production of Indonesian tangerine citrus commodities reached >2.8 
million tons in 2023 (Badan Pusat Statistik [1]). Meanwhile, citrus fruits 
have abundant nutritional content [2], a good shelf life, and relatively 
affordable prices [3]. This makes the public’s interest in the consump­
tion of citrus fruit very high. The national consumption level of citrus 
fruit reaches 4 kg per capita in 2020 (Balai Penelitian Tanaman Jeruk 
dan Buah Subtropika [4]Jestro]). Hence, the cultivation of this com­
modity holds promising prospects and stands as one of the most signif­
icant commodities.
Citrus cultivation exhibits promising potential, yet its development 
still encounters certain challenges. One of the challenges that occurs is 
the relatively high cost of harvesting oranges using human labor. The 
cost of harvesting citrus fruits per hectare is about 25 % of the total labor 
force for production [5]. Meanwhile, the number of workers is 
decreasing and wages are increasing in the agricultural sector over the 
last 5 years (Badan Pusat Statistik [[6], 2021]. This causes business 
profits to be increasingly eroded and threatens the sustainability of 
citrus cultivation. In addition, manual harvesting has a risk of devel­
oping musculoskeletal disorders due to picking fruit that is located high 
and carries a heavy harvest load [7]. Therefore, a citrus fruit harvesting 
system was developed using automated harvesting robots to overcome 
labor shortages, reduce the risk of injury to humans, and increase pro­
ductivity while increasing the profits of citrus cultivation.
The first challenge in developing citrus harvesting robots is the 
detection of citrus fruits through computer vision. The harvesting robot 
must be able to identify the oranges before taking the next action [7]. In 
addition, the application of object detection computation can also be 
implemented for predicting yield values. This can assist farmers in 
planning, management, marketing, risk control, as well as evaluation 
and improvement in agricultural activities. However, the next challenge 
in developing fruit detection is the complex tree structure, varying 
lighting conditions, and the color of the fruit, which is almost similar to 
the leaves [8]. The development of computer vision in digital

A Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) is a representation of the 
performance of the human brain in a mathematical modeling system to 
recognize digital image objects through simulation and computational 
learning. CNN is known to have excellent performance in detecting 
objects [10]. Several architectural models from popular CNNs, including 
Region-based Convolutional Neural Networks (R-CNN), mask R-CNN, 
Single-Shot Detectors (SSD), You Only Look Once (YOLO), and others 
[11]. The use of CNN in several object detection studies that have been 
carried out includes face detection [12], ship detection [13], apple 
detection [7], ear detection [14], vehicle detection [15], and others. 
Citrus fruit detection research has also been carried out using the Mask 
R-CNN architecture [16]. The Mask R-CNN architecture, on the other 
hand, must go through two stages of prediction in the learning process 
and segment more specific image objects, increasing the computational 
burden of the learning process [17].
The development of single-stage detection models such as YOLO has 
made fantastic progress and improved performance. Recently, the sev­
enth generation of the YOLO architecture (YOLOv7) was released with 
much better performance than the previous generation. The study con­
ducted by Wang et al. [18] highlights YOLOv7 as an object detection 
model that combines high precision with fast processing capabilities. 
Meanwhile, research on the detection of citrus objects using the seventh 
generation of YOLO has not been carried out. Therefore, this study uses 
the seventh generation of YOLO as the main architectural model for 
carrying out the learning process. To the date, YOLOv7 has been 
recognized as the most powerfull object detection algorithm [19].
Among YOLOv7 architecture family, YOLOv7 and YOLOv7-X shows 
better accuracy compared to YOLOv7-tiny, however, YOLOv7-tiny also 
have some advantages. In this research we would like to compare the 
performance of all version of YOLOv7 model for citrus fruit object 
detection. Furthermore, the accuracy of the DCNN model could be 
different if it is used to detect single and multiple object. Thus, this study 
will also investigate the performance between single and multilabel 
training YOLOv7 model. This multilabel detection i.e. immature and 
mature citrus is required to evaluate the harvesting time of the citrus 
orchard. By evaluating the performance and accuracy of both type of 
YOLOv7 and labeling methods, we aim to provide valuable insights for 
the development of robust and efficient fruit detection systems that can 
contribute to the agricultural industry, automation, and yield estimation 
processes.
2. Materials and methods
2.1. Citrus images acquisition
Citrus tree sample images were obtained from the Research Institute 
for Citrus and Subtropical Fruits (Balitjestro) in Batu City. The superior 
citrus variety used as the object is Batu-55 Tangerine Orange. By 
focusing on a particular citrus variety, the study aims to investigate the 
unique characteristics, growth patterns, and detection aspects associ­
ated with that specific variety. This choice allows for a more in-depth 
understanding of the specific citrus type, including its distinctive 
traits, development stages, and potential challenges. A total of 75 sam­
ples of citrus trees were utilized in this study.
Based on data from [20], the usage of Android operating system 
constitutes 89 % of the total users of other operating systems, thus 
justifying the utilization of Android-operated smartphones as tools for 
digital image acquisition. The digital image acquisition tool used is a 
standard apllication in a smartphone with the Xiami brand type Redmi 
Note 10S, has an Octa-core processor (2 × 2.05 GHz Cortex-A76 & 6 ×
2.0 GHz Cortex-A55), 8 MP camera, f/2.2, 118◦(ultrawide), 1/4.0″, 1 
.12 µm. The lighting of the object is sourced from sunlight without using 
a camera flash. Images are acquired from the time span between 7:00 
AM and 4:00 PM. The camera’s distance from the tree object varies; 
shooting focuses on the image frame that contains an orange tree. The 
shooting configuration on the camera is set automatically by smart­
phone application features, which include ISO, shutter speed, and 
aperture.
The digital image of each Citrus Tangerine Batu-55 tree was acquired 
from four different sides. An illustration of digital image acquisition can 
be seen in Fig. 1. This approach aims to increase the data variation by 
capturing images from different perspectives, thereby incorporating 
diverse lighting conditions. Thus, this dataset reflects the variation of 
lighting situations that may occur in real-life scenarios. The utilization 
of different angles in capturing images within this dataset is expected to 
enhance the convolutional neural network’s ability to recognize and 
classify objects accurately, even under varying lighting conditions. The 
images were captured by focusing on the objects that were visible and 
disregarding those that were hidden or covered by leaves. The approach 
involved counting only the objects that were captured in the images, 
without taking into account any concealed or obscured objects. This 
means that the analysis focused solely on the objects that were clearly 
visible, while any objects hidden or covered by leaves were not 
considered in the counting process.

ensures that the objects in the image are not distorted or stretched. 
Resizing the images to a specific size reduces the computational load 
during training and inference. A smaller image size requires fewer 
computational resources, enabling faster processing and more efficient 
memory utilization. However, the chosen size may strike a balance be­
tween having sufficient resolution for object recognition and classifi­
cation tasks while also considering the available computational 
resources or memory constraints. It allows for an optimal compromise 
between image quality and efficient resource utilization.
The dataset is divided into 264 (90 %) training and 31 (10 %) vali­
dation samples. In object detection tasks using the same dataset, the 
classes are divided into two categories: single label and double label. In 
the single label class, each image is annotated with a single object class, 
specifically "orange," without considering color variations. Conversely, 
the double label class divides the annotations based on color, dis­
tinguishing between "green" and "orange". This approach allows for 
studying and training the model to recognize oranges as a whole or 
based on their color, enabling more detailed and specific modeling of 
object variations within the dataset.
2.3. Training model
The object detection for the learning model was performed using 
three different variants: YOLOV7-Tiny, YOLOV7, and YOLOV7X. This 
choice was made to assess the performance and suitability of these 
models in the context of the study. Object detection is a critical step in 
computer vision tasks as it involves identifying and localizing objects of 
interest within an image. The selection of multiple variants, allows for a 
comparative analysis to determine the most suitable model for the 
specific requirements of the study. This evaluation helps to ensure that 
the chosen object detection model provides optimal performance and 
meets the desired objectives of the research.
The consideration and selection of appropriate hyperparameters play 
a crucial role in the model learning process. In this study, two key 
hyperparameters, namely batch size and epoch, were investigated as 
part of a comparative analysis. The batch size represents the quantity of 
training data utilized in a single iteration, influencing the computational 
efficiency and convergence behavior of the learning algorithm. To 
explore the impact of batch size on model performance, four different 
values were examined: 4, 8, 10, and 32. On the other hand, the epoch 
parameter signifies the total number of forward and backward passes 
over the training data during the learning phase. By varying the number 
of epochs, specifically 25, 50, 100, and 150, the study aimed to assess 
the model’s learning capacity and convergence properties. These in­
vestigations into the batch size and epoch hyperparameters are essential 
for determining optimal settings that strike a balance between compu­
tational efficiency and model performance.
2.4. Model evaluation
The study’s YOLOV7 architectural designs were carried out to 
determine performance as a comparison of the model’s learning out­
comes. The Confusion Matrix (CM) is the basis for the prediction results 
and is a matrix that can be used to measure the performance of an object 
detection model. CM in the object detection model shows the classifi­
cation truth value of each checkmark detected in an image. The CM 
matrix values in the object detection model consist of: 
• True Positive (TP) is the value of the detection model with pre­
dictions of the presence of objects, and the results are correct.
• False Positive (FP) is the value of the detection model with an object 
prediction that is incorrect.
• False Negative (FN) is the value of the detection model with the 
prediction that there are no objects and the results are wrong.
The following metrics have been calculated and reported to evaluate 
the effectiveness of the detection model: Mean Average Precision (mAP), 
Precision Value, Sensitivity, Average Recall (AR), and F1-score. These 
metrics were derived using the CM value, which serves as a basis for 
performance evaluation. The study’s YOLOV7 architectural designs 
were examined for accuracy using the mAP performance evaluation 
criteria. By providing these metrics and analyzing their results, the 
paper aims to demonstrate the reliability and effectiveness of the pro­
posed technique. The following is the equation formula for the object 
detection model’s performance evaluation parameters:

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Comparison of YOLOV7 models peformances as object detection
The best model in this study is the result of the dual label image data 
learning model using the standard YOLOv7 architecture with a batch 
size 4 configuration. The results of the best model values include Mean 
Averege Precision (mAP50), Precision, Recall, and F1-Score respec­
tively, of 0.906; 0.850; 0.825; and 0.837. Also, the size of the YOLOv7- 
Tiny model can be considered, as it is 12.4 MB in size and its perfor­
mance is not far from the standard YOLOv7. The performance of each 
type of model can be seen in Table 1.
The results show the diversity of mAP values for different types of 
architecture. The average YOLOv7-X architecture mAP results are lower 
than the YOLOv7-Tiny architecture mAP results. This can happen 
because too many parameters are used or considered in the YOLOv7-X 
model, leading to overfitting. As a result, the mAP results for the 
YOLOv7-X architecture are lower than those for the YOLOv7-Tiny ar­
chitecture. This can happen because too many parameters are used or 
considered in the YOLOv7-X model, causing overfitting. This means that 
some parameters in the YOLOv7-X model are not required for training 
the object detection model with this research dataset [21]. However, the 
performance of YOLOv7-X improves as batch size increases. This shows 
that increasing the batch size of models with multiple labels can reduce 
the weight of information from data sources so as to prevent overfitting.
Based on the result data from a single class, the YOLOv7 architecture 
with a standard type and a larger batch size shows a worse map result. 
YOLOv7 with a batch size of 10 produces the lowest mAP value of 0.480. 
The larger the batch size, the more information is processed by one 
computational learning model, and this has the potential for under­
fitting to occur. This means that there is a lot of important information 
that is not considered in the computational learning process [22].

In a previous study, which observed the detection of Citrus reticulata 
objects using Faster-RCNN, a high accuracy value of 0.90 was achieved 
[23]. Considering Faster-RCNN’s focus on accuracy, it is noteworthy 
that the YOLOv7 model demonstrated superior performance. Further­
more, YOLOv7 exhibits lighter computational requirements and a 
smaller model size compared to Faster-RCNN. While Faster-RCNN 
resulted in a model size exceeding 100 MB [24], the YOLOv7 model 
has a compact size of approximately 73MB. These findings highlight the 
advantages of YOLOv7 in terms of performance and efficiency, posi­
tioning it as a promising choice for object detection tasks in the context 
of Citrus reticulata detection.

compared to the number of labels as validation produces a regression. 
The best model for calculating tangerine objects has very good perfor­
mance with an R square value of 0.9661. The regression graph in the 
form of a scattering plot between the predicted value of the number of 
objects and the actual value of the number of objects can be seen in

3.3. Speed of model learning
The speed of learning that is carried out on each type of architecture 
has different abilities. The speed and learning outcomes of the best 
model in this study is the YOLOv7 architecture type. The YOLOv7 mAP 
value at the 25th epochs has reached 0.693 and the final YOLOv7 mAP 
value at the 150th epochs has reached 0.906. The best type of archi­
tecture after YOLOv7 is YOLOv7-Tiny. The YOLOv7-Tiny mAP value 
was 0.873 and almost close to the best YOLOv7 model in this study. The 
use of models on different architectures can be adjusted according to 
needs [25]. YOLOv7-Tiny has the advantage of being a lighter model 
size so that the implementation of the technology used can be more 
affordable. The visualization of the speed performance differences for 
each type of 7th generation YOLO architecture in this study can be seen 
more clearly in Fig. 5.
4. Conclusion
This study shows that data acquisition produces 295 images, con­
sisting of 264 training images and 31 validation images. Image data 
segmentation for single labels produces 1278 orange labels. Image data 
segmentation for double labels produces 585 green orange labels and 
693 orange labels. The best model in this study is the learning model 
results of double label image data using the standard YOLOv7 archi­
tecture with configuration batch size 4. The results of the best model 
values are Mean Averege Precision (mAP50), Precision, Recall, F1- 
Score, and R square respectively, of 0.906; 0.850; 0.825; 0.837; and 
0.9661. The results of the CNN-based tangerines object detection model 
using YOLOv7 have the potential to be implemented in automatic 
harvester robot technology.
