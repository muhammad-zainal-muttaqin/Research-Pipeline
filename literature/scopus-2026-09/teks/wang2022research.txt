 
  
Abstract— Accurate monitoring of fruit quantity in apple 
orchards will allow growers to manage their orchards more 
efficiently, leading to higher yields. In addition, the rapid and 
accurate inspection of fruit in the orchard is also one of the basic 
technologies needed for smart agriculture. This paper proposes a 
real-time method for apple tracking and yield estimation. UAV 
carrying RGB camera is used as an inspection platform, which 
analyzes the video in real time during the inspection. The 
algorithm is built according to the Tracking-by-Detecting 
framework, where YOLOV5 target detection model is used to 
obtain apples’ exact position. Meanwhile, the apples’ position in 
next frame is estimated according to the optical flow calculated 
from FlowNet2 model. Then, the predicted position and detected 
position is matched by the Hungarian algorithm. A dataset close 
to the actual situation in the orchard is constructed to verify the 
effectiveness of the proposed method. To improve the detection 
accuracy of apple under actual scene, the data enhancement 
strategy of random occlusion and mosaic enhancement is used 
for model training. As a result, the accuracy of apple detection 
achieved by the algorithm in this paper is 85.5%, which is 11% 
higher than previous studies. Besides, it can keep well tracking 
of detected apples even under the influence of complex occlusion 
or other factors, and achieve an accuracy of 90.39% in apple 
yield estimation. More importantly, this algorithm can reach a 
speed of 20FPS on the experimental platform, which meets the 
real-time requirements of UAV inspection. 
I. INTRODUCTION 
Nowadays, apple is one of the most widely grown and sold 
fruits all over the world. China accounts for nearly half of the 
worldwide apple production according to the data from the 
Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations. 
However, apple cultivation in China is still dominated by 
manual labor, with low level of mechanization and automation 
[1]. With the aging of the population and the migration of 
labor to cities and towns, China's apple industry urgently 
needs to be automated and intelligent. Among them, the

picking technology can be realized. Among them, apple 
detection, tracking and counting methods based on machine 
vision is the key technology. Relevant researches have been 
carried out and applied in apple pruning [2], yield estimation 
[3-4], harvesting [5] and other aspects. 
In terms of apple's visual detection, early studies mainly 
used manual extracted image features combined with machine 
learning methods for fruit detection [6]. However, recent 
studies have shown that deep learning-based methods can 
achieve better display results. Inkyu Sa et. al. [7] fused RGB 
image and NIR image, and then adopted Faster R-CNN model 
to realize the detection of fruits, which provides a method to 
merge multispectral data. LJ Biffi et. al. [8] used Adaptive 
Training Sample Selection (ATSS) deep learning method to 
reduce the cost of data annotation. X Wang et. al. [9] designed 
an apple detection network for apple harvesting robot and 
used the method of instance segmentation to detect apples. K 
Zhang et. al. [10] also designed the apple instance 
segmentation network for apple picking robot, and fused the 
detection result from two cameras. Other recent studies are not 
too detailed here [11-13]. The above research is aimed at 
apple detection under the ideal environment of close distance 
and no serious occlusion, and most algorithms cannot run in 
real time on the inspection robot. However, what flying 
inspection robot needs to face is real-time fruit detection for 
small targets under the condition of serious illumination and 
occlusion influence. And there are few studies concern on this 
issue. 
Besides, in terms of apple’s visual tracking and counting, 
researchers mainly focus on off-line algorithms. Nicolai Hani 
et. al. [4] simplified the problem into two parts: detection of 
apple clusters and classification of apple clusters by number 
of apples in the cluster, and the algorithm could only calculate 
the number of apples in a single image. Xu Liu et. al. [14] 
obtained the apple center by means of semantic segmentation, 
and realized apple matching in continuous frames through 3d 
reconstruction of apples, so as to realize apple counting, 
which is time-consuming. Uddhav Bhattaraia et. al. [15] 
designed a network based on VGG16 to estimate the number 
of apples directly from a single image. This method also 
cannot be applied to apple counts in video sequences. Similar 
to apple's detection, methods suitable for flying inspection 
robots are rarely studied.

This paper aims to provide a real-time visual tracking 
algorithm used for apple yield inspection with UAV. As 
shown in Fig.1, the algorithm includes YOLOv5 apple 
detection module, position estimation module based on 
FlowNet2 and matching counting module based on Hungarian 
algorithm. Combined with the UAV inspection method 
proposed in this paper, the apple yield in the orchard can be 
obtained in real time during the inspection. The main 
contributions of our paper are:(1) a flying inspection method 
for apple orchard; (2) a real-time apple visual tracking 
algorithm used for apple quality inspection; and (3) a strategy 
for counting both healthy and diseased apples based on 
Hungarian matching algorithm. 
II. PROPOSED METHOD 
A. UAV Inspection method 
 
Figure 2.  UAV inspection method in apple orchard 
In order to improve the efficiency of inspection, UAVs 
carrying RGB cameras are used as inspection platforms. The 
inspection method of UAV determines the characteristics of 
the images obtained during inspection. Apple trees in orchards 
are usually planted in rows. Therefore, the UAV inspection 
method designed in this paper is shown in Fig. 2. For tall and 
densely planted arbor trees, UAV will take images from the 
top down for inspection; for dwarf trees with large planting 
width, the UAV will fly over the middle of the tree path and 
take images from the horizontal direction for inspection. 
Therefore, in the images obtained by inspection, fruits are 
usually small and seriously blocked by leaves and other 
objects, accompanied by strong light changes. In addition, due 
to the high flight speed of UAV, the target movement distance 
between frames in the inspection video sequence is large. 
Therefore, the apple tracking algorithm designed in this paper 
based on the framework of tracking-by-Detecting. And the 
problem about apple’s yield inspection is divided into three 
sub-problems: (1) Visual detection of small sized apples that 
are shaded and affected by light and shadow (2) Position 
estimation of detected apples in consecutive video frames (3) 
Matching between estimated position and detected position of 
apples followed by the counting of both kind of apples. The 
detailed algorithm architecture is shown in Fig. 1. The input of 
the algorithm is real-time images taken by the UAV. 
B. Detection with YOLOv5s 
Compared with traditional machine vision methods, the 
object detection method based on deep learning has more 
advantages in accuracy and generalization ability. They are 
further divided into one-stage and two-stage, among which the 
method detection speed of one-stage is faster. Therefore, this 
method is more suitable for real-time detection application

scenarios. YOLO series of algorithms are representative of 
one-stage class detection methods, which are widely used in 
various target detection tasks. Fan et. al. [16] applied the 
pruned YOLOv4 model to the apple detection task on the 
apple sorting line, and obtained an average accuracy of 
93.9%.  
As an upgraded version of YOLOv4, YOLOv5 is widely 
used by researchers for its advanced detection speed and 
accuracy. Its improvement was reflected in three aspects :(1) 
Mosaic data augmentation, adaptive image scaling and other 
image preprocessing methods were adopted, which greatly 
optimized the performance of the model; (2) CSPNet is used 
in backbone and head to strengthen the ability of network 
feature fusion; (3) The structure of FPN and PANet in head 
accelerates the information flow between various levels. 
According to the results presented by the authors, YOLOv5s 
can run at a speed of 6.4ms per frame on COCO dataset, which 
shows the great real-time performance. 
In order to complete the accurate detection of apple in the 
complex environment and ensure the real-time direction of the 
algorithm, the lightweight YOLOv5s model is used as the core 
module of the detection. The S model of YOLOV5 also has 
several versions, the most commonly used ones are Yolov5S 
and YOLOV5S6. On the basis of the former, the latter uses 
convolution to replace the Focus structure, and uses CSPNet 
containing three layers of convolution to replace various types 
of CSPNet in original model, which increases the depth of the 
network and the number of parameters. The detection 
accuracy is improved at the expense of running speed. In the 
training stage of the model, the transfer learning strategy is 
applied to accelerate the model training speed. The 
parameters of the YOLOv5s model are initialized by its 
pre-trained model on the COCO dataset. 
C. Position Estimation Based on Optical Flow 
Under the framework of tracking-by-detecting, the core of 
target tracking lies in the matching of corresponding objects 
between successive frames. Predicting the target's possible 
position in the next frame can greatly improve the accuracy of 
matching. In multi-target visual tracking, Kalman filter, 
particle filter, optical flow and other methods are often used to 
estimate targets’ position [17]. Since video images may 
contain many apples, it is not practical to build tracking 
models for each individual. Therefore, it is a more feasible 
scheme to directly calculate pixel-level image motion 
information by optical flow method and then obtain the 
estimation of apples’ position.  
Studies in recent years show that optical flow diagrams 
obtained by deep learning-based methods have higher quality 
and faster inference speed compared with traditional methods 
such as Farneback [18]. In the experiment of this paper, the 
traditional method takes about 100ms for each frame of 
optical flow, which cannot meet the real-time requirements of 
the algorithm. FlowNet2 is a deep learning optical flow 
method with good performance at present [19]. It 
continuously optimizes the optical flow diagram by stacking 
FlowNetC and FlowNetS on the basis of FlowNet. In order to 
ensure the real-time performance of the tracking algorithm, 
the FlowNet2CS module, part of FlowNet2, is selected as the 
reasoning model of optical flow. 
 
Figure 3.  Apples’ position estimation based on optical flow

In the binary graph model described above, our goal is to 
find as many matches as possible. The Hungarian algorithm is 
usually used to solve the maximum matching problem of 
binary graphs. Therefore, the Hungarian algorithm is used to 
obtain the matching relationship between apple's estimated 
position and the new detected position. In the matching results, 
it is possible that some apples are not matched, as shown in 
Fig. 6. The apple that is not matched in the predicted position 
means that the apple is not detected in the new frame, and the 
apple that is not matched in the detected position is the new 
apple in the image. To avoid double counting, the counting 
strategy is designed as follows: 
1. Open a video sequence and detect the apples in first 
frame to initialize the count. 
2. Read a new frame from the video sequence. 
3. Calculate the optical flow and estimate the position of 
detected apples. 
4. Detect the apples in new frame. 
5. Matches detected apples with newly detected apples 
6. For the matched individual in detected apple, the 
location is updated based on matching results. 
7. For the unmatched individual in the detected apple, the 
actual position is updated with the estimated position. If 
the unmatched individual does not appear in 10 
consecutive frames or the detection frame is beyond the 
range of the image, the individual is deleted. 
8. For unmatched individuals in newly detected apples, 
add them to the current apple queue and update the 
count. 
9. Go to 2 if the video does not end, otherwise save the 
video processing result and end the program. 
III. EXPERIMENT AND RESULT 
 
Figure 5.  Experimental principle 
The experimental principle is shown in Fig. 5. Model 
apples were hung on trees to simulate real orchards. Then, the 
orchard inspection images and videos obtained by UAV are 
used to construct apple detection dataset and apple tracking 
and counting data set respectively. Manually marked apple 
detection dataset is used for training and testing the apple 
detection module. The apple tracking and counting dataset is 
used to verify the performance of the whole algorithm. The 
experiment is carried out on NVIDIA GeForce 940MX 
platform with only 2GB video memory, which can well 
simulate the computing performance of embedded platform.

A.  Data Acquisition 
The data set constructed in this article consists of partial 
images in the Minneapple dataset [20] as well as images 
captured in the simulated experiment, of which 62.2% were 
captured experimentally. The dataset contains 1790 simulated 
real inspection images for Apple inspection and 9 videos for 
verification of the Apple tracking and production estimates. In 
the image, 28084 apples were manually labeled for training 
and testing the network. The dataset used to detect Apple was 
split into separate training and testing set, with 1,211 and 579 
images, respectively. 
 
Figure 6.  Images in dataset 
The images in the dataset are shown in the Fig. 6. The left 
image is the selected Minneapple dataset and the right image 
is the inspection image collected from the experiment. In the 
experiment, the apple model was hung on the outdoor fruit 
tree according to the growth status of apple on the actual fruit 
tree, and DJI Mavic 3 was used as the inspection platform to 
shoot inspection images and videos. The data set fully 
simulates the characteristics of small apple targets, which are 
seriously affected by occlusion and illumination.

IV. CONCLUSION 
A real-time UAV visual tracking algorithm for apple yield 
inspection is proposed. A dataset suitable for apple orchard 
flying inspection was constructed to train and verify the 
algorithm. The YOLOv5s6 model is used as the apple 
detection model, and the detection accuracy reached 85%. 
FlowNet2CS model is used to get optical flow for apple 
location prediction. By building the bipartite graph model, 
applying Hungarian algorithm and combining with the 
counting strategy, tracking and counting of apple in the 
orchard was realized. And the average counting accuracy 
reached 90.39%. Besides, the whole algorithm can run at the 
speed of 20FPS, which means this algorithm can work on the 
UAV in real time.  
Future work includes adding apples with diseases and 
insect pests to the data set, train the algorithm to realize the 
detection of diseases and insect pests, and finally realize the 
rapid inspection of apples in the orchard. In addition, this 
method will be transferred to apple picking robot to achieve 
precise apple picking.
