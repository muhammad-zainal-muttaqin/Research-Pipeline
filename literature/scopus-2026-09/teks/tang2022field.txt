Abstract—Automated grape yield estimation is one of the
important aspects of intelligent vineyard management. In or-
der to meet requirements of fast estimation and large-scale
grape yield estimation in complex ﬁeld environments, this paper
proposes a Spatial Attention Grape Berry Counting Network
(SAGBCNet). Firstly, the Spatial Feature Network (SFNet) is
used to extract more spatial information of grape berries in
image. Then attention mechanism is used to adaptively ﬁt the
long-distance context features of location and channel, so as to
effectively distinguish the grape berry from background. Finally,
through Feature Fusion Network, feature maps of different scales
are fused to generate the predicted density map, and counting
results of grape berries in the ﬁeld are obtained. The experiment
on the ﬁeld grape counting dataset showed that SAGBCNet
obtained a lower counting error for grape berries. The mean
absolute error (MAE) and mean squared error (MSE) were 35.39
and 47.39 respectively.
Index Terms—grape berry, spatial attention, feature fusion,
object counting
I. INTRODUCTION
In recent years, China’s grape planting area and output
have increased. Traditional vineyard management has begun
to change to a more efﬁcient and intelligent way. Grape
automatic yield estimation is one of the important links to
realize intelligent management of vineyards. At the same
time, it is of great signiﬁcance to reduce production costs

key step in yield estimation process. Currently, the counting
of grape berries still relies mainly on traditional manual
observation methods. This method has low efﬁciency, strong
subjectivity and high labor cost. Some efforts have been made
to automate the counting of grape berries [1, 2]. However,
it has been studied in a single context, with a high degree of
individual differentiability and a low number of grape bunches.
It is just as inadequate for rapid estimation and large-scale
grape yield estimation in complex ﬁeld environments [1].
Therefore, ﬁeld grape berry counting needs a gradual shift
from tedious, limiting working methods to low-cost, efﬁcient
and robust approaches. The computer vision technology and
deep learning get quickly development in the decades, object
counting method based on computer vision is expected to
become an effective means of ﬁeld grape counting automation.
Object counting is one of important research directions in
the ﬁeld of computer vision. In the ﬁeld of agriculture, object
counting is often used to solve practical agricultural problems
such as yield estimation and planting density. Generally, object
counting based on detection and object counting based on
regression are two traditional counting methods. The counting
method based on detection usually adds number of detection
boxes in the image to obtain counting result. Chen et al.
[3] tried to count spruce by using the improved YOLOv3
algorithm. Zhang et al. [4] obtained fruit quantity through
the automatic detection of holly fruits and other fruits in the
orchard by YOLOX. However, because the detection based
method depends on the accuracy of individual detection,
this method will underperform when the object is seriously
occluded. Therefore, it is unsuitable to solve the problem of

on regression is to learn mapping relationship between low-

Compared with the method based on regression, the better
counting result is to establish the linear mapping of object
features, which makes use of the rich spatial information in
the corresponding density map as much as possible. In the
ﬁeld of crowd counting, for the purpose of collecting multi-
scale context information, dilated convolution is used in the
crowded crowd scene studied by Li et al. [6]. As the same
time, Liu et al. [7] adaptively encoded the multi-layer context
information into features of this layer, so as to obtain the
crowd counting results. In the ﬁeld of agriculture, the counting
method based on density map also performs well. Lu et al. [8]
obtained local counts of corn whiskers from density maps by
dividing the images. Huang et al. [9] obtained a high-quality
density map by using density grade classiﬁcation, and then
counted the number of cotton bolls under the background
of complex cotton ﬁelds. Bereciartua-P´erez et al. [10] used
complete convolution regression network to obtain probability
density map and calculate whiteﬂy in eggplant leaves. Wang et
al. [11] proposed used attention module to reﬁne wheat panicle
characteristics to obtain an improved EffectiveDet-D0 wheat
panicle counting model. Wen et al. [12] used BiFPN to better
fuse multiscale features of wheat spike, and then detected and
counted wheat spike in complex ﬁeld environment. However,
the above methods ignore spatial correlation between object
features, which limits the counting performance to a certain
extent.
This paper proposes a Spatial Attention Grape Berry Count-
ing Network (SAGBCNet) to solve the problem of grape berry
counting in complex ﬁeld environment. The algorithm ﬁrst
extracts spatial information features of grape berries. Then
uses attention mechanism to ﬁt the long-distance location
features. Finally, multi-scale feature fusion was carried out
to obtain predicted grape berry density map. The experiments
compared different counting methods and validated the effec-
tiveness of SAGBCNet, obtaining better counting results on
the ﬁeld grape counting dataset.
II. MATERIALS AND METHODS
A. Dataset
The publicly available Embrapa Wine Grape Instance Seg-
mentation Dataset (WGISD) [13] was used as the experimental
dataset for this paper. Considering the image quality and
annotation feasibility of WGISD [13], we found that the
dataset can also be used for study of grape berry counting
in the ﬁeld, and selected 225 images to build the ﬁeld grape
counting dataset in this paper. It consists of 177 images with
a size of 3456×5184 and 48 images with a size of 864×1296
RGB image composition. 169 images of them were used as
training set and 56 images as test set. Labelme 3.16.2 is used
to manually label each grape berry in each image. The number
of grape berries in each image ranged from 206 to 1585, and
a total of 156272 grape berries were ﬁnally labeled.
Grapes of different varieties show various forms in fruit
yield, berry outline, color and other characteristics. In order to
assess effectiveness and robustness of the counting algorithm
in this paper, the ﬁeld grape counting dataset is divided
into ﬁve grape species as shown in Fig. 1. The species are
Chardonnay, Cabernet Franc, Cabernet Sauvignon, Sauvignon
Blanc and Syrah. There are 50, 42, 38, 47 and 48 images for
each grape variety. These ﬁve types are included in training

B. SAGBCNet Architecture
The architecture of SAGBCNet based on MobileCount [14]
will be introduced in this section. As shown in Fig. 2, Spatial
Feature Network (SFNet), attention module and multi-scale
feature fusion form SAGBCNet.
Spatial feature network In order to reduce parameter scale
and not lose grape berry pixels, we choose bottlenecks in Mo-
bileNetV2 [15] to form the backbone network of SAGBCNet.
To make SAGBCNet lighter, we added a 3 × 3 convolution
of stride 2 and padding 1, and a 3 × 3 max pooling layer of
stride 2 before SFNet. The max pooling layer has been proved
by researchers that adding this max pooling layer can reduce
ﬂoating point operations (FLOPs) by more than 70% [14].
The extraction of grape berry features in SAGBCNet structure
mainly depends on SFNet, and its detailed structure is shown
in Fig. 3. SFNet is composed of four spatial-bottlenecks in
total. After max pooling layer, the input grape berry original
map has been mapped to

spatial-bottlenecks successively undergoes a 1 × 1 Point-wise
convolution, a 3×3 Depth-wise convolution and a 1×1 Point-
wise convolution. In particular, a dilated convolution is added
after the second 1×1 Point-wise convolution. The ﬁnal output
is a pixel-by-pixel sum of dilated convolved features and initial
input features. The effect of dilated convolution is to increase
the perceptual ﬁeld of convolution kernel with reduced spatial
feature loss. This operation allows features output at each layer
to contain spatial information about a larger area around the
grape berry. The region of interest in the image information
is paid more attention by the density map regression through
this spatial information, improves counting performance [16].
Speciﬁcally, to avoid losing local information, we set dilation
rate of the dilated convolution to four numbers that gradually
increase with the number of spatial-bottlenecks, so that the
dilation rates are 1, 2, 3, and 4. In addition, to obtain more
robust counting effect, padding of each dilated convolution
layer is set to the same value as dilation rate, keeping feature
map size constant, so as to reduce loss of detailed features.
At this point, we have proposed a feature extraction module
that can obtain a larger range of spatial information spatial-
bottlenecks around the object, which in turn constitutes SFNet.

features such as colour and texture of the objects. The spatial
relationship features between objects also become blurred.
Therefore, to enhance detailed features of grape berries in
the deep features, Dual Attention Network (DANet) [17] was
used. This module captures the relationship between differ-
ent channels and positions through an attention mechanism.
DANet [17] consists of two parts, Position Attention Module
(PAM) and Channel Attention Module (CAM), and the speciﬁc
structure is shown in Fig. 4. PAM and CAM are used to
calculate position and channel correlation respectively. The
PAM calculates the similarity between each pixel and all other
pixels, where a higher similarity indicates a higher degree of
dependency between the two and a higher weight is assigned
to the corresponding location when aggregating other location
features. Similarly, CAM uses a self-attentive mechanism to
calculate contextual relationship between different channels to
obtain the inﬂuence weights of the different channels. The
feature maps from PAM and CAM are summed pixel by pixel
to obtain ﬁnal output. It is important to note that output feature
maps are kept at the same size and number of channels as
input feature maps during the training process. DANet [17]
is able to adaptively ﬁt the contextual relationships of long-
range features at position and channel. The aim is to improve
model counting performance by effectively distinguishing
grape berries from background.
Fig. 4. DANet network structure.
Feature Fusion The four layers of feature maps generated
by SFNet are fused using Feature Fusion network. In the
current research, FPN [18], SSD [19] and others have proposed
methods that fusion multiple layers of feature maps at different
scales. Generally speaking, the details are expressed more
in shallow feature layer, and the semantic information is
expressed more in deep feature layer. When shallow features
are fused to deep features, spatial contextual information is
propagated to deep features along with more noise [20]. For
the purpose of getting more accurate counting results, the four
layer feature map is gradually fused from deep to shallow
by SFNet. Firstly, the size normaliser upsamples deep feature
map to the same size as the previous layer feature map on a
scale basis. Then the number of output channels of the 1 × 1
convolution is adjusted to
1
2 the number of input channels
to ensure consistency in the number of channels. Finally, the
features are added and fused pixel by pixel from deep to
shallow. The above steps are repeated to complete fusion of
the four-layer feature map. After multi-layer feature fusion, the
size of feature map is H
4 × W
4 ×32. After removing redundant
information and upsample, a predicted density map of size
H × W × 1 is obtained and ﬁnal counting result is obtained.

The evironment for the algorithm experiment is Ubuntu
20.04, Pytorch 1.7.1 open source framework, Python 3.8
programming language, Intel Core i9-10900X CPU@3.70GHz
and GeForce RTX 3090.

resized to 864×1296 to ensure the same data distribution. In
addition, all images in the dataset were preprocessed with
random cropping (size 256×1024) and ﬂipping operations to
reduce computational effort and increase training sample size.
The batch size and epoch are were to 5 and 300 respectively.
The initial learning rate was set to 1×10−4 and model training
is adjusted to one-tenth of the current learning rate at 100 and
200 iterations to ensure that the model converges and is close
to the optimum. Adam algorithm [21] was used to optimise
model parameters. This paper chose to use Euclidean distance
as loss for network training, with loss function is deﬁned as

CONCLUSION
In this paper, an algorithm named SAGBCNet for ﬁeld grape
berry counting is proposed, which is based on spatial attention.
The algorithm solves problem of counting grape berries in a
complex background in ﬁeld in a simple and efﬁcient way. In
addition, WGISD was re-labelled to construct a ﬁeld grape
counting dataset containing 156,272 grape berries. On this
dataset, this paper is compared with four commonly used
counting algorithms, CSRNet [6], CANNet [7], TasselNetV2+
[8] and MobileCount [14]. The experimental results show
that our counting performance is all better than comparative
algorithms in the table, achieving lowest counting errors.
In future work, we consider task of semi-supervised crop
counting by using a portion of unlabelled images. At the
same time, object number estimation can provide image pre-
processing results for subsequent object segmentation. It is
hoped that the results of this paper will inspire work of other
researchers.
