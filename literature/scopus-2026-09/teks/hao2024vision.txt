A Vision-Based Two-Stage Fruit Outer Diameter Detection Method
Yifei Hao, Shuaizheng Yan, Zhengxing Wu, Senior Member, IEEE, Bingwei He, Chenghua Li,
and Junzhi Yu, Fellow, IEEE
Abstract— Regarding
the
mobile
monitoring
system
in
agricultural
automation,
the
outer
diameter
monitoring
of
greenhouse
vegetables
has
signiﬁcant
implications
for
accurately regulating fruit growth and cultivation plans. In this
paper, a novel vision-based two-stage outer diameter detection
method is proposed, including the segmentation and outer
diameter calculation of greenhouse tomato images. To address
the issues of multiple tomatoes in a single image and the
similarity in color between tomatoes and the background, a
YOLOv8 network embedded with convolutional block attention
module is constructed for detection and segmentation in the
ﬁrst stage. Additionally, to solve the problem of inaccurate
outer diameter detection caused by different tomato poses and
pedicles, a deﬁned central axis method is proposed, which can
adaptively ﬁlter out the maximum outer diameter candidates
and calculate accurate values utilizing depth information in the
world coordinate system. The backbone network is ﬁne-tuned
on the pre-trained YOLOv8 model with 1000 tomato images.
Extensive measurement experiments, involving 215 tomatoes in
80 images, is conducted to validate the effectiveness of our
proposed approach. Compared to the ground truth, we obtain a
measurement error of 4.27 mm, the coefﬁcient of determination
value R2 is 0.9349.

Currently, vision-based outer diameter detection primarily
targets spherical fruits, such as apples, mangoes, oranges,
and tomatoes. The previous approaches can be categorized
into two main methods. The ﬁrst method involves geometric
ﬁtting of segmented fruit images, using elliptical major axis
to calculate fruit outer diameter [6]. The second method
uses bounding boxes to select fruits, outer diameter with
the length and width of the bounding box [7]. Several other
methods extract the area within the bounding box and assume
the fruit as a circle to measure outer diameter [8]. Both
methods can obtain the outer diameter of fruits and perform
well on speciﬁc datasets. However, regarding greenhouse
tomatoes, various challenges may arise during the detection
process, as showed in Fig. 1. Firstly, green tomatoes
may confuse neural networks in the learning process due
to their color similarity with the background. Secondly,
some tomato images captured from an overhead perspective
will inevitably include stem and leaves, which cannot be
effectively removed and may interfere with geometric ﬁtting
of the ﬁrst method. In contrast, the second method requires
the detection box to be perpendicular to the photo borders,
while tomatoes may grow in various orientations. In addition,
the inﬂuence of lighting is signiﬁcant, especially in dim or

II. RELATED WORK
Image processing technology is widely used in plant
phenotype research owing to its capabilities of feature
extraction and analysis [12], [13]. The early detection
methods for tomato outer diameter mostly used traditional
vision algorithm to detect tomato edges. For instance, Ji et
al. [14] proposed a method to segment the tomato using
the color difference, and then measured the vertical and
horizontal outer diameters of tomato employing the mask
image. However, traditional methods are only applicable to
harvested tomato images, and its accuracy in identiﬁcation
and measurement is relatively low.
With the developments of deep learning and neural
network,
there
have
been
great
changes
in
tomato
recognition. Bai et al. [15] employed a various of algorithms
to extract tomato area, perimeter, and closed circle radius,
improved tomato classiﬁcation. Zu et al. [16] utilized
Mask R-CNN for segmenting tomato images and obtaining
phenotype data. The experimental results showed that the
F1-Score of bounding box and mask region achieved 92.0%.
Zheng et al. [17] modiﬁed the YOLOv5n-seg model to
achieve better performance with a smaller model size.
While most studies focus on improving tomato recognition
effectiveness, little attention has been given to accurately
measuring the outer diameter of tomatoes in real greenhouse
environments due to factors such as posture variations and
stem/leaf obstructions. Therefore, this paper proposes a
method to precisely identify the maximum outer diameter
of greenhouse tomatoes during their recognition process.

A. Detection and Segmentation of Tomato
You Only Look Once (YOLO) is a single-stage object
detection model based on convolutional neural networks with
end-to-end training capabilities. In the ﬁeld of agricultural
robots, the model has been widely used in the detection of
agricultural products, such as horticultural ﬂowers [18], food
crops [19], fruits and vegetables [20].
While the original YOLOv8 network demonstrates high
accuracy in detecting tomatoes in most images, it encounters
difﬁculties in scenarios with poor lighting, tomatoes scattered
around the image edges, and fruits that blend closely in
color with the background. To address this issue, we have
chosen to integrate an attention mechanism. This addition
aims to enhance the network’s ability to prioritize and
effectively on identifying tomato fruits amidst complex
visual environments.
Considering
multiple
fruits
and
color
confusion
in
greenhouse tomato images, we adopt the Convolutional
Block Attention Module (CBAM) proposed by Woo et
al. [21]. This module combines channel attention module
and spatial attention module, aiming to process the feature
layer through two modules. The overall architecture can be
summarized by

The CBAM module is adopted for capability to resolve the
speciﬁc requirements of greenhouse tomato images. Firstly,
due to the similarity in color between green tomatoes and
background, the channel attention allows for amplifying
subtle differences between two color channels. Secondly, as
some tomato fruits are scattered around the image periphery,
the spatial attention emphasizes the importance of different
locations
where
tomatoes
may
appear.
Regarding
the
embedding approach of CBAM module, hotspot experiments
conducted on the original YOLOv8 network reveals that
attention is primarily concentrated in layers 14, 17, and
20. Therefore, we integrate the CBAM module after these
layers and construct the modiﬁed YOLOv8_CBAM network
structure, as shown in Fig. 2.
In addition, the algorithm usually fails to accurately detect
outer contours and diameters when more than 50% of
the fruit is covered by leaves. Therefore, we utilize the
proportion of a mask pattern to the segmented outer circle,
which can evaluate the level of occlusion in segmentation
results, to ﬁlter bad instances. A higher ratio indicates
a more comprehensive segmentation outcome for tomato
samples, while a lower score signiﬁes severe occlusion and
indicates it unsuitable for subsequent estimation of outer
diameters. Through extensive experiments, we determined
that a threshold score of 0.5 is effective for ﬁltering.
B. Selecton and Measurement of Tomato Outer Diameter
In the second stage of the method, a diameter estimation
algorithm based on geometric assumptions is presented.
To simulate the manual measurement, tomato is generally
regarded as a spheroid, thus, the stalk connects at the short
axis, and the long axis diameter of the tomato is considered
to be the maximum outer diameter. However, in practice, we
ﬁnd that the ﬂower pedicle seriously affects the selection of
the maximum outer diameter of the tomato, since the ﬂower
pedicle part is difﬁcult to be removed in the segmentation.
Thus, two different cases should be discussed. For tomatoes
that do not have the ﬂower pedicle, we take the line of
the furthest two pixels as the maximum estimated outer
diameter. Regarding those tomatoes with ﬂower pedicles,
we propose a novel diameter estimation method according
to manual measurement principle. After determining the
maximum outer diameter of tomatoes in the image, we
calculate the true size of the maximum outer diameter relying
on the transformation between the pixel coordinate system
and the world coordinate system. The algorithm ﬂow chart
is shown in Fig. 3.
Through comparison of pixels’ RGB values, we ﬁnd that
the color of ﬂower pedicle is darker than that of fruit. In
particular, this discrepancy of contrast is more intense from
the green channel. Hence, an algorithm is proposed to extract
edges from the green channel to determine the presence or
absence of a ﬂower pedicle. First, we segment tomatoes and
transform them into green channel. Thereafter, we employ
the Canny edge detection algorithm to extract the edges of
the entire tomato, and then ﬁll the pattern of tomato with
white pixels. If there are still large edge pixels in the image at

IV. EXPERIMENTS AND ANALYSES
To evaluate effectiveness of the proposed method, we
conducted experimental validations for both the improved
network model and the overall two-stage outer diameter
detection algorithm. The main emphasis was on the practical
effectiveness of the tomato detection and the accuracy of
the designed algorithm for measuring the outer diameter of
tomatoes.
A. Model Training and Validation
To train the network, we captured RGB images of tomato
fruits in the greenhouse using a handheld Femto Bolt
depth camera, totaling 1125 images. After selection, 1000
high-quality images were retained. To ensure experimental
robustness, no standardized shooting angles or conditions
were imposed during data collection. The 1000 tomato
images were classiﬁed into training, testing, and validation
sets using an 8:1:1 ratio. For the training process, we set the
period to 300 epochs with a batch size of 2. The learning
rate was 0.01, and the input size was resized to 640×640.
Regarding
the
evaluation
metrics
of
network,
the
Precision, Recall, the average accuracy of the IOU threshold
of 0.5, and the average accuracy of different IOU thresholds
(from 0.5 to 0.95, with step size of 0.05) were employed.

B. Performance Evaluation Index
We employed the R-Squared (R2), Mean Absolute Error
(MAE), Standard Deviation of Errors (SDE), and Mean
Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) as the evaluation metrics.
The R2 ranges from 0 to 1. The closer the value is to 1, the
more consistent the measured value is with true value.
C. Experimental Results Analysis
A total of 215 tomatoes were measured in 80 images,
with an average processing time of 34 ms per image. The
experimental results indicate that 23 tomatoes were not
recognized, and 12 tomatoes were obscured to measure their
outer diameter. The method achieved an effective detection
rate of 89.3%. The comparison between the measured values
and the ground truth of remaining 180 tomatoes is shown
in Fig. 8, and quantitative metrics are shown in Table II.
The results show that MAE is only 4.61 mm, which is
within an acceptable range. However, the lower effective
detection rate had an impact on the measurement accuracy of
the outer diameter, thus, we conducted further experimental
analysis. Concerning the tomatoes that were not detected, we
observed that out of the 23 tomatoes, 21 had fruit diameters
smaller than 15 mm, as shown in Figs. 9(a) and 9(b). These
immature tomatoes were not recognized and labeled during
manual annotation, hence they were not detected. Secondly,
regarding the tomatoes with errors in diameter measurement,
examination of their depth point cloud revealed severe

D. Discussion
The proposed vision-based two-stage method for tomato
diameter
detection
can
effectively
measures
sizes
of
tomatoes
in
greenhouse
environments.
The
introduced
attention mechanism notably enhances detection accuracy
through addressing the problem of similar colors between
tomatoes
and
backgrounds.
Furthermore,
the
proposed
deﬁned central axis method can rectify inaccurate tomato
diameter positioning caused by various growth postures.
Given
the
potential
for
color
interference
causing
segmentation errors in fruits, it is essential to incorporate
and construct a three-dimensional model of the fruits while
leveraging precise depth information [22], [23]. How to
deeply integrate point cloud processing and image processing
to achieve better segmentation effect is the key to the
problem. Besides, The classiﬁcation of tomato fruit could
be more detailed. Defective tomatoes, shielded tomatoes, and
young tomato fruits should be completed in the detection and
segmentation step, like the work in Ireri et al. [24] By using
a system to identify defects in tomatoes and grade them, we
could establish a dynamic growth model for tomato fruit to
monitor the growth state of tomato dynamically [25].
V. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
In this paper, a novel vision-based two-stage detection
method is proposed, which achieves good measurement of
outer diameter for greenhouse tomatoes. We achieve the
detection and segmentation of tomatoes by embedding the
CBAM module into YOLOv8 model, solving problems such
as dispersed tomato positions and similarity in color between
background and tomatoes. Furthermore, a deﬁned central
axis method is developed to address the issue of difﬁculty
in determining the position of tomato diameter due to
interference from fruit pedicles. Measurement experiments
demonstrate the effectiveness and detection accuracy of the
proposed method, which provides a promising autonomous
monitoring technology for agricultural robots.
In future, we will focus on improving the segmentation of
different fruits and depth estimation accuracy to monitor the
entire growth process of different fruits.
