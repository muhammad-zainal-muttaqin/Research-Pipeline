A B S T R A C T
Multiple Object Tracking (MOT) has emerged as a hot issue in the field of computer vision recently. MOT has
academic and commercial potential in urban public security, military, self-driving and other fields. Although
researchers have put forward different approaches to solve the problem, it is still challenging due to factors
such as appearance mutation and severe object occlusion. This study makes a comprehensive research on the
problem of detection-based video multi-object tracking. The main contributions of this review are as follows:
(1) The important aspects of MOT system are studied, including the basic principles, main framework, technical
difficulties, classification and evaluation; (2) This work review the classification and latest progress in object
detection due to its close relevance to the field of MOT; (3) This work introduce a taxonomy based on the MOT
problem on this basis, the original and improved versions of each classical tracking algorithm are discussed,
analyzed and compared; (4) This work summarize the results of 40 algorithms on popular datasets to provide
quantitative comparison and analysis of experimental results. Finally, the possible research directions for the
future are put forward, by analyzing the commonness of several methods with excellent performance.
1. Introduction
Multiple Object Tracking is one of the basic problems in the field of
computer vision, which has important academic research significance
and broad application prospects. The concept of MOT is first put
forward by WAX [1] in the 1960s. In 1997, thirteen universities and
research institutions, including Carnegie Mellon University, established
the Video Surveillance and Monitoring Project [2], funded by the
US Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency. The project mainly
uses image processing, compression and video tracking technology
to improve intelligent monitoring and surveillance of the city. MOT
combined with motion detection at the core of the whole project. As
an important part of computer vision technology, the study on MOT
has produced many excellent research results. At present, MOT can
be widely used in public security, city traffic, military field, autopilot,
sports broadcasting and other fields.

ries can reflect some abnormal states. Intelligent trajectory tracking is
suitable for places with high security requirements, such as railroad
stations, banks, shopping malls and hotels, etc. In these places, in
addition to tracking pedestrians, intelligent monitoring [3] is usually
accompanied by auxiliary functions such as analyzing the abnormal
behavior of pedestrians and calculating the flow of people. At present,

2. An overview of MOT
2.1. The basic principle of MOT
MOT [12] analyzes the appearance and number of objects in a video
image without prior knowledge. Thus, it can identify and track objects
that belong to one or more categories (i.e., pedestrians, animals, cars,
and inanimate objects). Unlike the object detection algorithm (i.e., the
output is a set of coordinates, height, and width of the identified
rectangular bounding box), the MOT algorithms also must associate the
object ID with each box to distinguish the objects within each class.
In single object tracking (SOT), the appearance of the object is a
prior, while in MOT, a detection step is needed to identify the object
entering or leaving the video surveillance. There are two main problems
in tracking multiple objects simultaneously, the first is the similarity
of object appearance, the second is the occlusion and interaction be-
tween objects. Therefore, simply using the SOT models to solve the
MOT problems will lead to object drift and numerous of ID switching
errors [13].
2.2. A basic framework of MOT algorithm
Generally, there are three issues to consider when designing MOT
algorithms: First, how to extract a set of accurate detection data from
large video frames to guide the tracking process.
Second, determining how to measure the similarity of objects in
a frame, including the modeling of motion, appearance, crossover,
repulsion and collision. Third, determining how to judge whether the
objects between frames are the same based on similarity, which is
mainly related to data association. Fig. 1 shows the basic framework of
the MOT algorithms. After studying the literature in the past decade,
the author finds that most of the MOT algorithms adopt some or all of
the following steps [14]:
(1) Object detection. The bounding box of the object in each input
frame is obtained from the object detection algorithm.
(2) Feature extraction/motion prediction. One or more feature ex-
traction algorithms are used to extract object appearance, motion and
interactive features.
(3) Similarity calculation. The apparent and motion models are used
to calculate the object similarities between frames.
(4) Data association. Assign the same ID to the same objects for
adjacent frames, and then associate the object tracks.
2.3. Object detection technology
The object detection is both the premise and foundation of MOT.
Therefore, finding an object detection method, which method that
can consider both accuracy and speed is one of the important task
of MOT. At present, the commonly used object detection methods
in computer vision can be divided into two categories, namely, the
traditional artificial feature extraction methods and the deep learning
methods. The earliest traditional detection method was proposed by
Dalal et al. [16] at the International Conference on Computer Vi-
sion and Pattern Recognition in 2005. It is based on multi-channel
directional gradient histogram features and supports the vector ma-
chine pedestrian detection algorithm [17]. The main idea is to use the
block gradient direction histogram to describe the local appearance
of the object. The edge features of pedestrians are classified by a
support vector classifier, and the optimal segmentation hyperplane
is constructed in the feature space. However, traditional detection
algorithms still need to be manually designed for feature extraction and
description. It requires researchers to have an in-depth understanding
of the detection object. Thus, the shortcomings of traditional artificial
features (e.g., complex structure, limited representation ability and
poor generalization ability) limit the development of image recognition
methods to a certain extent.
The different deep learning models and approaches employed in
object detection show strong representation and modeling abilities.
Correspondingly, they can learn the feature representation of objects
layer by layer and use supervised or unsupervised training to improve
hierarchical abstract description of objects. In 2012, the deep convolu-
tional neural network constructed by Krizhevsky et al. [18] improved
great success in large-scale image classification in the field of image
recognition. In the task of object detection, the method based on a
deep neural network can successfully overcome the shortcomings of

Recently, the mainstream object detection algorithms based on deep
learning can be roughly divided into two categories:
Two-Stage. These methods mainly use convolution neural net-
works to extract the feature description of candidate regional objects.
In the whole process network training, two steps are needed: (1)
The algorithms use a regional proposal network (RPN) to select the
region set on the basis of the deep convolution feature; (2) The al-
gorithms actively combine the selected regions and depth features
for object classification, boundary box matching and other tasks. In
2014, Girshick et al. [19] first proposed Region-based Convolutional
Neural Networks (RCNN) and published it in CVPR. In 2015, the
team proposed a faster and more efficient Faster-RCNN algorithm.
Compared with RCNN, Spatial Pyramid Pooling in Deep Convolutional
Networks (SPP-Net) [20], Fast-RCNN [21] and other methods, Faster-
RCNN [15] integrates region generation and object prediction into a
unified network for the first time. Fig. 2 shows a simple flowchart
of the Faster-RCNN algorithm. Faster-RCNN creatively uses the con-
volution network to generate its own suggestion box and shares the
convolution network with the object detection network. As the number
of suggestion boxes has been reduced from about 2000 to 300, the
quality of suggestion boxes has also been substantially improved. The
accuracy of this method and the recall rate of the object is high. The
speed is slow, thus it is difficult to meet the requirements of practical
engineering application. In the future, the improvement direction of
Two-stage will be the network with multi-scale feature fusion or the
lighter CNN network [22].
One-Stage. Such detection algorithm can directly generate the prob-
ability and position coordinate values of object categories without
going through the region recommendation stage. The classical methods
include YOLO [23], SSD [24] and CornerNet [25], etc. Taking YOLO
algorithm as an example, object detection can be directly transformed
into a regression problem solving. After the original picture is inputted
through the object detection network, the location and category of the
object are output directly. The operation speed of the method is quick,
but the scene detection accuracy and recall rate are low for small object
detection. The YOLOv3 [26] algorithm has been improved to resolve
this problem. The algorithm adopts the techniques of characteristic
pyramid network, batch normalization, multi-scale training and so on.
Multiple independent logical regression classifiers are used to replace
the Softmax layer to solve the problem of multi-label classification. The
improved algorithm can effectively improve the detection accuracy and
recall of small objects. While achieving a similar accuracy of Faster-
RCNN algorithm, the detection speed is increased by 8 to 10 times. In
2020, YOLOv4 [27] add many practical skills to the traditional YOLO
to improve the dynamic balance of detection speed and accuracy. The
performance of the object detection algorithm is mainly determined
by the accuracy and speed of the detection model. Among them, the
detection accuracy mainly considers the object coordinates and the
accuracy of classification. Generally, the detection accuracy of Two-
Stage algorithm is higher, whereas the detection speed of the One-Stage
algorithm is faster. However, with the deepening of the research, the
two kinds of algorithms have improved their own shortcomings, which
cannot only ensure the accuracy, but also improve faster speed. Fig. 3
summarizes the major milestones of the post-2012 object detection
algorithm.
2.4. The technical difficulties of MOT
The implementation of MOT technology is more challenging than
SOT. The multi-object trackers should distinguish not only the fore-
ground and background images of the object but also the feature
differences between different objects. It is worth noting that a good
MOT algorithm can maintain the correctness of each object label for
a long time. The following discussion take the detection-based MOT
methods as an example. The methods use the data association models

current moment detection response. At this stage, the authenticity of
the object feature model and the accuracy of the detection response
features directly affect the performance of the trackers. In view of the
various complex situations faced by MOT algorithms, researchers put
forward a variety of improved models and strategies. However, there
are still many challenges, which can be summarized as follows:
Uncertain number of objects. Because the objects in the monitor-
ing field of vision may appear, leave, or return at any time. Therefore,
the number of objects is uncertain [28]. MOT algorithms must be able
to deal with the emergence and disappearance of objects.
Video background is complex. In the actual tracking application
scene, we will face the changes of environmental factors (e.g., weather,
shadow, background and light brightness). Due to the influence of
these factors, the color, shape, texture, edge and other information of
the object cannot be accurately collected by the camera. These factors
affect object detection and tracking performance.
Long-term full occlusion of the objects. In the process of being
tracked, there will be an occlusion between the objects. Tracking
objects in subsequent frames may be transferred to the occlusion. The
system loses the model corresponding to the object and determines that
the object has disappeared, which leads to the failure to track [29,30].
Appearance similarity between objects. The object appearance
features include object texture, color, shape and other information [31,
32]. In video surveillance, how to extract discriminative object appear-
ance features has become the difficulty and focus of research.
The appearance deformation of the objects. The human body is a
non-rigid object, and the local swing or torsion of the body will occur in
the process of movement. The local plain change of the observed objects
may lead to the loss of object information. This reduces the accuracy
of tracking [33,34].
Real-time requirements. The video image contains much data,
thus it is necessary to choose a low-complexity algorithm to ensure the
real-time performance of object tracking. However, accuracy, is another
important performance of object tracking, which is often based on
complex operations and processing. The real-time performance and the
accuracy of the algorithm are a contradiction. A good MOT algorithm
must consider both real-time and accuracy.
Therefore, compared with the SOT, the MOT faces more challenges.

to improve the expected results, the above six problems need to be fully
solved.
2.5. The solution to the challenge of MOT
Many problems in tracking multiple objects can cause tracking to
fail. For example, illumination changes, long-term occlusion, changes
in object appearance, sudden changes in motion, similar appearance
between objects, etc. In order to solve these problems, it is critical
to design a reliable similarity measurement method. General MOT
methods include five components, namely, Appearance Model, Motion
Model, Interaction Model, Exclusion Model and Occlusion Model. Fig. 6
shows the problems of MOT and their solutions.
The appearance information of an object is the most commonly used
information in similarity measurement, which is mainly composed of
visual representation and statistical measurement. The commonly used
visual representation features include Color Histogram [35], Histogram
of Oriented Gradient [36], Local Binary Pattern [37], Scale-Invariant
Feature Transformation [38], Speeded Up Robust Features [39], etc. In
the early tracking methods, distance measurement and other methods
are often used to measure the similarity of the appearance characteris-
tics of different objects. For example, Ref. [40] uses the Babbitt distance
to measure the color histogram of the test results to get similarity.
Ref. [41] uses a covariance matrix. Recently, with the development
of depth neural network, various depth features have emerged. The
online feature learning model is also designed to correlate the object
and trajectory of detection. Additionally, there are other different net-
work structures, e.g. Siamese network [42], Triplet network [43] and
Quadruplet network [35], which are also applied to the discriminant
characteristics of learning objects.
The motion information of the object is mainly calculated by its
dynamic model. The existing dynamic motion models can be divided
into two categories: single-objective model [44–46] and multi-objective
model [47,48]. In general, the single-objective model employs lin-
ear [49,50] or nonlinear methods [51,52] to estimate the motion
patterns of objects between adjacent frames. In an early work, Kalman
filter [53] is used by some MOT algorithms. In recent years, numerous
deep learning models have been designed to model the motion of
objects. The more commonly used model is the Long Short Term Mem-
ory network (LSTM) [30], which is used to describe and predict the
complex motion patterns of the objects. Different from the single-object
motion model, the multi-object motion model predicts the motion of all
objects in each frame at the same time. The common method is to use
Optical Flow [54] and Three-dimensional Point Cloud [55] to estimate
different motion patterns. All methods apply the motion of point pixels
in each frame as a feature to match the object. Fig. 5 shows a detailed
classification of appearance information and motion information.
The interactive information between objects mainly considers the
situation in real life, i.e., two or more objects move together. The
typical concerns are the Social Force Models proposed in Ref. [56] and
Crowd Motion Pattern Models proposed in Ref. [57].
The mutual repulsion between tracks mainly constrains different
object trajectories to avoid collision or crossover. It is used mainly for
object tracking in three-dimensional space. In Ref. [58], two mutually
exclusive models are proposed to constrain the locus. They are mutual
exclusion at the detection level (i.e., different detection results in the
same frame should not be assigned to the same object) and mutual
exclusion between tracks (i.e., the two tracks cannot be infinitely close).

3. MOT algorithm
3.1. Classification of the MOT algorithm
According to the standard processing mode, the MOT algorithm can
be divided into online and offline tracking. The difference between
them lies in whether the observation of the future frame is used when
dealing with the current frame.
The construction and solution of graph-based model is the main
framework in offline tracking. However, in online tracking, the basic
algorithm flow is still a framework based on state prediction update.
How to effectively design and learn the matching affinity between
detection, or the consistency of trajectory and observation is a very
critical step. The offline tracking methods can use future frame infor-
mation to determine the object identity in the current frame. Note that
global information is generally used to optimize the data association

The Simple Online and Realtime Tracking (SORT) algorithm pro-

determine whether the match is successful or not.
3.2. Online MOT algorithm
3.2.1. SORT
At that time, most of the solutions to improve the accuracy of
MOT were obtained at the cost of real-time. This directly leads to the
slowest tracking speed of the most accurate tracker, which is difficult
to meet the needs of practical applications. The SORT algorithm has
the advantages of low complexity, simple framework and fast running
speed, which lays the groundwork for many excellent algorithms. The
most important thing is that it can better associate tracking objects and
realize an online real-time application. In the year when the algorithm
was proposed, it quickly became the most concerned algorithm in
the industry because of its excellent performance. However, the SORT
algorithm ignores some questions:
(1) Motion estimation and data association only use the position and
size of the frame. While, the method ignores the appearance of features
outside the detection components.
(2) The SORT algorithm does not consider the problems related to
long-term and short-term occlusion. As shown in Fig. 7, the ID switches
frequently.
(3) When all types of edge conditions and detection errors occur,
the algorithm ignores them directly. Finally, it is determined that the
object is lost or that a new object appears.
3.2.2. DeepSORT
In 2017, the DeepSORT algorithm proposed by Alex Bewley
et al. [64] is an improve version based on the SORT algorithm.
There are three main improvements. First, the DeepSORT algorithm
introduces a deep learning model for offline training on pedestrian Re-
identification datasets [67]. Second, DeepSORT combines appearance
features (i.e., 128D vectors extracted by a small CNN) with motion
features (i.e., the result of Kalman filter prediction) to calculate the
cost matrix. Then, the Hungarian algorithm when matching. Third,
the DeepSORT uses cascading matching to give priority to objects
that occur more frequently. It sets the initialization, rebirth and disap-
pearance of tracking. Compared with the SORT algorithm, DeepSORT
algorithm can effectively solve the situation that the object is occluded
for a long time. The ID-switch frequency of the algorithm is reduced
to 45% in the private detector of the MOT16 datasets. The running
speed of the algorithm is close to the real-time requirement (i.e., about
20FPS). However, the core idea of the DeepSORT algorithm is still a
traditional single-hypothesis tracking method, which does not eliminate
the limitations of SORT algorithm. There are still some problems: the
Re-ID network and the detection network of the DeepSORT algorithm
are two separate parts. It adds a burden on the calculation. Addition-
ally, detection and tracking are two complementary problems. The
problem of missing detection can be made up for by good tracking,
and good detection can solve the problem of track drift. The problem
of using tracking to make up for missed detection is not considered
in DeepSORT. The linear velocity model of DeepSORT is not suitable
for the camera motion. Fig. 8 indicates a flowchart of the DeepSORT
algorithm.
3.2.3. MOTDT
In 2018, Chen et al. [68] proposed an MOTDT algorithm. Com-
pared with the DeepSORT, there are two main improvements. First,
the MOTDT algorithm introduces a trajectory scoring mechanism to
solve the problem of redundant in the candidate set. It stipulates
that the longer the trajectory, the higher the credibility. Based on
this score, the output of detection and tracking is combined as the
input of non-maximum suppression (NMS). The test results with high
confidence can prevent the tracking drift caused by long-term tracking.
Simultaneously, the trajectory prediction can deal with the noise caused
by occlusion. Secondly, from the viewpoint of data association, the
author introduces a hierarchical data association strategy to solve
the problem of category occlusion in crowded scenes. It not only
combines appearance representation and spatial information with the
Re-ID function but also hierarchically associates existing orbits with
different candidates. The appearance representation draws lessons from
the DeepSORT algorithm, which uses the depth neural network to train
the pedestrian re-recognition data set. The MOTDT algorithm gives
priority to data association between the detected candidate objects and
the existing trajectories in the aspect of hierarchical association. The
IOU associates the remaining candidates with the unassociated tracks.
The experimental results on the MOT16 dataset show that the track-
ing speed can meet the needs of practical applications. The problem of
ID switching caused by long-time occlusion is effectively reduced by
using the Re-ID feature as the reference of the appearance model. IDF1
has increased to 50.9%, which is a significant improvement over other
algorithms. Fig. 9 is a test example of the MOTDT algorithm on the
MOT16 dataset.

In 2019, the JDE algorithm is proposed by Wang et al. [69]. The
algorithm combines a detection network with associated network for
the first time. Before this, the detection-based MOT system usually must
go through two steps: (1) in the detection phase, the object position in
each video frame is detected; (2) in the correlation phase, the detected
object is associated with the previous trajectory. This means that the
previous MOT models have two intensive computing parts: detection

this way, the model can detect the object at the same time and output
the corresponding object appearance embedded. Second, a simple and
fast association method is proposed, which can be combined with the
joint model. Compared with the previous two stages, the JDE uses a
network that simultaneously outputs the position of the detection box
in the image and the embedding of the objects in it. At the same time,
its tracking accuracy is comparable to that of the SOTA model with
independent detection and embedded learning. The JDE reduces the
amount of calculation on the premise of keeping the accuracy similar.
However, it only outputs the check box and embedded information
at the same time. The subsequent correlation still matches the object
through the Hungarian algorithm and Kalman filter. In essence, it is
still a two-stage detection and matching. Fig. 10 shows a structural
comparison between SDE and JDE.
3.2.5. FairMOT
In 2020, Huake and the team of the Microsoft Asian Research
Institute [70] put forward the FairMOT algorithm: a joint detection and
tracking framework based on CenterTrack. The researchers discussed
the integration of the detection framework and Re-ID feature task in
depth. The main contributions are as follows:
(1) The current single-step methods, such as the Track-RCNN [71]
and JDE [69] trackers, are based on anchor-based object detection,
i.e., adding Re-ID feature vector output to the detection side of MaskR-
CNN and YOLOv3 directly. Through the experiments, the researchers
find that there are two reasons why the anchor is not suitable for
learning the Re-ID function. First, multiple anchor points corresponding
to different image blocks may be responsible for estimating the ID of
the same objects, which leads to serious ambiguity. In addition, the size
of the feature graph needs to be reduced by 1
8 to balance accuracy and
speed. This is acceptable for detection tasks, but a bit rough for Re-
ID because the object center may not be consistent with the features
extracted at rough anchor points. Therefore, FairMOT algorithm uses
the method based on Anchor-Free. This effectively solves the problem
of anchoring center deviation.
(2) The FairMOT algorithm uses multi-feature fusion Re-ID informa-
tion. This includes the semantic information of the high-level network
and the semantic information of the moderate low-level network, such
as color and texture, etc.

3.3. Offline MOT algorithm
3.3.1. POI
In 2016, Fengwei Yu et al. [72] proposed the POI tracking algo-
rithm. The detection part of the algorithm uses FasterR-CNN method
and combines multi-scale features to replace the previous methods. The
appearance feature part uses a network similar to GoogLeNet to extract
128D features. The algorithm combines Softmax and Triplet loss to
train the network to ensure that the appearance features have good
discrimination.
3.3.2. IoU
In 2017, Bochinski et al. [73] proposed an IoU data association
strategy for high-frame rate video object tracking. When the video
frame rate and detection accuracy are high, the problem of MOT will
become relatively simple. At present, in order to solve this problem,
researchers mainly use the method of multi-threshold object detection
or have calculated the coincidence rate of the front and back frames.
Because the same object IoU between frames is always greater than
0, when the video frame rate is high. The IoU strategy calculates the
Euclidean distance between the same object in adjacent frames. The
method combined with the Hungarian algorithm to determine that the
shortest distance is the same object.
This approach also suffers from two disadvantages:
(1) The method relies too much on the detector and does not add
the relevant information between frames.
(2) For object tracking that moves too fast, IoU strategy will fail.
Aiming at the shortcoming 2, this team proposed the V-IoU tracking
algorithm [74] in 2018. V-IoU tracker adds a visual single object tracker
to the tracking process of the IoU tracking algorithm. V-IoU improves
the problem of matching errors caused by objects moving too fast.
3.3.3. LSTM
The LSTM algorithm [75] proposed by Shangtang, Beihang and the
University of Sydney in 2019 adopts a unified MOT model framework.
Use both long-term and short-term cues to deal with complex MOT sce-
narios. The authors combine the overlapping use of multiple modules.
The detector is designed to detect the object, and the Re-ID module is
used to identify the object. Short-term clues can be captured by single
object tracking method to improve the accuracy of detection. Long-term
clues were obtained by using Re-ID network. In order to bridge the mis-
takes caused by other components for object tracking. The framework
also introduces a single object tracker SiamRPN [76,77], which enables
the network to have partial discovery and recognition functions. For
occlusion problems, including area occlusion makes the SOT tracker
drift. As a result, most short-term clues become unreliable, but long-
term clues remain stable. Through the experiment, it is found that
the previous work did not make full use of these two clues. The SOT
tracker for short-term cues cannot distinguish between similar objects.
The network used for long-term clues cannot locate the exact location
of the objects. In other words, it is difficult to effectively combine
the two clues into one network. Therefore, the authors proposes a
unified framework for MOT. It cannot only generate long-and short-
term cues, but also adaptively select them for data association. In
addition to long-term and short-term clues, the author also uses local
interactive information to solve the problem of ID switch. They find
that the switcher is crucial for the correct match. The author makes the
switcher-aware classifier (i.e., implemented by enhancing the decision
tree) to encode the potential switcher information, thus improving
the robustness of the tracker. Fig. 11 summarizes the classification
diagrams for online and offline tracking.

4.3. Settings for benchmark results
In this section, This work present public results of forty algorithms
on MOTChallenge datasets. In order to make a direct comparison be-
tween different algorithms and facilitate the follow-up works. Limited
to space, This work will focus only on the MOTChallenge datasets. Since
compared with other datasets, it is newer, more comprehensive, and
more convenient to obtain data. Please note that, it may not be fair to
make a direct comparison based on the results obtained on the dataset.
There are two main points to consider: First, as described in Sec-
tion 2.3, the performance of the detector is one of the important factors
that determine the final tracking result. Therefore, this work divide
the benchmark results on the MOTChallenge datasets into algorithms
using public detection and private detection. Second, as mentioned in
Section 3.1, the offline method has an advantage in the process of
MOT. Thus, this work further divide the results into offline and online
methods.
Based on the above factors, in order to make a fair comparison. this
work do not put some papers only to discuss or train incomplete verifi-
cation data results in the table. For example, some methods synthesize
the observations of multiple views, while others take advantage of the
information of a single view, which are not comparable.
Tables 3 and 4 show the results of the algorithm on MOT16 and
MOT20, respectively. Each table is grouped by private detection and
public detection, respectively. For each group, the results are sorted by
year, with online methods first and offline methods last.
4.4. Analysis and comparison of benchmark results
As expected, the best-performing algorithms on MOT16 and MOT20
datasets use private detection, confirming the fact that the quality of
detection determines the overall performance of the tracker. Although
this work mentioned the fact that the offline methods use global time
information for estimation. In fact, we find that the performance of the
online algorithms are slightly better than that of the offline algorithms.
However, this may be the result of more attention to developing
online methods, which is a trend in the MOT research community.
A common problem with online methods is the large number of IDS
and Frag. It is easy to understand that when occlusion or detection is
missing, the online algorithms cannot obtain information about future
frames. Although some online algorithms can re-identify lost objects
after occlusion, they are usually unable to track when they are invisible,
which leads to IDS and Frag. On the other hand, the offline methods can
reconstruct the fragmented trajectory by inferring the location of the
objects given past and future information. The improvement in perfor-
mance over the years from 2016 to 2022 is not surprising. We suspect
that factors such as better models and advances in object detection
based on deep learning have contributed to the development of MOT.
It should be noted that over time, the research community has focused
on specific datasets. Some methods may be the result of overadaptation
to the datasets and cannot be well generalized. Tables 5–8 are detailed
comparative data.

As for the future research direction, we think that it can be considered
from the following aspects, and some possible solutions are given.
MOT based on deep learning. In recent years, the SOT algorithms
based on deep learning have made great progress. At first, when we
talk about deep learning, we usually say that the handcrafted features
are not good, and deep learning is an end-to-end network. In fact, the
network structure of deep learning is also designed by hand. At present,
after the rise of the network structure search Neural Architecture
Search (NAS) [129,130], We think there may be more improvements
in this area. We can integrate some routine operations, including some
conventional modules, to constantly optimize the network structure
instead of handcrafted design. Relatively speaking, the application of
deep learning in the field of MOT is more limited to the learning
of matching metrics. The main reason is that in the field of image

14
Y. Dai et al.
recognition, such as image classification and pedestrian re-recognition,
the progress of deep learning can be directly applied to MOT. However,
considering the interaction between objects and the complexity of
tracking scene, the application of deep learning algorithm in MOT
problem is far from being fully studied. With the in-depth research
and development of the field theory of deep learning, in recent years,
deep learning based on generative network model and reinforcement
learning [131] is the research trend in the field of MOT.
Multiple 3D object tracking. At present, most of the MOT methods
are focused on 2D, i.e., in the image plane, even in the case of multiple
cameras. 3D tracking [132–134] is not only limited to the field of
3D scene reconstruction, but also some excellent work has emerged
recently, such as detection and segmentation based on 3D vision. The
2D data collection does not fully reflect the whole problem, such as
pedestrian occlusion, location and size estimation, etc. Is it possible to
be inspired by 3D vision in the case of limited data learning? If the
3D information of the whole behavior of pedestrians can be captured,
we can remove these effects in the open environment and reconstruct
them. For example, a shooting range can be constructed, which is very
important. No matter what system we do, we have to test it. But if
we only test on limited data or one-sided data, we may be limited in
real application. Therefore, if we can embed 3D into the current MOT
dominated by 2D images, it may have another dimension to promote
the development of our whole MOT in the next 3 to 5 years. So why do
3D? Another thing is the issue of data privacy that may be being talked
about all over the world right now. The privacy issue of data collection
is becoming more and more important, and this privacy issue does not
exist at all if you are using a virtual shooting range.
Joint detection and tracking framework. At present, most MOT
algorithms are still carried out separately. The above two steps are
carried out twice. The method is a very heavy calculation for the whole
pedestrian tracking system. The difficulty of progress lies in how to
ensure the operation speed and improve the accuracy at the same
time. Whether Section 3.2.4 mentions Oneshot MOT using multitasking
learning, or detecting SDE MOT that runs separately from embedded
modules. These two methods have obvious advantages and disadvan-
tages. In joint-tracking–detection framework, the detection step and the
tracking step are parallel and multi-task, end-to-end learning, which
greatly simplifies the data association step. Therefore, these methods
can better improve the speed of the algorithms.
Multi-Modality correlation. The third trend worth paying atten-
tion to is the combination of different modalities. Biological perception
has never depended on a single modality. One of the problems that
need to be solved in multi-modality is the alignment and causality
between different modalities. If there is information obtained from
multiple modalities at the same time, spatio-temporal alignment is a
very important challenge. Another problem related to spatio-temporal
alignment is causality. Although we want to get causality, most of
the time we only get correlation. In most cases, we prefer to explore
correlation rather than causality, especially in data-driven models,
where it is difficult to try to discover causality without the mechanism.
However, the combination and correlation of different modalities is an
important trend in the future research.
