Abstract—Accurate apple size measurement is crucial both 
for yield estimation and quality assessment in modern precision 
agriculture. The aim of this paper is to introduce a new RGB-
only approach for on-tree apple size estimation without the use 
of a dedicated depth sensor. We propose a framework that 
integrates the Occlusion-aware Mask R-CNN for apple 
detection, amodal segmentation, and diameter estimation from 
mask geometry with the Depth Anything V2 model for 
monocular metric depth estimation. By combining amodal 
masks with calibrated depth prediction, the proposed system 
converts pixel-based diameter measurements into real-world 
millimeters using a pinhole projection model. Experiments on 
the PApple RGB-D-Size dataset show that the calibrated 
monocular approach achieves a mean absolute error of about 
7.72 mm while maintaining strong segmentation accuracy. The 
results confirm that monocular depth, if properly calibrated, is 
an excellent substitute for sensor-based depth in practice and a 
low-cost solution to automated yield monitoring and orchard 
management.

I. 
INTRODUCTION 
Accurate fruit-size measurement is important in modern 
agriculture for yield estimation, grading, and harvest planning. 
Manual tools such as Vernier calipers [1] are reliable but slow, 
labor-intensive, and prone to operator error. This has 
motivated the development of automated RGB-D and 
learning-based approaches for fruit sizing, including apples 
[2], strawberries [3], and broccoli heads [4]. RGB-D imaging 
enables precise measurement even under challenging 
conditions, such as overlapping fruits or occlusions. Prior 
studies show that RGB-D pipelines can detect and measure 
apples [1] and that multitask networks using RGB-D data can 
jointly perform detection and size estimation. However, most 
existing systems depend on annotated fruit sizes and accurate 
ground-truth depth [2], which are difficult to obtain at orchard 
scale [5] and therefore limit practical deployment. 
Occlusion-aware models have further advanced the field. 
ORCNN [6] extends Mask R-CNN [7] with amodal and 
occlusion masks to infer complete object shapes, improving 
sizing performance under heavy occlusion, as demonstrated 
for broccoli heads [5]. Depth-based measurement has also 
been applied outside agriculture, including aquaculture [8]. 
and monocular fish sizing [9], highlighting the feasibility of 
low-cost, non-invasive measurement. 
This research extends these developments by presenting 
an RGB-based method for the estimation of apple sizes, 
including monocular depth prediction without the use of 
dedicated depth sensors. Accordingly, the proposed method 
fuses RGB imaging, camera parameters (focal length and 
image scale), and amodal segmentation masks to perform 
apple size estimation in real-world metric units. Unlike 
existing methods, which rely on sensor-based or manually 
annotated depth information, the approach introduced here 
utilizes visual cues and learned depth priors, offering a cost-
effective, scalable, and practical solution for automated apple 
size estimation. 
II. 
LITERATURE REVIEW 
Over the years, various methods have been developed for 
estimating fruit sizes from images, differing in complexity, 
hardware requirements, and environmental robustness. This 
work focuses on three core components of fruit-size 
estimation: 
object 
detection 
and 
segmentation, 
size 
estimation, and depth estimation, which together enable 
accurate RGB-based fruit sizing in orchard environments. 
A. Object Detection 
Object detection and segmentation are fundamental for 
locating fruits and producing the masks needed for geometric 
measurement. RetinaNet [9] addresses class imbalance in 
dense detection with Focal Loss for fast and accurate one-
stage performance. MobileNet [10] provides a lightweight 
backbone using depthwise-separable convolutions, while 
MobileNetV2 [11] provides further efficiency improvements 
using inverted residuals and linear bottlenecks, both suitable 
for mobile or edge deployment. Faster R-CNN [12] uses a 
two-stage region-proposal pipeline (RPN + Fast R-CNN [13]) 
for improved detection accuracy at the cost of slower 
inference. Mask R-CNN [7] extends this two-stage approach 
by adding a segmentation head and RoIAlign to produce 
accurate instance masks. ORCNN [14] provides further 
capability through the joint prediction of visible and amodal 
masks that enable the reconstruction of occluded object parts 
for a more complete instance understanding. 
B. Size Estimation 
Reference objects and geometric patterns can recover real-
world scale when placed in the same image plane as the target. 
An 8×8 cm ArUco marker [15] has demonstrated sub-
centimeter accuracy under ideal conditions. A bank-card 
homography method [16] can estimate 3D dimensions and 
volume from a single image with relative error below ~3.5%

Regression-head detectors embed diameter estimation into the

occluded regions to reconstruct full object shapes under 
occlusion, improving geometric consistency and downstream 
diameter/volume estimates. 
C. Depth Estimation Methods 
Depth estimation is necessary to convert the pixel 
measurements to real-world units when using only RGB 
images in orchards with varying light and occlusion 
conditions. Deep learning has allowed the field of monocular 
depth estimation (MDE) to rapidly advance, enabling dense 
depth maps from a single RGB image without additional 
hardware. MiDaS [20] learns robust relative depth from large, 
diverse datasets using a scale and shift-invariant loss, which 
yields strong generalization but does not produce metric-scale 
depth out of the box. ZoeDepth [21] extends this idea by 
adding metric bins and domain-specific heads in a two-stage 
pipeline to obtain scale-aware predictions while retaining 
transferability. Depth Anything V2 [22] further improves 
metric consistency and visual detail by training on very large 
synthetic and pseudo-labeled real datasets, offering a good 
balance between accuracy and generalization. Depth Pro [23] 
produces very sharp, high-resolution metric depth using 
transformer-based, multi-scale designs, but its heavy 
computational cost limits practicality for real-time field 
deployment. 
Based on these insights, our research will leverage the 
ORCNN model for object detection and segmentation, mask 
geometry to estimate the apple diameter from the amodal 
mask, while also including a regression head to predict the 
diameter of the apple from the amodal mask and combined 
with Depth Anything V2 for monocular depth estimation. 
Given the model we selected, we aim to deliver a feasible and 
accurate approach to measuring apple size in actual orchard 
contexts, using natural RGB imagery and avoiding the 
necessity of ground-truth depth data or additional hardware. 
We anticipate delivering robust outcomes under a range of 
lighting and occlusion conditions while making our method 
applicable for scaled purposes in agriculture. 
III. 
METHODOLOGY 
 The methodology estimates apple size from RGB images 
through a structured pipeline. First, images are preprocessed 
for consistent input, and Depth Anything V2 predicts a metric 
depth map that is later calibrated to match real-world scale. 
The ORCNN model then detects apples and generates both 
modal and amodal masks, from which the apple diameter in 
pixels is derived. Using the calibrated depth and camera 
parameters, this pixel diameter is converted to millimeters, 
enabling accurate apple-size estimation from RGB images 
alone without ground-truth depth data. 
A. Dataset and Preprocessing 
The PApple RGB-D-Size v1 dataset was used, containing 
4,017 RGB images with depth maps, modal and amodal 
masks, and ground-truth diameters measured using a Vernier 
calliper. All images were captured with a Canon EOS 60D (18 
MP APS-C sensor) and an EFS 24 mm f/2.8 STM lens under 
diverse orchard conditions, including varying lighting, 
occlusions, and viewpoints. 
Camera intrinsics were estimated using a standard 
OpenCV checkerboard calibration, assuming zero skew and 
square pixels. After resizing all images to 1,300×1,300, the 
effective focal length was approximately 𝑓𝑝𝑥≈2180pixels. 
Lens distortion was corrected during preprocessing; 
remaining radial distortion was below 0.5% and therefore 
omitted in the geometric projection model. 
Amodal masks supervised full-shape prediction for 
diameter regression, while modal masks were used at test time 
to extract average depth. Depth maps were aligned to the same 
1,300 × 1,300 resolution for one-to-one pixel correspondence. 
Very small or empty instances were removed, and the dataset 
was split 60:20:20 for training, validation, and testing with 
balanced size and occlusion distributions. 
B. Experimental Setup 
The model was implemented with the PyTorch 
environment in conjunction with the Detectron2 library on the 
Kaggle. The architecture was adapted from the original Mask 
R-CNN to have the amodal segmentation head and an 
additional regression branch to estimate diameter. Rather than 
ground-truth depth maps, the model incorporated monocular 
depth estimation during testing with the Depth Anything V2 
model, enabling size inference in the real world using only 
RGB images. All training and testing were conducted in 
Kaggle with an NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU (16 GB VRAM) and 
2 CPU cores. A Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) optimizer 
was used for the model. The dataset was split into 60:20:20 to 
maintain balanced distributions of apple size and oclussion 
levels across training, validation, and testing.

estimation model that predicts metric depth from a single 
RGB image. Unlike RGB-D sensors or photogrammetry, 
Depth Anything V2 does not require specialized hardware or 
multiple camera views. The model leverages learned visual

E. ORCNN 
 
 The second main component, ORCNN, was used to 
perform apple detection, amodal segmentation, and diameter 
regression. The ORCNN extends the standard Mask R-CNN 
architecture with a branch that performs amodal segmentation 
predicting the full apple shape even though part of the apple 
may be occluded by leaves or by other apples. This allows the 
model to reason about occluded areas and infer the full shape 
of the apple.

Region Proposal Network (RPN) generates bounding boxes 
for potential apple regions, which are then refined using 
RoIAlign to preserve spatial alignment. The model outputs 
bounding boxes, visible (modal) masks, and complete 
(amodal) masks. The modal mask is used to estimate the

F. Testing phase and Evaluation Metrics 
In the testing stage, the trained ORCNN model was 
applied to the unseen test subset. For each detected apple, the 
modal mask was used to extract the corresponding region 
from the predicted depth map. The mean depth value within 
this region, denoted as 𝑍mm, represents the camera-to-object 
distance. 
The diameter of each apple in pixel units 𝑑px was obtained 
using mask geometry, which calculates the diameter from the 
spatial extent of the predicted amodal mask. Using the 
calibrated depth and the estimated pixel diameter, the real-

Each test sample was assessed independently so all 
inferences were consistent across images and to prevent bias 
in any of the comparisons. The predicted diameters were 
compared directly with their corresponding ground-truth 
values. 
G. Evaluation Metrics 
To measure the performance of the proposed framework, 
two sets of evaluation metrics were considered: segmentation-
based and regression-based. For the measure of segmentation 
quality, Precision, Recall, and F1-score were used to measure 
how well the model is able to detect and segment apples. In 
unison, these metrics assess the model’s ability to confidently 
detect all instances of the apples (recall) and to minimize false 
detections (precision).  
To evaluate the estimated diameters, the study used Mean 
Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), 
Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE), and the Coefficient 
of Determination (R²).  MAE captures the average difference 
between the estimated diameters and actual diameters, RMSE 
gives more emphasis to larger differences indicating stability, 
MAPE indicates the difference as percent relative to actual 
diameters, and R² indicates the suitability of the model in 
predicting diameter values. All these measures were selected 
such that collectively they provide a holistic view of accuracy, 
proportion, and stability giving an objective view of the 
geometric and predictive quality of the model. 
IV. 
RESULT AND DISCUSSION 
The experiments were analyzed in two phases: depth 
estimation and apple size estimation, each evaluated both 
quantitatively and qualitatively to assess the robustness of the 
proposed RGB-based approach. 
A. Depth Estimation Performance 
The first phase compared ZoeDepth-NK, a fine-tuned 
model on the PApple RGB-D-Size dataset, and Depth 
Anything V2, pretrained on large synthetic outdoor data. As 
summarized in Table I, Depth Anything V2 consistently 
achieved lower errors across all metrics, confirming that large-
scale pretraining yields better generalization than domain-
specific 
fine-tuning. 
ZoeDepth-NK 
showed 
degraded 
performance due to overfitting and limited texture diversity. 
A visual comparison in Fig 3 illustrates that Depth 
Anything V2 produces smoother and more coherent depth 
structures, particularly around object edges and in shaded 
regions. Accurate and continuous depth predictions are critical 
since the mean depth within the apple region is later used to 
convert pixel diameters into real-world measurements. 
B. Size Estimation Using Predicted Depth 
Building on the depth results, the pipeline was evaluated 
for estimating apple diameters in millimeters. ORCNN was 
trained on RGB images and masks, with multiple depth 
sources tested. As shown in Table II, Depth Anything V2 
achieves a strong performance once calibrated. 
Without calibration, the model produced correct relative 
but incorrect absolute depths, resulting in overestimated 
diameters. A global calibration reduced MAE by ~18%, while 
per-image calibration achieved the best performance 
(MAE≈7.72 mm, R²≈0.45). The relatively low R² indicates 
that although the average error is small, the prediction 
variance is larger for certain scenes. This reflects instability 
due to depth noise in occluded or distant fruit regions. 
Although effective, this step still depends on ground-truth 
diameters to recover metric scale, meaning any reference error 
propagates directly into the final size estimate. To assess 
calibration stability, the predicted metric scale was also 
validated against a small set of physically measured apples 
using Vernier calipers. The average deviation between the 
calibrated monocular depth and physical measurements 
remained within 2-3 mm, confirming that the scale regression 
is consistent when the camera pose is stable. However, the 
approach remains sensitive to inaccuracies in the reference 
diameters used during calibration. Since diameter estimation

can replace sensor-based depth for estimating apple sizes. The 
proposed method achieves high segmentation accuracy and a 
mean absolute error of about 7.72 mm using only RGB 
imagery, demonstrating a practical, low-cost, and scalable 
approach for precision-agriculture applications. 
V. 
CONCLUSION 
The proposed system integrates ORCNN for amodal 
segmentation and diameter regression with Depth Anything 
V2 for monocular metric depth. This combination allows 
pixel-level diameter measurements to be converted into 
millimetres through calibrated projection, enabling accurate 
apple-size estimation using RGB images alone. The proposed 
approach achieved a mean absolute error of about 7.72 mm 
after calibration to correct the depth scale.  
These findings demonstrate that monocular depth, once 
calibrated, is a viable alternative to sensor-based depth in real 
orchard environments. Most remaining errors arise from 
variations in camera distance and lighting rather than random 
noise. Per-image calibration helps reduce such discrepancies 
and improves prediction stability across different scenes.
