A B S T R A C T
Picking robots have become an important development direction of smart agriculture, and the accurate 
detection, counting and lightweight deployment of fruits are the technical basis for realizing robot picking. 
However, due to complex weather conditions and the possible mutual occlusion between branches and citrus, 
it is challenging to accurately detect and count citrus in orchards. This study proposes a lightweight small 
target detection model for detecting and counting citrus, and deploys it on the citrus detection platform. 
The model first introduces FasterNet Block into the cross-stage partial feature fusion module of the backbone 
network to reduce the number of parameters and calculations while improving the detection accuracy of the 
network. Secondly, a multi-scale attention mechanism is added to the backbone network to enhance the feature 
extraction ability of the network. Finally, a bounding box loss function based on a dynamic non-monotonic 
focusing mechanism is used to increase the model convergence speed and further improve the model accuracy. 
Experimental results show that the model has an accuracy of 92.7%, an average precision of 91.7%, and a 
model size of only 5.37 megabytes. The lightweight model is applied to the citrus detection platform. Based 
on this application, a citrus counting method is proposed, which obtains a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.92, 
a root mean square error (RMSE) of 1.28, a determination coefficient (𝑅2) of 0.98, and a frame rate of 80.6 
per second, which meets the requirements of real-time citrus detection and counting. This provides technical 
support for the subsequent deployment and counting research of picking robots.
1. Introduction
Citrus is the world’s largest fruit and one of the most important 
cash crops, and is widely grown around the world (Raghavan and 
Gurunathan, 2021). As an important agricultural product, it has been 
produced in more than 140 countries, with an estimated global an-
nual production of more than 48.8 million tons (USDA, 2020). As 
the demand for citrus increases, citrus yield estimation is becoming 
increasingly important. Currently, traditional methods for estimating 
citrus yield usually rely on manual counting, which requires a lot of 
time and labor. With the increase in orchard management costs and 
the decrease in skilled labor, the use of traditional labor-intensive 
methods to harvest and estimate citrus yield has become increasingly 
unsustainable (Gongal et al., 2015). Therefore, an automated method 
is needed to accurately detect and count citrus.
The development of citrus picking robots has important research 
prospects. Citrus detection is the basis for determining the spatial 
position of the object picking point in the citrus picking robot vision

system, which directly affects the accuracy and efficiency of the robot’s 
picking operation (Zhuang et al., 2018). Especially in the complex 
environment of citrus orchards, the picking robots are disturbed by 
factors such as lighting changes, branch occlusion, and fruit overlap, 
which poses a huge challenge to the picking robots.
Traditional image-based fruit detection methods mainly extract in-
herent features, such as color features (Chen et al., 2020), shape 
features (Bargoti and Underwood, 2017), texture features (Sengupta 
and Lee, 2014), edge features (Xiang et al., 2012), etc. Traditional 
machine learning algorithms detect fruits by combining artificially 
designed features. The feature information extraction capabilities of 
these algorithms are limited, and most algorithms are only applicable 
to single background experimental conditions. When there are com-
plex scenes, such as scenes with changing lighting, scenes blocked 
by branches, and scenes with overlapping fruits, the performance of 
fruit detection will deteriorate. In such scenes, the detection of cit-
rus fruits may result in missed detections and false detections, so its 
generalization ability needs to be improved.

In recent years, deep learning has become a cutting-edge technology 
due to its ability to extract features at different semantic levels and its 
strong generalization ability to changes in work scenarios (He et al., 
2016). With the rapid development of deep learning-based object de-
tection algorithms, object detection models are mainly divided into two 
different types according to the number of detection stages. The first 
category is the two-stage object detection method. Representative algo-
rithms of this type are R-CNN (Dong et al., 2023), Fast R-CNN (Wang 
et al., 2017), and Faster R-CNN (Ren et al., 2016). The two-stage object 
detection method generates region proposal boxes from the input image 
in the first stage, and obtains category and location predictions through 
CNN in the second stage (Mai et al., 2020). Gao et al. (2020) used 
an improved Faster R-CNN algorithm to detect apples in dense scenes, 
classifying apples into four categories: unobstructed, leaf-obstructed, 
branch-obstructed, and fruit-obstructed, achieving a high average de-
tection accuracy. Parvathi and Selvi (2021) proposed a Faster R-CNN 
model with a ResNet-50 feature extraction network to improve the 
detection accuracy of coconuts in complex backgrounds. However, the 
Faster R-CNN algorithm has huge memory requirements, which limits 
its detection speed and makes it difficult to deploy on the mobile end 
of the harvesting robot (Zhang et al., 2020). The other type is the one-
stage target detection method, with representative algorithms being 
SSD (Peng HongXing et al., 2018) and the YOLO series (Tian et al., 
2019; Li et al., 2020; Wu et al., 2020; Kang and Chen, 2020; Ji et al., 
2023). The one-stage target detection method uses CNN to directly 
obtain the target category and position prediction of the input image. 
The one-stage detection method has high target detection efficiency, 
fast speed, short training time, and wider application. Among them, 
the YOLO series is a representative example, and has achieved certain 
research results in fruit detection. Zheng et al. (2021) proposed a multi-
scale convolutional neural network YOLO-BP to detect green citrus in 
natural environments, achieving accurate detection of citrus in complex 
environments. Xu et al. (2023) used the lightweight feature extraction 
network GhostNet to improve YOLOv4 and introduced a channel atten-
tion mechanism to enhance the network’s feature extraction capability 
for citrus targets. Sun et al. (2022) proposed a lightweight apple 
detection method based on improved YOLOv5s, replacing the backbone 
network of YOLOv5s with the ShuffleNet network. The average accu-
racy of the improved model was improved by 0.58 percentage points 
compared with the original YOLOv5s model.
Researchers have conducted extensive research on real-time de-
tection and counting tasks using deep learning models. Lyu et al. 
(2022) introduced an improved YOLOv5 to identify and count citrus 
fruits. They modified the loss function and used the cosine annealing 
algorithm in the YOLOv5 model to improve the accuracy of green 
citrus recognition in natural environments and achieved real-time citrus 
detection and counting at 28 FPS on edge devices. Mirhaji et al. 
(2021) used the YOLO detection model to identify and count oranges 
originating from Iran, and evaluated the model on 100 sample trees. 
After detecting and counting all the oranges in the orchard, an orchard 
yield change map was generated. The results showed that the YOLO 
model provides an efficient and fast way to detect and estimate the 
yield of citrus fruits, and the error of the fruit tree yield change map 
was 9.19%. Yu et al. (2024) proposed a winter jujube detection and 
counting method based on SOD-YOLOv5n, and conducted a winter 
jujube counting experiment on 30 jujube trees. The test results showed 
that the root mean square error (RMSE) was 1.06, the determination 
coefficient (𝑅2) was 0.97, and the average detection time of winter 
jujube was 60 ms.
Fruit target algorithms based on deep learning have made signifi-
cant progress, but there are still problems such as low target detection 
accuracy, slow detection speed, and large model calculation volume 
that makes it difficult to deploy. In the natural environment, citrus 
fruits grow in different states, are randomly distributed, overlap, and 
are easily blocked by branches and leaves. Real-time high-precision 
detection and accurate citrus counting in such a complex scenario 
remains a huge challenge. Since there is only one type of target to 
be detected in this paper, the target types are relatively simple, the 
amount of data is small, and it is not suitable for a large model, so 
the lightweight model YOLOv8n is selected as the baseline model. 
At the same time, compared with other YOLOv8 variants, the main 
advantage of YOLOv8n lies in its lightweight, efficient, fast reasoning 
capability and low storage requirements, which makes it very suitable 
for deployment on mobile devices and edge devices. This model first 
introduces FasterNet Block in the backbone network C2f module to 
reduce the number of parameters and calculations while improving 
the detection accuracy of the network. Secondly, a multi-scale atten-
tion mechanism is added to the backbone network to enhance the 
feature extraction ability of the network. Finally, Wise-IoU based on 
a dynamic non-monotonic focusing mechanism is used as the bound-
ing box regression loss function to improve the model convergence 
speed and further improve the model accuracy. The improved FEW-
YOLOv8n model was deployed and applied to the citrus detection 
platform to achieve real-time, high-precision detection and accurate 
citrus counting.
This study aims to address several key challenges in citrus de-
tection, including the slow detection speed and low accuracy of the 
YOLOv8 series models in complex natural environments, the large 
amount of computation required to deploy on mobile devices, and the 
real-time detection and counting of citrus after deployment. The main 
contributions of this study are as follows:
(1) A comprehensive citrus dataset with multiple angles, multiple 
lighting conditions, multiple weather conditions, and different occlu-
sion conditions was established.
(2) An efficient and lightweight algorithm named FEW-YOLOv8n 
was proposed, which achieved 92.4% accuracy, 91.7% average preci-
sion, and 80.6 frames per second (FPS) in a GPU running environment.
(3) FEW-YOLOv8n was compared with other advanced object de-
tection algorithms in the citrus detection task. FEW-YOLOv8n showed 
excellent overall performance, especially in terms of detection speed, 
number of parameters, and model complexity, which is conducive to 
the deployment of agricultural picking robots.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows: Section 2 introduces 
the materials and methods used in this study; Section 3 presents the 
experiments and analysis of the results; Section 4 presents and discusses 
the results; and Section 5 draws conclusions.

2.1. Dataset collection
The dataset of this study was taken at the characteristic Wogan 
planting base in Shuangqiao Town, Wuming District, Nanning City, 
Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region. The images were captured with a 
Daheng industrial camera, featuring a resolution of 4160 × 3120 pixels, 
at a shooting distance ranging from 0.5 to 3 m. This study collected 
the citrus fruit dataset in a natural environment three times, from 8 
am to 6 pm. To prevent overfitting of the network model caused by 
the dataset’s limited feature variety, citrus samples with varying light 
intensities, occlusion levels, and fruit sparsity were collected. A total 
of 2126 citrus images were collected, and the schematic diagram of 
the citrus dataset collection is shown in Fig. 2. Finally, the dataset is 
divided into training set, validation set and test set in the ratio of 8:1:1.

2.2. Augmentation of datasets
Data augmentation offers benefits such as reducing annotation ef-
fort, mitigating overfitting, and enhancing the model’s ability to gener-
alize. In the training process of the detection model, insufficient image 
data may lead to overfitting. Therefore, it is necessary to perform data 
enhancement on the citrus image data. Image processing techniques 
such as cropping, rotation, mirroring, adding noise, and changing 
brightness are used to enhance the original image data. Cropping 
randomly selects a portion of an image for training. Cropping can 
help the model focus on different image regions, thereby improving its 
ability to recognize objects in different positions. Rotating an image can 
change the angle of an object, and can learn the features of an object 
at different rotation angles, thereby improving the model’s robustness 
to the direction of the object. Mirroring helps the model learn the 
symmetric features of an image, which can improve the model’s gener-
alization ability. Adding noise can improve the robustness of the model, 
making it better able to adapt to low-quality, noisy images in the real 
world. Changing the brightness of the image can simulate images under 
different lighting conditions, which helps the model’s adaptability to 
different ambient lighting conditions. Finally, 7400 valid images are 
obtained. The specific effect is shown in Fig. 3.
2.3. Annotation of datasets
In this study, manually labeled images were manually labeled using 
LabelImg (https://github.com/tzutalin/labelImg) annotation software 
for the citrus dataset. A rectangular frame is used for labeling. First, the 
boundary of the citrus fruit is selected, and then the rectangular frame 
is used for selection to frame the entire fruit within the boundary. Citrus 
fruits with an occlusion overlap greater than about 80% are processed 
without labeling. Since the robot only focuses on the target situation 
on the current plane during the picking operation, the citrus fruits that 
are far away in the background are also processed without annotation. 
The labeled xm file is stored in the format of the VOC dataset. Fig. 4 is 
an example of labeling annotations.

2.4.1. C2f-faster
In order to improve the running speed of the network model, 
this paper replaces Bottleneck in the C2f module with a lightweight 
and high-precision FasterNet Block to obtain a new feature extraction 
module C2f-Faster, which effectively reduces the amount of calculation 
and model complexity. FasterNet is a new type of fast network with 
lower latency and higher throughput built on the basis of the Partial 
Convolution (PConv) module. It not only utilizes the computing power 
of the hardware more effectively, but also performs well in spatial 
feature extraction (Chen et al., 2023). The FasterNet Block module is 
composed of a 3 × 3 PConv layer and two 1 × 1 Conv layers. The 
two convolutional layers together form the structure of the reverse 
residual block. The number of channels in the middle layer has been 
expanded, and a quick connection has been added to reuse the input 
features. Replace the Bottleneck in the C2f module with FasterNet Block 
to obtain a new feature extraction module C2f-Faster. C2f Faster divides 
the input data into two branches after the convolution layer. One 
branch is directly passed to the feature connection module Concat, and 
the other branch is processed by FasterNet Block. Finally, the results 
from these two components are merged along the channel dimension, 
and the final output is produced through a convolutional layer. The 
C2f Faster structure improves the network’s feature extraction ability 
while simultaneously lowering computational cost and reducing model 
complexity. The specific structure of FasterNet Block and C2f Faster is 
shown in Fig. 7.

2.4.2. EMA attention module
In the natural environment, Citrus fruits go through various stages 
of growth, random distribution, overlap, and occlusion of branches and 
leaves. The background of citrus images is often complex and contains 
a lot of feature information, which may reduce the training efficiency 
of the model. The attention mechanism performs well in local impor-
tant information, enabling the model to selectively focus on effective 
information, thereby extracting interesting features and suppressing 
other features. EMA is an efficient multi-scale attention mechanism 
that can retain the information of each channel while reducing the 
computational cost. This is because it can transform a portion of the 
channel dimension into the batch dimension and organize the channel 
dimension into multiple sub-features. This approach ensures that spa-
tial semantic features are evenly distributed across each feature group, 
effectively preventing the issue of channel dimensionality reduction. 
The EMA attention mechanism is shown in Fig. 8.
In the EMA module, three parallel paths are employed to extract 
attention weights from the grouped feature maps. Two of these paths 
operate within a 1 × 1 branch, while the third path is part of a 3 × 3 
branch. Specifically, in the 1 × 1 branch, 1D global average pooling is 
applied separately along the two spatial dimensions. The two encoded 
features are then concatenated along the image height direction and 
passed through the same 1 × 1 convolution, without any dimensionality 
reduction occurring within this branch. After decomposing the output 
of the 1 × 1 convolution into two vectors, two nonlinear Sigmoid 
activation functions are used to fit the 2D binomial distribution on the 
linear convolution. The two channel attention maps within each group 
are combined through simple multiplication, enabling different cross-
channel interaction features to be captured between the two parallel 
paths in the 1 × 1 branch. In the 3 × 3 branch, local cross-channel 
interactions are captured by 3 × 3 convolutions, expanding the feature 
space. EMA not only captures inter-channel relationships, but also pre-
serves detailed spatial structural information within the channel. The 
traditional attention mechanism needs to reduce the channel dimension 
to establish the relationship between different channels, while EMA 
effectively overcomes the shortcomings of this traditional method. This 
paper adds a multi-scale attention mechanism EMA (efficient multi-
scale attention) to the backbone network, which enhances the model’s 
ability to focus on the target features of citrus, so that it can obtain 
more feature information.

2.5. Experimental environment and evaluation indicators
2.5.1. Experimental environment
This study evaluates a citrus dataset in a natural environment, 
employs various structural models to develop a citrus detection model, 
and assesses the effectiveness of these different structures. The exper-
iment was conducted on a hardware platform with an image size of 
640 × 640. Image size directly affects detection accuracy. Larger images 
can help the model detect more details, while smaller images and batch 
sizes can speed up training and reasoning. Batch size and the number 
of training rounds affect the stability of model convergence and the 
final performance. Optimizing the number of training rounds helps 
the model learn more features, thereby improving accuracy. According 
to the requirements of the citrus detection task, a balance is found 
between accuracy and speed. These hyperparameters are optimized 
through experiments to ensure that the model can achieve the best 
performance in both detection accuracy and reasoning speed. The 
hardware platform specifications are shown in Table 2, and the training 
hyperparameter settings are shown in Table 3.

3.2. Ablation experiment
This paper uses YOLOv8n as the baseline model. In order to verify 
the effectiveness of the improved module methods proposed in this 
paper, ablation experiments are carried out in the same experimen-
tal environment through different combinations of various improved 
modules. Table 4 shows the ablation test results.
From the analysis of the test results in Table 4, we can see that 
after the FasterNet Block is introduced into the Backbone network C2f 
module of the original YOLOv8n model, the accuracy and average 
precision of the model increase by 1.7 and 1.9 percentage points 
compared with the original model, the number of parameters is reduced 
from 3.01M to 2.69M, and the amount of calculation is reduced from 
8.1G to 7.2G. The results show that the introduction of FasterNet 
Block into the backbone network C2f module not only reduces the 
number of parameters and the amount of calculation of the model, 
but also improves the accuracy and average precision of the model. 
On the basis of improving the backbone network model, after adding 
the EMA attention mechanism, the average accuracy and computational 
complexity of the model slightly decreased, and the accuracy rate 
increased by 1.8 percentage points. This is because the introduction of 
the EMA attention mechanism enhances the feature extraction ability 
of the network model, enabling it to better focus on those easily 
overlooked feature information in the target to be detected, thereby 
significantly improving the accuracy of the model. After introducing 
the three improvement strategies at the same time, the accuracy and 
average precision of the model reached 92.4% and 91.7% respectively. 
Compared with the original model, the accuracy and average precision 
increased by 3.8 and 1.9 percentage points respectively, the number 
of parameters was reduced from 3.01M to 2.69M, and the amount of 
calculation was reduced from 8.1G to 7.3G. The number of parameters 
and the amount of calculation of the model were reduced by 10.6% and 
9.3% respectively compared with the original model. Comprehensive 
ablation experiments show that adding three improvement strategies 
can achieve the best recognition effect and make the model lighter.

5. Conclusions
This study introduces FasterNet Block, EMA attention mechanism 
and Wise-IoU as loss function, and proposes a lightweight FEW-
YOLOv8n model, which is lightweight and deployed on the citrus detec-
tion platform for real-time detection and counting of citrus. Compared 
with YOLOv8n, the precision of the FEW-YOLOv8n model was 92.4%, 
and the mean average precision (mAP) was 91.7%, which were 3.8% 
and 1.9% higher respectively. The number of parameters, the amount 
of calculation and the model size were 2.69MB, 7.3G and 5.37MB re-
spectively, which were 10.6%, 9.3% and 10.1% lower than the original 
model respectively. On this basis, it was lightweight and deployed 
on the citrus detection platform for citrus counting experiments. The 
mean absolute error (MAE) and root mean square error (RMSE) of the 
FEW-YOLOv8n model were 0.92 and 1.28, respectively, which were 
reduced by 48% and 44.8% compared with YOLOv8n. In addition, 
the determination coefficient 𝑅2 of the FEW-YOLOv8n model was 
0.98. Experiments show that the FEW-YOLOv8n model has achieved 
good detection performance in complex orchard environments, espe-
cially in terms of detection speed, number of parameters, and model 
complexity. FEW-YOLOv8n is capable of accurately detecting small 
targets with varying degrees of occlusion and can perform real-time 
citrus detection and counting on the citrus detection platform, offering 
technical support for future citrus yield prediction. In future research, 
more advanced algorithms will be designed to further improve the 
robustness of the model under extreme conditions, while combining 
it with agricultural robots for engineering applications.
