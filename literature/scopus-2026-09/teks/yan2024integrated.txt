Abstract—Aiming at the problems of the dense target 
distribution, poor positioning ability to pick robots, and 
inaccurate ripeness judgment in the picking orchard scene, this 
paper proposes an apple image recognition model with a high 
recognition rate, high speed, and high accuracy, which can 
effectively analyze the data of quantity, location, ripeness and 
quality estimation in the apple image. Firstly, the Faster R-
CNN network is improved by introducing Efficient Channel 
Attention (ECA) and multi-scale fusion feature pyramid (FPN) 
for fruit detection and recognition localization. Then the 
distance transform-based watershed algorithm is used for 
image segmentation to fit the apple edge image while 
combining with the fitted circle determination algorithm to 
establish a mathematical model for apple volume estimation to 
calculate the quantity as well as the quality of apples. Finally, 
the apples are classified into four categories according to their 
ripeness, and the improved Faster R-CNN network is used to 
improve the ripeness detection effect, and the results show that 
the average fruit recognition accuracy of the improved method 
proposed in this paper is 95.42%, which significantly improves 
the accuracy of fruit detection. 
Keywords—Apple, Faster R-CNN, Watershed algorithm, 
Counting, Localization, Maturity testing  
I. INTRODUCTION
To completely replace humans to complete the picking 
operation, the first problem to be solved by the picking robot 
is how to quickly and accurately locate and detect the 
picking object in the complex orchard environment with 
dense distribution of fruit targets and severe branch and leaf 
cover. This requires that the picking robot must have 
detection and localization algorithms with stronger target 
recognition capabilities. 
A. Related Work
Numerous picking robots utilizing deep learning
techniques have also been developed [1-8]. Deep learning-
based target detection is categorized into two groups[9-10]: 
two-stage detection algorithms and single-stage detection 
algorithms [11]. The two-stage detection algorithms are 
exemplified 
by 
Region-based 
Convolutional 
Neural 
Networks (R-CNN), specifically, the R-CNN family of 
algorithms. This algorithm exhibits superior detection 
accuracy, albeit at the cost of reduced detection speed. Sun et 
al. [12] introduced a detection model for identifying key 
tomato organs utilizing Faster RCNN. They employed 
Resnet50 for the feature extraction network instead of vgg16 
and K-means clustering instead of manual adjustment of 
anchor frame sizes. This modification led to enhanced 
detection accuracy. Jia et al. [13] enhanced the detection 
accuracy by integrating the residuals from a Mask R-CNN 
network with those from an R-CNN network, building upon 
the Mask R-CNN framework. The network combined a 
Residual Network (ResNet) with Dense Convolutional 
Network (DenseNet) to achieve precise detection of apples. 
Chu et al. [14] enhanced the accuracy of apple detection by 
incorporating inhibitory branches into Mask R-CNN. This 
modification helped filter out non-apple features acquired by 
the backbone network, leading to an F1 score of 0.905 and 
improved detection speed. 
Another category of single-stage detection algorithms 
includes SSD, YOLO, among others. MAZZIA V et al [15] 
utilized the enhanced YOLOv3-Tiny network to attain a high 
detection speed. Wu et al. [16] demonstrated favorable 
outcomes by employing the enhanced EfficientNetYOLOV4 
dataset and a method for enhancing leaf shading data. Haili 
Zhou and colleagues [17] devised a pollination model 
utilizing YOLOv5 to enhance the pollination process of 
kiwifruit flowers. The algorithm's speed was enhanced by 
employing the K-means++ clustering method to group the 
anchors in proximity to the target size. However, the 
aforementioned methods either detect individual apples or 
concentrate on categorizing the background. Feng Xiao and 
colleagues [18] introduced a lightweight detection approach 
utilizing enhancements to the YOLOv5 algorithm. The 
experimental findings demonstrated the method's efficacy in 
detecting blueberry fruits and determining their ripeness 
stages within an orchard setting. Nevertheless, during 
practical orchard harvesting, the picking robot [19] might 
encounter challenges in directly harvesting fruits when they 
are obstructed by branches or other fruits. The current 
method cannot comprehensively detect apples and is unable 
to simultaneously identify both the ripeness and quality 
characteristics of the apples.

paper adopts ResNet50, a residual network of Fused Feature 
Pyramid Networks (FPN) with strong expressive ability, as 
the backbone network, and embeds an attention module to 
enhance the model's learning ability of fruit features. Then 
we adopt the watershed algorithm based on distance 
transformation for image segmentation to identify the

II. IMAGE PROCESSING AND MODEL IMPROVEMENT 
A. Image pre-processing 
This paper takes the apples to be picked in the orchard as 
the object of detection and positioning. Firstly, 200 public 
datasets in jpg format with 270*180 pixels were selected on 
Google, and the original images were processed by resizing, 
filtering, segmentation, enhancement, etc. At the same time, 
in order to satisfy the requirements of the detection model 
that requires a large amount of training image data, and to 
improve the robustness of the algorithm in detection, this 
paper adopts the image enhancement technology for image 
pre-processing, and the comparison of the effect before and

B. Improved Fast R-CNN Model for Target Detection 
The Faster R-CNN comprises two essential networks: a 
Region Proposal Network (RPN) responsible for generating 
region proposals and a detection network for identifying 
objects. The RPN evaluates the region boxes, proposing 
boxes that are highly probable to contain objects. The 
architecture of the enhanced Faster R-CNN model is 
depicted in Fig. 2 and comprises four primary elements: 
feature extraction network, region proposal network, ROI 
pooling, and classification with bounding box regression. 
The enhanced model employs ResNet50 [20] for feature 
extraction. The initial stage involves a 7×7 convolutional 
layer and 3×3 maximal pooling for downsampling the input 
image. Subsequently, Conv2, Conv3, Conv4, and Conv5 
residual structures are utilized to extract high-dimensional 
features in the second stage. Finally, the extracted high-
dimensional features are forwarded to the fully connected 
layer in the third stage for classification. The architecture of 
the enhanced ResNet50 model is illustrated in Fig. 3. 
 
Fig. 2. Structure of the improved Faster R-CNN model 
The feature extraction capacity of Faster R-CNN is 
improved through the incorporation of the Convolutional 
Attention Module (CBAM) as illustrated in Figure 4. The 
CBAM module introduces an uncomplicated yet efficient 
attention mechanism for feed-forward convolutional neural 
networks. When presented with an intermediate feature map, 
the CBAM module proceeds to deduce the attention map 
along two distinct dimensions in a sequential manner. 
Subsequently, it combines the attention map with the feature 
map to execute adaptive feature optimization [21]. In this 
study, the low-complexity and high-efficiency CBAM 
module has been integrated into the ResNet50 backbone 
network to augment the model's representation, thereby 
enhancing 
the 
model's 
recognition 
and 
localization 
performance.

In order to differentiate between various sizes of fruits 
depicted in an image, it is essential to take into account 
features of diverse scales. Given that the features obtained 
through Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) undergo a 
gradual abstraction process leading to a loss of detailed 
information, the decision is made to integrate Feature 
Pyramid Networks (FPNs) with robust semantic features [22]. 
In this study, a multi-scale feature fusion Feature Pyramid

Network (FPN) architecture is developed by integrating 
features obtained from the C2, C3, C4, and C5 stages of the 
ResNet50 network. Four types of feature maps are derived 
by extracting the feature maps from the final residual block 
of each residual unit. Subsequently, feature refinement and 
filtering are conducted using the channel CBAM module to 
emphasize the pertinent feature information captured by the 
network. These processed feature maps are then fed into the 
FPN structure illustrated in Fig. 5. 
Given the distinct characteristics of various targets, it is 
essential to horizontally connect feature maps at different 
scales to construct the Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) 
structure. The feature maps P2-P5 generated by the FPN 
correspond to C2-C5 in ResNet50, while P6 is derived from 
the more abstract features of P5 through maximum pooling. 
These feature maps at different scales, ranging from P2 to P6, 
serve as inputs to the Region Proposal Network (RPN) for 
the detection of Regions of Interest (ROIs). 
C. Contour segmentation based on watershed algorithm 
In order to more accurately identify the parameters such 
as the number, location and quality of apples in the 
preprocessed image, to address the problems of the 
watershed algorithm, this paper improves the watershed 
algorithm by combining the morphological processing 
algorithm and the distance change algorithm. The method 
uses the distance transformation algorithm to process the 
apple target image and transforms the shape information of 
apples in the image into uniformly distributed grey scale 
information. As apples have circle-like features, local 
minima are only generated at the center of each apple target 
region, so the appearance of pseudo-local minima can be 
reduced and the number of regions generated by watershed 
segmentation can be controlled. 
Because the watershed algorithm based on the distance 
transform can only solve part of the over-segmentation 
problem, this paper optimizes the image in the pre-watershed 
segmentation 
processing 
and 
the 
post-processing 
respectively, which can make the apple target region 
segmentation effect more ideal. Fig. 6(a) shows the contour 
segmentation process, and Fig. 6(b) shows the image after 
contour segmentation.

Due to a number of factors, pseudo-contours of the apple 
target synthesized from small partial fits may occur. In order

each fitted circle. If the radius of a fitted circle is less than 
two-thirds of the mean value, the fitted circle is discarded; if 
it is greater, it is retained. The remaining fitted circles are 
finally obtained, and these fitted circles are considered to be 
the apple target data in the possible images, which can be 
used to perform the next calculations. Formula (1) shows the

single apple being fitted to multiple circles, in this paper we 
choose to compute the coordinates of the obtained fitted 
circles. Specifically, we calculate the Euclidean distance

calculated distance is less than a predefined threshold, then 
the two circles are identified as fitted circles for the same 
apple target. Then the average of the centroid coordinates 
and radii of these two circles are calculated and used as the 
centroid and radius of the new fitted circle and continue to 
perform the same calculations for the other fitted circles.

Considering that apple weight prediction is applied to the 
special characteristics of apple grading, according to the 
common sense of fresh fruit processing in the fruit market, 
apple fruits are ripe and have high freshness and high 
moisture content, so the weight of apples is mainly 
considered in terms of horizontal and vertical diameters and 
volume of apples. Through the multivariate linear function 
model and multivariate mixed function model [23], after 
fitting the data with MATLAB software, the correlation 
coefficients of the multivariate linear fitting model and the 
multivariate mixed function fitting model were 0.9386 and 
0.8758, respectively, and therefore the multivariate linear 
fitting model was selected to detect the quality of apples. 
Apple ripeness was classified according to peel color and 
market demand [24], and divided into four levels of ripeness, 
i.e. over-ripeness (deep red peel), ripeness (basically all-red
peel), semi-ripeness (red-green peel), and non-ripeness
(basically all-green peel). The improved Fater R-CNN model
can be used to determine the maturity level of apples. 
IV. CONCLUSION
In this study, we present a methodology for recognizing 
apples, which includes detection, counting, localization, and 
quality assessment. The Apple dataset has been enhanced 
through the application of image enhancement techniques, 
and a refined Faster R-CNN model has been introduced for 
training the dataset. The enhanced Faster R-CNN model 
achieves a mean Average Precision (mAP) of 95.42%, a 
Mean Intersection over Union (MIoU) localization accuracy 
of 97.14%, and processes frames at an average computation 
time of 134ms. The enhanced Faster R-CNN model 
suggested in this study enables apple localization and 
maturity analysis. The model utilizes the watershed 
algorithm to segment images for detecting both the quantity 
and quality of apples. Additionally, the recognition method 
introduced in this research offers a reasonable and effective 
approach for automated picking systems.
