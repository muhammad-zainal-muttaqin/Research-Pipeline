ABSTRACT   
Malaysia's oil palm industry faces many challenges in sustaining manual oil palm harvesting operations. This work 
investigates an effective oil palm Fresh Fruit Bunch (FFB) image processing method for robot harvesting automation. 
This research explores the proposed image processing method that first detects the Fresh Fruit Bunch (FFB) category 
which involves 6 different categories of FFB growth stages and then detects its 6D pose estimation for harvesting. Next, 
this research proposes a novel image processing framework that utilises the convolutional neural network deep learning 
classification and is followed by markerless feature-registration-based oil palm FFB for 6D pose estimation with the 
public FFB dataset. Furthermore, this work introduced view obstruction to the public FFB dataset as noise for practical 
robot harvester applications in plantation field operation. Moreover, the experiment results show the proposed model can 
maintain a high F1 score performance up until 70% of view obstruction before the F1 score performance is reduced. 
Keywords: FFB, Image Processing, Markerless, Oil Palm, 6D pose estimation, Classification, Deep Learning 
 
1. INTRODUCTION  
Malaysia is the world’s second-largest palm oil agriculture industry producer [1]. However, Malaysia's agriculture 
industry faces many difficult challenges such as Work-Related Musculoskeletal Disorders (WRMSDs) issues among oil 
palm plantation workers [1] and socioeconomic matters [2] which are not sustainable in the long run. Therefore, robotic 
automation solutions (see Figure 1) to resolve Malaysia’s agricultural challenges are crucial for Malaysia's agriculture 
industry [2]. To ensure the sustainability of the Malaysian agriculture industry to operate efficiently, we proposed the 
novel oil palm Fresh Fruit Bunches (FFB) image processing for the robot harvester in the oil palm plantation field. This 
research objective focuses on the image processing contribution of the research where we aim to detect the oil palm FFB 
categories and then detect FFB 6D pose estimation for mechanisation of oil palm robot harvesting operations. Based on 
our knowledge, our proposed method is novel in the oil palm robot harvesting research area. The 6D pose estimation is 
an important feature for robot harvesting because it enables the robot to manipulate the FFB for harvesting. The research 
questions are: 1) How does the proposed image processing detect the oil palm FFB? 2) What is the F1 score performance 
comparison between different view obstruction percentages with our proposed method? 
The oil palm FFB robotic harvesting operation involves removing objects (e.g., leaves, branches, dirt) and other view 
obstacles before oil palm FFB can be harvested. Hence, the robotic harvester’s image processing must be equipped with 
the capability of image object detection with view obstruction tolerance. Therefore, the robotic harvester can effectively 
locate the oil palm FFB from the robot’s view during operation in the plantation. Next, the harvester robot needs the 6D 
object pose estimation (See Figure 2) to locate oil palm FFB on the ground. Once the oil palm FFB 6D pose is estimated 
on the plantation ground, the robot harvester utilises the technique of visual servoing [3] to move itself nearer to the FFB 
for the grabbing or harvest process (See Figure 3). Furthermore, the markerless visual servoing approach is utilised 
instead because it is not feasible to implement a marker approach in a large oil palm plantation environment.

2. LITERATURE REVIEW 
2.1 Markerless Feature Registration 6D Pose Estimation of Novel Objects.  
MegaPose [4] is a 6D object pose estimation method that is trained with a huge number of artificial scenes with different 
objects. It can also be utilized without retraining to estimate the pose of different unseen items. Furthermore, if 
MegaPose is provided with a CAD model, it can also be utilized as a visually guided robot manipulation system in 
unseen scenes containing novel objects.  
Next, this work [4] focuses on a key point and single-level-based method for category-level object 6D pose estimation. 
Furthermore, this method also works on single RGB images of unseen objects within a known category. The proposed 
method performs object detection, identifies 2D key points, estimates 6-DoF pose, and generates the bounding cuboid 
dimensions.  
Additionally, category-level object pose estimation via neural analysis-by-synthesis [5] This work combines a parametric 
neural image synthesis module with a gradient-based fitting process, synthesis eliminates the need for explicit CAD 
models for each item instance by implicitly describing the appearance, shape, and pose of entire object categories. To 
employ model capacity to jointly capture shape and local appearance (i.e., texture) variations, the image synthesis 
network is built to span the pose configuration space efficiently. 
Moreover, PolarMesh [6] can be recorded in a discrete 2D map that permits a simple conversion between it and the 
object surface. This object surface approximation's fixed-grid 2D nature guarantees that an auto-encoder neural network 
can estimate it from an image patch of the object.  
Besides, this research  [7] is a new technique for metric shape retrieval and class-level monocular 6D pose estimation. 
Additionally, this study suggests the concept of synthetic-to-real domain transfer for class-level 6D poses using self-
supervised learning, which eliminates the need to gather a large number of manual annotations. To improve later 
inference, they essentially use current developments in differentiable rendering to self-supervise the model using 
unannotated real RGB-D data after training our suggested technique fully supervised with synthetic data.  
Next, KPAM [8] is a brand-new approach to category-level manipulation that represents objects using semantic 3D key 
points. With the help of this key point representation, the manipulation objective may be easily and understandably 
specified as geometric costs and constraints on the key points, hence flexibly generating existing pose-based 
manipulation techniques. With this formulation, they include the manipulation policy into local dense-geometry-based 
action execution, optimization-based robot action planning, instance segmentation, and 3D key point detection. 
Additionally, Catgrasp: Learning category-level task-relevant grasping in clutter from simulation [9] is a one-step, 
keypoint-based method for category-level object posture estimation that uses a single RGB image as input and works on 
unknown object instances within a known category. The suggested network regresses relative bounding cuboid 
dimensions, estimates 6-DoF posture, detects 2D objects, and finds 2D key points. 
2.2 Markerless Feature Registration 6D Pose Estimation of Known Objects.  
This work BB8 [10] is a brand-new technique for estimating poses and detecting 3D objects from colour photos alone. 
To identify items of interest in 2D, even when there are partial occlusions and a cluttered background, they first employ 
segmentation. In contrast to current patch-based techniques, they use a "holistic" strategy: They apply a convolutional 

 
 
 
 
 
 
neural network trained to forecast the 3D postures of the identified objects in the form of 2D images of their 3D 
bounding box corners. 
Next. This work  [11] detects image objects in the context of 6-DoF posture estimation of known objects from a single 
RGB image, they investigate the reality gap. They also demonstrate that a straightforward combination of randomized 
and realistic data can effectively bridge the reality gap for this problem. They present a one-shot deep neural network 
that can outperform a state-of-the-art network trained on a combination of real and synthetic data using synthetic data 
created in this way. 
2.3 Visual Servoing  
ViSP [3] describes a fully functional modular architecture that enables the rapid creation of applications involving visual 
servoing. The platform is a library that is separated into three primary modules: real-time tracking, canonical vision-
based tasks with the most traditional connections, and control procedures.

3.1 Deep Learning Image Classification 
The convolution neural network deep learning [12] for image classification is utilised to predict the six maturity level oil 
palm FFB dataset [13]. The Ubuntu 22.04 LTS OS, Anaconda, Tensorflow, Jupyter Notebook, Nvidia Quadro P5000

3.2 Model Optimization   
The proposed image processing method in Figure 2 indicates capturing the Fresh Fruit Bunches (FFB) model for 6D 
pose estimation. The first step is using RGB Images with Spectacular AI or COLMAP application to capture raw image 
data for 3D model construction. Then, NeRF will process such data to generate the Fresh Fruit Bunches (FFB) 3D 
model. Then, use the Blender application to edit Fresh Fruit Bunches (FFB) 3D model refining. Next, the coarse 
estimator will use image rendering of the 3D Fresh Fruit Bunches (FFB) model. The iterative refiner will optimise the 
6D pose prediction before the final pose is used.  
 
Figure 2. The 6D model optimisation flow chart for harvester robot visual processing preprocessing of the 3D model. 
3.3 Visual Servoing   
This research proposed a visual servoing framework in Figure 3 which shows the use of a harvester robot to perform 
harvesting by moving to the targeted oil palm FFB after 6D pose estimation of FFB. Figure 3 shows the flow chart 
performs harvester robot RGB image input capture. Then, the framework identifies the region of interest and performs 
deep-learning image classification to predict the FFB’s category. Next, the framework will select a suitable 3D model 
based on the predicted FFB category. Then, the framework extracts the selected 3D model features followed by a coarse 
estimator and iterative refiner. Once the 3D model has refined its pose position, the final FFB 6D pose prediction will be 
generated. After that, the harvester robot will be based on the generated FFB 6D pose and approach it for harvesting by 
using motor-encoder odometry. Hence, 6D pose estimation is important to indicate the pose and distance of FFB for the 
robot harvester to perform harvesting.  

 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Figure 3. The proposed approach flow chart of feature alignment visual servoing technology without any marker. 
3.3 Experiment Settings   
The oil palm FFB dataset [13] consists of six categories: overripe, underripe, unripe, ripe anomalous fruit and empty 
bunches. This dataset has been used to annotate and label the growth degree of oil palm FFB as categories. A total of 
4160 annotated photographs for the six maturity level groups and 14559 samples have been labelled. In this research, we 
applied the noise of view obstruction to the test dataset for performance evaluation.

5. RESULTS ANALYSIS 
Table 1 shows the experiment results for convolution neural network deep learning image classification with different 
view obstruction percentages (0% to 80%) and six oil palm FFB maturity levels. The 0% view obstruction achieved the 
highest weighted F1 score in all categories with consideration of its sample portion weight of each category. 
Furthermore, as the view obstruction percentage increases, the weighted F1 score decreases as expected. However, the 
decrease in weighted F1 score is not significant until 70% of the view obstruction percentage and then the model starts to 
reduce its performance. The highest weighted F1 score is 0.2226 with 0% view obstruction percentage and the lowest 
weighted F1 score is 0.0438 with 80% view obstruction percentage which is the expected outcome of the experiment. 
The ripe FFB category F1 score achieved the highest score at 0.44989 among other categories even at a 20% view 
obstruction setting. Based on our experiment data observation, this FFB category has the highest training sample, which 
is distinguishable from different categories (See Figure 4) in the FFB dataset [13]. Hence, we believe this is why this 
category can perform the highest F1 score than other categories.  

 
 
 
 
 
 
6. CONCLUSION  
This research introduced a novel image-processing approach for FFB robot harvesting automation. This work utilises 
convolution neural network deep learning image classification of six FFB categories with 0% to 80% view obstacle 
obstruction percentages. Then, our framework also proposes the novel 6D pose estimation framework after the FFB 
category is identified and then performs visual servoing for robot FFB harvesting. The experiment results show that the 
image classification weighted F1 score can maintain its performance up to 70% view obstacle obstruction conditions to 
simulate the actual environment in an oil palm plantation.  
7. LIMITATION AND FUTURE WORK 
The limitation of this work is the published oil palm FFB dataset [13] did not provide enough training sample data for 
deep learning machine learning. Hence, the image classification F1-score did not perform well for low training sample 
categories. Due to the limited research funding in this project, we did not implement real-time robot harvester processing 
because computation cost required a high-performance GPU laptop with at least RTX 4080Ti or better GPU computing 
specifications to be mounted on the harvester robot during harvesting. For future work, we will gather and label larger 
training oil palm FFB sample data for better image classification F1-score performance with robot harvester 
mechanisation implementation in the oil palm plantation field experiments.
