Non-Destructive Evaluation of Oil Palm Fresh Fruit Bunch 
Quality Using Thermal Vision 
D Cherie1, N Fatmawati2, and M Makky1,*  
1Dept. of Agricultural Engineering, Universitas Andalas, Padang 25163, West Sumatra, Indonesia 
2Dept. of Physic. Universitas Andalas, Padang 25163, West Sumatra, Indonesia. 
 
 
*muhmakky@ae.unand.ac.id 
Abstract. Oil palm (Elaeis Guineensis Jacq) is the main product of Indonesian agro-industry. It 
supports the country's economy. After harvest, the Fresh Fruits Bunch (FFB) should immediately 
be processed to extract the oil. Delayed will reduce the oil quality significantly. Nonetheless, due 
to poor road-conditions, the FFB often delivered to the palm mills beyond the acceptable time. 
To reduce the decrease of the fruit freshness, generally cold storage was applied. In this study, 
the FFB immediately freeze after harvest. Fruits stored at -20 oC, upon delivered to the mills. On 
arrival, acclimatization process was perform to restore fruit temperature before processing.  A 
pseudo-color thermal vision used to monitor the fruits surface temperature continuously. Various 
fruits maturities observed in this study were: 120, 140, 160, 180, and 200 days after antheses 
(DAA). Temperature calibration was done by manually measured the fruit's surface using a 
thermometer. Models developed to correlate the actual surface temperature of fruits with pseudo-
color displayed by the thermal-vision. Results showed the model of thermal vision correctly 
measured fruits temperature with R2 of 0.979, 0.973, 0.976, 0.976, and, 0.973 for stored fruits 
harvested at 120, 140, 160, 180, and 200 DAA respectively. The color of images on the camera 
can be used to observe the temperature of the fruits. The highest correlation obtained with 
redness index (R2 of 0.7677-0.8037), and the lowest is bluish index (R2 of 0.1101-0.561). FFB 
harvested at 180 DAA has the strongest correlation (R2) of temperature when observed by 
thermal-vision. 
1. Introduction  
Palm oil is the main plantation commodity in Indonesia, and support the economy [1]. The vegetable oil 
derived by milling the fruits. It mainly consumed by industrial sector, including food oleochemicals and 
industry [2]. The oil quality is strongly influenced by the postharvest handling and fruits ripeness upon 
harvest. Properly harvest and handle the Fresh Fruits Bunch (FFB) can prevent the damages [3]. 
The FFB should immediately process upon harvest to maintain the freshness. Milling the fruit in 
fresh condition (less than 22 hours after harvesting) will prevents the deterioration of oil due to 
enzymatic activities and oxidation process [4]. Delayed will increase the level of free fatty acids (FFA) 
in oil [5]. The FFA formed due to the oxidation and enzymatic process. The FFA level indicates the oil 
quality. A high FFA level showed a lower oil quality [6]. Other oil quality indicator Deterioration of 
Bleaching Index (DOBI) normally correlated to the ripeness of FFB when harvested [7]. Therefore, 
harvesting the FFB should be performed when fruits reached optimal ripeness. The ripeness can be 
determined by the ratio of oil and moisture content in the fruits’ mesocarp [7]. When the fruits ripening

Previously, evaluation of ripeness level, damage, and delaymaned process of FFB had been studied 
[4, 9, 13], and direct influence to FFB quality and oil extracted were found [4, 7, 10-15]. However, these 
studies were manually performed, and required high cost and resources. Different methods were 
developed enable the quality observation of FFB in-situ. The methods delivered the results in rapids and 
nondestructive nature using NIR spectroscopy [9]. However, the study only performs measurements on 
FFB at certain temperature. The effect of different temperature regime has not been explored yet.  
Reducing fruits temperature immediately after harvest can extend it shelf life [Cecilia, 2008]. Among 
other methods, freeze storage has the advantages of maintaining the quality of agricultural product [16-
17]. The advantages of freeze storage were evaluated on several fruits such as dates and mangoes [16-
17]. Dates shelf life when stored at -18 oC can be extended up to 6 months [16]. Sliced mangoes quality 
can be maintained for 6 months when stored at -30 oC [17]. However, no reports of storing the FFB 
using freeze method. Therefore, in this research we explore the possibility of storing FFB below the 
freezing temperature.  
For processing frozen foods, acclimatization process was required to restore the product temperature. 
The temperature set point is unique for each processing procedure. For FFB milling, the temperature 
should be risen to 121 oC [18]. However, moisture content can influence the temperature profiles of 
products during acclimatization. When FFB developed, ripening process caused the moisture content in 
fruits’ mesocarp to decrease due to the accumulation of oil. It is shown by changes in physiological 
properties, such as appearance and chemical compound. Moreover, the different rate of changes during 
acclimatization process will be difficult to measure the temperature of fruits constantly. Therefore, a 
reactive, sensitive, and accurate device is needed for observing the changes. With this challenge, we 
performed thermal imaging measurement to continuously monitor FFB temperature during 
acclimatization. This technique will be useful for more efficient milling in palm mills.  
2. Material and Methods 
In this study, Oil Palm FFBs were observed from antheses to harvest. The bunches were selected from 
7 years trees or older. The samples located at 0°41'44.6" South and 100°58'54.7" East. The plants were 
Marihat cultivar and cultivated according to standard practice (regularly fertilized, pruned and irrigated). 
To determine the effect of ripeness level on the FFB acclimatization the fruits were harvested at two 
ripeness levels (ripe and unripe). 
The unripe FFB harvested between 120 and 140 days after antheses (DAA). In this period, the 
accumulation of oil begins in the mesocarp and kernel fruit [18]. The accumulation continues until the 
process of decomposition occurs due to oil degradation in the fruit. The degradation process occurs 
when the fruits passed its optimum ripeness [19]. Therefore, the second group of samples consist of FFB 
harvested between 160 and 200 DAA. For each samples group three replications are performed.  
Since FFB freshness correlated to its oil quality, all fruits were stored at -20 oC immediately after 
harvested.  This is to prevent degradation of oil in fruits’ mesocarp. In this study the FFBs stored for 90 
~ 210 days (Table 1) to maintain the freshness before being milled. For processing the FFB the fruits 
were acclimatized from storage temperature (-20 oC) to room temperature. A thermal camera was used 
to monitor changes on fruits temperature continuously. The data was recorded every 4 minutes until 
room temperature was reached by the fruits. Then, the FFB was sterilized, and temperature observation 
was performed until the fruits reached 50 oC. Measurements were performed at similar interval. 
A simple statistical analysis was done to correlated current fruits temperature and observation from 
thermal camera. The results were then plotted in to a graph, and the R2 was determined quadratic 
polynomial. The samples images from thermal camera then subsequently process using image 
processing software. The background was segmented and the object features were extracted to obtain 
R, G and B data. The features then grouped according to FFB ripeness when harvested.  The data then 
correlated to samples temperature, using linear regression model. The FFB thermal properties can be 
determined based on the coefficient of correlation (R2) from the model. The R2 calculated based on 
quadratic polynomial equation. The model explained the linearity of FFB temperature during

3. Results and Discussion 
In the beginning the FFB contains high MC and only little oil presented. The MC mostly present in 
fruits' mesocarp. The fruits start to develop after pollination. Young fruits consist of mesocarp and empty 
kernel. In this stage, the fruit mesocarp is dominated by fiber and moisture. As fruit ripe the moisture in 
mesocarp decreases due to the translocation of oil formation [8]. The accumulation of oil in fruit replace 
the moisture [20], and oil started to developed through synthesis process from 120 DAA [9, 18, 19]. The 
oil content in fruit changes during the ripening in parallel to the metabolism [19]. This process catalyzed 
due to physiological and biochemical processes in fruits. The process continuously occurred from 
antheses until fruit fully ripe [4, 9]. 
 Water's thermal capacity is higher than oil [21]. The difference in thermal properties can be observed 
using a thermal camera [9]. Thermal cameras record the intensity of infrared radiation emitted by the 
object. The radiation is captured by the microbolometer sensor made from vanadium oxide []. The signal 
generated by sensor processed by software and displayed on the device screen. The image display uses 
a pseudo color technique was used to display the image in color. The image recorded in internal memory 
and converted into temperature data through linear regression model.  Using the model, the thermal 
camera can precisely measure the sample's surface temperature in a non-contact manner. 
In this study, FFB was harvested at different ripeness. Optimal ripeness determined by the ratio of 
oil and water content in fruits’ mesocarp [7]. After harvest, the senescent started and fatty acids 
immediately form in FFB. Therefore, the bunches should be processed within 22 hours [4]. Formation 
of fatty acids promote by fruit physiological process, which reduce the quality of oil. 
The shelf life of the fruit can be maintained with freeze storage. Freeze storage can extend the life of 
dates and mangoes by up to 6 months [16-17]. In freeze storage the enzymatic and physiological 
processes are stopped, so that fruit damage can be prevented. For further processing, acclimatization is 
needed to avoid damage in fruit cells. 
In this study, acclimatization of palm fruits was performed by placing the fruits on petridishes. The 
dish diameter was 100 mm, and fruits were arranged in single level. The samples then placed inside a 
sterilizer, where temperature was set to 50 oC. The temperature changes of fruit were measured using a 
thermometer. At the same time samples were recorded by thermal camera. The observation interval is 
set every 4 minutes until sample reached 50 oC. The data is presented in Figure 1. 
The rate of temperature change in the acclimatization process is closely related to the thermal 
properties of samples. It is influenced by cells and physical structure, the chemical composition of the 
object, and MC [22]. When heated, unripe fruits were slower to reach room temperature (25 oC) when 
compared to the ripe one. Afterward, sterilization process started until samples reached 50 oC. In this 
stage, the unripe fruits were faster to reach the maximum temperature, while the ripe fruits were slower 
to get to the same condition. Increase of temperature of the object was faster due to higher MC, with 
lower oil content. This condition can be found in unripe FFB. Thermal properties have positive 
correlation with thermal capacity, and moisture absorb heat better at higher temperature than oil [21]. 
The condition is vice versa when the objects were cooled. In addition the rate of temperature changes 
influence by FFB ripeness when harvested.

While difference were observed in acclimatization process due to various concentration of oil and 
moisture in the samples, other phenomena such as hysteresis and nonlinearity can further influence the 
temperature profile during heating. These two phenomenons generally occur in all cycled process 
naturally. Chemical composition, physical parameter, and thermal capacities have different nature 
during heating and cooling process. The difference of sample properties provides challenges to 
accurately predict the temperature of oil palm during acclimatization process. Continuous measurement 
is difficult, and therefore, observation of fruit temperature during the acclimatization process requires a 
device that is reactive, sensitive, and accurate and does not require physical contact. 
 Thermometer has different sensitivity than thermal cameras. Higher sensitivity is required to rapidly 
measure temperatures of an object. The thermal camera can respond significantly to the temperature 
changes in the minute medium under temperature measurements. The sensor used in the thermal camera 
is a microbolometer made of vanadium oxide with a sensitivity of less than 0.05 ° C or equal to smaller 
than 50 milli K. With this sensitivity the thermal camera can obtained a more linear measurement as 
compared to regular thermometer, especially when the object is sphere and has small surface area. The 
comparison of linearity for temperature measurement between thermal camera and thermometer is 
shown in figure 2. 
The level of linearity of the thermal camera in the measurement of the temperature of the palm fruit 
has a strong attachment (R2 0.9). Strong attachment indicates that the whole model is able to predict 
temperature with the help of a thermal camera. The temperature distribution of the object, affeceted by 
the composition of the cellulose and lignin ingredients. Cellulose is a fiber that makes up the structure 
of tissues and strengthens cells [23]. Lignin is part of the cell wall with the most polymers after cellulose 
[24]. 
The accumulation of lignin content increases with the development of plant age, while the cellulose 
content decreases [25]. Lignin is very resistant to degradation, biologically, enzymatically, or 
chemically [25]. Therefore the rate of change in fruit temperature at the unripe level is faster than fruit 
at ripeness level. This is caused because the fruit at the unripe level has a low lignin content so that the 
resistance to heat is low. 
The thermal capacity of an object influences the rate of change in the object's temperature. The rate 
of change in temperature of the palm fruit during acclimatization and sterilization can be seen visually 
based on the recording results on the visible camera and thermal camera (Table 2). The results of the 
thermal camera image are then evaluated using the image processing program. Image processing is 
performed to present the results of thermal images into the color channel values, namely the R, G, and 
B color channels (figure 3). The value of this color channel is related to the intensity of the radiation 
captured by the infrared sensor on the camera; the sensor then translates the radiation that hits the sensor 
surface into an electrical quantity. The electrical scale is then converted into a digital scale with a
