1. Introduction
Palm oil is one of Indonesia’s leading agricultural products [1, 2] that had 58% share in the global palm oil market
in 2019 [3, 4], according to the data released by the U.S. Department of Agriculture. Marketing must go through a
process of checking the maturity level of the palm based on changes in the color of the fruit skin [5]. The maturity
levels are grouped into 3 classes, namely ripe (red-orange), intermediate (red-black), and unripe (black) [6]. The
maturity of the palm affects the quality of the crude palm oil produced [7]. The process used to identify the maturity

Abstract
This study aims to classify the maturity level of oil palm using the K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) method based on the extraction of
RGB and L*a*b color features. This classification determines the optimal oil production based on the color of the palm. However,
this process is often carried out by humans, so sorting is not optimal because it is done manually, and it takes a long time. Thus,
we propose automatic detection using machine learning based approach. In this study, the image processing methods are used to
classify the level of oil palm maturity. The process starts with taking 150 photos with a smartphone. Each of them is then grouped
into 3 classes, namely ripe (50), intermediate (50), and unripe (50). The oil palm image is pre-processed using the segmentation
method (removing background), resizing, and cropping. Then, color features using RGB and L*a*b are extracted. Each of these
extracted color features becomes input in the training and testing process of the KNN algorithm and is validated using k-fold cross-
validation, with the results of accuracy being 95.4% and 97.4%. The combination of KNN and L*a*b color extraction approaches
has better accuracy than KNN and RGB in terms of model accuracy (by 2–4.8 percentage points).

to limited human vision during harvesting and sorting [12]. Based on this, several applications of computer vision and
image processing technology in grading the maturity of palm oil were proposed [6, 9, 12, 13, 14].
In image processing, the classification process uses input from the image feature extraction [15]. The color feature
extraction is a part of image processing [16] which is implemented as input in the classification process [15, 17].
RGB color feature extraction can classify the maturity level of oil palms with commonly available light (bright con-
ditions) [18, 19, 20]. Several mature oil palms detection methods have used color feature extraction concepts such as
RGB (red, green, blue) [21, 22, 23] and L*a*b [24, 25].
RGB color space is a popular method of color extraction, especially for images that have high resolution [22].
RGB in oil palm maturity classification is susceptible to light changes [21]. The tests were carried out both inside and
outside the box. The detection accuracy of palm ripeness inside the box with stable lighting was higher (92%) than
outside the box with direct sunlight (84%). The detection process is carried out directly without preprocessing, so
images with backgrounds are still subject to color feature extraction. Thus, we do preprocessing and segmentation to
separate the object from the background. In addition, RGB and L*a*b were used to detect the ripeness of fresh fruit
bunches of oil palm and comparison with other feature extraction methods showed that L*a*b had a relatively high
accuracy of 97.2% [26]. However, the reference in our research is that RGB has the accuracy of only 68.8%. In this
case, L*a*b approach ensures that the proposed model is not sensitive to light reflected by objects when compared
to RGB. Thus, our research applies RGB and L*a*b to compare and improve it by preprocessing and changing the
brightness. The previous research used the SVM (support vector machine) method in classification, so we propose to
use the KNN method [27].
In some cases, KNN is superior to SVM [28, 29, 30]. In the case of palm oil classification for several color regions
(Red, Green, Blue, NIR), KNN is superior to SVM [30] with an average difference of 7% accuracy. In addition, KNN
has a greater accuracy of 14% in classifying bananas, but requires a long processing time [28]. KNN can detect oil
palm fruit ripeness by using Sobel edge detection during preprocessing [31]. Thus, in the approach proposed in this
paper, we added preprocessing by resizing and cropping to speed up the image processing [32, 33].
This study focuses on classifying oil palm maturity based on feature extraction. The feature extraction procedure
is applied to the color obtained from the image preprocessing, which is used to improve the image quality. We present
this paper in four parts. The second section describes the methods and concepts we propose in this research, and the
experimental results obtained will be presented and discussed in the third section. Finally, we summarize the obtained
results in the fourth section.
This section describes several proposed approaches of classifying oil palm maturity, based on the concept of image
processing. The steps of the method proposed in this paper are presented in Fig. 1. The method uses classification
based on the results of image processing to produce the desired output. The image processing is carried out from the
preprocessing to segmentation, to obtain image objects from which color features will be extracted (RGB and L*a*b).
The results of the feature extraction will be the input to the KNN classification method, which will provide the results
of oil palm maturity classification (Unripe, Raw, and Ripe).
1.1. Image Processing
The initial stage of the image processing is image acquisition, which aims to obtain digital images (data) to be
processed. We collected a dataset using the Vivo Y15 smartphone camera, and the resulting image is a JPG file. The
resulting dataset included a total of 150 oil palm images, 50 for each category (unripe, raw, and ripe)—the samples
are shown in Fig. 2.
The dataset (Fig. 2) is then preprocessed using resizing and cropping [17]. The use of the resizing method is
intended to reduce and unify the size of the images, so that the subsequent image processing works faster than on the
original images. The images in the dataset used for this study are of various sizes, so they will be resized to 300 × 500
pixels. In contrast, cropping aims to detect certain parts of the image by cutting the image at specific coordinates.
The cropping results are certain parts of the image that are smaller than the original image [32]. The sample cropping
process is shown in Fig. 3.
As shown in Fig. 3(a), the cropped image is obtained based on a segmentation process, namely detecting image
objects by identifying background and oil palm objects. Once the object is detected, the image can be cropped to the

1.3. Image Classification
The image classification is a task in which an algorithm is trained to recognize and classify objects or scenes in
images [37, 38]. This is a common problem in both computer vision and image processing, and there are many ap-
proaches to solving it. This study uses the K-nearest neighbors (KNN) method, which is a machine-learning algorithm
for image classification [39, 40]. KNN is the most straightforward supervised learning algorithm. The classification
process of this algorithm refers to all stored data and uses similarities from new data points. Thus, a new data will be
categorized into a group according to the proximity of the distance. The KNN is a lazy learner because it does not
perform automatic learning during the training process, but uses distance of the datasets for classification. The new
data classification process can be seen in Fig. 5 [41].
Based on Fig. 5, KNN classifies new data using Euclidean distance calculations, as in Eq. (9) [17, 42, 43]. The
object distance (d(i, j)) from i to j in the p dimensional data is obtained from objects that are in i coordinate (xik) and
j coordinate (xjk) in the k dimension.

This section presents the experimental results of oil palm maturity classification using the image processing ap-
proach. The results obtained during the image processing and classification process will be analyzed and evaluated.
The image processing procedure produces images with each color processed by the feature extraction method so that
the results can be used for classification using the KNN method.

2.2. KNN Classification Results
The KNN classification process applies several scenarios for K values ranging from 1 to 9. The classification
process uses the ED (Euclidean Distance) calculated on new data from the dataset, according to the Eq. (9). The
accuracy of each K scenario that has been carried out for each color is shown in Fig. 6.
Fig. 6(a) shows the accuracy for different values of K for KNN with RGB feature extraction—the optimal value is
K=5 with an accuracy value of 97.77%. In contrast, the average accuracy of the KNN scenario calculation is 95.4%.
The accuracy of the RGB color model is affected by the object’s lighting, where the lowest accuracy is caused by the
lack of lighting during the acquisition process.
Fig. 6(b) shows the results of the KNN-L*a*b classification, with the best accuracy being 97.77% and the average
K value being 97.4%. The accuracy of KNN-L*a*b is greater than that of KNN-RGB because the calculation and
conversion process of the resulting RGB color is not affected by light.
2.3. K-fold Cross-Validation and Testing
This research uses K-Fold Cross Validation to validate the data on the model by splitting the data into two types,
namely training and validation [46]. In evaluating the performance of the machine learning method (KNN), the dataset
is divided into K-folds, where K-1 folds serve as training and the other as testing data. This process is repeated K
times, with different folds used for validation in each iteration. Then, the overall performance estimate is computed
by calculating the average value to get the model performance. This method divides data into several segments and
does not depend on the ratio [47]. This study uses the value of K = 5, so the calculated value can be seen in Table 4.

3. Conclusions
The combination of feature extraction and classification methods contributes to the research on oil palm maturity
identification. The proposed approach uses random data from the dataset (sampling) to train and test the model. KNN-
L*a*b approach obtained better model accuracy (by 2 percentage points) and validation results (by 4.8 percentage
points) than KNN-RGB. This is because RGB feature extraction is heavily influenced by lighting conditions, so
classification results may change if lighting changes.
Thus, based on the obtained results, we recommend the KNN-L*a*b method for classifying oil palm maturity,
which has accuracy of more than 97%, for a K value of 5. In the future, it would be necessary to apply other color
feature extraction methods such as YCbCr, HSV, YUV, and XYZ to find the optimal RGB color conversion. The
future work plans may also consider the use of some other machine learning methods for classification, additionally
supported by the application of bio-inspired agent-based algorithms for multi-modal optimization [48, 49, 50] or
multi-objective optimization [51, 52, 53, 54].
