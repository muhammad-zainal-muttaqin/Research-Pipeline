Abstract— Palm oil is a critical plant in the vegetable oil 
industry, with Indonesia as one of its largest contributors. To 
enhance the management and monitoring of palm oil plantations 
efficiently, remote sensing technology using drones (Unmanned 
Aerial Vehicles, UAVs) is increasingly used. UAV aerial imagery 
allows direct monitoring of palm tree conditions; however, the 
main challenge is accurately detecting palm trees amid varying 
environmental conditions such as changing lighting and 
overlapping vegetation. Therefore, object detection algorithms 
like You Only Look Once (YOLO) are often applied in related 
studies. YOLO is known for its ability to detect objects directly 
with high precision, making it ideal for monitoring the growth 
and distribution of palm trees over vast plantation areas. 
Previous studies have shown that the application of YOLO for 
palm tree detection yields good performance, although the 
challenge of distinguishing between palm trees and other species 
like dates and coconuts remains a significant issue. This study 
aims to provide a comprehensive literature review on the 
application of the YOLO algorithm in palm tree identification 
and analyze current research trends and challenges. Through 
keyword network visualization analysis, some main topics include 
"YOLO, RIPENESS, AND YOLO MODEL. The YOLO models 
used for detection are Yolov3, Yolov4, Yolov5, Yolov7, and 
Yolov8, which are not only for counting trees but also for 
classification. 
Keywords— YOLO, Palm Tree Detection, UAV Remote Sensing, 
Deep Learning, Systematic Literature Review. 
I. INTRODUCTION  
Palm oil is a vital commodity in the global vegetable oil 
industry, with Indonesia being one of the largest producers 
[1]. As an important contributor to the national economy, 
effective supervision and management of palm oil 
plantations are crucial[2]. Drones, as remote sensing tools, 
play a significant role in monitoring oil palm plantations 
by providing accurate, timely, and efficient data, thereby 
supporting sustainable agricultural practices[3].UAV-based 
aerial imagery enables more accurate, efficient, and cost-
effective monitoring, particularly for real-time observation 
of oil palm plantation conditions[2]. However, accurately 
detecting oil palm trees from aerial imagery remains 
challenging due to frequently changing environmental 
conditions, such as illumination variations, different 
viewing 
perspectives, 
and 
overlapping 
vegetation 
canopies. Therefore, object detection algorithms capable 
of achieving high accuracy and computational efficiency 
are required[4]. One of the most widely used algorithms 
for this purpose is You Only Look Once (YOLO), which is 
well known for its ability to perform real-time object 
detection with high accuracy[5]. YOLO has also been 
applied to analyze the growth and spatial distribution of 
oil palm trees across large plantation areas[6]. In previous 
studies, the palm oil industry has increasingly adopted 
remote sensing technologies for oil palm cultivation 
management[7]. Research and development efforts have 
explored various applications, including oil palm tree 
detection and age estimation, to support effective and 
sustainable plantation monitoring[8]. Machine learning 
and deep learning techniques have been widely employed 
to achieve these objectives. Since its introduction in 2016, 
the YOLO algorithm has continued to evolve, resulting in 
multiple 
variants 
designed 
to 
improve 
detection 
performance and efficiency[9]. Therefore, to understand 
the research development of YOLO-based approaches for 
oil palm tree detection, a bibliometric analysis was 
conducted[10]. This study also aims to systematically 
review existing literature on the application of YOLO for 
oil palm tree detection by analyzing trending keywords 
and research topics. 
II. METHODS 
A. Research Objective 
The objective of this research is to identify the main 
research topics related to oil palm tree detection using the 
YOLO algorithm and to determine which YOLO model 
variants have been most frequently used by researchers. 
This study also aims to review existing literature on oil palm 
tree detection using YOLO-based approaches and to analyze 
the trends in algorithm selection and application reported in 
previous studies.

III. BIBLIOMETRIC ANALYSIS 
This paper describes how bibliometric analysis is 
conducted as a systematic approach to examining scientific 
literature in order to identify research patterns and trends[2]. 
his technique is essential for gaining deeper insights into the 
application of the YOLO algorithm for oil palm tree 
detection. Bibliometric analysis is designed to trace research 
literature both quantitatively and qualitatively by examining 
elements such as publication characteristics, relationships 
among studies, thematic clusters, and emerging research 
trends[11]. This study reviews various bibliometric analysis 
tools, including Publish or Perish, and examines trends in 
bibliometric software research that facilitate researchers in 
accessing and extracting bibliometric data from multiple 
academic sources[10]. In addition, bibliometric mapping 
tools are utilized to construct and visualize bibliometric 
networks, such as co-citation and collaboration networks, 
which help illustrate the structure and development of 
research in this field[12]. VOSviewer is a software tool that 
integrates the VOS mapping technique with an advanced 
visualization interface for constructing and viewing 
bibliometric maps[13]. It is specifically designed to support 
graphical representation and to efficiently handle large 
bibliometric datasets[14]. VOSviewer can be used to 
construct and visualize various types of bibliometric 
networks, 
including 
co-citation 
and 
collaboration 
networks[15]. In conducting the bibliometric analysis, this 
study follows five main stages: (1) literature search, (2) 
literature selection, (3) classification and categorization, (4) 
bibliometric analysis, and (5) synthesis and visualization of 
results [21].

A. Systematic Literature Analysis 
Based on the results of the bibliometric analysis, it was 
found that research on oil palm tree detection is still 
relatively limited, making it a suitable and relevant topic for 
further investigation through a systematic literature review. 
B.  Systematic Literature Review Protocol 
This study follows a simplified Systematic Literature 
Review (SLR) protocol to ensure transparency and 
reproducibility. The literature search was conducted using 
Google Scholar, Scopus, and IEEE Xplore databases. The 
search period covered studies published between 2016 and 
2025, corresponding to the development timeline of YOLO-
based object detection. 
The main search string used was: 
("YOLO" OR "You Only Look Once") AND ("oil palm" OR 
"palm oil tree") AND ("UAV" OR "remote sensing"). 
Inclusion criteria were: (1) peer-reviewed journal or 
conference papers, (2) studies applying YOLO-based 
models for oil palm detection or related tasks, and (3) 
articles written in English. 
Exclusion criteria included: (1) non-palm-related object 
detection studies, (2) non-technical or conceptual papers, 
and (3) duplicate publications. 
The study selection process consisted of title and abstract 
screening followed by full-text review. The final set of 
selected studies was then analyzed and synthesized 
qualitatively to identify research trends, challenges, and 
gaps.

IV. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
A. Bibliometric Analysis 
The secondary data used was collected through Harzing's 
Publish or Perish (POP) application. This data comes from 
publishers like Google Scholar and Scopus over the last five 
years, from 2020 to 2025. The search included up to 1000 
keywords with the keyword "YOLO in Oil Palm Detection". 
The collected data was then saved in an RIS file (Research 
Information Systems) on the POP application. The RIS-
formatted data was then analyzed using VOSviewer 
software. VOSviewer analyzes text data from metadata in 
RIS format. Abstracts and titles indicate the field to be 
summarized. The following chart illustrates the process of 
collecting article data for analysis.

mapping, and research density mapping—will be provided 
as a result of the VOSviewer program analysis. Based on

least ten times and formed four clusters of items. Of these

D. Overlay Visualization 
Each network relationship shows a different color in the 
overlay visualization. All items have a value; blue indicates 
the lowest value, green indicates the highest value, and 
yellow indicates the highest value. If an item does not have 
a value, the overlay visualization cannot be accessed. The 
colors indicate the influence of a journal. For example, blue 
color has an influence factor below 1, green has an influence 
factor of 2, and yellow has an influence factor greater than 
3. A higher score for each item indicates the journal's 
influence on the research topic.

F. Limitations of YOLO-Based Oil Palm Detection 
Even though YOLO-based detection is efficient, it 
encounters several hurdles when applied to oil palm 
scenarios. A significant issue is differentiating oil palm trees 
from similar-looking species, such as coconut or date palm 
trees, especially in areas with mixed vegetation. The dense 
overlap of canopies and the presence of trees of various ages 
add further complexity to achieving accurate detection. 
Moreover, variations in lighting, shadows, and differences 
between plantation types (such as estate versus smallholder) 
can affect the model's ability to generalize. These challenges 
suggest that despite YOLO's effectiveness for real-time 
detection, implementing additional strategies like data 
augmentation, domain adaptation, or combining with 
segmentation methods may be necessary. 
G. Future Research Directions 
Future research might investigate the combination of object 
detection and segmentation methods to enhance the 
precision of canopy delineation. Domain adaptation 
strategies and multi-task learning models have the potential 
to boost robustness when applied to various plantation 
settings. Additionally, lightweight versions of YOLO, 
tailored for edge processing or on-drone use, represent an 
exciting avenue for real-time monitoring of plantations.
