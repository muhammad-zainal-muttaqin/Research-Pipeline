Abstract—Tomatoes are a widely cultivated crop with 
various applications in the food industry. Automating the 
process of detecting and counting tomatoes during different 
growth stages is crucial for optimizing farming practices, 
enhancing harvesting efficiency, ensuring quality control, and 
streamlining food processing and distribution. In this paper, a 
mechanism for tomato detection and counting using YOLOv8 
is presented. The proposed system is able to identify tomatoes 
within images or video frames, localize their positions with 
bounding boxes, and provide an accurate count of tomatoes in 
real time. The custom model is evaluated for average precision, 
accuracy, and loss. The challenges faced are apples are false 
positively identified as tomatoes. The objective is to use the 
model by integrating it into different applications, such as 
smart framing and quality control.  
Keywords—YOLOv8, SORT, object detection, IoU. 
I. INTRODUCTION 
Tomato detection is essential across the tomato 
production and distribution chain, contributing to efficiency, 
quality control, and informed decision-making in agriculture, 
food processing, retail, and research sectors. Tomatoes are 
classified based on their size and shape. There are various 
types of tomatoes, and their size can vary widely. The 
different variations are cherry tomatoes, grape tomatoes, 
plum tomatoes, standard or medium tomatoes, and large or 
beefsteak tomatoes [17]. Tomatoes contain vital nutrients 
such as lycopene, carotenoids, vitamin C, potassium, and 
fiber in a nutritionally dense package and have various health 
benefits [15]. In Fig 1, the tomato production in India is 
represented state-wise, with Andhra Pradesh contributing 
16.22 %. The application of tomato detection and counting in 
real-time scenarios contributes significantly to efficiency, 
quality control, and decision-making processes across 
various industries involved in tomato production, processing, 
distribution, and retail.

II. RELATED WORK 
In [1], the authors introduce a novel approach to object 
detection using You Only Look Once (YOLO) architecture. 
It reframes object detection as a regression problem and 
predicts 
the 
bounding

introduce YOLO9000, a real-time object detection system 
that identifies over 9000 object categories. YOLOv2 
achieves high mAP (mean average precision) rates, 
surpassing Faster R-CNN (Region-based Convolutional 
Neural Network) and single-shot detection (SSD) while 
maintaining impressive speed. In [3], the authors introduce 
YOLOv3, an updated version with design enhancements and 
improved accuracy while maintaining speed. Despite being 
slightly larger, YOLOv3 achieves 28.2mAP in 22ms at 
320x320 resolution, matching SSD accuracy but three times 
faster. In [4], the authors introduce a CNN-based architecture 
for detecting defective apples on a fruit sorting machine. In 
[5], the classification of ladies' fingers (okras) is performed 
using a deep learning model based on the size of the okras. In 
[6], the authors present an enhanced fruit detection method 
for tomatoes in greenhouses using an improved YOLOv4-
tiny model. It incorporates modifications to the backbone 
network, introducing a more efficient feature extraction 
process while reducing computational complexity. In [7], the 
YOLOv3 model is proposed to identify ripe and unripe 
tomatoes. The limitation it overcomes is small size tomatoes 
are considered for identification, by modifying the activation 
function to Mish activation. The missed detections are 
avoided by using spatial pyramid pooling (SPP). In [8], the 
authors employ an enhanced HSV (Hue, Saturation, and 
Value or brightness) color space and refined watershed 
segmentation to detect ripe tomatoes accurately. The process 
involves initial background removal and identification of red 
tomatoes using improved HSV transformation, followed by 
morphological operations to refine the fruit detection. 
Subsequently, a modified watershed algorithm separates 
connected fruits, facilitating the harvesting robot's precise 
picking of the ripe tomatoes. In [9], the authors introduce

(CNNs) for automating tomato fruit detection in farming.

immature, and damaged cherry tomatoes, the YOLOv5m + 
BoTNet model achieves high detection accuracies. In [10], 
the authors introduce CAM-YOLO, an enhanced YOLOv5 
algorithm tailored for challenging tomato detection scenarios, 
addressing issues like complex backgrounds and partial 
occlusion. Integrating the Convolutional Block Attention

III. APPLICATIONS OF TOMATO DETECTION AND COUNTING 
Tomato detection and counting find application in 
various real-time scenarios across different industries, some 
instances where tomato detection and counting are applied in 
real-time applications are: 
A. Agriculture and Farming: 
• Harvesting Automation: Automated tomato detection 
helps in robotic systems or machinery that harvests 
ripe tomatoes by identifying and counting them. This 
streamlines harvesting processes, reducing manual 
labor and increasing efficiency. 
• Crop Monitoring and Yield Estimation: Real-time 
counting and detection assist in monitoring tomato 
crops' growth stages, estimating yields, and assessing 
crop health. This information aids in making timely 
decisions related to irrigation, fertilization, or pest 
control. 
• Quality Inspection: Automated systems can detect and 
count tomatoes while simultaneously assessing their 
quality parameters like size, shape, color, and 
ripeness. This ensures that only high-quality tomatoes 
are selected for packaging and distribution. 
B. Food Processing and Sorting: 
• Sorting and Grading: Tomato detection helps in 
sorting and grading systems within food processing 
plants. It segregates tomatoes based on their size, 
color, and quality, ensuring uniformity in packaging. 
• Quality Control in Packing Lines: Real-time detection 
ensures that only the specified number of tomatoes 
per package are included, maintaining consistency 
and meeting industry standards. 
C. Retail and Inventory Management: 
• Inventory Control: Automated counting aids in 
inventory management within warehouses or retail 
stores, accurately tracking the number of tomatoes in 
stock. This information helps maintain optimal stock 
levels.. 
• Price Determination: Real-time counting might assist 
in determining pricing based on the number and 
quality of tomatoes, facilitating efficient pricing 
strategies.

IV. OBJECT DETECTION USING YOLO 
Object detection in an image or a video frame includes a 
classification mechanism to classify the object and a 
regression technique to predict the bounding box for the 
object using the ground truth data. The YOLOv8 (You Only 
Look Once version 8) model, as presented in Fig 2a, utilizes 
the following techniques to perform object detection on an 
input image. 
• Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs): YOLOv8 
heavily relies on deep CNNs, such as Darknet, as its 
backbone network. These networks involve numerous 
layers of convolutions (mathematical operations 
involving 
kernel 
convolutions 
and 
activation 
functions like ReLU) to extract hierarchical features 
from input images. 
• Grid-based Detection: YOLOv8 divides the input 
image into a grid and performs detection at each grid 
cell. It predicts bounding boxes and class probabilities 
for objects within these grid cells. YOLOv8 divides 
the input image into an S×S grid, where S represents 
the number of cells along each dimension of the 
image; for each grid cell at position (i, j), the model 
predicts: 
• Bounding box coordinates (x,y,w,h) relative to the 
cell's boundaries, where (x,y) represents  the center 
coordinates of the predicted bounding box and (w,h) 
denotes the width and height of the bounding box as 
presented in Fig 3 for different tomatoes sizes. 
• Confidence score (Conf) indicating the presence of an 
object within the cell.  
• Class probabilities (P(classi|object)) for different 
classes. 
• Spatial Pyramid Pooling with Fusion (SPPF) is a 
technique used to handle input images of varying 
sizes and aspect ratios without the need for resizing or 
cropping. This technique divides the input feature 
map into a grid and computes pooling operations in 
each grid cell at multiple scales. The results from all 
levels are then flattened, and the concatenated vector 
is used as a fixed-size representation of the input 
regardless of its original size. 
• Loss Functions: YOLOv8 employs various loss 
functions like the localization loss (to minimize 
bounding box localization error) and the confidence 
loss (to measure objectness scores) calculated using 
Mean Squared Error (MSE) or Binary Cross-Entropy 
(BCE) loss. 
• Mean Squared Error (MSE) Loss: The MSE loss 
measures 
the 
discrepancy 
between 
predicted 
bounding 
box 
coordinates 
and 
ground 
truth 
annotations. 
• Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) Loss: The confidence 
loss evaluates the 
model's 
confidence 
scores 
(objectness) for predicting the presence of objects 
within grid cells. 
• Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS): After predicting 
bounding boxes, YOLOv8 utilizes NMS to select the 
most confident bounding boxes while suppressing 
highly overlapping ones. NMS is a technique used to 
filter out redundant or highly overlapping bounding 
boxes based on their confidence scores. 
• Post-processing involves filtering out low-confidence 
detections based on a predefined threshold.  
V. METHODOLOGY 
In the proposed system as presented in Fig 2, the custom 
model is built and trained to detect and count the number of 
tomatoes in the given image or video using the YOLOv8 
architecture. The problem statement is, “To develop tomato 
detection and counting system using YOLOv8, capable of 
identifying tomatoes within images or video frames, 
localizing their positions with bounding boxes, and providing 
an accurate count of tomatoes in real-time”. The objectives 
of the proposed system are as follows: 
1. To detect tomatoes within images or video frames using 
the YOLOv8 model. 
2. To provide accurate bounding boxes around individual 
tomatoes. 
3. To count the number of detected tomatoes in real-time.

VI. OBJECT TRACKING AND COUNTING 
The SORT (Simple Online and Realtime Tracking) 
algorithm is used to track the multiple tomatoes in videos. 
The SORT algorithm is combined with an object detection 
mechanism for counting the tomatoes in a video sequence. 
The Kalman Filter is applied to enhance the tomato tracking 
predictions.  
Data Collection and Preprocessing: The video containing 
tomatoes is given as the input to the pre-trained Yolov8 
model. 
Object Detection: The tomato detection is performed 
using the YOLO algorithm on the first frame. The bounding 
box coordinates and class probabilities for each detected 
tomato are extracted. 
SORT Initialization: Initialize the SORT tracker with the 
bounding box coordinates of the detected tomatoes from the 
first frame. SORT uses a combination of a Kalman Filter for 
object prediction and a Hungarian Algorithm for data 
association to track objects across frames. 
Tracking and Counting: The subsequent frames are 
processed by applying object detection on each frame to 
detect tomatoes. The bounding box coordinates for the 
detected tomatoes are obtained next. These are given as input 
to the SORT tracker. SORT will associate the detected 
tomatoes with existing tracks or create new tracks for 
unassociated detections. The state estimation is updated for 
position, velocity, and direction for each tracked tomato 
using the Kalman Filter. The count is incremented whenever 
a new tomato is identified.

The YOLOv8n model is used with 225 layers, 3157200 
parameters, 3157184 gradients, and 8.9 Giga Floating Point 
Operations Per Second (GFLOPs) for evaluating object 
detection. The Fig 15, shows the different evaluation metrics 
used to assess the performance of the model on the coco128 
dataset. The box loss measures the difference between the 
predicted bounding box coordinates and the ground truth 
bounding box coordinates. The classification loss measures 
the difference between the predicted class probabilities and 
the true class labels. The box loss, classification 
loss, distribution focal loss (dfl), precision, recall, and mAP 
scores for ten epochs are presented in Fig 15. The mAP50-95 
calculates the mean Average Precision over a range of 
confidence thresholds from 50% to 95%. The classification 
process is optimized  using focal loss, and location of the box  
is learnt by Dirac delta distribution [24]. These losses are 
combined to form the overall loss function during training. 
The total loss is then backpropagated through the network, 
and the model's parameters are updated to minimize this 
combined loss, improving the model's performance in both 
bounding box regression and object classification.

VIII. CONCLUSION 
Tomato detection and counting enhance farming practices 
by providing accurate yield estimation, crop monitoring, and 
food processing and distribution quality control. The 
YOLOv8 model architecture is used to detect the tomatoes. 
The custom data set is generated, and the model is trained 
and tested for tomato detection. The object tracking is 
performed in the event of video input, and the count is 
obtained in real-time. The parameters taken for evaluation of 
the model are average precision, IoU, accuracy, and loss. The 
future plan is to integrate with the Internet of Things (IoT), 
which can be used in smart farming.
