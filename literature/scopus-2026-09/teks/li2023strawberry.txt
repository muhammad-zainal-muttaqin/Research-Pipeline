A B S T R A C T  
Computer technology's rapid advancement has significantly enhanced global agricultural modernization, notably 
improving agricultural production efficiency. Given the vulnerability of strawberries during the harvesting 
process, the automatic harvesting technology for strawberries necessitates a highly accurate recognition algo­
rithm. In this paper, we introduce a model, Strawberry R-CNN, designed for intelligent recognition and counting 
of strawberries in natural environments. The Strawberry R-CNN model enhances strawberry recognition accu­
racy by refining the Faster R-CNN through several key modifications. Firstly, we replaced the VGG16 in the 
original Faster R-CNN with an improved multi-cascade network structure for feature extraction. This change 
allows for the capture of rich location data and detailed information typically absent in higher-level features. 
Secondly, the RoiPooling operation was replaced with RoiAlign to eliminate the error associated with the 
rounding method in two quantization stages. Lastly, we utilized a bilinear interpolation method for computation, 
preserving the floating-point number, and reducing model error. For strawberry counting, we proposed an 
efficient and practical evaluation method by creating an error set for strawberry counting. Experimental results 
demonstrated that the Strawberry R-CNN model achieved an average precision (AP) of 0.9019 for ripe straw­
berries and 0.8447 for immature ones, with a mean average precision (mAP) of 0.8733. The counting accuracy 
for ripe and immature strawberries was 99.1% and 73.7% respectively. The method presented in this work 
exhibits strong detection and counting capabilities, suitable for automatic monitoring, harvesting, and yield 
estimation of strawberries.   
1. Introduction 
Strawberries are among the most globally cultivated fruits (Sønsteby 
and Heide, 2017). In China, strawberries have gained significant 
prominence, holding the top rank in annual production worldwide (Lei 
et al., 2021). The European market also experiences a substantial de­
mand for strawberries, leading numerous firms to expand their planta­
tions to meet this need. However, the swift growth of the worldwide 
strawberry cultivation industry has brought about labor scarcity issues, 
particularly in the harvesting process. In Japan, strawberry harvesting 
consumes a quarter of the working hours dedicated to the strawberry 
industry (Yamamoto et al., 2014). In Norway, the costs related to 
strawberry picking account for 40–60% of total labor costs (Xiong et al., 
2018). Moreover, in China, the aging labor force combined with the 
labor-intensive nature of the strawberry industry - characterized by high 
input and high output - results in an annual labor shortage and esca­
lating labor costs during the strawberry planting and harvesting seasons. 
Consequently, it is imperative to develop automatic monitoring and 
harvesting systems to enhance the efficiency of strawberry picking and 
reduce reliance on manual labor costs. 
The first step towards achieving automated strawberry harvesting is 
the accurate positioning and identification of strawberries. The evolu­
tion of this technology has seen progress from classifying strawberry size 
to color differentiation (Liming and Yanchao, 2010; Oo and Aung, 
2018), and subsequently to pinpointing strawberry location (Girshick 
et al., 2014). Most recently, developments have emerged in achieving 
both strawberry location identification and classification (Habar­
agamuwa et al., 2018), indicating the gradual maturation of automatic 
strawberry harvesting technology. 
The rapid progression of target detection technology has spurred its 
application in agriculture, with numerous techniques now deployed in 
the identification and detection of fruits and vegetables (Bai et al., 2022; 
Rao et al., 2021). For instance, Wang et al., (Wang et al., 2023) refined 
the Faster R-CNN by integrating an attention mechanism, enabling the

Numerous studies have been undertaken on strawberry image

sketching lines on strawberry images and classifying them using K- 
means clustering methods (Hartigan and Wong, 1979). Their results

exceeding 90%. In another study, Isaac et al. improved the Mask R-CNN

reducing complexity without compromising performance (Perez-Bor­

exceeding 86% (Zhou et al., 2020). Harshana et al. introduced a deep

unripe strawberries respectively. In a recent study, Wang et al. proposed 
the DSE-YOLO algorithm (Wang et al., 2022) for detecting different 
stages of strawberry growth, reaching a mean average precision (mAP) 
of 86.58. 
While the current recognition rate of ripe strawberries is relatively 
high, the accurate identification of unripe strawberries remains chal­
lenging and necessitates further improvement. This is because precise 
identification of immature strawberries provides insights into their 
growth status, facilitating better planning for the harvesting process. 
Furthermore, this identification is crucial for strawberry counting and 
yield estimation. Therefore, the recognition model requires further 
refinement to enhance its performance, meeting the demands of real- 
time monitoring and automated harvesting. Additionally, there is a 
current deficiency in effective evaluation methods for strawberry 
counting. Taking these factors into account, this paper makes the 
following key contributions:  
(1) We developed the Strawberry R-CNN model to enable automatic 
recognition and counting of strawberries in natural environ­
ments. An effective evaluation method was proposed, which in­
volves establishing an error set for strawberry counting.  
(2) To capture rich location data and detailed information typically 
absent in high-level features, we designed a multi-cascaded 
network structure for backbone feature extraction in the Straw­
berry R-CNN model.  
(3) RoiAlign was used in place of RoiPooling from the original Faster 
R-CNN model, making our model more adept at detecting small 
target objects and thereby improving the detection accuracy of 
the Strawberry R-CNN model.  
(4) We compiled a strawberry dataset comprising 9084 images, with 
1514 source images collected from natural field environments 
and 7570 images amplified. 
(5) Using the Strawberry R-CNN model as a foundation, we devel­
oped front-end applications and a back-end online strawberry 
recognition platform. These can be implemented for real-time 
strawberry positioning by strawberry harvesting robots and 
real-time monitoring of strawberry growth by growers.  
(6) We compared the recognition effects between the original model 
and its various improvements through ablation experiments, 
visualizing the enhancements rendered by the modifications and 
the final model's performance.  
(7) We reorganized the validation set in the dataset by selecting 
images with complex environments and strong lighting to form 
two new types of validation sets. The trained models were tested 
in these challenging environments to evaluate whether the model 
performance would be significantly impacted. 
2. Related work 
Vision-based methods are increasingly employed in strawberry 
identification, contributing to the advancement of agricultural 
modernization. These methods encompass image classification, target 
detection, and image segmentation. A review of existing literature re­
veals that each vision method possesses unique features and varying 
degrees of effectiveness. In recent years, the continual development of 
computer algorithms (Hinton and Salakhutdinov, 2006; LeCun et al., 
2015) and hardware has facilitated rapid advancements in machine 
vision techniques, such as image classification and target detection, 
propelled by various deep learning algorithms. Broadly, target detection 
can be categorized into two primary frameworks: two-stage detection 
frameworks and single-stage detection frameworks (Liu et al., 2020). 
2.1. Two-stage detection framework 
A two-stage framework initially extracts regions of interest from the 
input image. Subsequently, a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) 
(Krizhevsky et al., 2017) extracts features from these regions, leading to 
the identification of each class through a multi-classification Support 
Vector Machine (SVM). Several renowned two-stage detection frame­
works exist, such as the RCNN (Girshick et al., 2014, 2015), developed 
by Girshick et al., which employs CNNs for feature extraction from 
candidate regions and reduces task size through transfer learning. 
However, it suffers from slow processing speed and a complex training 
process. Girshick et al. improved upon the RCNN and Spatial Pyramid 
Pooling Network (SPPNet) (He et al., 2015) to propose the Fast R-CNN, 
which offers enhanced speed and efficiency. The Faster R-CNN frame­
work, proposed by Ren et al. (Ren et al., 2015), replaces traditional 
candidate region generation methods with convolutional operations, 
thus enhancing the overall performance. Zhou et al. (Zhou et al., 2020) 
refined the Faster R-CNN through transfer learning and used it for 
strawberry recognition, achieving a recognition accuracy of 86%. Con­
rady et al. (Conrady et al., 2022) utilized Mask R-CNN to detect and 
classify Roman snapper, achieving a final mean average precision (mAP) 
of 81.45%. Liu et al. (Liu and Wang, 2021) embedded convolutional 
kernel adaptive selection units in the backbone feature extraction 
network of the Faster R-CNN and incorporated other improvements, 
thereby increasing the model's accuracy for detecting benthic organisms 
from 93.25% to 96.32%. 
2.2. Single stage detection framework 
The single-stage detection framework entirely eliminates the region 
proposal stage, enabling the completion of localization and classification 
tasks in one stage via a single network that encompasses all operations. 
For instance, Redmon et al. proposed the YOLO (Redmon et al., 2016), 
which discards the region proposal generation phase, utilizing the global 
features of the entire image for foreground and background classifica­
tion learning. The single network output of YOLO contains both target 
location and category. Liu et al. (Liu et al., 2016a) introduced the SSD 
framework, which amalgamates the features of Faster R-CNN and YOLO, 
offering higher detection accuracy and faster detection speed. Cui et al. 
(Cui et al., 2021) employed YOLOv4 for strawberry recognition and 
made lightweight improvements to YOLOv4 to enhance training effi­
ciency using transfer learning and staged training methods. Xu et al. (Xu 
et al., 2023) designed MAD-YOLO, incorporating VOV-DarkNet as a

2.3. Approaches to model improvement 
While the aforementioned studies have addressed issues such as the 
low recognition rate of unripe strawberries and the challenge of iden­
tifying strawberry fruits in complex environments independently, none 
have combined multiple aspects of the real-world scenario, including 
target recognition, complex environments, recognition of ripe and un­
ripe states, and strawberry fruit counting. 
In strawberry recognition, unripe strawberries typically pose a sig­
nificant challenge due to their small size and their color, which closely 
resembles that of leaves, making accurate detection difficult. To enhance 
the detection accuracy of strawberries in complex environments, this 
paper employs the Faster R-CNN algorithm. This model is further refined 
by incorporating specific challenges associated with strawberry recog­
nition, resulting in a target detection model optimally suited for iden­
tifying strawberries. 
3. Materials and methods 
3.1. Materials 
3.1.1. Image acquisition 
Our study utilized a total of 1514 strawberry images captured in an 
actual field environment, as illustrated in Fig. 1. These images were 
taken at an organic strawberry planting base located in Longhu Moun­
tain, Qingdao City, Shandong Province, China. All images were shot 
using a smartphone, boasting a resolution of 4608 × 3456 and saved in 
the JPG image format. During the photography process, the camera was 
positioned approximately 0.2–0.3 m from the strawberries. The original 
dataset used in our study can be accessed at https://github.com/Jerr 
yLeeSir/Strawberry-recognition/tree/master /Data%20set. 
3.1.2. Image processing 
The collected images were adjusted to an aspect ratio of 666:500 to 
conform to the input requirements of the Strawberry R-CNN model. To 
improve the model's training effectiveness, the strawberry image dataset 
was augmented using various image enhancement techniques. Each 
image underwent five different transformation methods: mirror flip, up- 
and-down flip, Gaussian blur, random sampling, and affine trans­
formation, as depicted in Fig. 2. 
3.1.3. Data labeling 
Following the data enhancement, we amassed a strawberry dataset 
encompassing 9084 images. These images were uniformly named, and 
ripe as well as immature strawberries within each image were labeled 
using LabelImg annotation software. This study employed the PASCAL 
VOC2007 dataset format, with an illustrative example of image labeling 
presented in Fig. 3. 
3.1.4. Data set partition 
The 9084 images were randomly partitioned into three sets: training, 
validation, and testing, maintaining a ratio of 6:2:2, as detailed in 
Table 1. 
As depicted in Fig. 4, the original image and its five augmented 
variations exhibit high similarity. Therefore, during dataset division, we 
ensured that the original image and its augmented counterparts belong 
to the same set, whether training, validation, or testing. This approach 
enhances the model's generalization capacity, thereby ensuring its uni­
versality. TTA (Shanmugam et al., 2021) was shown to improve the 
model prediction results, so we performed data augmentation on the test 
set as well. 
3.2. Methods 
3.2.1. Faster R-CNN 
Introduced in 2015, Faster R-CNN emerged as a leading object 
detection algorithm, securing top positions in both the ILSVRV and 
COCO competitions that year (Ren et al., 2015). The algorithm builds 
upon Fast R-CNN by incorporating an RPN candidate frame generation 
algorithm (Girshick, 2015), significantly accelerating object detection. 
Fig. 6 delineates the detailed network architecture of Faster R-CNN. 
Prior to exploring the structure of Faster R-CNN, we delve into the 
architecture of VGG16. As delineated in Fig. 5, VGG16 is composed of 13 
convolutional layers, 3 fully connected layers, and 5 pooling layers - the 
convolutional and fully connected layers often referred to as weight 
layers. Parameter transmission within this neural network occurs from 
left to right, progressing into deeper channels and reducing in size until 
they are flattened into a singular 1 × 1 vector for the fully connected 
layer. 
In Faster R-CNN, VGG16 serves as the backbone feature extraction 
network, employed to extract features from the entire strawberry image 
while retaining only the convolutional layer and discarding the fully 
connected layer. The resultant feature images are inputted into the 
enhanced Region Proposal Network (RPN) and ROIPooling respectively. 
The classification network then classifies the candidate regions gener­
ated by the RPN (Zhang et al., 2022b), further refines the coordinates of 
these candidate regions, and eventually outputs the detection results. 
3.2.2. Strawberry R-CNN detection model 
We adapted Faster R-CNN to enhance its suitability for strawberry 
recognition, leading to the development of Strawberry R-CNN. This 
section delineates the network architecture of Strawberry R-CNN, along 
with our modifications to Faster R-CNN. Instead of the VGG utilized in 
Faster R-CNN, we engineered an improved multi-cascade network 
structure for feature extraction. Additionally, we replaced RoiPooling 
with RoiAligns in the original model, thereby mitigating the errors 
induced by rounding operations in double quantization.  
(1) Multi-cascade convolutional network 
Convolutional networks systematically broaden the field of view 
from input to output, thereby extracting higher-level semantic features. 
While lower-level features offer high resolution with abundant location 
and detail information, they possess fewer semantics and more noise due 
to limited convolutional operations. Conversely, higher-level features 
carry richer semantic information but suffer from low resolution and 
poor detail perception (Poudel et al., 2018).

In the context of strawberry recognition, unripe strawberries are 
typically small and their color closely resembles that of the leaves. Given 
these attributes, green unripe strawberries may be misidentified as noise 
akin to the leaves during the convolutional network's transition from 
shallow to deep layers, causing the loss of unripe strawberry information 
in the high semantic features. 
To address this issue, we developed an improved multi-cascade 
network structure for feature extraction within the Strawberry R-CNN 
model. This enhanced structure has proven to boost the detection ac­
curacy of smaller objects. As depicted in Fig. 7, two shallow networks 
were integrated into the original model, each comprising five 5 × 5 
convolutional kernels and three 7 × 7 convolutional kernels. It is crucial 
that each cascade network includes an identical number of pooling 
layers to ensure the feature maps used for fusion are of the same size 
(Zhou et al., 2019), thereby generating new feature maps. Furthermore, 
the final output from the two shallow networks matches the size of the 
original model's output but offers a higher resolution and more detailed 
information.  
(2) Pooling based on RoIAlign 
In practical strawberry cultivation settings, some strawberries, 
particularly the immature ones, grow small and densely, posing sub­
stantial challenges to strawberry detection. 
Within Faster R-CNN, ROIPooling maps the candidate frames 
generated by RPN onto the feature map, thereby creating a fixed-sized 
region of interest on the feature map. Consider a candidate frame of 
size 665 × 665 in the strawberry image and a feat_stride of 32 in VGG16. 
The size mapped to the shared feature map would be 20.78 × 20.78. 
During computation, a rounding operation is carried out, resulting in a 
feature map size of 20 × 20 after the first quantization. Following 
ROIPooling, the feature map size is fixed at 7 × 7. The 20 × 20 feature 
map is divided into 7 × 7 equally-sized smaller regions, each with a side 
length of 20 /7 = 2.85. After the second quantization, the feature map 
size becomes 2 × 2. Subsequently, the maximum pixel value in every 
small area represents the area, forming the 7 × 7 feature map. These two 
rounding operations may introduce errors that could impact the net­
work's positioning accuracy in later stages, thereby negatively affecting 
the detection of small immature strawberries. 
ROIAlign, introduced in 2017(He et al., 2017), effectively addresses 
the region mismatch issue caused by dual quantifications in ROI Pooling 
operations. As depicted in Fig. 8, ROI Align employs bilinear interpo­
lation to replace ROI Pooling's rounding operation, thereby preserving 
floating-point numbers. Assuming there are four sampling points, each 
2.972.97 small area is divided into four equal parts, with each part 
serving as the centroid position. By extension, 49 small regions produce 
49-pixel values, forming a 77 size feature map, which offers superior 
accuracy. As a result, in this study, we adopted ROIAlign over ROI 
Pooling to achieve more precise recognition results.

CNN, we conducted a comparative analysis between Strawberry R-CNN 
and Faster R-CNN across diverse perspectives. Fig. 10 illustrates the loss 
curves (Ni et al., 2020) of both models after 1000 training iterations. 
As observed, the Strawberry R-CNN model converges to a lower 
value of 0.5 after approximately 4000 training iterations, while the 
Faster R-CNN model reaches the same value after 6000 iterations. This 
comparison underscores that the Strawberry R-CNN model achieves 
faster and superior convergence than the Faster R-CNN.

4.2. Performance in complex environments 
In uncluttered or straightforward environments, the detection per­
formance of Strawberry R-CNN closely mirrors that of Faster R-CNN, as 
illustrated in Fig. 12. Both models adeptly identify strawberries without 
errors or omissions, demonstrating relatively high recognition accuracy. 
In more complex environments, the superiority of Strawberry R-CNN 
becomes apparent, as depicted in Fig. 13. In Fig. 13a, the Faster R-CNN 
misidentifies a leaf as an unripe strawberry, a mistake also evident in 
Fig. 13b and c. Similarly, it confuses a finger for an unripe strawberry in 
Fig. 13d, and soil for unripe strawberries in Fig. 13e and f. Such 
recognition errors are seldom observed in Strawberry R-CNN, as 
demonstrated in Figs. 13g to l. This underlines that implementing the 
cascade network can enhance the distinction between unripe straw­
berries and background elements, thereby improving the model's ability 
to recognize unripe strawberries. 
The incorporation of RoIAlign, by eliminating the quantization 
operation, has enhanced the accuracy of Strawberry R-CNN. As pre­
sented in Table 4, the precision for ripe strawberries has achieved a score 
of 0.9769, surpassing the Faster R-CNN by 0.005. Similarly, the preci­
sion for unripe strawberries has reached 0.8019, exceeding the Faster R- 
CNN by 0.1239. 
4.3. Performance of different light environments 
The quality of strawberry images and the efficiency of the detection 
model can be influenced by variables such as the shooting time and 
location of the strawberries, which may result in the occurrence of

5. Discussion 
5.1. Model architecture 
In response to the challenge of low detection accuracy of straw­
berries in natural environments, we put forward a strawberry detection 
method that incorporates a cascade network composed of two shallow 
networks into Faster R-CNN. This approach retains rich location and 
detail information, enhancing the overall target detection performance. 
We also replaced Roi Pooling in the original model with RoiAligns to 
eliminate the error induced by two quantization rounding operations, 
employing a bilinear interpolation method for the computation to retain 
floating-point numbers and reduce model error. Sun et al. utilized the 
cascade network in their research on vascular plaque identification from 
OTC images, and Li et al. adopted RoiAligns approaches in their inves­
tigation of synthetic aperture radar target detection (Li et al., 2020; Sun 
et al., 2021), both yielding significantly improved recognition results.

5.2. Issue of identifying immature strawberries 
The recognition of immature strawberries poses a greater challenge 
compared to mature strawberries due to the similarity in color between 
immature strawberries and strawberry branches and leaves. He et al. 
utilized YOLOv4 for strawberry detection (He et al., 2022), achieving an 
Average Precision (AP) value of 91.73 for ripe strawberries and 87.71 
for immature strawberries, indicating a noticeable challenge in recog­
nizing immature strawberries. Among immature strawberries, some 
newly formed strawberries are extremely small, as depicted in Fig. 17, 
causing some disruption to the recognition and counting process. In a 
study by Xu et al. on tea bud identification (Xu et al., 2022c), there were 
instances where some tea buds could not be identified, primarily due to 
the color match with the leaves. 
While both immature strawberries and leaves share a green hue, 
strawberry fruit exhibits a lighter green compared to the darker green of 
branches and leaves. Furthermore, branches and leaves differ in shape 
from the strawberry fruit. In this study, we enhanced the model's 
recognition performance by incorporating a cascading network. Future 
efforts will aim to increase the number of training iterations and the 
quantity of immature strawberry datasets to reinforce the distinction 
between immature strawberries and their branches and leaves, thereby 
further advancing the model's recognition performance.

5.3. Comparison with related methods 
Our model's enhancement predominantly targets strawberry recog­
nition. To be more precise, we have analyzed several related works on 
strawberry recognition. Considering our dataset was captured in a real 
plantation environment, which poses the greatest challenge for straw­
berry recognition, the accuracy of other models applied to our dataset 
will not surpass their original accuracy. Hence, the subsequent com­
parison directly employs the data provided in the relevant literature. 
Wang et al. proposed the DSE-YOLO algorithm (Wang et al., 2022) for 
detecting different strawberry growth stages, achieving a mean Average 
Precision (mAP) of 86.58, slightly lower than our model. Xu et al. (Xu 
et al., 2022a) utilized an enhanced YOLOv3 for identifying a variety of 
fruits. The Average Precision (AP) value for ripe strawberries was up to 
89.5, considerably lower than that of our model for ripe strawberries, 
which was applied in a real plantation environment. Kim et al. (Kim 
et al., 2022) proposed a two-path model with semantic segmentation for 
strawberry detection, reaching an accuracy of 90.33, significantly lower 
than our model's 97.69. In terms of counting, while we counted the 
number of ripe and immature strawberries, Yoon et al. (Yoon et al., 
2023) estimated strawberry yield by detecting strawberry flower stocks. 
Their mAP of 0.6587 was lower than that of our model. 
5.4. The benefits to ecology and agriculture 
Firstly, strawberries, being significant crops, influence both agri­
cultural production and ecological balance significantly. The model 
proposed in this study facilitates real-time monitoring and recording of 
the distribution, quantity, and growth status of strawberries, aiding 
agricultural managers in making enhanced decisions and planning 
(Azadnia and Kheiralipour, 2022) to safeguard and improve the 
ecological environment for strawberries. Secondly, strawberries are 
prone to various pests and diseases, such as gray mold and red spider. 
Strawberry R-CNN lays the groundwork for future high-precision iden­
tification of strawberry diseases (Naik et al., 2022), minimizing the loss 
and environmental impact (Yu et al., 2023) triggered by pests and dis­
eases and promoting sustainable agricultural development. Thirdly, 
Strawberry R-CNN can be employed to identify strawberries for auto­
mated picking and processing, enhancing agricultural production effi­
ciency, reducing labor costs, optimizing agricultural management 
measures (Devi et al., 2023), and improving strawberry yield and 
quality. In conclusion, Strawberry R-CNN offers considerable benefits 
for ecoinformatics, fosters sustainable development of strawberry 
cultivation and ecology, enhances agricultural production efficiency, 
and provides valuable data support for ecosystem research and 
conservation. 
6. Conclusions 
In this paper, we have developed an efficient strawberry detection 
model, Strawberry R-CNN, by constructing a cascading network for 
extracting strawberry image features and integrating RoiAligns in place 
of RoiPooling in Faster R-CNN to enhance the detection performance of 
ripe and unripe strawberries. Our experimental findings indicate that 
the proposed Strawberry R-CNN significantly outperforms Faster R- 
CNN, demonstrating robust performance in extreme environments, such 
as leaf shading and intense light. Furthermore, Strawberry R-CNN ex­
hibits superior detection performance compared to some prevalent 
detection methodologies and improved Faster R-CNN methods. In terms 
of counting accuracy, the Strawberry R-CNN model achieved a 
remarkable 99.1% rate for ripe strawberries and 73.7% for immature 
strawberries. The Strawberry R-CNN proposed in this paper exhibits 
both robust detection performance and counting capability, offering a 
theoretical foundation and technical support for automatic strawberry 
harvesting and yield estimation. 
The implementation of the cascade network enhances the accuracy 
of our model, albeit at the expense of increased computational load, 
thereby reducing the speed of recognition. The model is currently hosted 
on a cloud platform, enabling the picking robot to connect to the 
network and access the recognition program to accomplish its picking 
task. Future endeavors will focus on optimizing the structure of the 
cascade network to minimize its computational demands while main­
taining accuracy. This optimization allows the model to be directly 
deployed on the end device, eliminating the necessity for online oper­
ation. Such applications could refine ecological planting patterns, 
decrease long-term cultivation expenses, and further catalyze the evo­
lution of eco-informatics within the integrated automatic agricultural 
monitoring and picking industry.
