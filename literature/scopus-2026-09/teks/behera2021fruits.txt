Fruits yield estimation using Faster R-CNN with MIoU

Abstract
Fruit yield estimation is one of the challenging tasks using on-plant images to
support smart farming and provide information about the product so that storage
and export facility can be arranged. The detection and counting of on-plant fruits is a
challenging task in complex vision. In this paper, we modify the intersection of
union (IoU) in original Faster R-CNN (FR-CNN) for on-plant fruit detection. The
modified IoU (MIoU) introduces good distance metric with the minimum area
containing ground truth and predicted bounding box. Again, the MIoU pays extra
attention to overlapping areas, which overcome the inefficiency of original R-CNN
and enhance the detection accuracy. The proposed FR-CNN with MIoU achieved
the correlation coefficient (R2) of mango, pomegranate, tomato, apple & orange are
0.98,0.92, 0.96, 0.98 & 0.95 respectively with the variation of imaging condition. In
the same image sample, FR-CNN achieved the correlation coefficient (R2) of
mango, pomegranate, tomato, apple & orange are 0.81,0.91,0.89, 0.90 & 0.92
respectively. So, the FR-CNN with MIoU enhances the detection accuracy compared
to original FR-CNN. Again, the F1 score for apple, orange, tomato, pomegranate
and mango is 0.9534, 0.9794, 0.9424, 0.9534 and mango 0.9383 respectively. In
addition, the proposed method is efficient enough with less complex compared to
state-of-art models for fruit detection. Again, the proposed methodology is evaluated
using other state-of-art fruits datasets, namely ACFR dataset and KFuji RGB-DS
dataset. The proposed methodology achieved F1 score of 0.9523 & 0.9432 for yield
estimation of apple & mango of ACFR dataset and 0.8912 for KFuji RGB-DS
dataset.

1 Introduction
As rural nation like India and its wide extent of environment and physic-topographical
conditions are most appropriate for producing different varieties of plant yields, for example,
fruits, vegetables, flowers, nuts, spices and crops [7]. India has increment in horticulture
creation throughout the most recent couple of years. Since the last decade, 2.6% and 4.8%
per annum are increased in the horticulture area and production rate respectively. According to
horticulture statistics, the growth rate of fruits production has risen from 50.9 Million Tonnes
to 97.35 Million Tonnes since 2004–05 to 2017–18 [17] as depicted in Fig. 1. A large amount
of fruits is wastage every year due to poor management of the post-harvesting process. The
post-harvesting process includes the facilitation of exporting and storage. The wastage of fruits
can be avoided only by proper estimation of yield before harvesting so, that adequate
arrangement facilitates for storing and exporting. Again, the manual yield estimation practice
is labour-intensive, time-consuming and prone to error. In this consequence, it is required to
develop an automated system for fruit yield estimation.
The detection and counting of on-tree fruit are very difficult because lots of problems
arise during this work. The issues are like: (1) As the light varies; (2) most of the fruits
are overlapped; (3) occlusion of fruits with leaves and stem. For these problems, many
solutions have been suggested by many research groups. For detection of fruits like
mangoes, almonds and apples, FR-CNN was developed by [2] from outdoor orchard
images. An architecture based on semantic segmentation, namely “MangoNet” was
designed [19] for yield estimation of mango. “MangoNet” is capable of detecting the
mango fruits in occluded and overlapped views. A system based on fully convolutional
network (FCN) with computer vision was developed [16] to estimate the fruit count of
apple from the clusters and achieved the accuracy in the range of 91.2% to 94.8%.
Another algorithm proposed by [9] for fruit detection and counting using deep learning
strategy for different environments. However, developing yield estimation systems is a
challenging task due to complex vision such as the appearance of leaves and branches in
front of fruits and the presence of multiple fruits in one cluster [15, 20, 21].
In recent years, there is considerable research on fruit counting [30], fruit classifica-
tion [28, 37] and plant classification [35, 45] based on machine learning, especially deep
learning. Besides agricultural field, the deep learning is applied in medical image
processing [1, 27, 32, 38, 44] and multimedia [29, 41, 42]. Again, with the rapid
development of flexible vision sensors and visual sensor networks, deep-learning-based

and machine learning. However, the performance is vulnerable to the overlapping of

with MIoU. Again, FR-CNN with VGG16 model is used for detection of mature passion
fruit by fusing RGB and depth images [36] and claim VGG16 performed well for feature
extraction. Hence, in this proposed system, also VGG16 is used for feature extraction.

2 Material and methods
The methodology for yield estimation of fruits is detailed in the following subsections.
2.1 Collection of sample
The suggested system considered five Indian fruits, namely mango, apple, orange,
pomegranate and tomato. A dataset of 1000 on-field images of each 200 images is
prepared for training the network with 800 × 600 standard resolutions. In addition, 100
on-field images with each of 20 are used for testing the network. The image samples
used in this research are collected from the different farming site of Sambalpur and
Sundargarh district of Odisha, India. The images are captured using the primary camera
(64MP) of Redmi Note 8 Pro smartphone. The images are saved in JPEG format and
resized to 800 × 600 standard resolutions.

2.3 Proposed system
Faster R-CNN architecture has two modules, namely the regional proposal network (RPN) and
Fast R-CNN. Object proposals generated by RPN and fed into fast R-CNN detector for
classification and outline with boundary box around the fruits.
The combination of feature extraction network and subsequent two sub-networks are called
a regional proposal network (RPN). The RPN is used to train for the generation of proposals.
The actual class of each proposal is predicted by the second sub-network. Here, the feature
extraction is done by VGG16. Adjust the negative overlap range and positive overlap range to
train the detector to ensure training samples are tightly overlapped with ground truth.
In the object detection system, Intersection over Union (IoU) indicates the distance between
the predicted bounding box and ground truth box. It is an important indicator, and formula for
calculating IoU is as follows:

MIoU ¼ IoU−A−PUG

Where P is predicted bounding box and G is the Ground truth bounding box and A is the
minimum area containing P and G. The range of IoU is [0,1], and the range of MIoU is [−1,1].
The MIoU score is 1 when the P and G coincide like IoU. Again, when there is no intersection
between P and G, the MIoU score is −1 instead of 0 in the case of IoU. So, MIoU is a good
distance metric compared to IoU. Again, MIoU introduces the minimum area A containing P
and G; it focusses extra attention to overlapping areas, which overcome the inefficiency of IoU
based norm loss function. The main speciality of MIoU than IoU is pointed below.
1.
If, |P∩G| =0, IoU is 0. That means the IoU does not reflect if no intersection between
P and G. This is the major weakness of IoU. But, in the case of MIoU, it converges
to −1.
As, MIoU = −1 even if the ratio of |PUG| and A tends to 0.
2.
When, P and G overlay perfectly, IoU = MIoU = 0.
Therefore, MIoU keeps major properties of IoU with rectifying its weakness.
The architecture of Faster R-CNN model consists of two modules. The first, called the
regional proposal network (RPN), is a fully convolutional network for generating object
proposals that will be fed into the second module. The second module is the Fast R-CNN
detector which classifies individual proposals and regresses a bounding box around the
fruits. The key idea is to share the same convolutional layers for the RPN and Fast R-
CNN detector up to their own fully connected layers. The output from the final
convolutional layer is a high-dimensional feature map, which is then connected to two
sibling layers, including a box-regression layer and a box-classification layer. These
layers define the RPN, and they detect and classify Regions of Interest (RoIs) in the
image. The RoIs are subsequently propagated through the fully-connected layers which
refine their object probability and associated bounding box label. The output from the
network is a set of bounding boxes with the probability of fruit. In the regression layer,
the original IoU is modified so that the network increases its ability to detect the fruits in
overlapping and occluded views. The proposed system for fruit detection is illustrated in
Fig. 3. The training parameters are detailed in Table 1.
In this paper, we adopted the FR-CNN with MIoU for yield estimation of fruits using
on-tree images. The hyperparameters used in the proposed model are: learning method is
stochastic gradient descent, learning rate is 0.01, weight decay is 0.001, validation
frequency is 30, maximum epoch is 50, minibatch size is 1, momentum is 0.9 and IoU
threshold is 0.5. For each image, 512 anchors are generated with a weight decay of 0.001
and momentum 0.9 is used for updating the weights and bias the model. To reduce
redundancy, non-maximum suppression (NMS) is performed over the proposals accord-
ing to their ensemble objectness scores with an IoU threshold of 0.5 to eliminate some
overlapping region proposals in the Proposal layer. Some novel NMS methods can be
employed to further improve the performance [11].

5 Conclusions

methodology is evaluated with five types of fruits, namely mango, pomegranate, tomato, apple
and orange. In each variety of fruits, the image samples of different imaging condition are
included. The image samples include overlapped, heavy & light colluded, adjacent and
separated fruits. The main advantages of FR-CNN with MIoU are that it can detect the
overlapped and heavy occluded fruits, which is the limitation in the case of FR-CNN. The
FR-CNN with MIoU achieved the coefficient of correlation range from 0.95 to 0.98, which is
