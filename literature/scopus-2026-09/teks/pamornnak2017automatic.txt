A B S T R A C T
In a trading market, price of oil palm (Elaeis guineensis) is negotiated depending some key parameters of the fresh
fruit bunch (FFB). Inspectors have been hired by a buyer to grade FFB to accept or reject. The classiﬁcation
results made by human inspection are skeptical and not very reliable if workload is high. We have developed a
system to grade FFB depending on its quality. Several palm features are extracted from RGB, near infrared, and
depth images, captured with a Microsoft Kinect camera version 2.0 installed in a light-controlled environment on
the conveyor line. Two main algorithms for classiﬁcation have been developed. The ﬁrst algorithm is called a
volume integration scheme (SVIS), which measures the relative volume of palm bunch. The second developed
algorithm classiﬁes palm bunch into three grades (L-Grade, M-Grade and H-Grade) based on oil content from
Soxhlet extraction. The system achieves 83% accuracy for grading palm bunch within 6 s per one sample, which
shows the possibility of using the system in a trading market.
1. Introduction
Oil palm (Elaeis guineensis) is an important oil plant since it provides
the highest yield per unit area of vegetable oil compared to other
sources. Main product of oil palm is palm fruit which forms in a bunch.
Crude palm oil (CPO) and crude palm kernel oil (CPKO) are produced
by milling palm fruit. These CPO and CPKO are used in various con-
sumer products such as food and oleochemical industry (Basiron,
2007). CPO can also be used as biodiesel substrate (Bari et al., 2002;
Nikhom and Tongurai, 2014) which can reduce consumption of fossil
energy.
Though, oil palm has been planted in several countries around the
world, the supply chain in each country is diﬀerent. In Malaysia and
Indonesia, which are two most cropped countries, each plantation area
is rather large and owned by entrepreneurs and the crop management is
very well performed. Fresh fruit bunches (FFBs) have continuously been
supplied to milling factories owned by the same entrepreneurs. Unlike
two mentioned countries, small plantations in Thailand or in some
countries in Africa have been owned by individual agriculturists. A
plantation area is rather small, normally is less than 50 ha. Therefore,
upstream activities in the supply chain, such as FFBs transportation and

collect FFBs from their plantations and take to a nearby trading market,
called a palm yard market. A yard market owner, a middlemen, buys
FFBs from agriculturists and stores FFBs in agriculture warehouses.
When the stock of FFBs in each warehouse is suﬃciently large or
reaches certain point which is worth to sell, they will transport their
stock to milling factories for selling. Pricing of FFBs is determined by its
qualities. The inspector will check history record of these agriculturists
where the pricing mechanism is mainly related to FFB appearances such
as color, size and weight of FFB. These inspectors to justify whether
each FFB should be “accept” or “reject” for trading, based on their
experience and knowledge. Everyday inspectors have a large workload
because there are several thousands of FFBs. Because of large workload
and stress for having short time for classiﬁcation of each group of FFB,
human classiﬁcation results are prone to be biased as well as unreliable
(Abbas et al., 2005; Pamornnak et al., 2013; Yeow et al., 2010).
Generally, there are several key qualities of FFBs, but the key
quality for milling and reﬁnery factories is OER of each FFB, as it is
related to yield for producing CPO and CPKO. Currently OER of FFB is
determined by repeated washing of mesocarp ﬁber in glassware with
boiled hexane solvent. The OER can determined by the ratio between
weight of washed and unwashed ﬁber, called “Soxhlet extraction”

nique and artiﬁcial intelligence are the core technologies used for ob-

color and so on (Kumar et al., 2015), and making decisions. Several
research studies proposed using image processing for palm problem.

Recently, an application for determining OER in palm fruit with an
automatic color correction was developed for a mobile device platform
(Pamornnak et al., 2015). Based on hue-saturation color model, a
neural network classiﬁer was proposed for palm grading. An algorithm
based on hue color model for predicting harvest time (Razali et al.,
2011, 2009) was developed for a mobile device. Several authors pro-
posed algorithms for grading palm bunch based on several color
models; RGB color model (Alfatni et al., 2008), HSI model (Tan et al.,
2010), and RGB color model with multi-layer neural network classiﬁer
(Fadilah et al., 2012). A simple classiﬁcation system, accept or reject,
for palm bunch based on an image color model was designed. The
system was speciﬁcally designed to be compatible with a conveyor line
(Makky and Soni, 2013). Hyperspectral imaging technique working
with invisible light and NIR sensors were used for classifying palm
bunch (Junkwon et al., 2009). A ﬁber optic probe equipped with a NIR
spectrometer was proposed to estimate oil and moisture content
(Rittiron et al., 2012). A portable NIR spectrometer was proposed for
estimating ripeness degree and oil content in palm bunch. A neural
network and sum of weighted-wavelength reﬂectance (Makky and Soni,
2014) were proposed for bunch classiﬁcation. However, this research
uses only one feature to determine the quality. Though many research
studies have been proposed but none of them is suitable for ﬁeld ap-
plications such as a trading market case.
In the ﬁeld work, several disturbances from environment such as
light, moisture, temperature etc., aﬀect sensing parameters and need to
be encounter. On one hand the functionality of sensors with diﬀerent
selectivity (such as 3D and spectral characteristics) is required, on the
other hand the integration of these multi sensor option in a single
sensor system would reduce complexity (Strothmann et al., 2017). So,
using phenotyping features from a visible range RGB sensor such as
color and texture may not enough. A phenotyping system needs mul-
tiple data from sensors diﬀerence to compensate these variations, such
as 3D cameras, RGB cameras, spectrums cameras etc. (Ruckelshausen
and Busemeyer, 2015). For 3D camera, the 3D time-of-ﬂight (ToF)
camera is used in various research areas with minimized error, low
computation time, compact design and low power consumption (Foix
et al., 2011). For agricultural products, the ToF cameras have been
continuously applied for phenotyping and quality inspection. For ex-
ample, leaf segmentation applications (Kazmi et al., 2014), 3D image
reconstruction system to determine sugar beet taproot shape volume
and leaf area (Paulus et al., 2014), ground based crop localization by
using point cloud data (Wong and Lim, 2012) and so on. The cheap
price ToF depth sensor, RGB and Infrared image sensors have been
integrated in RGB-D camera, Microsoft Kinect which was designed for
gaming application (Leyvand et al., 2011). The 3D applications in
various research areas have been proposed with Kinect.
In this paper, we propose an automatic system based on three sen-
sing parameters for grading palm bunch. Parameters relating to quality
in palm bunch of interest will be measured from multiple sensors,
which are integrated in a single device. A Microsoft Kinect 2.0 camera
not only ﬁts, but also is a low-cost solution. The proposed parameters
obtained from this camera include volume, color appearances, and re-
ﬂectance intensity of palm bunch. Section 2 illustrates algorithms for
camera calibration and volume computation and the design detail of
the proposed system. The mechanism and performances of these algo-
rithms with several palm bunches will be discussed in Section 3. The
system is implemented and experimented. Finally, the conclusion is in
Section 4.

In this section, the palm grading system is described. Microsoft
Kinect camera is the main sensor for acquiring three features, RGB
image, infrared image and point cloud. An algorithm for camera cali-
bration and computing bunch volume will be described ﬁrst, following
by hardware-software system design and the algorithm for grading an
oil palm bunch into three groups (L-Grade, M-Grade and H-Grade),
respectively.

2.3. Palm grading hardware and software system
In this subsection, the hardware and software design of system for
grading palm bunch is presented. The design is developed based on the
algorithm presented in Section 2.2. Fig. 6(a) shows a hardware diagram
of the design. It consists of three basic parts, a conveyor belt, a module
for image acquisition, and a control module for the conveyor. A con-
veyor belt is proposed for interfacing a conveyor line in a milling plant.
A V-shaped belt made of rubber is driven with a 90W AC motor with a
chain-drive system, which allows 75 kg maximum load. For an image
acquisition module, a Microsoft Kinect 2.0 (1920 × 1080 pixels for RGB
and 512 × 424 pixels for point cloud and infrared images) is installed
1 m above the conveyor line to capture a top view, as shown in
Fig. 6(a). Three image types; RGB, infrared and point cloud; are si-
multaneously recorded. Two light bulbs (6500K 783 Lumens, CRI 80)
were installed beside Kinect 2.0 camera to provide a light-controlled
environment. The brightness condition is controlled by the control
module. A light dimmer is used to turn the light on and oﬀas well as
lightness adjustment. The third part is a conveyor control module,
which consists of a motor speed control module and a microcontroller.
An emergency stop button is added for safety measures.
We designed the system to be on a personnel computer (PC) based,
as shown in Fig. 6(b). The Kinect camera interfaces the PC via USB 3.0.
The control module for conveyor interfaces the PC via a serial port. The
software was programmed using Microsoft Visual C# 2015 on a Mi-
crosoft Windows 10 platform. We develop algorithms using two li-
braries, OpenTK 1.1.4 for 3D point cloud drawing and EmguCV
(OpenCV 2.4.10) for RGB and IR image processing. The system software
contains three modules: for detecting palm bunch, controlling feeder
and grading palm bunch. Fig. 6(c) shows two photographs of the
complete system. The top-view photograph shows that the Kinect
camera is installed 1 m over the conveyor belt and between two light
bulbs. A computer as shown in the side-view is used for controlling and
classifying palm bunch.
2.4. Bunch detector and feeding control
In this paper, we propose a simple algorithm for bunch detection,
which is based on an image processing technique. The algorithm can
detect palm bunch on the conveyor belt simply because color appear-
ance of palm bunch and conveyor belt are rather diﬀerent. Near in-
frared sensor was chosen for low light interference. There are four steps
in the bunch detection algorithm as illustrated in Fig. 7(a). In the ﬁrst
step, the near infrared sensor in the Kinect camera captures an infrared

For the conveyor control process, a reference stop area which is 5%
of the frame width on the center of horizontal line shows in Fig. 7(b) is
set. This area, called “stop zone”, is determined to ensure that the
system takes a whole bunch snapshot. Palm bunch of interest is loaded
to the conveyor belt moving at the speciﬁc speed of 0.125 m/s. The
centroid of the mask image was calculated continuously during the
conveyor moves. The conveyor stops when the algorithm detects that
the centroid point is at the center line of the “stop zone”. An image
snapshot of the palm bunch is taken immediately the conveyor starts
moving again. The process takes 5 s to get RGB and near infrared
snapshots. Finally, two images of the palm bunch, RGB and near in-
frared, are obtained by AND the RGB and near infrared snapshots with
the mask image. This process is illustrated in Fig. 7(c). After this pro-
cess, both RGB and near infrared images of the bunch will be applied to
the feature extraction algorithm in the next step.

• Average Hue and Average Infrared Intensity
Grading palm bunch from its volume or weight is not suﬃcient. In
many cases, a ripe palm bunch with small weight contains more oil
content than palm bunches with larger weight. Some physical proﬁles
of palm bunch should be utilized, as well. It is well known that light
reﬂectance of palm fruit is related to its ripening interval, which in turn
is correlated with the oil content (Abdullah et al., 2002; Makky and
Soni, 2014). Ripening mechanism of oil palm depends on the biological
processes. During maturation, chlorophyll and anthocyanin in unripe
fruit absorbs visible light at 430, 530 and 670 nm wavelength (Tan
et al., 2010). Therefore, the color of palm fruit becomes deep violet-
black. During ripening, oil and carotenes which contribute to yellow-
orange-red colors (Mortensen, 2006), increase in the mesocarp layer
(Tranbarger et al., 2011) while anthocyanin in the fruit drops (Hazir
et al., 2012). This makes color of palm fruit bunch reddish-orange in
−10 to 70 degree hue range (Tan et al., 2010). Color gradient of fruit is
speciﬁc and characteristic, and can be described into small fruit part
(Junkwon et al., 2009; Pamornnak et al., 2015). Fig. 10 shows the
example of ripe and unripe oil palm bunches, the average hue value
from RGB image is 36 degree, which is lower than unripe bunch which
is 46 degree.

parameters for QPF.

4. Conclusion
An automatic system for grading palm bunch has been presented.
The system consists of a continuous belt with a control module, in-
stalled Kinect camera in a light-controlled environment, and a com-
puter with the grading software. Three features for grading palm bunch
are obtained from sensors in a Kinect camera. These features are H
value from RGB snapshot, volume index from point-cloud snapshot, and
infrared intensity from infrared snapshot. We developed the programs
for the system based on EmguCV (OpenCV) and OpenTK library func-
tion sets. One of the key proposed technology is an algorithm to eval-
uate volume index. This algorithm is called a Simple Volume Integration
Scheme (SVIS). Based on the obtained results from the algorithm, we
achieved 2.49% volume error for 9200 cm3 object. We have tested this
algorithm with several palm bunches while their weight is measured.
We have found that the volume index values of the palm bunch are
linearly correlated with bunch weight of R2 = 0.911. This proves that
the SVIS can be applied to determine bunch weight. We also proposed
to apply two more features to grade palm bunch and three features of a
palm bunch have been applied to formulate a grading function. The
function is obtained by averaging three second-order polynomial
functions. Each function is formed with two features, either hue and
volume index, hue and infrared intensity, and volume index and in-
frared intensity. We proposed to grade quality of palm bunch into three
grades: low, medium and high grade; depending on its quantity (in kg)
of oil in palm bunch. The system with the grading function was applied
to test 200 palm bunches and the system can grade 10 palm bunches per
minute with 83% success rate. This shows the possibility to use the
system in a trading market. The system can also be applied to mobile
systems, such as agricultural machines or autonomous robots.
