A B S T R A C T
This study proposes a real-time fruit volume estimation model based on YOLOv9 (RTFVE-YOLOv9) and 
binocular stereo vision technology to address the challenges of low automation and insufficient accuracy in 
fruit volume measurement in complex orchard environments, particularly in scenarios with diverse canopy 
structures and severe branch-leaf occlusion. The model achieves effective recognition of occluded fruits through 
the innovative design of a Dual-Scale and Global–Local Sequence (DSGLSeq) module while incorporating a 
Multi-Head and Multi-Scale Self-Interaction (MHMSI) module to improve the detection performance of small 
fruit targets. Systematic validation experiments conducted on major economic fruit tree varieties, including 
apples, pears, pomelos, and kiwifruit, demonstrate that RTFVE-YOLOv9 improved the mean Average Precision 
(mAP) by 2.1%, 1.6%, 4%, and 3.8% respectively on the four fruit datasets compared to the baseline YOLOv9-
c model. The model’s internal working mechanisms were thoroughly revealed through multi-dimensional 
evaluation, including ablation experiments, Heatmap Analysis, and Effective Receptive Field (ERF) analysis, 
providing a theoretical foundation for subsequent optimization. The research findings enrich the application 
theory of computer vision in smart agriculture and provide reliable technical support for achieving precise 
orchard management.
1. Introduction
Fruit volume assessment holds particular significance in the fruit 
cultivation industry. This key indicator is vital in fruit tree yield pre-
diction, quality evaluation, and market pricing. It supports agricultural 
production management, disease control, and research on tree physio-
logical mechanisms (Yu et al., 2024). Major global economic fruit tree 
varieties, such as apples, pears, pomelos, and kiwifruits, require accu-
rate volume measurements to help farmers optimize planting density 
and estimate yields, providing precise information for market sales to 
enhance economic benefits (SS et al., 2024). Meanwhile, these measure-
ment data are crucial in agricultural production management, disease 
control, and research on tree physiological mechanisms (Muneer et al., 
2024).
In actual orchard environments, precise fruit volume measurement 
still faces numerous challenges. The diversity of canopy structures, vari-
ations in tree height, and complex natural environmental conditions 
all present difficulties for measurement. Traditional manual measure-
ment methods are time-consuming and labor-intensive and struggle to 
meet modern agricultural production’s precision and efficiency require-
ments (Yi et al., 2025). This problem is particularly prominent in fruit

However, despite the enormous potential shown by object detec-

innovations include:

2. Related work
2.1. Object detection
Object detection is a core technology in computer vision, enabling 
automatic identification and classification of objects of interest in 
images or videos. With the rapid development of artificial intelligence 
and computer vision, object detection algorithms play an increasingly 
important role in various applications. The advent of deep learning 
techniques, especially CNNs (Li et al., 2021), has significantly improved 
object detection performance. CNNs demonstrate superior automatic 
feature extraction capabilities and generalization performance com-
pared to traditional shallow machine learning methods. In recent years, 
CNN-based object detection models have become a research hotspot, 
with numerous networks emerging, such as Fast R-CNN (Girshick, 
2015), Faster R-CNN (Ren et al., 2015), YOLO (Redmon et al., 2016), 
SSD (Liu et al., 2016), and EfficientDet (Tan et al., 2020). Faster R-
CNN achieves high-precision detection by generating candidate regions 
through a Region Proposal Network and performing classification and 
regression but faces speed bottlenecks. The YOLO algorithm divides 
images into grids, classifying objects and regressing bounding boxes 
within each grid, significantly improving detection speed but still has 
room for improvement in detecting small objects in complex scenes. 
The SSD algorithm utilizes multi-scale convolution operations to detect 
objects of different scales, showing good performance but still lags be-
hind YOLO in real-time processing. Additionally, algorithms like Mask 
R-CNN and RetinaNet continue to advance object detection technology, 
constantly improving accuracy and speed.
2.2. YOLO object detection
The YOLO series of algorithms has achieved remarkable results 
in object detection. Mbouembe et al. (2023) proposed an enhanced 
YOLOv4-tiny network for tomato detection in complex environments. 
Li et al. (2023) improved recognition accuracy and speed in com-
plex environments by optimizing the YOLOv5 model structure and 
parameters. Xia et al. (2022) optimized the YOLOv7 model, enhancing 
detection accuracy and real-time performance across various scenarios, 
particularly kiwifruit detection. Xiao et al. (2023) proposed a fruit 
ripeness recognition method based on YOLOv8, providing scientific 
guidance for agricultural production.
YOLOv9 (Wang et al., 2024), the latest iteration in the YOLO 
series, has achieved new mAP heights on the COCO dataset. It intro-
duces the concept of programmable gradient information, helping to 
address gradient vanishing or saturation issues. The GELAN module 
in YOLOv9 improves traditional convolution operations by optimizing 
convolutional layer design, introducing auxiliary training heads, and in-
corporating the ADown downsampling-like module, enhancing feature 
extraction capabilities and expressiveness.
2.3. Integration of binocular vision and YOLO
Binocular vision technology mimics the human binocular vision 
system, acquiring stereoscopic information of scenes through two cam-
eras (Lu and Dong, 2023). In agriculture, binocular vision can monitor 
crop growth, assess volume and growth progress, and guide irriga-
tion, fertilization, and harvesting activities. Liu et al. (2023) combined 
binocular stereo vision with an improved object detection model to 
achieve precise detection and localization of pineapple fruits. Tang 
et al. (2023b) explored the integration of binocular stereo-vision tech-
nology with the YOLOv4-tiny model to realize fruit localization and 
tracking in orchards.
However, occlusion problems in orchards still need to be improved, 
and the models’ precise understanding and localization capabilities 
must be improved. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) excel in 
local feature extraction (Alzubaidi et al., 2021), but convolution kernel 
sizes limit their receptive fields (Ding et al., 2022). Yang et al. (2024) 
introduced Large Convolutional Kernels (L-CKs) to expand receptive 
fields, but this approach still relies on fixed-size kernels for feature 
extraction. The ConvNet model proposed by Liu et al. (2022) effectively 
learns local features while preserving global information through a 
hybrid local information adaptive understanding module, improving 
model performance and generalization ability.
Small object detection remains challenging due to limited appear-
ance representations that are difficult to distinguish from the back-
ground. The multi-scale attention (EMA) module proposed by Ouyang 
et al. (2023) improves spatial semantic feature distribution by re-
shaping channels and dividing sub-features. The multi-head attention 
mechanism introduced by Vaswani et al. (2023) excels in captur-
ing contextual information but may have limitations in processing 
small objects. The MHSA-Net model designed by Tan et al. (2022) 
enhances small object detection capabilities through multi-scale feature 
interaction and spatial-channel dimension alignment strategies.
In summary, current research primarily focuses on precise fruit 
identification. In contrast, real-time fruit volume prediction in com-
plex environments with object occlusion and small targets remains a 
comprehensive challenge to be addressed. This study aims to propose a 
real-time fruit volume estimation method by integrating YOLOv9 with 
binocular stereo-vision technology to address these challenges.

3.2. Real-time fruit volume measurement method
This study designed a complete real-time fruit volume measure-
ment process to achieve precise fruit volume measurement in complex 
orchard environments. This process primarily consists of two core com-
ponents: the RTFVE-YOLOv9 network and a precise binocular imaging 
system.
Fig. 1(a) shows that the RTFVE-YOLOv9 network represents an 
innovative improvement over the original YOLOv9 (Wang et al., 2024). 
Specifically, this study integrated the DSLGSeq module into YOLOv9’s 
backbone network, replacing the original RePNCSPELAN4 structure 
to form the YOLOv9-DSLGSeq model. The MHSI module was also 
applied after the RePNCSPELAN4 in the neck network and acted on the 
prediction network, establishing the YOLOv9-DSM network to obtain 
more accurate detection results.

internal and external parameters. The specific steps are as follows:
Step One: Using a 9 × 9 black and white checkerboard calibration 
board with 33 mm square sides, collect 30 sets of binocular images at 
different positions and angles (as shown in Fig. 2).
Step Two: Calibration obtains the 3 × 3 internal parameter matrix 
𝐾 and 1 × 5 distortion coefficient matrix 𝐷 for both left and right 
cameras. As shown in Fig. 3, the average errors of 𝐾 and 𝐷 parameters 
for both cameras are controlled within 2%, verifying the reliability of 
the calibration results.
Step Three: After calibration, use the OpenCV library to read the 
generated left_cam.yml and right_cam.yml parameter files to obtain 
the external parameters of the binocular cameras (baseline distance, 
rotation matrix 𝑅, and translation matrix 𝑇) for subsequent coordinate 
transformation (as shown in Table 2). 
Step Four: The acquired images contain distortion due to the optical

3.4. Model design
Based on the YOLOv9 backbone network (Wang et al., 2024), this 
study designs the Dual-Scale and Global–Local Sequence (DSGLSeq) 
module and Multi-Head and Multi-Scale Self-Interaction (MHMSI) mod-
ule, integrating them to construct a fruit object recognition model. As 
shown in Fig. 4, the model consists of three parts: Backbone, Neck, and 
Head. The Backbone section includes:
(1) 5 Convolutional modules (Conv): Extract basic image features 
(such as edges, textures, etc.) through convolution operations.
(2) 4 DSGLSeq modules: Specifically designed to address occlusion 
issues in fruit recognition, effectively fusing information at different 
scales to enhance feature extraction capability for fruits occluded by 
branches and leaves. This module improves recognition accuracy by 
capturing local and global features of fruits.
(3) Finally, feature enhancement processing through YOLOv9’s SP-
PELAN module.
The Neck section consists of:
(1) 2 Conv: Further extract and refine features to enhance feature 
expression capability.
(2) 2 Upsample modules: Restore low-resolution feature maps to 
higher resolution through upsampling operations, facilitating multi-
scale feature fusion.
(3) 4 Concat modules: Concatenate feature maps of different scales 
in the channel dimension to achieve feature fusion.
(4) 4 RePNCSPELAN4 modules: Perform feature enhancement and 
optimization. The Head section includes 3 MHMSI and 3 Conv modules, 
processing detection tasks for small targets (P3), medium targets (P4), 
and large targets (P5) in the form of a Feature Pyramid Network (FPN).

3.4.1. DSGLSeq module
This study designed the DSGLSeq and embedded it into YOLOv9’s 
Backbone to replace the original RePNCSPELAN4 structure to enhance 
the model’s ability to recognize occluded fruits. As shown in Fig. 5, 
the DSGLSeq module mainly consists of an adaptive selection module 
and an inverted bottleneck layer, with the adaptive selection module 
employing a dual-branch structure design. The module extracts features 
in parallel using two sizes of depth convolution kernels, 3 × 3 and 
7 × 7, where the 3 × 3 convolution branch primarily captures local 
fine features such as fruit surface textures and edges. In contrast, the 
7 × 7 convolution branch obtains contextual information about the fruit 
and its surrounding environment through a larger receptive field. This 
multi-scale feature extraction strategy enables the module to simul-
taneously focus on detailed features and global semantic information 
of fruits, allowing accurate feature extraction even when branches 
and leaves partially occlude fruits. Finally, the module fuses feature 
information from different scales through the inverted bottleneck layer, 
further enhancing feature extraction capability in complex occlusion 
scenarios.

the strong generalization capability of the YOLOv9-c model in fruit

5.2. Ablation analysis
Detailed ablation experiments were conducted to assess the specific 
contributions of the DSGLSeq and MHMSI modules to the YOLOv9 
model’s performance. As shown in Table 7, introducing these two 
modules improved the model’s mAP across multiple fruit datasets, 
particularly excelling in handling occlusion and small fruit detection 
tasks. The experimental results emphasize the complementarity of the 
DSGLSeq and MHMSI modules in enhancing model performance. As 
illustrated in Table 7, after integrating MHMSI with YOLOv9-c, the 
mAP increased by 0.3%, 0.4%, 2.8%, and 0.2% across the four datasets, 
highlighting the positive impact of MHMSI. Further incorporating DS-
GLSeq on top of MHMSI resulted in mAP improvements of 2.1%, 1.6%, 
4%, and 3.8% compared to the original YOLOv9-c, underscoring the 
importance of DSGLSeq.
When DSGLSeq and MHMSI modules work in conjunction with 
YOLOv9-c, the significant mAP improvements (2.1%, 1.6%, 4%, 3.8%) 
indicate a synergistic effect between these two modules, effectively 
enhancing the overall performance of the YOLOv9 model in fruit object 
detection tasks.

5.4. Effective receptive field analysis
The Effective Receptive Field (ERF) is an important indicator for 
measuring the feature extraction capability of convolutional neural 
networks (Ding et al., 2022), with its distribution range and dark area 
size directly reflecting model performance levels. As shown in Fig. 
11, this study comparatively analyzed the effects of different module 
combinations on ERF. Experimental results show that the expansion 
effect on ERF is insignificant when combining the MHMSI module with 
YOLOv9-c and YOLOv9. In contrast, combining the DSGLSeq module 
with YOLOv9-c significantly enhanced the model’s effective receptive 
field range. This enhancement primarily benefits from the average 
pooling and max pooling operations designed in the DSGLSeq module, 
where the combination of these two pooling methods strengthens the 
model’s spatial feature aggregation capability, enabling it to capture 
global information of target objects better.

5.5. Cross-validation analysis
This study conducted K-fold cross-validation experiments on the 
proposed RTFVE-YOLOv9 model to evaluate the model’s stability and 
generalization ability. Tests were performed on Apple, Pear, Pomelo, 
and Kiwifruit datasets, examining cross-validation schemes from 2-fold 
to 10-fold. As shown in Fig. 12, experimental results indicate optimal 
model performance under 5-fold cross-validation, achieving Precision 
rates of 86%, 88%, 84%, and 75%, and Recall rates of 75%, 80%, 
66%, and 55% respectively for the four fruit types. These results verify 
the model’s reliability and provide an important reference for model 
validation strategies in subsequent practical applications.

6. Conclusion
This study proposed the RTFVE-YOLOv9 real-time estimation model 
to address the challenges of precise fruit volume measurement in com-
plex orchard environments. The model innovatively integrates YOLOv9 
with binocular stereo vision technology and designed two key func-
tional modules: the DSGLSeq for enhancing feature extraction capabil-
ity in occlusion scenarios and the MHMSI for improving small target 
fruit recognition accuracy. The model significantly outperformed ex-
isting mainstream detection methods in core indicators such as mean 
Average Precision (mAP), Precision, and Recall through systematic val-
idation on multiple typical fruit datasets. Through multi-dimensional 
evaluation, including ablation experiments, EigenCAM interpretability 
analysis, and Effective Receptive Field (ERF) analysis, this study deeply 
revealed the model’s internal working mechanisms, providing a novel 
theoretical reference for complex scene object detection in agricul-
ture while offering reliable technical support for intelligent orchard 
management.
Although RTFVE-YOLOv9 demonstrates excellent performance,
some limitations remain in practical applications. First, model per-
formance is closely related to training data quality and diversity, 
potentially affecting its generalization ability to new fruit varieties; 
second, the model’s processing capability still needs improvement in 
complex scenarios with multiple fruit types coexisting. Based on this, 
future research will focus on improving network architecture optimiza-
tion, dataset expansion, transfer learning, and few-shot learning to 
enhance model performance and applicability. Meanwhile, this study 
plans to extend this technology to various aspects of full-cycle orchard 
management, including harvest time prediction, yield estimation, and 
quality evaluation, providing comprehensive technical solutions for 
digital orchard construction.
