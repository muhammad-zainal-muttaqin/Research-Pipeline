Abstract— This study presents a novel on-tree sapota fruit 
grading and yield estimation based on the latest computer 
vision methods. The study is based on an extensive dataset of 
500 sapota fruit images – 250 Grade A and 250 Grade B. The 
image processing pipeline is based on three strong algorithms: 
U2-Net as coarse segmentation, GrabCut as the refinement 
mask step, and YOLOv8 as the accurate object detection and 
localization 
step.
The
Jaccard
index
is
the
baseline
segmentation
performance metric, and standard object 
detection metrics (precision, recall, F1-score, mAP) measure 
the accuracy of the YOLOv8 model. Results of this study show 
higher accuracy of sapota grading
and yield estimation
compared to conventional, manual methods, demonstrating a 
strong and 
effective 
tool 
for 
sapota 
cultivation 
and 
management optimization, with YOLOv8 showing high 
accuracy U2-Net, GrabCut, and YOLOv8 models were tested
for finding the stages of fruit maturity. F1-score, Precision, and 
Recall
were measured for the models. U2-Net gave an
mAP of 0.959, YOLOv8 gave 0.983, and GrabCut gave 0.990, 
which indicates that GrabCut gives the highest accuracy 
among the three models. Additionally, GrabCut was utilized 
with YOLOv8 and U2-Net for other research and proved itself 
effective for improving refinement in segmentation to enhance 
the quality of classification.
Keywords— Sapota , object detection model, precision curve, 
recall curve, F1 curve, class loss, GrabCut, YOLOv8, U2-Net, 
Jaccard Index.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Accurate yield estimation is highly crucial for effective 
agriculture management and resource planning The conven-
tional yield estimation techniques, based on manual sam-
pling and subjective visual estimation, are time-consuming, 
labor intensive, and non-uniform in nature[1] .Subjectivity of 
the techniques leads to inconsistency and decreases the 
accuracy of  yield estimation[2].Computer vision, a fast-
emerging field of  artificial intelligence, offers a chance for 
automatic 
and 
objective 
fruit 
grading 
and 
yield 
estimation[3] .The capability of computer vision to process 
images rapidly and with high precision has the potential to 
greatly enhance the efficiency and accuracy of agricultural 
processes. Although numerous research studies have applied 
computer vision techniques to different fruit species fewer 
research studies have been reported on sapota [4].The earlier 
studies have been focused on artificial intelligence-based 
fruit detection systems for supporting digitalization in 
agriculture [5].

III. 
METHODOLOGY 
Fig. 1 describes the workflow of the proposed method. 
Input images are preprocessed before passing through the 
neural networks to detect objects, thus making possible the 
iden- tification 
and 
classification 
of 
Sapota 
fruits. 
Normalization of pixel values and data augmentation are the 
two common preprocessing techniques for robustification of 
the model. Image segmentation techniques, including 
GrabCut and U2- Net, are utilized to improve the accuracy of 
fruit area detection. Then, there is the model architecture, the 
core of the process. In this case, YOLOv8 was the 
architecture chosen. It predicts class probabilities and 
bounding boxes for each cell from a grid produced from the 
input image. The model is trained from an annotated dataset, 
which enhances its ability to detect fruits and predict 
bounding boxes. After training, the model is tested    with a 
test dataset where it predicts class labels and bounding boxes 
for unseen fruit images. Model evaluation is then conducted 
using performance metrics such as accuracy, recall, and F1-
score to measure detection and classification efficiency. 
Using object detection, particularly with YOLOv8 and 
GrabCut and U2-Net in segmentation, offers accurate 
identification of Sapota fruits. The output classifies fruits as 
Grade A or Grade B. 
 
Fig. 1. Proposed Methodology 
A. Datasets 
Fig. 2 and Fig. 3 present the process of dataset 
construction, with an equally well-annotated set of 500 
images of Sapota fruits Grade A (small fruits) as presented in 
Fig. 2 and Grade B (larger fruits) as presented in Fig. 3. The 
images were directly acquired from Sapota trees grown on a 
private farm in Mysuru using an Android smartphone, in 
standardized lighting conditions to reduce variability and 
enhance consistency. In order to guarantee high data quality, 
several images for every fruit were shot from various 
directions to acquire different perspectives and mitigate 
occlusion effects.

B. U2-Net Segmentation 
Image segmentation is performed using U2-Net, as 
shown in Fig. 4,5, where segmented Sapota fruits, 
categorized as Grade A and Grade B, are extracted from 
complex backgrounds to enhance further processing. With a 
goal of strong foreground- background segmentation, U2-
Net avoids including Grade A as well as Grade B Sapota 
fruits along with its tree foliage, or other extraneous objects 
for a clean-cut separation. That’s the core objective of a good 
segmentation prior to grading. After a good pre-processing 
step on regions, analysis on fruit parts would be smooth, and 
non-fruit area discrimination would significantly contribute 
to classifying, accurate size- based grading, and higher 
estimation of the yield.

C. GrabCut 
In GrabCut segmentation, the image is represented as a 
graph, where each pixel is a node. The algorithm performs 
min-cut/max-flow optimization to segment the image into 
foreground and background. Original images are shown in 
Fig.6.Image segmentation is performed using GrabCut, as 
shown in Fig. 7, where segmented Sapota fruits, categorized 
as Grade A and Grade B extracted from complex 
backgrounds to enhance further processing. These images are 
the output for the original images as in Fig.6.GrabCut is 
employed for precise foreground-background separation, 
ensuring that both  grades are accurately isolated from tree 
foliage and other distractions.

D. Jaccard Index 
The Jaccard Index (Intersection over Union, or IoU) is 
used to evaluate the segmentation’s performance. The 
segmentation accuracy can be quantitatively assessed by 
comparing the over- lap between the ground truth and the 
expected segmentation mask as shown in the Fig. 8 and Fig.9. 
The greater the segmentation per- formance, the higher the 
Jaccard Index value. As a result,we achieved 0.76 for Grade 
A and 0.87 for Grade B, respectively of Sapota fruit isolation 
guarantees accurate detection and categorization.

V. 
CONCLUSION 
The proposed work presents an automated grading 
system of sapota fruit using deep computer vision 
approach.The pipeline uses U2-Net to carry out coarse 
segmentation , Grabcut to refine the mask and YOLOv8 for 
precise object detection and grading as Grade A and Grade 
B.Segmentation performance is  evaluated based on the 
Jaccard Index, and object detection performance is evaluated 
based on precision, recall, F1-score, and mean Average 
Precision (mAP). Results indicate that GrabCut is best in 
accuracy and has higher accuracy compared to U2-Net and 
YOLOv8 with an mAP of 0.990, and is the best method to 
segment sapota fruit and grade it. 
Future work may be focused on enhancing detection 
accuracy under adverse conditions such as varying 
illumination and occlusion. Further expansion of the dataset 
with sapota fruit images of different types will enhance 
model robustness and generalizability and enable practical 
deployment of automated grading systems in intelligent 
agriculture.
