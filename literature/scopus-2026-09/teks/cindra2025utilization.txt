Abstract—The palm oil industry is crucial for tropical 
economies like Indonesia, where the success of Fresh Fruit 
Bunches (FFB) harvesting significantly impacts Crude Palm 
Oil (CPO) quality. One challenge is accurately assessing fruit 
ripeness, typically done manually, which is prone to errors due 
to subjectivity and environmental factors. This study develops 
an automated detection system using machine learning and 
computer vision to classify FFB ripeness. Convolutional Neural 
Networks (CNN) and YOLO object detection were utilized, 
delivering real-time, high-accuracy results despite varying 
conditions. The YOLOv10n model achieved the best 
performance, with Precision = 0.9453, Recall = 0.8504, and 
Inference Speed = 7.34 ms/img. This demonstrates AI's 
potential to enhance harvesting efficiency, reduce costs, and 
improve CPO quality, offering a promising solution for palm 
oil industry digitalization. 
Keywords—Machine Learning, YOLO, Fresh Fruit Bunch, 
Ripeness Classification, Image Processing 
I. INTRODUCTION 
The palm oil industry is a strategic sector in the economy 
of tropical countries such as Indonesia. The success and 
accuracy of the Fresh Fruit Bunch (FFB) harvesting process 
greatly determines the quality and quantity of crude palm oil 
(CPO) produced. One important aspect of the harvesting 
process is the ability to identify the level of fruit ripeness 
accurately and timely. If the FFB is harvested in an unripe or 
overripe condition, it can cause a decrease in oil yield and an 
increase in processing costs [1]. Currently, the assessment of 
palm fruit ripeness is generally carried out by human labor 
through visual observation which is highly dependent on 
experience. This approach has many weaknesses such as 
subjectivity, fatigue, and the influence of environmental 
factors such as lighting and viewing angles that cause a 
decrease in accuracy and consistency [2]. 
With the advancement of image processing and machine 
learning technology, especially deep learning approaches such 
as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), there is potential 
to automate this process accurately. The implementation of an 
artificial intelligence (AI)-based computer vision system can 
be an alternative solution that replaces manual methods to 
identify FFB ripeness efficiently in the field. This study aims 
to develop a smart system that can recognize the ripeness level 
of oil palm fruit by utilizing images from RGB cameras. It is 
expected that the system will be able to provide real-time 
ripeness detection results and remain accurate amidst 
variations in plantation conditions such as lighting and 
shooting angles. Assessing the ripeness level of FFB in the 
field has many challenges, especially in ensuring the 
consistency and reliability of detection results. Human visual 
assessment can vary depending on lighting conditions and 
individual experience. In addition, manual methods. require a 
lot of time and effort, especially for large plantations. 
Detection errors can cause harvesting at the wrong time, thus 
reducing the quality of CPO. Therefore, a technology-based 
automation system is needed that can detect ripeness quickly 
and accurately. The development of artificial intelligence, 
especially machine learning and computer vision, offers 
innovative solutions that can overcome the limitations of 
conventional methods and support the digitalization of the 
palm oil industry [3]. 
CNN has been proven effective in classifying visual 
objects in various fields including agriculture [1]. In this 
context, CNN can be trained to recognize visual features of 
ripe oil palm fruits such as color changes and surface textures 
[2]. The trained model is able to classify ripeness quickly and 
consistently. Research by Huda et al. (2020) showed that CNN 
can achieve ripeness detection accuracy of up to 95% [4]. 
Kamaluddin et al. (2021) developed a YOLOv3-based 
detection system to quickly identify ripe fruits although 
accuracy decreases in low light conditions. Rafique et al. 
(2023) used Faster R-CNN for apple and tomato ripeness 
classification and achieved mAP above 90%. However, most 
previous studies were conducted in controlled environments 
and did not consider varying field conditions. Therefore, this 
study proposes the development of a CNN-based ripeness 
detection system using real-world data from plantations and 
high-resolution cameras to address these challenges. Through 
this approach, it is expected that FFB ripeness classification 
solutions can be applied in real-time to improve the efficiency 
and productivity of the palm oil industry.

Classification is one of the main techniques in data 
processing that aims to group data into certain classes based 
on their characteristics. In the context of oil palm, 
classification is used to detect the ripeness level of Fresh Fruit 
Bunches (FFB) based on visual characteristics such as color 
and texture. Several previous studies have utilized various 
algorithms, such as Random Forest, K-Nearest Neighbor 
(KNN), Support Vector Machine (SVM), and Convolutional 
Neural Network (CNN). CNN excels in its ability to 
automatically extract features with a high level of accuracy, 
while SVM has proven effective in classifying FFB based on 
color features [5]. Due to the availability of labeled data such 
as ripe and unripe FFB images, supervised learning 
approaches are more commonly used. Recent research trends 
also show that hybrid models and deep learning techniques 
provide more accurate and efficient classification solutions 
[6].  
B. Machine Learning 
Machine Learning (ML) is a branch of artificial 
intelligence that focuses on developing algorithms that allow 
computer systems to learn from data without having to be 
explicitly programmed. In the digital agriculture sector, ML 
is widely used to process visual data such as object detection 
and classification, plant condition management, and data-
based decision making [4]. One example of its application is 
the classification of the ripeness level of oil palm FFB 
through fruit images [6]. Classic ML models such as Decision 
Tree, SVM, and Random Forest have been widely used and 
have shown good performance, especially when combined 
with color and texture features from image extraction [7]. 
However, deep learning approaches, especially CNN, are 
becoming more popular due to their ability to automatically 
recognize 
complex 
visual 
patterns 
without 
manual 
preprocessing stages. Kamaluddin et al. [8] have applied 
YOLOv4 for real-time detection and classification of ripe oil 
palm fruit with high accuracy, even in varying lighting 
conditions and viewing angles. Meanwhile, research by 
Nurhudatiana et al. [9] showed that CNN can achieve more 
than 90% accuracy in classifying the ripeness level of oil 
palm fruits based on RGB images, especially when combined 
with data augmentation and parameter optimization 
techniques. Overall, the application of ML in image-based 
agricultural systems is a superior solution for realizing 
efficient, accurate, and large-scale automation of the 
harvesting process, as well as driving the transformation of 
the palm oil industry towards digitalization and precision 
agriculture. 
C. YOLO 
YOLO (You Only Look Once) is one of the deep learning-
based object detection algorithms known for its speed and 
accuracy in detecting objects in real-time [21]. Unlike 
traditional object detection methods that process images in 
stages (such as Region Proposal Networks), YOLO performs 
object detection and classification in a single forward 
process. forward pass). This approach is well suited for real-
time applications, including in agricultural environments. In 
the context of oil palm FFB ripeness detection, YOLO has 
been shown to be effective in identifying ripe fruits on trees, 
even under changing natural lighting conditions [3]. Various 
YOLO variants such as YOLOv3, YOLOv4, and YOLOv5 
have each shown improved performance in terms of accuracy 
and computational efficiency [[7]. Kamaluddin et al. [8] 
successfully implemented YOLOv4 to detect ripe oil palm 
fruits with high accuracy and fast inference time [22], making 
it suitable for field applications using edge devices such as 
drones or mobile cameras. Furthermore, YOLOv5 as a lighter 
and more modular version supports model training on smaller 
datasets without sacrificing accuracy [5]. This is an ideal 
solution in plantations with limited data. Recent studies have 
also shown that YOLO performance can be improved through 
data augmentation and adjustment of backbone architectures 
such as CSPDarknet53, to strengthen the model's resilience 
to visual variations such as viewing angle and lighting 
intensity [6]. With its advantages in terms of speed and 
precision, YOLO is the main choice in the development of 
computer vision systems for automatic classification and 
detection of oil palm fruit ripeness [12]. 
D. CNN 
Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) is a deep learning 
architecture specifically designed for image processing, and is 
very effective in tasks such as classification, segmentation, 
and object detection. CNN recognizes visual patterns by 
extracting local features from images through multiple layers 
of convolution, pooling, and activation. This architecture has 
become a major foundation in modern computer vision 
systems[19]. In the realm of digital agriculture, especially in 
detecting the ripeness level of oil palm FFB, CNN has been 
widely used to classify fruit images based on their ripeness 
level [18][20]. CNN excels because it can extract color, 
texture, and shape features automatically, making it more 
effective than conventional methods that rely on hand-crafted 
features [13][17]. A study by Nurhudatiana et al. [9] showed 
that CNN can achieve classification accuracy above 90% 
using RGB images. Generally, CNN-based processes are 
combined with data augmentation techniques to improve the 
model's robustness to variations in lighting, appearance, and 
background. More sophisticated CNN variants such as 
VGGNet, ResNet, and EfficientNet have also been used in 
several studies to improve accuracy and computational 
efficiency. Several studies have combined CNN with object 
detection methods such as YOLO to develop end-to-end 
systems that can detect and classify ripe fruits automatically 
and quickly [7]. Due to its flexibility and superior 
performance in visual feature extraction, CNN is a technology 
that plays a very important role in the development of an 
automatic system for digital image-based identification of oil 
palm fruit ripeness.

III. MATERIAL AND METHODS 
A. .   Data Collection 
The dataset used in this study is the result of curation of 
Fresh Fruit Bunches (FFB) images of oil palm sourced from 
plantations in Johor, Negeri Sembilan, and Perak, Malaysia. 
The dataset was then further processed and publicly available 
under the Creative Commons BY 4.0 license [14]. 
The final dataset consists of 450 images that have been 
annotated using the polygon method. The images have been 
classified into five maturity condition categories, namely 
Damaged, Empty, Overripe, Ripe, Unripe. The sample for 
each class is provided in Fig 2. 
The initial dataset consists of 450 unique images. To 
improve the robustness and generalization ability of the 
model, 
the 
training 
dataset 
undergoes 
an 
offline 
augmentation process before training begins. A total of 300 
unique images from the initial dataset are allocated as the 
basis for the training set. Meanwhile, the validation and 
testing set still use unique images without the same weight 
augmentation, each consisting of 80 unique images for 
validation and 70 unique images for testing. This division 
ensures an objective evaluation of the model's ability to 
generalize on previously unseen data. The distribution of the 
number of object instances (polygons) for each maturity class 
across the entire 450 initial unique images is visualized in 
Fig. 3.

In Fig 3, there are two significant class imbalances. The 
classes 'Ripe' and 'Unripe' are the majority classes, while the 
classes 'Damaged' and 'Empty' have very limited instances. 
Augmentation efforts are expected to help mitigate some of 
the impact of this imbalance by providing more variety from 
the existing samples. 
B.   Hardware and Tools Used 
Experiments and training of machine learning models 
were conducted using hardware with an AMD Ryzen 7 
5800H CPU, 13.86 GB of RAM, and an NVIDIA GeForce 
RTX 3060 Laptop GPU (6 GB VRAM). The development 
environment ran on Python Version 3.11.11, using the main 
framework Ultralytics YOLO Version 8.3.96 supported by 
PyTorch and Torchvision. 
C.   Data Preprocessing 
Data preprocessing step is performed to prepare all 
images to be used in model training, with all dataset 
configurations stored in the data.yaml file. The preprocessing 
steps begin with automatic orientation correction (Auto-
Orient) to all images to ensure consistency. After that, all 
images will be resized to 640x640 resolution using the “Fit 
(black edges)” method. To increase the variety of datasets, 
data augmentation techniques are applied to 300 images from 
the training dataset, with a result in a total of 900 images. The 
image augmentation technique consists of flipping images 
(horizontal and vertical), rotation (ranging from -10° to 
+10°), hue adjustment (ranging from -15° to +15°), saturation 
(ranging from -30% to +30%), brightness (ranging from -
20% to +20%), exposure (ranging from -15% to +15°), 
blurring (maximum 0.5 pixels), and noise addition 
(maximum 1.6% of pixels). 
D.  Machine Learning Model 
This study conducted a comparative evaluation of 15 
variants of the YOLO object detection model with versions 
v8, v9, v10, v11, and v12 with nano, tiny, small, and medium 
sizes. Each model has three main components: backbone for 
feature extraction, neck for multi-scale aggregation, and head 
for object classification and bounding box prediction [11]. 
Training of each YOLO model variant was carried out 
separately using Python scripts and the Ultralytics library. In 
this training process, several main parameters were used that 
had been adjusted to optimize the performance of each model. 
The maximum number of epochs was set at 300, with an input 
image size (imgsz) of 640x640 pixels. The batch size was 
adjusted dynamically based on the model type: 32 for the ‘n’ 
and ‘t’ variants, 16 for ‘s’, 8 for ‘m’, and 4 for other variants.

The optimizer used is AdamW, with an initial learning rate 
(lr0) of 0.005 and a final learning rate (lrf) of 0.00005. The 
learning rate scheduling uses a cosine approach (cos_lr = 
True), while the warmup epoch is set for 3.0 epochs, with a 
warmup bias learning rate (warmup_bias_lr) of 0.01. In 
addition, the weight decay value used is 0.001. To prevent 
overfitting scenarios, an early stopping mechanism is applied 
with a patience value of 20 epochs.

the 15 YOLO model variants supporting instance 
segmentations, using training sets of 900 augmented images 
combined with 80 sets of unique images as validation set. The 
training process for each model was run with a maximum of 
300 epochs or stopped by early stopping mechanism if there 
are no improvements in mAP@0.5 metric in validation set for 
20 consecutive epochs. All models have succeeded in training 
process with all provided parameters. To provide an overview

of the key metrics at the epoch where each model achieved

best model (best.pt) and then used for final evaluation on test 
set.

component of a detection or segmentation model, by

that the model predicts. Generally, the row dimension of the 
confusion matrix specifies the predicted classifications, and 
the column dimension specifies the true classifications. Each 
cell of the matrix represents the number of times the 
occurrences belong to that combination of actual and 
forecasted classes. The principal diagonal of the matrix (top

V. CONCLUSION 
This study has successfully developed and evaluated an 
automatic detection system for the ripeness level of oil palm 
Fresh Fruit Bunches (FFB) using various variants of the 
YOLO deep learning model. From a series of experiments, it 
was found that the YOLOv10n model showed the best 
performance with an mAP@0.5 achievement of 0.9007 and 
an F1-Score of 0.8953 on a test dataset consisting of five 
ripeness classes. This model, along with strong alternatives 
such 
as 
YOLOv8s 
and 
YOLOv11s, 
can 
perform 
classification with high accuracy without significant 
indications of overfitting, proving the great potential of AI-
based computer vision technology in improving the 
objectivity and efficiency of the FFB ripeness determination 
process compared to manual methods. The implementation of 
this system is supported by a representative public dataset and 
careful pre-processing and data augmentation methodology. 
For future development, several important steps are 
recommended. First, expanding and diversifying the dataset, 
especially adding samples for minority classes and covering 
more diverse field conditions, are crucial to improve the 
robustness of the model. Second, further exploration of class 
imbalance handling techniques and model optimization for 
edge devices (e.g. through quantization) would be very 
beneficial for practical applications. Third, implementing and 
testing the system in real-time operational scenarios in the 
field, e.g. using drones or handheld devices, is a logical next 
step to validate its feasibility and effectiveness. Finally, 
comparative analysis with other detection architectures and 
economic feasibility studies can further strengthen the 
contribution of this research. With continuous improvements 
based on these suggestions, it is expected that the AI-based 
FFB ripeness detection system (with the YOLO framework) 
can be a significant innovative solution for the palm oil 
industry towards more productive precision farming 
practices.
