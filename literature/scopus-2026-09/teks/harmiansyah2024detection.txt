Abstract. The Indonesian oil palm plantation experiences a production rate of fresh fruit
bunches and crude palm oil (CPO) influenced by the quality of fruits with excellent and uniform
maturity accepted by the palm oil processing factory. The uniformity of fruit quality produced
from the harvesting process through fruit maturity criteria, namely fractions 00, fraction 1,
fraction 3, and fraction 4, necessitates the need for maturity detection based on deep learning
with a pre-trained YOLOv5 model capable of detecting the maturity of new oil palm fruit
bunches. The aim is to develop a deep learning-based maturity detection model based on visual
images and analyse the deep learning algorithm's performance by classifying the maturity
level of new oil palm fruit bunches. The method collects a dataset of new oil palm fruit
bunches with 4180 images. The collected dataset will be annotated with Roboflow, so the
annotation results will undergo training, validation, and testing processes using the deep
learning script. The result obtained for the data found to detect the ripeness levels of Fresh
Fruit Bunches (FFB), shows that the YOLOV5 model demonstrates strong performance with
an accuracy reaching 72.5% in testing, which falls into the category of fair annotation. These
results indicate that the system created and used is functioning well.
1. Introduction
The palm oil plantations in Indonesia have a Fresh Fruit Bunch (FFB) production rate of 45.15
million tons from 2017 to 2021, serving as the raw material for Crude Palm Oil (CPO) [1]. The CPO
production was 48.42 million tons or 12.92% from 2014 to 2019 [2]. However, in early 2020, palm
oil (CPO) production experienced a decline of 44.76 million tons or 5.01%, attributed to the COVID-
19 pandemic. The CPO yield in oil palm is influenced by mesocarp thickness, low free fatty acids (1.7
to 2.1%), and low moisture content [3,4]. A good oil palm yield can reach up to 22% of the fresh
fruit bunch weight [5].
The CPO yield in oil palm is influenced by the quality of fruit with excellent and uniform
maturity, which the palm oil processing plant accepts. The uniformity of fruit quality resulting from
good harvesting and transport processes is generally visually assessed by harvesters and transport
workers. There is a need to develop camera-based technology equipped with YOLO (You Only Look
Once) deep learning algorithms to recognise fruit quality. Previous research conducted by
researchers in the application of YOLO-based cameras includes a system for detecting the maturity
of new oil palm fruit bunches were produced using the YOLOv4 method. This method demonstrates
effective modelling by distinguishing targets according to the oil palm fruit maturity class [6].

You Only Look Once (YOLO) is an algorithm from deep learning that uses a Convolutional Neural
Networks (CNN) approach. It is applied by processing images in real-time at 45 frames per second to
detect objects [7]. Based on previous developments using YOLOv4 to detect the maturity level of oil
palm, the YOLO model significantly aids harvesters in determining which fruit is suitable for harvesting.
However, YOLOV4 requires the assistance of technologies such as Path Aggregation Network (PAN),
Cross min-batch Normalization (CBN), Spatial Attention Module (SAM), and others, which have been
modified to reduce the expensive GPU usage in object recognition. On the other hand, YOLOv5 has the
advantage of processing images in rmapeal-time using the PyTorch framework in various versions,
including small, medium, large, and Xlarge, for effective object detection [6]. Therefore, there is a need
for maturity detection based on deep learning using the YOLOV5 model that can detect the maturity of
new oil palm fruit bunches. The aim is to develop a deep learning-based maturity detection model based
on visual images and analyse the performance of the deep learning algorithm by classifying the maturity
level of new oil palm fruit bunches.
2. Material and Methods
Data analysis
The tools and materials used are a laptop, Roboflow, Google Colab, a camera, Google Drive, and oil
palm fruit bunches. The computer is used for data processing, Roboflow is a platform for annotating oil
palm fruit bunches according to their classes, Google Colaboratory is a platform for developing and
running YOLOV5 Python code, a mobile phone camera is used for capturing image data, Google Drive
is a Google software used for storing data and images, and oil palm fruit bunches serve as the research
object, with 4180 pictures taken by maturity criteria.
This research consists of problem formulation, literature review, and identifying issues arising from the
fruit maturity classification process in the valuation process. The collection of images involves capturing
fresh fruit bunch (FFB) images, followed by labelling them according to the provided maturity criteria
using the YOLOV5 model. Labelled images result in creating the original dataset, which then undergoes
training using Google Colab. After the training process, the datset will undergo validation testing. The
validation testing results will provide accuracy, precision, recall, and mean-Average Precision (mAP)
values. If the obtained mAP value exceeds 65%, the testing process will proceed; otherwise, the dataset
will be revisited if the mAP value is less than 65%. A lower mAP value may indicate errors in the dataset
provided to the model. The testing results include the accuracy of the tested images and the model's
capability to interpret the provided images.
2.2.1. Data Collection. Data was collected in farmers' fields in Wonosari Village, Gunung Sugih
Subdistrict, Central Lampung Regency. The data were acquired using a camera on a mobile phone with a
quad-camera Al operating system featuring a Dual Camera 50MP with a pixel density of 405ppi. Images
were categorized into several fractions: very raw, underripe, ripe II, and overripe. Criteria and examples
of pictures for each fraction can be found in Table 1.

be used in the YOLOv5 training process.

The box loss is used as a metric to measure the loss value of bounding boxes in the readable dataset.
The box loss graph in Figure 1 shows a decreasing trend over 320 epochs, with the highest box loss
reaching 0.0912% and the lowest at 0.0285%. The initial high value of box loss is influenced by the
model learning from the oil palm fresh bunch maturity images dataset. Objectness Loss (Figure 2),
representing the loss value for objects within bounding boxes, has a value of 0.025919 (2.6%), indicating
a low error rate, and the better the model, the lower the loss value. Figure 3 depicts a graph of the
classification loss values obtained from the training process, showing a decreasing trend as the training
progresses. Classification loss reflects the error in recognizing objects based on class labels, decreasing
during training to reach 0.012076 (1.2%). Total loss (Figure 4), a combination of box loss, objectness
loss, and classification loss, shows a decreasing trend to reach 0.066493 (6.65%). The system using the
pre-trained YOLOV5 model has a low error rate in object recognition, with increased accuracy as
training data and epoch values increase.

4. Conclusion
Based on the new data found to detect the ripeness levels of Fresh Fruit Bunches (FFB), it can be
concluded that the YOLOv5 model demonstrates strong performance with an accuracy reaching 72.5%
in testing, which falls into the category of fair annotation. Precision and recall have a range of values
between 63% and 100%, indicating the model's suitability for ripeness detection. Although the mean
average precision (mAP) at loU 0.5 shows good performance, the mAP at loU 0.95 indicates a slight
decrease in accuracy. Overall, YOLOv5 has proven effective for fruit maturity classification in oil palm
plantations despite variations in performance across all classes and loU thresholds.
