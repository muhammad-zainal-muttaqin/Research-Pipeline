Abstract
We introduce PineSORT, a novel Multiple Object Track-
ing (MOT) system for drone-based agricultural monitoring,
specically tracking pineapples for yield estimation. Our
approach tackles key challenges such as repetitive patterns,
similar object appearances, low frame rates, and drone
motion effects. PineSORT enhances the tracking accuracy
with motion direction cost, camera motion compensation,
a three-stage association strategy, and overlap manage-
ment. To handle large displacements, we propose an ORB-
based camera compensation technique that signicantly
improves the Association Accuracy (AssA). Evaluated via
5-fold cross-validation against BoTSORT and AgriSORT,
PineSORT achieves statistically signicant gains in our
Identity-Switch Penalized IDF1 (ISP-IDF1) metric, along
with gains in IDF1 (Identity F1 Score), HOTA (Higher Or-
der Tracking Accuracy) and AssA. These results conrm its
effectiveness in tracking low-FPS drone footage, making it
a valuable tool for precision agriculture.
1. Introduction
Multiple Object Tracking (MOT) is a fundamental task in
computer vision that involves detecting and tracking mul-
tiple objects across a sequence of video frames. The goal
is to determine the object locations in each frame while
maintaining their identities over time, even as they move,
interact, or become occluded temporarily. MOT has broad
applications in elds such as action recognition, pose es-
timation [28], surveillance [2], autonomous driving [40],
sports analysis [9], and human-computer interaction. How-
ever, their application in agricultural environments, partic-
ularly for crop monitoring using drones, presents unique
challenges that require specialized solutions.
Drones have become valuable tools in agriculture ow-
ing to their ability to capture high-resolution data over large
areas. However, applying MOT in agricultural elds in-
troduces several challenges related to the characteristics of
both the environment and objects being tracked. For in-
stance, pineapples, similar to many agricultural products,
exhibit high visual similarity and are arranged in repeti-
tive patterns, making it difcult to distinguish individual
instances accurately.
Additionally, occlusions caused by
leaves or other vegetation can partially or completely ob-
scure fruits. These factors increase the likelihood of identity
switches in tracking algorithms and reduce reliability.
Further complexity arises because of the motion of the
drone. Even in cases where the drone moves in a single di-
rection while the pineapples remain stationary, the objects
in the image undergo continuous changes in position, scale,
and perspective. Motion blur and changes in the frame rate
can exacerbate tracking errors, particularly when the drone
moves at high speeds, leading to inconsistent object associ-
ations.
Accurate MOT in agriculture is crucial for applica-
tions such as yield estimation, harvesting automation, and
crop monitoring. However, several challenges hinder real-
world implementation, including occlusions, dense object
arrangements, and redundant detections. The latter occurs
when multiple bounding boxes are generated for a single
object, leading to tracking inconsistencies. Moreover, the
sparse distribution of pineapples in elds, with large empty
spaces between them, adds another layer of difculty. Tra-
ditional tracking approaches often struggle with scale varia-
tions, occlusions, and high visual similarity among objects.
To tackle these challenges, it is crucial to develop ad-
vanced MOT methods that can effectively handle dynamic
scenes caused by drone movement while maintaining real-
time performance. These methods have to be robust against
occlusions, redundant detections, and the repetitive patterns
commonly found in pineapple elds.
Overcoming these
limitations will enhance MOT systems, improving agricul-
tural monitoring and automation.
This, in turn, enables
more efcient and precise crop management. Additionally,
ensuring accurate object counting is essential for reliable
yield estimation and preventing multiple detections of the

and a three-stage object association based on Intersection

• We introduce a novel MOT SORT-based tracker based on

• We propose the use of motion features and Oriented

2. Related work
2.1. Multiple Object Tracking
The Simple Online and Realtime Tracking (SORT) [6] fam-
ily of algorithms has become a cornerstone in MOT, em-
ploying a tracking-by-detection paradigm that involves de-
tection and association stages. In the detection stage, ob-
ject detectors like YOLO [29], Faster R-CNN [31], or
SSD [22] localize objects in each frame using bounding
boxes, while the association stage links detections across
frames using the Hungarian algorithm [20]. SORT variants
have evolved to address various MOT challenges. Deep-
SORT [36] enhances SORT by incorporating appearance
information, while ByteTrack [41] introduces a hierarchi-
cal data association strategy to leverage low-score detec-
tions. BoTSORT [1] integrates motion and appearance cues
with camera-motion compensation, and StrongSORT [11]
improves DeepSORT with tools like AFLink for track re-
covery and GSI for handling temporary object losses. OC-
SORT [8] addresses Kalman lter limitations by incorpo-
rating observation-centric corrections, and DeepOCSORT
[24] further integrates appearance cues. HybridSORT [39]
leverages both strong and weak cues for improved perfor-
mance in occlusion and clustering scenarios. Finally, SF-
SORT [26] introduces the Bounding Box Similarity Index,
replacing the Kalman Filter. The SORT family is favored
for its simplicity and adaptability in MOT tasks.
2.2. Multiple Object Tracking in Agriculture
There have been many recent advancement in precision
agriculture [12–14, 16, 38].
Regarding multiple object
tracking, it has focused on addressing challenges specic to
dynamic eld environments. NTrack [27], a novel frame-
work, utilizes dense optical ow and particle ltering to
track cotton bolls, outperforming existing methods. An-
other approach employs fusion point cloud data and an im-
proved Graph Neural Network (GNN) method for object
tracking in farmlands, achieving high accuracy and preci-
sion [17]. An EKF-based multi-object tracking framework
has been developed for mobile robots in precision agricul-
ture, specically for tracking table-grape bunches in vine-
yards [4]. AgriSORT, a simple online real-time tracking-
by-detection pipeline, focuses on efciency and exibility
for agricultural applications, particularly in challenging en-
vironments like table grape vineyards [34]. These methods
enhance crop monitoring, yield prediction, and robotic har-
vesting but lack drone-based precision for yield estimation.
3. Proposed Method
PineSORT introduces a cutting-edge approach to MOT in
agricultural drone footage, incorporating three key innova-
tions that signicantly enhance tracking performance. Our
novel pipeline integrates (1) a camera compensation using
ORB features (Fig. 1-Estimate Camera Motion), (2) a three-
stage association process (Fig. 1-Third Association), and
(3) a novel overlapping management (Fig. 1-Overlap Man-
agement based on IoU) that effectively handles challenging
detection scenarios. These enhancements collectively ad-
dress the unique challenges of drone-based tracking, such
as low frame rates, repetitive patterns, and camera-induced
motion distortions.
Our proposed pipeline works as follows: Upon receiving
an input frame at time t, the system estimates camera mo-
tion compensation using ORB features between frames at
times t  1 and t (see details in Sec. 3.1). It then computes
the afnity matrix transformation and correct the Kalman
lter for each tracked object’s state, executing the predic-
tion step within the Kalman lter.
The object detector generates detection proposals cate-
gorized into low and high condence detections. These de-
tections are processed in the rst association step, which
employs a threshold  based on the IoU metric to match ob-
ject detections with tracked states. The second association
step similarly utilizes IoU for matching. Unmatched detec-
tions and tracklets from the rst two association steps are
processed in a third association step (see Sec. 3.2), which
identies matches among the remaining unmatched detec-
tions and tracklets.
Matched tracklets from all three association steps are
subsequently managed by the tracklet management system
(Sec. 3.3). When a detection is absent, the system checks
for overlaps using IoU with other tracklets to minimize
redundancy.

3.3. Overlapping Management
The overlapping management mechanism computes the
IoU between track bounding boxes Btrack and detec-
tion bounding boxes Bdet when creating a new track
for detections not matched in the association steps.
If
the computed IoU exceeds a predened threshold t, i.e.,
IoU(Btrack, Bdet) > t, redundant detections are removed
to prevent ID switches. This mechanism is particularly crit-
ical in sparse agricultural environments, where overlapping
objects are uncommon but can still occur due to detection
errors, occlusions, or perspective effects. By ltering out
redundant detections, the system enhances tracking accu-
racy and robustness, ensuring reliable performance in ap-
plications such as yield estimation and automated harvest-
ing. The threshold t is empirically determined to optimize
the trade-off between minimizing false positives and pre-
serving valid detections, thereby maintaining the integrity
of object trajectories.
4. Experiments
This section outlines the experimental setup, evaluation
metrics, and benchmark results, comparing PineSORT to
the baseline and analyzing its performance.
4.1. Dataset
The dataset includes 10 videos from a pineapple planta-
tion from Upala Agricola [3] (the world’s largest exporter
of fresh pineapple), recorded with a DJI Mavic 3 drone at
1920×1080 resolution. Videos capture crop rows from ver-
tical (90°) and oblique (45°) angles, lasting 7–35 seconds
(183–882 frames each).
For MOT, we manually annotated the dataset using La-
belStudio [35]. The annotations were converted into the
MOT format [21], serving as the ground truth labels. The
nal annotations were exported at a frame rate of 25 Frame
per second (FPS).
4.2. Evaluation Metrics
We evaluate tracking performance using TrackEval [19],
which provides comprehensive metrics.
Overall perfor-
mance is assessed with Multiple Object Tracking Accu-
racy (MOTA) [5], considering false positives, missed detec-
tions, and identity mismatches, along with Identity F1 Score
(IDF1) [32] and Higher Order Tracking Accuracy (HOTA)
[23], the geometric mean of Detection Accuracy (DetA) and
Association Accuracy (AssA).
Given the importance of preserving object identities for
yield estimation, we emphasize AssA, which measures
identity consistency, and Identity Switches (IDSW) [5],
which counts identity switches. We also assess trajectory-
level metrics: Mostly tracked (MT) (trajectories tracked
for  80% of their duration), Mostly lost (ML) (trajecto-
ries lost for > 80%), and Partially tracked (PT) (objects
tracked for 20–80% of their lifespan). Additional measures
include Identication Precision (IDP) and Identication Re-
call (IDR) for identity-based analysis.
To account for identity preservation, we introduce the
Identity-Switch Penalized IDF1 (ISP-IDF1) metric incor-
porating an exponential penalty based on IDSW. This nor-
malization penalizes high ID switch counts while having a
minimal effect when IDSW is low, for futher analysis in the
evaluation dataset:

4.3. Implementation details
We use YOLOv11n [18] as the detector for its speed-
accuracy balance. The model was trained on the pineapple
dataset using k-fold cross-validation, training on one video
and evaluating on the rest. Training data included vertical,
oblique, and combined camera orientations.
4.4. Trackers for comparison
We evaluate our approach against state-of-the-art (SOTA)
SORT-based MOT methods, specically SORT, ByteTrack,
DeepOCSORT, StrongSORT, BoTSORT, and AgriSORT. In
addition, to isolate the impact of motion-based tracking, we
use BoTSORT without the Re-Identication (ReID) module
in our analysis.
We also include HybridSORT and OCSORT, which im-
prove robustness in occlusion and non-linear motion scenar-
ios. Finally, we examine SFSORT, which replaces the KF
with a custom cost function for better accuracy.

Among the evaluated trackers, AgriSORT exhibited the
least favorable performance metrics, characterized by a no-
tably high IDSW across various combinations of camera
evaluation settings and training protocols. This high IDSW
was the highest recorded among all trackers, coupled with
low ISP-IDF1 scores. However, AgriSORT demonstrated
a high PT, indicating effective tracking within 20%-80%
of the object lifespan. This suboptimal performance can
be attributed to its reliance on the Lucas-Kanade method
for sparse optical ow to estimate camera motion. Addi-
tionally, AgriSORT replaces the Kalman lter’s transition
matrix with an afne transformation between consecutive
frames t1 and t during the state prediction phase. The ef-
cacy of this ltering approach is highly contingent upon the
congruence of the afne matrix with the underlying system
dynamics. Discrepancies in the afne matrix can lead to sig-
nicant degradation of track predictions, particularly in low
FPS scenarios, where increased matching errors and chal-
lenges in accurately capturing motion components—such as
translation, rotation, and scaling—culminate in heightened
estimation errors. Furthermore, the optical ow technique
operates under the assumption of small camera displace-
ments, which limits its effectiveness in scenarios involving
substantial motion.
As observed in Table 2, SFSORT exhibits a consider-
able number of identity switches, resulting in a lower IDR
compared to other trackers. This indicates its inadequacy in
accurately maintaining object identities across frames in ac-
cordance with the ground truth. These performance metrics
highlight the limitations of its cost index function, which re-
lies solely on object bounding box characteristics and lacks
integration of a Kalman lter for motion prediction. The
absence of a Kalman lter compromises temporal consis-
tency, leading to an overreliance on shape similarity, dis-
tance, and bounding box overlap. When an object is only
partially visible upon entering or exiting the frame, its size
may be underestimated, causing a decline in shape similar-
ity scores as the object becomes fully visible in subsequent
frames. Moreover, larger displacements between frames at
FPS further increase the likelihood of identity switches, as
they contribute to a decrease in bounding box overlap.
BoTSORT demonstrates a lower ID switch rate com-
pared to most other tracking methods, with the exception of
PineSORT. However, it still experiences some ID switches
due to its reliance on sparse optical ow for feature track-
ing and RANSAC for afne matrix estimation. While this
approach aims to accurately estimate background motion,
the inherent assumptions of optical ow may not adequately
capture large displacements between frames.
We antici-
pated that StrongSORT and DeepOCSORT, which depend
on feature embeddings to enhance object association be-
tween tracks and predictions, would perform poorly since
they were not trained on our dataset for feature embeddings
comparison. StrongSORT, which relies heavily on appear-
ance features, exhibits a high frequency of ID switches in
our scenario, particularly when tracking homogeneous ob-
jects, leading to mismatches and inaccuracies, especially
at low FPS with signicant object displacement. In con-
trast, DeepOCSORT demonstrates improved performance
over StrongSORT, likely due to the introduction of Camera
Motion Compensation using optical ow and RANSAC, re-
sulting in a substantial difference in the ISP-IDF1 metric.
There is not a statistical difference between the track-
ers HybridSORT and OCSORT indicating similar perfor-
mance for our benchmark in agricultural applications, as
both tracking algorithms are predicated on the same SORT
framework. Given the unidirectional trajectory of the drone,
the implementation of camera compensation is critical for
accurately capturing the dynamics of this movement. In
instances where the object detector fails to identify a tar-
get in the subsequent frame, it may inadvertently assign
new identiers to newly detected objects in the next frame,
while neglecting to update the states of the previously
tracked objects.
The tracking algorithms that do not in-
tegrate this feature include SORT, HybriSORT, OC-SORT,
ByteTrack, and StrongSORT. Conversely, AgriSORT, BoT-
SORT, and PineSORT uniquely incorporate camera com-
pensation techniques to facilitate the updating and correc-
tion of object states based on camera movement.
The analysis of tracking performance across various con-
gurations of training data reveals that trackers utilizing
a combined dataset of vertical and oblique orientations
demonstrate superior performance. This enhancement can
be attributed to the increased volume of training data, which
allows the model to better capture the inherent variability
present in agricultural environments. These environments
are often characterized by clutter, diverse variations, and the
unique challenges posed by pineapples obscured by foliage.

performance. This observation suggests that recording agri-

optimally facilitates the detection and tracking of objects,
resulting in reduced variability and improved distinguisha-
bility of the target objects. Conversely, models trained and
evaluated using oblique data exhibit lower performance lev-
els. The diverse perspectives captured by the drone when
operating in an oblique orientation introduce increased vari-

5.3. Motion-based Method Qualitative Analysis
In our qualitative comparative analysis of motion tracker-
based methods, we evaluate our PineSORT algorithm
alongside BoTSORT and AgriSORT. As illustrated in Fig.
3, PineSORT consistently maintains object IDs across con-
secutive frames t  1 and t. In contrast, BoTSORT experi-
ences frequent ID switches due to signicant displacements
between tracked and detected objects, resulting in the as-
signment of new IDs in frame t and compromising iden-
tity consistency. This inconsistency is primarily attributed
to BoTSORT’s reliance on the Lukas-Kanade optical ow
method for estimating motion between frames.
Similarly, AgriSORT encounters challenges with un-
matched tracked objects resulting from large displacements,
leading to the generation of new IDs, akin to the issues
faced by BoTSORT. AgriSORT employs an afnity matrix
derived from Lukas-Kanade optical ow, which substitutes
the transition matrix F in the Kalman lter’s prediction step
from frame t  1 to frame t. This method does not remove
unmatched tracks during track management, leading to in-
creased identity switches and adversely affecting the consis-
tent identication of each object. Consequently, these lim-
itations hinder the performance of agricultural video drone
counting analyses.
6. Conclusion
In this paper, we introduced PineSORT, a novel MOT
tracker designed for drone videos in agricultural settings.
The framework integrates key components such as a three-
stage association process, motion direction cost, camera
compensation, and overlap management, signicantly im-
proving tracking performance, especially in dynamic condi-
tions with rapid object movement and low frame rates. We
improved bounding box predictions and reduced identity
switches in dense environments by integrating a KF with
a constant-velocity model and expanding the state vector to
include width and height.
Tested on drone footage from a pinneaple plantation
case, PineSORT leverages ORB for effective camera com-
pensation in low FPS drone footage, outperforming tradi-
tional optical ow method. PineSORT consistently outper-
formed baseline trackers, including BoTSORT, in terms of
IDF1 and AssA scores, demonstrating its robustness in agri-
cultural object tracking. Overall, PineSORT provides a re-
liable solution for agricultural tracking, laying the ground-
work for future developments in yield estimation and auto-
mated harvesting, with a focus on scalability and improving
performance in more challenging environments.
