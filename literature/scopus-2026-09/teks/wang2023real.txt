Abstract—The automatic fruit picking and yield estimation of
agricultural intelligent machinery relies on accurate fruit de-
tection and positioning technology. Since the complex environ-
ment of the orchard and the huge size of the existing models
hinder the real-time detection of fruits, we propose a real-
time fruit detection method based on RGB-D image fusion.
The method ﬁrst uses two extraction modules with the same
structure to extract multi-modal features, then makes full use
of complementary multi-modal features through an attention
fusion module, and ﬁnally sends the multi-scale single-input
and fusion features to the prediction module to generate the
results. In addition, to better ﬁt the apple shape and reduce
parameters for lighting the network to meet the requirement of
real-time, we replace the traditional rectangular bounding box
with a circular bounding box. We veriﬁed the deep network
model using the KFuji RGB-DS apple dataset and obtained
94.3%, 90.2%, and 94.0% results for AP50, Precision, and
Recall. The accuracy of our method is comparable with those
of YOLOv5s and Faster R-CNN in object detection tasks, and
our network obviously outperforms them in the number of
parameters and reasoning speed. In conclusion, our method
reduces the hardware equipment requirements to achieve real-
time rates and provide ﬂat accuracy and reasoning speed
synchronously.
Keywords–multi-modal; feature fusion; deep learning; real-
time detection; fruit detection
1. INTRODUCTION
The increasing sown area and output of fruits [1] aggravate
the problems of high labor cost, labor shortage and low efﬁ-
ciency [2] in agricultural production. The rapid development
of artiﬁcial intelligence promotes the development of agri-
cultural robot picking technology, among which fruit image
recognition plays an important role in the fruit robot picking
technology. The recognition accuracy represents whether the
precise picking of fruit can be achieved. Therefore, agricultural
mechanization and intelligence is the trend of future agricul-
tural development, and more accurate automatic fruit detection
and positioning will have a positive impact on mechanized
harvest and yield estimation.
In object detection tasks, the deep learning method based on
a single input, especially the deep learning method based on
RGB images, has become a common algorithm for fruit detec-
tion [3][4][5][6]. Wang [7] et al. proposed an improved Mask
Scoring region-based convolutional neural network (Mask
Scoring R-CNN), which can accurately detect and segment
apples under various conditions in orchards in real-time in
natural environments. Sun [8] et al. proposed an upgraded
lightweight apple detection method, YOLOv5-PRE, which is
more suitable for transplantation into embedded devices and
can well adapt to different lighting conditions in orchards.
Sekharamantry [9] et al. proposed an apple detection scheme
based on the Yolov5 algorithm, which can detect smaller
objects and improve feature quality to detect apples in complex
backgrounds. Other researchers use binocular cameras to cap-
ture apple data images [10] or enhanced multi-type occlusion
apple data sets [11] to solve complex situations such as target
occlusion.
These studies have greatly improved the performance of fruit
detection based on RGB images. However, this kind of al-
gorithm will produce error detection in the face of complex
tasks requiring multiple information, such as overlapping apple
recognition of demand depth information, but only has RGB
information. The multi-modal deep learning algorithm can
make up for the deﬁciency of the single-input algorithm and
bring better detection results by combining a variety of modal
information with complementary and consistency [2].
In the ﬁeld of computer vision, using multi-modal data to
improve model performance has become an effective way
[12][13]. For example, Xu [14] et al. proposed a weed
detection method based on RGB-D multi-modal information
fusion for accurate detection of weeds in wheat ﬁelds in
natural environment. Yu [15] et al. utilized the redundant
image background information, and then fed RGB features
into network to train a classiﬁcation model for litchi fruits
identiﬁcation. Tu [16] et al. proposed an improved method
based on multi-modal multi-scale Faster R-CNN, which can
stably detect smaller fruits.
Much progress has been made in multi-modal fusion fruit
detection, but the goal of fruit detection is to deploy on
agricultural machinery for real-time automatic yield estimation
or picking, which requires fruit detection networks deployed
on hardware to have as few parameters as possible. Therefore,
we propose a real-time fruit detection method based on RGB-
D image fusion. The system consists of an extraction module,
a fusion module, and a prediction module. This paper makes
the following main contributions:
• In order to extract data features, we design a feature
extraction network referring to several face detection deep
learning models and combine it with Network In Network
(NIN) structure [17] to reduce the number of parameters.
• For avoiding the noise effect of preprocessed depth images,
we use a feature fusion module based on the attention

2. RELATED WORK
Multi-modal learning models can be classiﬁed as multi-modal
shallow models and multi-modal deep models according to the
fusion methods of the network [18].
2.1. Multi-modal Shallow Models
Multi-modal shallow models are widely used in target de-
tection in the agricultural ﬁeld. This model type usually
combines multi-layer perception with morphological operators
and subspace learning [18] to process different modal input
data to obtain global features, which is applied to downstream
tasks. Such as, Bai [19] proposes an algorithm based on multi-
scale top-hat transformation and switching contrast operator,
which can retain more useful image information; Liu [20] et
al. utilize the time information in the ﬁne image sequence and
used the two-stream convolutional neural network StfNet to
solve the spatio-temporal fusion problem. Additionally, some
related follow-up research results [21][22] are proposed, using
more advanced fusion strategies to enhance the information
mixing ability between multi-modal data.
2.2. Multi-modal Deep Models
Multi-modal deep fusion networks can be divided into three
types according to the different fusion locations of features
in the network: data-level fusion, decision-level fusion, and
feature-level fusion [28]. Data-level fusion usually aligns and
fuses data collected by different sensors before sending it
to the network. In decision-level fusion, the ﬁnal result is
obtained through a discriminant decision model after the data
of different modes are fed into the deep learning network
respectively. Feature-level fusion considers the correlation
between different types of input data, which is superior to
feature-level fusion in some tasks, considering the correlation
between different types of input data [29].
With the popularity of Deep learning in recent years, deep
multi-modal fusion is also applied to fruit detection in agri-
culture. As a kind of algorithm inheriting the characteristics
of high accuracy of deep learning, this kind of algorithm

has made signiﬁcant progress [23][24] in multi-modal fruit
detection. Islam [25] et al. use an attention-based dilated CNN
to extract the most relevant features faster, generated high-
quality preprocessed images through a conditional generative

morphological feature values of the point cloud model for
yield predictions.
3. METHODOLOGY
3.1. Overview
The multi-modal fusion system proposed in this paper consists
of three modules: extraction module for feature extraction,
fusion module for feature fusion, and prediction module for
result predict. The illustration of the network is shown in
Figure 1.
The network sends the input RGB image and depth image into
two feature extractors (Extractor −A and Extractor −B)
with the same structure but different parameters. Then the
feature extraction module in the network extracts features of
different scales from RGB images and depth images (An
and Bn) and sends them to the attention fusion module
(Fusion n) for feature fusion, and obtains the fused features
(Cn). The network ﬁnally sends the fusion feature (Cn)
and the RGB image feature (An) to the pyramidal structure
prediction module to predict the target. Details of each module
will be added later.
3.2. Extract Module
Fruit detection as a single target detection task has similarities
with the research in the ﬁeld of face detection, so this paper
refers to several deep learning models in the ﬁeld of face
detection [30][31][32] and designs a lightweight feature ex-
traction network with a small number of network parameters.
The detailed structure of the network is shown in Figure 2,

of ensuring the effective extraction of feature information, the
kernel sizes of four convolution operations are set to 7×7,
3×3, 5×5, 3×3.
Feature extraction pipeline outputs feature maps of different
scales at Inception3, Conv3 2 and Conv4 2 to ensure
that targets of different sizes can be successfully detected.
Inception module detects target objects of different scales by
calculating different size receptive domains of input feature
maps. The Inception module is composed of multiple convo-
lutional branches with different kernels, so it has multi-scale
receptive ﬁelds to capture target objects of different sizes.
While increasing the adaptability of the network to multi-scale
targets, it also avoids overﬁtting and high computational costs
caused by a too deep network. The network implementation
of the Inception module is shown in Figure 3. The input of
the ﬁrst Inception module Inception1 in Figure 2 comes from
the result of Pool2. After 3 consecutive Inception modules,
the output of the last Inception module Inception3 is sent to
Conv3 2.
As shown in Figure 3, when the input features enter the
Inception module, it will be simultaneously sent to 4 different
processing branches to capture target objects of different
sizes. In the feature extraction module, after several Inception
operations, the size of the feature map gradually decreases,
and then a multi-scale feature map is formed for subsequent
multi-modal feature fusion and target prediction.

4. EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURE
4.1. Dataset
KFuji RGB-DS dataset [33] is used in this study for exper-
iments, which was collected at the commercial Fuji apple
orchard in Catalonia, Spain, between September 25 and 28
in 2017, as well as three weeks before harvest during the
growing phase [34]. The data acquisition device consisted
of two RGB-D cameras mounted on mobile platforms with
heights of 1 m and 3 m to capture data at all tree heights.
The RGB-D sensors are two Microsoft Kinect v2 consisting
of an RGB camera and a time-of-ﬂight depth sensor [33]. The
sensor provides 3 different types of data: a color image, a
depth image that can be used to generate a 3D point cloud
of the scene, and the received infrared backscatter intensity.
All captures were taken at night using artiﬁcial lighting, as
depth sensor performance degrades in direct sunlight. Table 1
shows the relevant speciﬁcations and parameters of the data
acquisition equipment.
For each frame, the sensor provides a 3D point cloud with
RGB and backscattered intensity data, as well as a raw RGB
image. First, a range correction is performed on the intensity
signal. The 3D point cloud is then projected onto a 2D plane
parallel to the sensor, producing a distance-corrected image
and a depth image. Finally, the projected images are resized
and cropped to register them with the RGB original images.
A 5-channel 3-modality image of the scene was obtained with
high resolution for the RGB channels.
Due to the large number of apples in each original image,
most images have more than 100 apples, and considering the

4.2. Experiment Settings
All the programs in this experiment are coded under the
PyTorch 1.10 deep learning framework by Python 3.7 and run
on one NVIDIA GTX 1060 GPU. For all convolutional neural
network experiments, the batch size is 32, and the models are
trained in 100 iterations. In addition, the initial learning rate is
set to 10−4, and the 10th and 40th iterations are set to 10−5,
10−6. In order to initialize parameters randomly, we utilize
the Xavier method [35]. At last, we set the weight ratio of
localization loss and conﬁdence loss λ to 2:1.

5. EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS
When a picture is an input for detection, each layer of the
general neural network will only use a small part of the
neurons, resulting in a waste of neurons and parameters.
However, our network has higher neuron coverage and fewer
network parameters due to attention mechanisms and targeted
optimization for speciﬁc tasks. Figure 8 compares the neuron
coverage in the ﬁrst eight layers between our neural networks
and Faster-RCNN, where the ordinate is the neuron coverage,

output value is greater than the threshold. Assuming that all

T = {t1, t2, t3, ...ts}, then the output of neuron n is out (n, t).

the circular bounding box, Params is reduced by 0.14 M, FPS
is increased by 3 fps, and TPF is reduced by 1 ms, which
shows that the use of circular bounding boxes reduces the
number of model parameters to a certain extent and improves
the inference speed.

RGB-D multi-modal detection effect achieved by our frame-

other multi-modal fusion methods.
For a fair comparison, we deﬁne the network composed of
a feature extraction module and prediction module in the
proposed framework as the baseline detector for multi-modal
detection. On this basis, different types of image data are
input to realize different types of detection. RGB and depth
images are concatenated along the channel dimension for the
data-layer fusion strategy before being fed into the baseline
detector. Regarding the results of two independently trained
single-input detectors, we take the intersection of the two-input
detection results with IoU greater than 0.5 as the decision-
layer fusion result.

It can be seen from Figure 9 and 10 that the detection results
based on RGB images are reliable. Especially when adopting
the YOLOv5s [39] method on RGB single-input detection,

because there is a signiﬁcant difference between the RGB
color of the fruit and the background. Most fruits can be
successfully detected only by RGB 2D data.
Due to different methods to multi-modal fusion, using multi-
modal data does not necessarily improve detection perfor-
mance. Compared with RGB single-input detection based
on YOLOv5s, multi-modal detection effect based on data-
layer fusion strategy [18] and decision-layer fusion strategy
[18] is much unreliable. Even compared to RGB single-input
detection based on FaceBoxes [30], AP50, Precision and Recall
of data-layer fusion strategy are reduced by 3.5%, 4.0% and
3.7%, respectively. The main reason is that the preprocessed
depth image still contains many noise, which directly affect
the feature extraction after fusion, making learning difﬁcult
and the detection results inaccurate. Although Precision of
decision-layer fusion strategy has increased by 5.6% compared
with RGB single-input detection based on FaceBoxes, both
AP50 and Recall have decreased signiﬁcantly, by 23.0% and
22.7%, respectively. That is to say, decision-layer fusion
strategy improves a little Precision by greatly reducing Recall,
which cannot meet the requirements.
RGB single-input detection relies heavily on image quality
and resolution in a complex orchard environment; while multi-
modal detection based on data-layer and decision-layer fusion
is also ineffective due to problems such as depth image
noise. Compared with these detection methods, our RGB-
D multi-modal detection framework shows its advantages. It
fuses the features of RGB data and depth data, selecting
useful information from low-quality depth images. Then, it
adaptively fuses complementary features of different input
data to improve detection accuracy. Our method achieves good
results in terms of AP50, Precision and Recall, reaching 94.3%,

5.2. Answer to RQ2
In terms of the number of parameters and inference speed,
among RGB single-input detection methods, FaceBoxes has
the least number of parameters and the fastest inference speed.
Its FPS reached 50, followed by YOLOv5s with 34 FPS.
Data-layer fusion strategy only needs to simply send multi-
modal data into multiple channels in the early stage, and the
rest of the network structure is basically similar. Therefore,
the number of parameters is basically the same as that of
FaceBoxes, and FPS is also 50. Decision-level fusion strategy
is to further process the results of optimized predictions after
two independent single-input detectors, so the speed is much
less, with only 12 FPS. Our RGB-D multi-modal detection
framework is designed for lightweight, and the number of
parameters and inference speed are signiﬁcantly better than
those of most networks, with an FPS of 41. At the same
time, the change of TPF value of different apple detection
methods is just opposite to that of FPS. Since the features
of RGB data and depth data are fused in the feature-layer,
and features of 6 different scales need to be processed in
the prediction module, the inference speed of our RGB-D
multimodal detection framework is slightly lower than that
of RGB single-input detection based on FaceBoxes, but it is
precisely because of using multi-modal framework that our
accuracy is greatly improved compared to FaceBoxes.
5.3. Answer to RQ3
Figure 11 visually shows the detection results of RGB single-
input object detection and our RGB-D multi-modal detection
framework.
In Image 1 of Figure 11, multiple apples overlap each other,
which directly interferes with the detection and positioning
of each apple, so it is easy to mistakenly detect two apples
as one. Our multi-modal framework can reduce this false
detection and detect overlapping apples separately. In Image
2 of Figure 11, the apples are covered by leaves. When
adopting RGB single-input target detection and multi-modal
detection based on data-layer or decision-layer fusion strategy,
false detection, missed detection or inaccurate positioning still
exist more or less. However, our multi-modal framework can
reduce the occurrence of such kind of problems. In Image
3 of Figure 11, the boundaries of densely growing apples
are occluded from each other, and many apples are covered
by a large area, making it difﬁcult to accurately detect each
apple. However, our multi-modal framework can improve the
detection accuracy of such occluded apples and boost the
conﬁdence of accurate detection boxes at the same time.
In general, the detection performance of our RGB-D multi-
modal detection framework is signiﬁcantly better than that of
multi-modal detection based on data-layer and decision-layer
fusion. The metrics of our framework are slightly lower than
those of RGB single-input detection based on YOLOv5s and
Faster R-CNN on accuracy but signiﬁcantly outperform both
in terms of the number of parameters and inference speed. In
terms of the actual detection effect, our RGB-D multi-modal
detection framework is basically equivalent to the two.
6. DISCUSSION
Although the proposed multi-modal fusion framework has
certain advantages in detection accuracy and inference speed,
it still has limitations in some images with complex back-
grounds. The inﬂuence of noise in depth data can be reduced
by the fusion of multiple input information, but when the depth
image is not accurate enough or the depth value is missing
in a large area, false detection will still occur. Therefore, we
can also use depth enhancement and hole-ﬁlling estimation
algorithms to improve the accuracy of multi-modal detection
in the future.
In addition, the multi-modal fusion framework we proposed
can not only be applied to apple detection, but also to other
fruit detection in the future, and even target detection tasks in
other industries. Therefore, in future research, we will extend
the method to detection tasks of other fruit types (e.g., smaller
fruits such as cherries), and also explore the use of other types
of modal data (e.g., lidar data, spectral data, etc.).

In this paper, we take fruit detection as the target task, explore
the multi-modal fusion framework, and propose an RGB-D
lightweight multi-modal fusion system framework that can be
used for fruit detection, aiming at the detection task offers an
ideal solution.

recognition, combine them with NIN structure, and design
