ABSTRACT In natural environments, green walnuts often experience occlusion by branches and leaves, fruit
overlap, and varying lighting conditions. To address the issues of low detection accuracy, missed detections,
and false positives in the YOLO model, this study proposes an improved YOLOv8n-based detection and
counting model for green walnuts, named YOLOv8n-RBP. First, a receptive field-concentrated attention
module (RFCBAM) is integrated into the backbone network to enhance feature extraction capabilities.
Second, a BiFPN-GLSA module is introduced to replace the Path Aggregation Network (PANet) in the neck,
improving the fusion of feature layers from the backbone and Neck networks and enhancing the model’s
ability to capture both global and local spatial features. Lastly, to address the weak generalization and slow
convergence issues of the CIoU loss function in detection tasks, the PIoUv2 loss function is employed to
accelerate bounding box regression and improve detection performance. Experimental results demonstrate
that the YOLOv8n-RBP model excels across multiple evaluation metrics. Specifically, the model achieves a
mean average precision (mAP@0.5) of 82.2% and a recall rate of 72.4%, with a model size of only 4.65 MB,
2.2 million parameters, and 8.3 GFLOPs. Compared to the original YOLOv8n model, the recall rate and
mAP@0.5 improve by 2.7% and 2.5%, respectively, while the number of parameters, FLOPs, and model size
decrease by 26.7%, 0.6%, and 22.0%, respectively. Further deployment on the NVIDIA Jetson Xavier NX
demonstrates the model’s robust performance under natural conditions, indicating its suitability for orchard
operations.

I. INTRODUCTION
Walnut (Juglans regia L.), belonging to the genus Juglans in
the Juglandaceae family, is also known by other names such
as qiangtao and hutaoren. It is widely cultivated as a woody
oil crop across many countries and has a long cultivation
history [1]. As an important economic tree species, walnuts
are rich in proteins [2], amino acids, and unsaturated fatty
acids [3], contributing to their high nutritional value [4],
[5]. They are also referred to as the ‘‘longevity fruit’’ or
‘‘everlasting seed’’ [6]. China is one of the world’s leading
walnut producers, with the crop being grown in more than

Traditional machine learning-based image processing and
recognition methods typically rely on manually designed fea-

However, these approaches struggle to efficiently process
large-scale data, suffering from high computational complex-

performance in real-time applications. Additionally, in com-

or varying lighting conditions, these methods often exhibit
reduced recognition accuracy and poor robustness [13].
In contrast, deep learning networks [14] excel at automatic
feature learning and large-scale data processing, not only
enhancing recognition accuracy and robustness [15] but also
significantly reducing the complexity of feature design, better
meeting the needs of large-scale applications and real-time
processing.
In recent years algorithms based on machine vision
have experienced rapid development and have been widely
applied across various scientific research domains [16], [17].
According to the detection stages, object detection algo-
rithms can be categorized into two-stage and single-stage
approaches [18]. Two-stage object detection algorithms, such
as R-CNN [19] and Faster R-CNN [20], first generate a set
of candidate regions and subsequently identify these regions
to achieve high-precision object recognition and localization.
For instance, Zhang et al. [21] improved Faster R-CNN to
identify peach tree yellow leaf disease in complex environ-
ments by replacing the original cross-entropy function in the
Region Proposal Network (RPN) with the RS-Loss function
to simplify training complexity and enhance performance.
Additionally, the Soft-NMS algorithm was adopted in place
of traditional Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) to reduce
missed and false detections. The improved model achieved a
mean average precision (m90.56%. However, due to the large
size of the network model and prolonged detection times,
it fails to meet real-time detection requirements.
In contrast, single-stage object detection algorithms do not
require the generation of candidate regions, enabling direct
completion of object classification and bounding box regres-
sion in a single step, thereby supporting end-to-end training
with faster detection speeds. Common single-stage networks
include SSD, RetinaNet, and the increasingly popular YOLO
series. YOLO algorithms treat object detection as a regression
problem, mapping image pixels directly to bounding boxes
and class probabilities, which makes them effective for crop
detection in complex environments. For example, Li and
Wu [22] proposed an improved YOLOv5 algorithm based
on shallow feature layers for wheat ear detection. They inte-
grated the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM)
into the neural network to address gradient vanishing issues
during training and effectively mitigate field environmental
noise, meeting practical demands for wheat ear detection and
counting.
Similarly, Yang et al. [23] used an improved YOLOv8
algorithm for tomato detection, employing Depthwise Sepa-
rable Convolution (DSConv) to reduce computational com-
plexity and enhancing the network’s ability to distinguish
tomatoes from the background through the Dual Path Atten-
tion Gate (DPAG) module. This achieved a balanced trade-off
between model size and accuracy. Chen et al. [24] enhanced
the original YOLOv7 network by incorporating a small object
detection layer, lightweight convolutions, and the CBAM
attention mechanism to enable multi-scale feature extraction
and fusion, effectively reducing model parameters. Wang
et al. [25] utilized an improved YOLOv4 model to detect
densely distributed plum targets in orchards, adding a 152 ×
152 feature layer to enhance dense target feature extraction.
They also integrated focal loss and Complete Intersection
over Union (CIOU) loss functions to balance overall loss
contributions among samples. Shen et al. [26] optimized the
YOLOv5s detection model using channel pruning, reducing
model parameters and size by 79% and 76%, respectively,
and developed a lightweight end-to-end counting pipeline to
enable efficient real-time video tracking and grape cluster
counting under field conditions. Yu et al. [27] combined a
Multi-Scale Attention (EMA) module and SPD-Conv mod-
ule to reduce computational load and address challenges in
YOLOv8, such as fine-grained information loss and ineffi-
ciencies in feature representation learning. Their improved
model achieved an mAP of 98.5% in tasks involving the
recognition of maize seeds across four health states.
Based on the above analysis, although relatively few stud-
ies have focused on the detection and counting of green wal-
nuts, the use of intelligent devices for crop yield detection and
evaluation in other domains has become a prominent research
area. These methodologies offer valuable insights for green
walnut detection and counting. Future research should priori-
tize optimizing detection accuracy, model lightweighting, and
real-time performance under actual field conditions, while
improving algorithms to address the unique characteristics of
small and densely distributed targets.
To address the issues of low accuracy, missed detections,
and false detections in identifying green-husked walnuts
under natural scenes, this study proposes a green-husked
walnut detection and counting model based on an improved
YOLOv8n network structure. Specifically, a Receptive Field
Concentrated Attention Module (RFCBAM) is introduced
into the backbone network to enhance the model’s feature
extraction capability. Additionally, a BiFPN-GLSA module
is proposed to replace the traditional Path Aggregation Net-
work (PANet) in the neck, improving the model’s ability to
capture both global and local spatial features. Furthermore,
CIoU is optimized to PIoUv2, significantly accelerating
the bounding box regression process and thereby improv-
ing the model’s convergence speed and detection accuracy.

II. DATA COLLECTION AND PRODUCTION
A. IMAGE ACQUISITION
This study focuses on green walnuts, necessitating the con-
struction of a target detection image dataset due to the lack
of publicly available datasets. The data was collected from
walnut orchards at the Xinjiang Institute of Technology in
Wensu County, Aksu City, Xinjiang. The imaging setup,
as illustrated in Figure 1, consists of an Intel RealSense D456
depth camera, a computer for data storage, and a tripod for
camera support.
FIGURE 1. Image acquisition device.
Data collection was conducted from June to July 2024, dur-
ing both clear and overcast conditions, with sampling times
between 9:00–13:00 and 15:00–20:00. The dataset comprises
images with varying lighting conditions, leaf occlusions, fruit
overlap, and both close-up and wide-angle, with a resolution
of 640 × 640 pixels in JPG format. After manual filtering
to remove blurred, redundant, and irrelevant images, a final
dataset of 3,688 valid green walnut images was obtained, with
sample images shown in Figure 2.

C. DATASET PREPARATION
Following data augmentation, LabelImg software was used
to annotate target regions and classes within the green walnut
images. Only the minimal bounding rectangle for each walnut
was considered, excluding samples with over 70% occlusion,

519 validation images, and 519 test images.
III. YOLOv8n-RBP NETWORK MODEL
A. YOLOv8 MODEL
In the field of computer, the YOLO series models are widely
used due to their high accuracy and fast detection capa-
bilities. YOLOv8, as a new state-of-the-art (SOTA) model,
introduces multiple features and improvements based on
YOLOv5. Compared to YOLOv5, YOLOv8 has made sig-
nificant advancements in both architecture and detection
performance. The YOLOv8 model is available in five differ-
ent versions-YOLOv8n, YOLOv8s, YOLOv8m, YOLOv8l,
and YOLOv8x-catering to diverse application needs. Under
the requirements of practical mobile deployment, the model
must feature a smaller number of parameters while sup-
porting real-time detection. Among the YOLOv8 series, the
YOLOv8n version is the most compact and efficient, making
it suitable for applications such as green walnut detection,
which demand high lightweight and real-time performance.
The network architecture of YOLOv8n consists of three main
components: the backbone network, the Neck module, and
the Head module. The backbone network replaces the C3

B. YOLOv8n-RBP MODEL
To improve the identification of green walnut husks in
natural environments, this study introduces modifications
based on the YOLOv8n model. Firstly, the Receptive Field
Concentration-Based Attention Module (RFCBAM) was
integrated into the Backbone to enhance C2f and improve fea-
ture extraction, thereby strengthening the YOLOv8 network.
Secondly, the BiFPN-GLSA network was employed in the
Neck to improve feature fusion, enhancing the accuracy of
walnut recognition. Lastly, CIoU was upgraded to PIoUv2 to
accelerate bounding box regression, further improving model
convergence speed and accuracy, resulting in the refined
YOLOv8n-RBP model. The network structure is shown in

1) BACKBONE NETWORK DESIGN
When identifying green-husked walnuts in natural environ-
ments, enhancing model accuracy hinges on effectively sup-
pressing background noise and ensuring the model focuses
on the characteristic regions of the walnuts. To address this
challenge, this study optimizes and enhances the C2f module
within the backbone network, with a focus on strengthen-
ing the spatial attention mechanism. This enables the model
to more accurately concentrate on the spatial information
within the receptive field, thereby overcoming the limita-
tions in representational capacity caused by convolutional
kernel parameter sharing. Specifically, the Convolutional
Block Attention Module (CBAM) [28] is introduced for
its spatial attention mechanism, and an improved network
structure, the Receptive Field Concentration-Based Atten-
tion Module (RFCBAM) [29], is developed, as shown in
Figure 4. The RFCBAM module enhances the model’s ability
to capture global and critical features by expanding the recep-
tive field and strengthening channel attention. The expanded

2) BIFPN-GLSA FEATURE FUSION MECHANISM
The Neck component of the YOLOv8n model adopts the
classic PANet (Path Aggregation Network) structure [30],
which represents a significant improvement over the tradi-
tional FPN (Feature Pyramid Network) [31]. As illustrated
in Figures 7, PANet introduces a bottom-up path augmen-
tation on top of FPN, thereby enhancing the transfer of
low-level features to higher-level features and enabling bidi-
rectional information flow. Specifically, this bidirectional
feature fusion mechanism not only substantially enhances the
semantic representation of feature maps but also preserves the
rich positional information contained in low-level features,
overcoming the limitation of unidirectional information flow
in FPN and significantly reducing the loss of shallow feature
information during propagation. However, when addressing
targets with small sizes and complex backgrounds, such as
green walnuts, PANet still faces certain bottlenecks in cap-
turing fine-grained details. Its limitations primarily lie in the
difficulty of accurately localizing and enhancing the features
of small objects, which can result in a decline in detection
accuracy under complex backgrounds. To further optimize
the feature fusion mechanism, BiFPN (Bidirectional Fea-
ture Pyramid Network) [32] was proposed. BiFPN achieves
improvements in feature fusion through two key modifica-
tions: first, it eliminates nodes with only a single input edge
to prevent unidirectional information flow disruptions; sec-
ond, it introduces multiple skip connections within the same
feature level, facilitating more efficient information exchange
across different feature scales.
Although BiFPN demonstrates exceptional performance in
fusing feature levels and enhancing semantic representation,
challenges remain in capturing and precisely localizing the
details of small objects. This highlights the need for further
innovation to improve small-object detection capabilities in
complex task scenarios, such as employing more efficient
context modeling or integrating multimodal information.
To address these challenges, this study proposes the
BiFPN-GLSA network, replacing YOLOv8’s Neck structure.
To better integrate the feature layers output from the Back-
bone and the Neck network, enhancing the model’s ability to
capture both global and local spatial features, the convolu-

3) LOSS FUNCTION OPTIMIZATION
The original YOLOv8 model employs the CIoU [34] loss
function, an enhancement of the IoU loss function that
accounts for the overlap region of bounding boxes, the dis-
tance between their center points, and their aspect ratio.
However, when the aspect ratios of the predicted and
ground truth bounding boxes exhibit a linear relationship,
the improvements offered by the CIoU loss function become
marginal, and its computational complexity leads to increased
training costs. To address these issues, this study further
refines the CIoU loss function into the PIoUv2 loss function.
PIoUv2 [35] integrates the strengths of EIoU [36],
SIoU [37], and WIoU [38], enabling it to more effectively
capture target displacement and shape variations. As a result,
it enhances target localization accuracy, ensuring that the
detection boxes align more closely with the true positions
of the targets. As an extended version of PIoU [39], PIoUv2
introduces a non-monotonic attention layer controlled by a
single hyperparameter. This mechanism allows the model
to assign varying levels of focus to anchor boxes of dif-
ferent qualities during training. For medium-quality anchor
boxes, this attention mechanism generates larger gradients,
driving the model to rapidly optimize them into high-quality
prediction boxes. For low-quality anchor boxes, the gra-
dients, though initially small, gradually increase, enabling
incremental improvement during regression. Additionally,
PIoUv2 reduces the number of hyperparameters, simplifying
the training process. This simplification not only makes the
model easier to tune and optimize but also minimizes the risk
of performance degradation due to improper hyperparameter
selection. The computation formula is as follows:

C. EXPERIMENTAL PLATFORM AND PARAMETERS
The training and testing of the model in this study were con-
ducted within the same environment. The computing setup
comprised an AMD Ryzen Threadripper PRO 3975WX pro-
cessor and an NVIDIA RTX A5000 GPU with 24 GB of
VRAM. The system ran on Windows 11, utilizing PyTorch
1.13.1 as the deep learning framework, with Python 3.8 as
the development environment, and CUDA 11.6 as the com-
putational backend.
During the model training phase, the stochastic gradient
descent (SGD) optimizer was employed for iterative opti-
mization. Due to its stochastic nature, SGD can mitigate the
risk of the model converging to a local optimum, thereby
accelerating the iteration process. Preliminary experiments
determined the training to run for 300 epochs, with a batch
size of 16, a learning rate of 0.01, and a weight decay coeffi-
cient of 0.0005. The input image resolution was set to 640 ×
640 pixels to enhance the model’s performance. To increase
dataset diversity and improve model robustness, mosaic data
augmentation was applied during training.
D. PERFORMANCE EVALUATION METRICS
This study aimed to maintain the accuracy of green walnut
detection while minimizing model parameters, computational
load, model size, and detection time. Thus, the primary eval-
uation metrics included the number of parameters, floating
point operations (FLOPs), mean average precision (mAP),
precision (P), recall (R), and model size.

B. ABLATION EXPERIMENT
To validate the advantages of the proposed method in detect-
ing green walnut husks, a series of comprehensive ablation
experiments were designed and implemented to evaluate
the performance of the improved model. Eight different
optimization schemes were developed, all conducted under
identical experimental conditions and parameter settings to
ensure fairness and accuracy in the results. The mean Aver-
age Precision (mAP) was selected as the primary evaluation
metric, complemented by assessments across multiple dimen-
sions, including parameter count, floating-point operations
(FLOPs), and model size, to provide a holistic comparison
of each scheme’s merits and drawbacks. The experimen-
tal results are presented in Table 2. By integrating the
RFCBAM module into the backbone network, the recall rate
and mAP@0.5 improved by 2.1% and 1.5%, respectively.
This enhancement in feature extraction capability, however,
came at the cost of a 3.3% increase in parameter count,
an 8.5% rise in FLOPs, and a 6.9% growth in model size,
indicating that while the RFCBAM module significantly
improves feature extraction accuracy, it imposes additional
computational demands. Replacing the original Neck struc-
ture with the BiFPN-GLSA module resulted in a 0.7%
improvement in mAP@0.5, accompanied by a reduction in
parameter count, FLOPs, and model size by 30.0%, 6.1%,
and 26.2%, respectively. These findings suggest that the
BiFPN-GLSA module enhances feature fusion capability,
achieving higher detection accuracy while markedly optimiz-
ing computational efficiency and storage costs. Substituting
the CIoU loss function with PIoUv2 yielded a modest 0.5%
increase in mAP@0.5, further demonstrating the potential

C. LOSS FUNCTION COMPARISON EXPERIMENT
To assess the impact of the PIoUv2 loss function on model

D. COMPARISON WITH OTHER MODELS
To further validate the performance of the improved model in

comprehensive benchmark for evaluating the performance of
the improved model. By comparing the proposed YOLOv8n-
RBP model with these alternatives, a more objective assess-
ment of its practical detection performance under varying
conditions can be achieved. All models were evaluated under
identical conditions using metrics including the number of
parameters, floating-point operations (FLOPs), mean average
precision (mAP), precision (P), recall (R), F1 score, and
model size to reflect their overall performance. The results,
as presented in Table 4, demonstrate that YOLOv8n-RBP
outperforms other models in terms of parameters, FLOPs,
mAP, and model size.

reduces parameters, FLOPs, recall, F1 score, and model
size by 68.6%, 47.8%, 3.2%, 1.1%, and 66.1%, respectively,

YOLOv8n-RBP reduces parameters, FLOPs, and model size

mAP, precision, recall, and F1 score by 0.6%, 4.0%, 2.7%,
and 2.4%. In summary, the proposed YOLOv8n-RBP model
demonstrates significant superiority over existing models
across all evaluated metrics. It not only maintains mAP
improvements but also enhances its suitability for detecting
and counting green walnut husks in natural environments,
with the added advantage of being deployable on edge
devices.

V. CONCLUSION
This study presents an improved YOLOv8n-based detection
model for green walnuts in natural environments, addressing
the issues of missed and false detections by redesigning the
Backbone, Neck, and Loss function modules. The following
conclusions were drawn:
