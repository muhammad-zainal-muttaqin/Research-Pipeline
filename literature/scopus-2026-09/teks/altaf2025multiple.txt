A B S T R A C T
Three-dimensional (3D) object tracking is crucial in computer vision applications, particularly in autonomous 
driving, robotics, and surveillance. Despite advancements, effectively utilizing multimodal data to improve 
multi-object detection and tracking (MODT) remains challenging. This study introduces ACMODT, an affinity 
computation-based multi-object detection and tracking framework that integrates camera (2D) and LiDAR (3D) 
data for enhanced MODT performance in autonomous driving. This approach leverages EPNet as a backbone, 
utilizing 2D–3D feature fusion for accurate proposal generation. A deep neural network (DNN) extracts robust 
appearance and geometric features, while an improved affinity computation module combines Refined Boost 
Correlation Features (RBCF) and 3D-Extended Geometric IoU (3D-XGIoU) for precise object association. Motion 
prediction is refined using a Kalman filter (KF), and Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM)-based data association 
ensures consistent tracking. Experiments on the KITTI car tracking benchmark for quantitative analysis and 
the RADIATE dataset for visualization demonstrate that our method achieves superior tracking accuracy and 
precision compared to state-of-the-art multi-object tracking (MOT) approaches, proving its effectiveness for 
real-time object tracking.
1. Introduction
As autonomous driving technology progresses and gains broader 
adoption, the demand for advanced perception and precise three-
dimensional multiple object tracking (3D MOT) frameworks [1–3] has 
increased significantly. These systems play a crucial role in understand-
ing the surrounding environment by continuously tracking multiple 
objects, including near or distant vehicles, in real-time, harsh weather 
conditions. This capability is fundamental in enhancing situational 
awareness, thereby improving the overall safety and dependability 
of self-driving systems [4,5]. Despite the significant advancements in 
MOT methodologies, existing approaches still encounter difficulties 
in highly dynamic and complex traffic conditions. Techniques that 
rely solely on camera-based tracking [6,7] are constrained by the 
limitations of visual sensors, leading to reduced accuracy under intense 
sunlight exposure or poor lighting conditions. Conversely, LiDAR-based 
tracking [8,9] provides precise spatial data. Still, they often generate

GMM-based data association scheme, distinguish ACMODT from earlier

known datasets: the KITTI car tracking benchmark [20] and 
RADIATE [21]. The experimental results indicate substantial 
improvements in tracking precision, accuracy, reduced ID-SW, 
and overall performance metrics compared to existing method-
ologies, with enhanced visual representations in the form of 2D 
image outputs.

2. Literature survey
2.1. Survey on multi-object tracking
MOT is typically approached using two primary paradigms. The first 
is the tracking-by-detection (TBD) framework [12,22], which processes 
detection and tracking as separate tasks. In Fig. 1, the detection and 
tracking are treated as distinct processes in TBD. Initially, the detection 
network identifies objects by producing their classification and BB 
coordinates. Subsequently, a separate tracking network extracts motion 
and appearance features to facilitate data association. It also introduces

by issues such as slower processing speeds and error propagation, stem-
ming from the sequential nature of object detection, data association, 
and track assignment. To mitigate these drawbacks, the joint detection 
and tracking (JDT) paradigm [23–25] was introduced, integrating both 
tasks within a unified end-to-end learning-based framework as shown 
in Fig. 1.
One notable contribution is the Track-to-Detect and Segment
(TraDeS) tracking model by Wu et al. [2], which improves MOT 
performance by feeding tracking information back into the detection 
process. Additionally, TraDeS incorporates a re-identification (Re-ID) 
loss function, aligning it more effectively with the detection loss. 
Several JDT-based approaches have since been proposed, including 
RetinaTrack [25], CenterTrack [7], ChainedTracker [26], JDE [27], 
JMODT [17], and BcMODT [18]. Conversely, the JDT approach em-
ploys a unified network that simultaneously extracts features for de-
tection and tracking tasks. This integrated strategy enhances computa-
tional efficiency and reduces overall processing costs.
For example, ChainedTracker [26] constructs tracklets by link-
ing bounding boxes between consecutive frames. Similarly, Zhang 
et al. [12] demonstrated that leveraging relationships between de-
tected object pairs can enhance overall tracking accuracy. While the 
JDT paradigm offers notable performance improvements, developing 
effective models remains a complex challenge. The success of this 
approach is highly dependent on the ability to incorporate and process 
multi-sensor data efficiently. Existing studies on 3D MOT with multi-
sensor fusion emphasize the importance of precise sensor calibration 
for achieving optimal tracking performance. However, a significant 
gap remains in understanding the attribute-based relationships between 
objects, which many current methods fail to address. To overcome 
these limitations, the proposed ACMODT framework adopts the JDT 
paradigm, aiming to enhance tracking accuracy and robustness through 
improved sensor fusion techniques.
2.2. Affinity estimation techniques for MOT
Determining object affinity is a crucial aspect of MOT, relying 
on appearance features, motion trajectory predictions, and geometric 
overlap. Unlike traditional fluoroscopy-based data fusion methods [28], 
modern approaches utilize feature fusion techniques that incorporate 
both camera images and LiDAR point clouds. These techniques typi-
cally process sensor modalities either independently or by integrating 
them using attention-based mechanisms [11]. Several existing affin-
ity computation models rely solely on camera-derived features, such 
as ODESA [29], SMAT [19], CenterTrack [7], ChainedTrack [26], 
JDE [27], and RetinaTrack [25]. Some methods extend affinity compu-
tation by incorporating both camera and LiDAR-based fusion strategies, 
such as JRMOT [11], which employs batch fusion, or JMODT [17], 
which performs point-wise feature fusion to align image pixels with 
corresponding LiDAR points. However, most of these models do not

Despite advances in affinity computation, challenges persist in ef-

2.3. Feature representation and similarity measures
Enhancing the accuracy of MOT requires refining the shared fea-
ture maps generated by the Region Proposal Network (RPN) [30,31], 
ensuring that only the most relevant features contribute to object de-
tection and association. The JMODT framework [17] improves feature 
representation by applying 3D-distance Intersection over Union (3D 
DIoU)-based filtering to eliminate irrelevant candidate features, fol-
lowed by absolute subtraction-based correlation computations [12] to 
measure similarities between detected objects across frames. Similarly, 
mmMOT utilizes a combination of dot product operations, subtraction, 
and absolute difference calculations to quantify the similarity between 
object features. However, these methods primarily focus on measuring 
the spatial distance between features across adjacent frames while 
failing to account for directional consistency, which plays a crucial role 
in maintaining reliable object associations.
To overcome these limitations in geometric affinity calculation, 
the 3D-XGIoU metric is utilized. Unlike conventional IoU approaches, 
3D-XGIoU enhances geometric affinity by considering not only the 
overlapping area but also the spatial alignment, aspect ratios, and 
orientation of 3D BBs. This refined approach helps minimize false 
associations, especially in cluttered or dynamic scenarios, and en-
sures consistent tracking accuracy across frames. Additionally, the 
proposed framework incorporates an RBCF module that simultane-
ously evaluates spatial distance-based similarity and directional feature 
alignment across frames. By integrating these components, the frame-
work significantly enhances the accuracy of object affinity computation 
derived from camera and LiDAR fusion. The refined representation re-
duces ID switches and incorrect associations in complex environments. 
Furthermore, the combined evaluation of spatial and directional fea-
ture domains strengthens the robustness of data association, enabling 
reliable tracking even under occlusions and rapid motion changes.
2.4. Methodological taxonomy of MOT approaches

presents a structured taxonomy of recent 3D MOT methods, highlight-
ing their strategies for object detection, correlation, affinity modeling, 
motion prediction, and data association techniques. These methods 
range from utilizing 2D image data to advanced 3D spatial fusion 
for object detection. For object correlation, some approaches focus 
on appearance-based re-identification (Re-ID), while others incorpo-
rate start-end probability estimation to enhance detection accuracy 
across frames. The appearance modality is also diversified, with some 
methods relying exclusively on camera data, while others employ multi-
modal fusion using LiDAR and camera inputs for improved object 
identification.
Motion prediction mechanisms vary across methods. KF is widely 
used for motion estimation due to its efficiency in linear motion mod-
eling. In contrast, some methods focus on 2D or 3D distance measures, 
or Intersection over Union (IoU), to assess geometric relationships. Ad-
ditionally, the strategies for data association include the Hungarian Al-
gorithm (HA), Mixed-Integer Programming (MIP), and GMM, ensuring 
effective matching of detected and tracked objects. The proposed AC-
MODT method integrates 3D detection, GMM-based data association, 
and robust affinity computation. This combination ensures reliable and 
consistent tracking performance, particularly suited for autonomous 
driving scenarios where precise object detection and trajectory man-
agement are crucial.

3. System architecture
The proposed system is designed to perform MOT by leveraging 
multi-sensor fusion and advanced affinity computation techniques. The 
architecture consists of multiple interconnected modules that work 
together to extract features, detect objects, compute affinities, and 
manage object tracks over time. A high-level overview of the frame-
work is illustrated in Fig. 2. The system begins with the input data 
and sensor calibration module, where data is acquired from two pri-
mary sensors: 2D images from cameras and 3D point clouds from 
LiDAR. To ensure accurate spatial alignment between these two data 
sources, a sensor calibration process is applied. Following data ac-
quisition, the RPN and feature encoding module processes the sensor 
data. EPNet serves as the backbone network by extracting deep 3D 
point features while incorporating image-based semantics. This module 
consists of three key components: a 2D Feature encoder and decoder, 
which extracts visual representations from images; a 3D feature en-
coder and decoder, responsible for processing geometric information 
from LiDAR point clouds; and a 2D–3D feature fusion module, which 
integrates these features to create more robust object representations. 
The RPN generates object proposals, which are then refined through 
subsequent processing stages. Once object proposals are generated, the 
object detection network classifies and refines these detections. The 
object classification module identifies object categories such as vehicles 
and pedestrians, while the box regression module optimizes the 3D 
bounding boxes (predicted by EPNet). A correlation network extracts 
appearance-based features to facilitate object association across frames. 
The re-identification (Re-ID) module ensures that previously tracked 
objects can be recognized if they temporarily disappear due to oc-
clusion or motion blur. Additionally, a start-end estimation module 
determines the initial and final positions of each tracked object.
To establish correspondences between detections across frames, the 
system employs affinity calculation and data association. This mod-
ule constructs an affinity matrix (cost matrix) where each element 
represents the similarity score between a detected object and an exist-
ing track. The matrix incorporates multiple affinity metrics, including 
classification confidence scores, IoU, Euclidean distance, and feature 
correlation scores from the Re-ID network. Additionally, the RBCF and 
3D-XGIoU further refine the affinity calculations by improving spatial 
and appearance-based similarity measures. To enhance tracking con-
sistency, the motion prediction module applies a KF to estimate object 
trajectories over time. The GMM-based data association method is then 
used to improve the matching of detections across frames by mod-
eling the feature distributions of detected and predicted objects. The 
match score ranking module prioritizes the most reliable associations, 
ensuring robust track continuity.
Finally, the track management Module oversees the state of tracked 
objects using a state estimation process. Each object follows a well-
defined lifecycle, transitioning through four possible states: Birth, when 
a new object is detected and initialized as a track; Tracking, when 
an object is actively followed across frames; Miss, when a temporary 
loss of detection occurs due to occlusion or sensor noise; and Death, 
when an object is permanently removed from the tracking system. 
This structured approach ensures a reliable affinity-based framework 
for multi-object detection and tracking, even in complex and dynamic 
environments.

4.1. Refined boost correlation feature for appearance matching
Traditional object tracking models, such as mmMOT [12], utilize 
absolute differences, element-wise subtraction, and multiplication to 
determine feature correlations between detected objects. The adjacency 
relationship between feature pairs is estimated to facilitate MOT, allow-
ing feature extraction to be performed independently of batch size and 
across multiple sensor modalities. In JMODT [17], an IoU-based filter-
ing approach is applied to remove irrelevant feature embeddings, while 
an absolute subtraction operation is used to measure feature differ-
ences across consecutive frames. However, these techniques overlook 
directional consistency in feature representation, which can negatively 
impact object association in dynamic environments.
To address these challenges, the RBCF method is introduced, which 
enhances object-level dependencies across frames by incorporating 
temporal smoothness and feature variation modeling. Unlike conven-
tional cosine similarity approaches [32], this technique integrates a

To evaluate the real-time capability of the proposed ACMODT 
framework and the other state-of-the-art methods, the experimental

5.1. Benchmark dataset and performance metrics
The effectiveness of the proposed ACMODT framework was assessed 
using the KITTI car tracking dataset [20], which consists of 29 test 
sequences and 21 training sequences captured through a forward-
facing camera and LiDAR sensors. Each labeled object in the dataset 
was assigned a unique identification number associated with a 3D 
BB. An object detection was classified as a true positive (TP) if its 
2D IoU [35] exceeded 0.5. To evaluate the MOT performance, the 
experimental setup adhered to the standard KITTI evaluation criteria, 
utilizing key performance indicators such as Multiple Object Tracking 
Precision (MOTP), Multiple Object Tracking Accuracy (MOTA), Multi-
ple Object Detection Accuracy (MODP), and Multiple Object Detection 
Accuracy (MODA). Additionally, False Negatives (FN), False Positives 
(FP), Mostly Tracked (MT), Mostly Lost (ML), fragmentation (Frag), 
and IDSW were recorded to quantify tracking consistency and overall 
system reliability [36].

6. Conclusion
This study presents a robust MOT framework specifically designed 
for vehicle detection in complex real-world scenarios. The proposed 
method achieves enhanced tracking accuracy and reliability, even in 
challenging environmental conditions, by leveraging a multimodal fu-
sion approach that integrates both 2D image data and 3D LiDAR 
point cloud data. The system effectively handles occlusions, motion 
variations, and real-time updates, demonstrating adaptability to dy-
namic traffic environments. The method has shown superior detection 
accuracy, precise object re-identification, and trajectory stability across 
various weather conditions through extensive experimental evalua-
tions. The ability to maintain consistent tracking IDs and generate 
reliable motion predictions underscores its potential for autonomous 
vehicle applications. The results confirm that the proposed frame-
work offers state-of-the-art performance, reinforcing its suitability for 
real-time deployment in intelligent transportation systems.
7. Challenges and prospective enhancements
Despite the effectiveness of the proposed tracking framework, cer-
tain limitations remain. One primary constraint is its focus on vehi-
cle tracking, excluding other critical road users such as pedestrians, 
cyclists, and motorbikes. This limitation may impact overall track-
ing performance in complex urban environments, where diverse road 
users interact dynamically. Additionally, while the system operates in 
real-time, its computational demands are relatively high, potentially 
limiting deployment on low-power embedded systems or devices with 
restricted processing capabilities.
To address these limitations, future research can extend the track-
ing framework to accommodate a broader range of road participants, 
ensuring a more comprehensive multi-object detection and tracking sys-
tem. Further optimization strategies can be explored to enhance com-
putational efficiency, making the method more suitable for resource-
constrained hardware and low-cost sensors. Moreover, expanding the 
evaluation across larger and more diverse datasets will help vali-
date the framework’s adaptability and scalability. Another promising 
direction is refining the RPN to improve detection performance for 
non-vehicle objects. Adjusting the anchor box dimensions and aspect 
ratios to match the sizes of pedestrians and cyclists can significantly 
improve tracking accuracy. Additionally, integrating multi-scale fea-
ture fusion techniques will enhance the detection of smaller, highly 
dynamic objects, ensuring greater robustness in autonomous driving 
applications.

Min Young Kim received a BS. M.S. and Ph.D. degrees from 
the Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology, 
South Korea, in 1996, 1998, and 2004, respectively. He was 
a Senior Researcher with Mirae Corporation from 2004 to 
2005. He was also a Chief Research Engineer in artificial 
vision systems for intelligent machines and robots with 
Kohyoung Corporation, from 2005 to 2009. Since 2009, 
he has been with the School of Electronic and Electri-
cal Engineering, Computer Science, Kyungpook National 
University, as an Assistant Professor. He was a Visiting 
Associate Professor with the Department of Electrical and 
Computer Engineering and the School of Medicine, Johns 
Hopkins University, from 2014 to 2015. Currently, he is 
serving as a full professor at the School of Electronics 
Engineering at Kyungpook National University. He is also 
a Director of the KNU-LG Electronics Convergence Research 
Center and the Director of the Research Center for Neu-
rosurgical Robotic Systems. His research interests include 
visual intelligence for robotic perception, the recognition of 
autonomous unmanned ground vehicles, and aerial vehicles.
