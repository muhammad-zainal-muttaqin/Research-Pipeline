Abstract- In agriculture sector the problem of identification and 
counting the number of fruits on trees plays an important role in crop 
estimation work. At present manual counting of fruits and vegetables 
is carried out at many places. Manual counting has many drawbacks 
as it is time consuming and requires plenty of labors. The automated 
fruit counting approach can help crop management system by 
providing valuable information for forecasting yields or by planning 
harvesting schedule to attain more productivity. This work presents 
an automated and efficient fruit counting system using computer 
vision techniques. The proposed system uses minimum Euclidean 
distance based segmentation technique for segmenting the fruit 
region from the input image. Further circle overlaying is done on the 
fruit region and in the last fruits are counted on the basis of the 
centroid of the fruit regions. This proposed system is correctly 
detecting and counting the apples on the test images. 
 
Keywords – Computer Vision, Fruit Localization, Euclidean 
distance, L*a*b Color space

A. Precision Agriculture and Yield Mapping 
 
The basic aims for all agricultural firms is to reduce the 
investment costs, reduction of time involved in processing, 
increasing harvest, and improving crop quality in order to get 
maximum profit and to compete and sustain its position in 
local and global markets. Through high levels of automation 
and computerization in agricultural industries, crops are 
managed based on considering units such as blocks rather than 
individual units [11][13]. 
 
Precision agriculture is a management technique that 
responds to spatial variability found in farming lands. Stages 
involving precision agriculture include detecting yield 
variability in a field, detecting its cause, deciding on possible 
solutions based on variety of factors such as costs involved, 
improving crop quality etc., implementing new techniques and 
implementing the process in a recurring manner. Precision 
agriculture techniques could also be made in use for 
improvement in economic and environmental sustainability in 
agricultural industry [7][13]. 
 
Amongst these techniques, the beginning step is yield 
mapping which is used to apply crop management and 
cultivation techniques on a specific site given a specific area. 
This mapping structure keeps account of the quantity of any 
crop cultivated at any given point of time in the given 
agricultural field. This useful and important composed data 
about the account of the quantity of any crop at any given 
point of time in any given specific site in a given area could 
be used to produce a yield map using software for mapping. 
Such maps are very important piece of information to 
recognize variability within any given or specific field of 
crop. Unpredictability in the field of agriculture can be due to 
various natural and man-made causes. Natural factors can be

B. Need of Automated Yield Mapping in Agriculture 
 
“Harvest estimation” holds significance to businesses in 
preparation of catering to the requirement of agricultural 
processes in planning of transport facilities and beforehand 
booking of transports for the transport of the crops. Also the 
industrial organizations require taking into account the yield 
prediction accurately so that there is no further problem in the 
market of selling and buying and also storage of the desired 
crops properly. Accurate and early estimation of crop harvest 
is an important work [5]. 
In the present scenario, contractors are being hired to count 
the fruit with the help of manual labor. Then the manual 
counts are used for the purpose of estimation of the harvest. 
Approximately $600,000 to $1000, 000 is spent on manual 
counting per year for a usual pack house. Also many other 
cost evaluations have to be done before hand and taken into 
account for estimation of financial budgets such as combine 
overhead, which includes downgrading, rate of interest, 
insurance cover, housing and finance, and repair charges in

estimated according to per hour. [2]. 
 
Having a robust automated counting technique facilitates a 
fast, consistent and convenient way of counting fruits. This 
further saves the money spent on manual counting as well as 
the loss due to erroneous estimations. 
 
II. LITERATURE WORK 
 
Image processing is the best proven effective tool for 
analysis in variety of fields and application. Computerized and 
automated counting techniques based on image analysis can 
be found in many areas of applications, for example, hospital 
and clinical (medical) purposes for detecting diseases of 
patients and also for their treatment, garden cultivation, crop 
production and management industry & research and analysis 
of soil growth and cultivation. This proves the effectiveness of 
systems that generate the count and that are automatically 
invoked without any labor or manual work involved and also 
they are dependable, constant, rapid and also more economical 
than other counting methodology adopted earlier or even now 
at some many places. A variety of computer technologies are 
being used in farming industry that is helping farmers to 
reduce budget costs via well-organized and operative 
applications of given inputs and it is indeed one of the rising 
and successful eras of change in industries.[4]. 
Computer vision techniques involve the extraction of 
meaningful and useful data as well as descriptions of physical 
objects present in images. Images can be created with the help 
of various physical devices, in such as video cameras, x-ray 
devices, electronic microscopes, ultrasound etc. These created 
images are used for a variety of applications and in various 
fields and industries, for example entertainment purposes, 
medicinal approaches, business organizations, military, civil 
(e.g. traffic), security, and scientific purposes [12]. The main 
aim in each purpose is to extract useful and meaningful data 
and description about the scene being imaged [10].  
 
To study the work that has been done in agricultural 
industry the related work in the field and problem are outlined. 
Ulzii-OrshikhDorj, Malrey Lee et al [1] adopted a newly 
designed counting algorithm for tangerine harvest estimation 
in order to obtain accurate results. In their work, partially / 
semi partially occluded tangerine and its clusters were 
considered. The prediction is done for tangerine fruits in the 
orchards which are present under natural lightning conditions 
and then the results are calculated and comparison is done 
between before harvesting and after harvesting tangerine 
fruits. The results of harvest estimation through tangerine 
flower recognition are approximately closer to 20% of yield 
estimation result. The data and results of the tangerine grove 
were not properly estimated which resulted in poor 
implementation and minimal practical usability for automation 
harvesting for identification of crops.  
Suvarna K Nandyal et al [2] presented a paper on 
predicting the crop growth. In this paper they used the method 
of circle fitting and also they used edge detection methods for 
detecting the edges. They have also used morphological 
operations for finding out the desired shapes of the crops. 
They have observed a very high efficiency in the crop growth 
prediction method. 
H P Patel et al [3] has researched a lot in the field of 
agricultural. He has observed that for identifying the fruit 
region, the color and shape features were used. After the 
identification, segmentation was performed on the identified 
region. And after that the noise was removed from that region 
and then for detection of the edges of identified region, circle 
was fit into those segmented region. In this paper also 
efficiency of identifying the fruit was very high.  
Michael M. Blanke et al [5] has implemented the desired 
algorithm for identifying the apple fruits from the orchards. 
He has used various color models for the segmentation of 
apples from the orchards. The testing was performed on some 
amount of images. After that the comparison was done on the 
count of those fruits by the system and further those counted 
manually.  
H N Patel et al [6] has worked on efficient locating the 
fruit on the tree which is one of the major requirements for the 
fruit harvesting system. This paper presents the fruit detection 
using improved multiple features based algorithm. To detect 
the fruit, an image processing algorithm is trained for efficient 
feature extraction. The algorithm is designed with the aim of 
calculating different weights for features like intensity, color, 
orientation and edge of the input test image. The Detection 
Efficiency is achieved up to 90% for different fruit image on 
tree, captured at different positions.

3) Sample selection: Sample region of fruit, leaf, branch 
and background is selected through polygon mapping. In 
this process cursor can be moved on the screen and the 
desired polygon can be drawn manually as shown in Fig 5. 
This is very efficient process as it helps us to select the 
desired color region for segmentation. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
4) 
Training Feature File Generation: The samples 
generated after the polygon mapping are in binary form. 
These samples are then multiplied with the original image to 
get the RGB samples of the fruit, leaf, branch and 
background region. These RGB samples are then converted 
into L*a*b color space. The L*a*b color is used as a 
method for segmentation where ‘L’ represents the lightness 
value and ‘a’ denotes red or green axes and ‘b’ denotes 
yellow or blue axis. Then the mean ‘a’ and mean ‘b’ value 
is used as a feature for segmentation. This process is 
repeated for the total 25 images of training database, and the  
mean ‘a’ and mean ‘b’ value for each sub-region(fruit, 
leaves, branches and background) is stored  in a feature file 
as shown in Fig. 6. This training feature file is then used to 
segment the test image on the basis on minimum Euclidean 
distance based comparison. 
 
 
Fig. 6 Training Feature file containing mean ‘a’ and mean ‘b’ values of 
training images 
B. Testing Phase: This phase is used for the run time 
implementation of the system. Testing phase consists of 
following steps: 
1) Preprocessing: The same pre-processing steps are 
then performed as training phase on the test image. 
2) 
Euclidean Distance based Segmentation: The next 
step of the algorithm is to segment the test image. The pre-
processed image is then converted into L*a*b plane. Then 
the each pixel of the input image is then classified on the 
basis of minimum Euclidean distance between input a and b 
values and the values of stored training feature file. Thus the 
fruit area is segmented on the input image. 
3) Circle Fitting: Overlaying of circles was then 
performed to get the desired boundaries of fruit regions. 
Circle 
overlaying 
basically 
does 
the 
function 
of 
superimposing on images. Thus with the help of circle 
overlaying we get the desired perfectly segmented apple 
regions.  
4) Individual Apple Segmentation: After that individual 
apples were separated from the occluded apples. And then 
the automated counting of fruits was performed. 
5) Automated Counting: In the last the counting of 
apples in the image is done.

V. CONCLUSION 
The proposed system for identification and apple fruit 
counting fully meets the intended objectives. This system 
can be used to automate the fruit counting process, which 
can be used further to save the money spent on manual 
counting as well as the loss due to erroneous estimations. 
This area has a lot of future scope, which when 
implemented in future will prove to be a very effective 
system to be used in the agricultural field. Future system 
can focus on the counting of green apples as well by
