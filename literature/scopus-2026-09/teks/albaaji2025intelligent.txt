A B S T R A C T
This research paper investigates automated fruit analysis methodologies, explicitly focusing on precise fruit
weight estimation, variety identification, origin determination, and sweetness prediction for four fruit classes
(mango, apple, guava, and orange). A deep synaptic neural framework is proposed, which includes an
object detection network and a multi-modal regressive convolutional neural network. A pair of separate
databases, FruitNet360++ and FruitBox++, have been rigorously chosen to assist the tasks performed within
the framework. The FruitRecognition dataset underwent meticulous restructuring based on geographic origin,
enhancing the effectiveness of fruit quality detection and localization. Utilizing the YOLOv8 methodology, our
framework achieved an impressive average mean accuracy score (mAP) of 92.1%, underscoring its robustness
in accurately identifying and localizing fruits within images. Furthermore, the model exhibits proficiency
in species identification 97.7%, origin determination 98.9%, and sweetness prediction. Incorporating the
representational learning features of residual networks, the framework was very good at estimating the
numerical weights of fruit boxes. It attained a best-case mean square error (MSE) of 0.002 with orange,
topping other fruit types in precision. Furthermore, in sweetness prediction, the framework achieved a
noteworthy MSE of 0.003, exhibiting its ability to accurately assess subtle features of fruit quality. The research
focuses on fully automated fruit weight estimation and yield prediction, particularly for challenging varieties,
which is significantly advancing the field. The framework is a leading option for automated fruit analysis
based on extensive training and testing on large datasets. Future studies aim to refine distance estimates,
broaden datasets, and enhance AI algorithms for comprehensive fruit trait evaluation, driving innovation
and sustainable practices in agriculture. Comprehensive comparisons with recent methods underscore the
accuracy and computational efficiency of the proposed framework. By tackling complexity, data scarcity, and
reproducibility, this study offers a robust, scalable solution adaptable to diverse agricultural contexts.

2. Related studies
The studies on fruits and vegetables are conducted based on specific
attributes such as colour, texture, and edge properties for disease identi-
fication (Wani et al., 2022). Application-based research is conducted to
classify fruits using edge detection, colour chromaticity, shape, and tex-
ture features (Garcia et al., 2016). Artificial vision techniques were em-
ployed to recognize fruits by extracting shape, colour chromaticity, and
texture features. Numerous deep learning methodologies are employed
for fruit and vegetable classification, extending beyond considerations
of colour, texture, and edge characteristics (Kanna et al., 2023). Schol-
ars explore diverse techniques such as gas sensors, near-infrared tech-
nology, and high-performance liquid chromatography instruments for
fruit analysis (Pardo-Mates et al., 2017). Nevertheless, despite utilizing
expensive equipment, these methodologies fail to yield satisfactory
outcomes, as their accuracy typically falls below 85% (Narenderan,
Meyyanathan, & Babu, 2020). To address such limitations, recent stud-
ies have proposed advanced solutions like transfer learning, GANs,
and self-supervised learning to mitigate data scarcity issues (Alzubaidi
et al., 2023). Subsequent researchers have directed their attention
towards image-based fruit classification to tackle this challenge, pri-
marily due to its cost-effectiveness and superior performance (Anjali,
Jena, et al., 2024). A growing dataset is fundamental to implementing
dependable fruit classification models and integrating readily available
benchmark datasets with convolutional neural networks (Ukwuoma
et al., 2022). Combining real-world, high-resolution fruit photos with
online data sources resulted in a comprehensive dataset that utilized
fruit morphology and size characteristics for fruit identification and
localization within the plant structure (Indira et al., 2023). A sep-
arate study introduced a five-layered convolutional neural network
(CNN) to classify a spectrum of 131 distinct fruits and vegetables
from an identical dataset (Joseph, Kumar, & Mathew, 2021). The
quest for heightened precision in forecasting outcomes for unfamiliar
data stems from the perceived limitations inherent in the straightfor-
wardness of existing learning models (Halstead et al., 2018). Using
more sophisticated pre-trained models is expected to result in higher
recognition rates, representing the most straightforward explanation
for this outcome (Ismail & Malik, 2022). Extensive assessment of mul-
tiple pre-trained learning models for fruit grading in the Fruit360
dataset culminated in the development of a real-time visual inspection
system. These inquiries surpassed preceding algorithms, showcasing
computational efficiency within their respective methodologies. Aside
from these developments, one noteworthy finding is the absence of
identification of the geographical origin of fruits, which is critical in
the context of export business. Identifying the origin of fruits and veg-
etables in the export sector is crucial for quality assurance, regulatory
compliance, market distinction, economic advantages, and efficient
supply chain management (Albaaji & Chandra, 2024).
Recent studies in automated fruit grading and yield estimation
have demonstrated the effectiveness of deep learning models in fea-
ture extraction and classification tasks, with accuracy rates often not
satisfied (Albaaji et al., 2023). Compared to these, our YOLOv8-based
framework achieves superior mAP and MSE values, reflecting enhanced
precision in fruit localization and weight estimation across multiple
fruit varieties. Data scarcity remains a challenge in deep learning
applications, especially in agriculture. Various methods, including data
augmentation, transfer learning, and synthetic data generation, have
been proposed to address this issue (Anand & Vinodchandra, 2018).
These methods can substantially enhance model performance in sce-
narios with limited data, which is pertinent to the objectives of the
investigation.

3.3. Fruit quality detector and localizer
The You Only Look Once (YOLO) frameworks represent a class of
single-stage object detection networks designed to efficiently identify
objects and their corresponding categories in a single forward pass.
YOLOv8. This approach is different to dual-stage networks, which
employ separate algorithms for region-of-interest (ROI) identification
and class assignment. It contributes to the precise identification of fruit
quality and localization within fruit boxes. The foundational architec-
ture of earlier versions comprises three interconnected independent
networks, namely, the backbone, neck, and head. The backbone, a con-
volutional neural network (CNN), is a feature extractor, adopting the
Cross Stage Partial (CSP) architecture. The CSPDarkNet53 is a variation
of DenseNet [cite] that enhances learning performance by integrating
gradient information from the base layer with residual feature maps
obtained from later layers. Depending on the dimensions of the CSP
component, different versions of the architecture are available. We
selected the YOLOv8l-seg variant to accommodate the segmentation
task in a comparatively large size of the real-time input data when
deployed. YOLOv8 effectively combines and merges visual data sourced
from the backbone. This integration occurs in a Bi-directional Feature
Pyramid Network (Bi-FPN), enabling the fusion of features across dif-
ferent scales of convolutional maps. The two-way flow of information
within Bi-FPN guarantees the capture of important semantic features
at various object scales during the forward pass while also preserving
local properties during the backward pass. The head layer of YOLOv8
generates box predictions for all potential object occurrences that the
network has been trained to recognize. These predictions comprise all

3.4. Yield estimator
The Yield Predictor Framework represents a significant advance-
ment in fruit analysis technology, offering multi-faceted predictions
crucial for optimizing fruit yield estimation and assisting distribution
and supply. Multimodal Siamses CNN (MSCNN) predicts key produc-
tivity attributes within a fruit box Fig. 3.
The framework learns by sharing learned representations from two
input images: an overall view of the fruit box and segmented portions
of individual fruits. Both images are standardized to a dimension of
224 × 224 pixels in RGB format. Utilizing a combination of Con-
volutional and MBConv (Mobile Inverted Bottleneck Convolutional
Networks) layers inspired by EfficientNets, the framework extracts
intricate features from the input images. These layers are adept at
discerning vitals for subsequent analysis. MBConvNets use a new mix
of inverted residual blocks and linear bottlenecks, which lets them
pull out important residual patterns and shape and texture data for
further analysis (Sobolu et al., 2023). The weights of MBConvNet
layers are trained in a Siamese fashion, with alternate learning from
individual fruit instances and fruit box images. Following feature ex-
traction from Siamese training, the network branches into distinct
paths, each serving a specific prediction task. The weight prediction
path (𝑃 𝑟𝑒𝑑_𝑤) estimates the weight of the fruit box, employing a sig-
moid activation function. Concurrently, the sweetness prediction path

4. Results

concerning fruit quality detection and weight estimation. Unlike tra-

noise, which helped the model generalize effectively. Additionally,
balanced datasets across different fruit types and qualities reduced
model bias, while regularization techniques, including dropout and L2
regularization, minimized overfitting. Performance evaluation metrics
MSE and MAE further confirm that the model maintains accuracy and

5. Discussion
This research addresses a significant gap in fully computerized fruit
weight estimates, especially for those that defy typical quantification
approaches, and contributes considerably to the field’s progress. Fur-
thermore, the study attempts to determine the varieties and locations
of origin of these fruits once they have been identified and evaluated
for quality. Four valuable fruit types were chosen for analysis: mangoes,
apples, guavas, and oranges, all frequently used in countries like India
and Iraq. Three subtypes were selected for each essential fruit group to
guarantee thorough coverage while representing their intrinsic variabil-
ity. Performance on the Fruitnet and FruitRecognize datasets during our
proposed model training and testing phases demonstrates its superiority
to existing methodologies. These datasets were meticulously reassem-
bled, classified, and enriched with real-life images, ensuring a robust
foundation for model evaluation. Our model underscores its efficacy
and advancement in fruit analysis by surpassing the benchmarks from
previous studies discussed in the literature. The comparison of the pro-
posed YOLOv8-based framework with other related works highlights its
superior performance in fruit classification tasks. As shown in Table 4,
models such as MobileNetV2, ResNet50, and CNN+SVM achieve high
accuracy, with values ranging from 85% to 94.36%, but face limitations
in generalization or efficiency in low-resource scenarios. In contrast,
the proposed YOLOv8 framework outperforms these models with an
impressive mAP of 92.1% and an accuracy of 97.7%, demonstrating its
ability to deliver high accuracy across diverse features. These results are
further supported by an MSE of 0.002 for weight estimation, underscor-
ing the advancements of a framework in fruit quality assessment and
yield estimation precision.
To evaluate the robustness of the model to noise and input distur-
bances, we incorporated image noise techniques during the YOLOv8
validation phase as present in Fig. 18. These techniques simulate real-
world challenges such as lighting changes, partial occlusions, and
noise, comprehensively assessing the ability of the model to generalize
effectively. The results in Table 5 demonstrate the performance of
the model under various noise conditions, including Gaussian, Salt
& Pepper, Saturation, and Mosaic noise, measured by precision and
detection time. Under Gaussian noise, increasing the variance from 10
to 50 reduced precision (0.921 to 0.801) while detection time remained
stable. For Salt & Pepper noise, precision declined slightly (0.954 to
0.874) as noise levels increased, with no effect on detection time.
Similarly, saturation noise caused a gradual precision drop (0.944 to
0.882) at higher saturation factors. In Mosaic noise, the performance
of the model decreased significantly with larger grid sizes; precision
dropped from 0.853 (Grid Size 10) to 0.781 (Grid Size 30), and at Grid

6. Conclusion
Implementing a computerized assessment for fruit weights, espe-
cially for kinds that are difficult to measure using standard methods,
represents an essential advancement in fruit investigation. This study
contributes a novel approach to fruit analysis by integrating YOLOv8
for precise fruit detection with a Multimodal Siamese CNN for advanced
yield and quality estimations. This dual-stage approach enhances ac-
curacy and practical application potential in machine learning for
agricultural automation. Due to accurate analysis and comprehensive
testing, our proposed model has proven helpful in various fruit eval-
uation uses, including weight estimation, identifying varieties, and
origin determination. Furthermore, using the YOLOv8 has improved
the performance of the model throughout the examination, highlighting
its potential for widespread adoption in fruit analysis and related
fields. Despite the significant progress achieved in this research, several
prospects still exist for deeper investigation and improvement. One
intriguing area for future study is improving the accuracy in calculating
the weights of fruits presented at different distances inside images. The
model architecture might undergo iterative changes or cutting-edge
image processing techniques to improve distance estimates. Addition-
ally, future research may examine extending the dataset to include a
broader range of fruit varieties and geographical regions, improving
the dependability and usefulness in various scenarios. Algorithms and
techniques may provide a more comprehensive review of fruit attribute
yield prediction methods. Admittedly, other studies and developments
in this field have the potential to spark innovation and improve agri-
cultural production efficiency, eventually contributing to the larger
objective of encouraging sustainability in farming operations. Future
fruit analysis efforts have the potential to solve essential concerns
and drive revolutionary change in the agriculture industry by using
technological improvements and multidisciplinary methods.
