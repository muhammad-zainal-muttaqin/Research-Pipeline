Abstract—Mango is an agricultural product that is of im-
portant economic significance in Thailand, requiring yield es-
timation in order to plan for the harvest stage effectively.
This research presents Mango fruit detection and counting on
a canopy tree using a deep learning-based object detection
model, Mask R-CNN and Cascade R-CNN. To acquire an
image of a mango tree in the current circumstances, partial
occlusion of mangoes usually occurs. We enhance the model
learning by combining the Random Erasing method for image
augmentation to improve performance in dealing with partial
occlusion issues. This paper compares the performance of the
different backbone layer for feature extraction. We utilize the
most optimal backbone, RestNext-101-32x8d-FPN is an essential
component of the model. The Mask R-CNN with Random
Erasing outperforms the Cascade R-CNN achieving the highest
performance with a precision score of 0.922, a recall score of
0.786, an F1-score of 0.849, a mAP@50 score of 0.883, and
counting performance MAE score of 1.106 fruits per image.
The best model can be deployed in real-world applications.
Index Terms—Mango fruit detection, Random Erasing, Mask
R-CNN, Cascade R-CNN, Instance segmentation
I. INTRODUCTION
Mangoes are an important agricultural product in Thailand;
this agricultural product is mostly cultivated for exportation
as fruit products and further processing such as desserts and
ingredients in food, also the country is conducive environ-
mental conditions for the cultivation of growth. In the normal
pre-harvest phase, it is necessary to perform preparations
pertaining to human labor, storage facilities, and downstream
transportation as part of the supply chain. Thus, having the
information about the fruit yield estimation as quantity is
advantageous.
The arrival of smart agriculture technologies is powerful,
and enabling the ability to capture and store information
from the area cultivation for the purpose of data-driven
management, it has effectively contributed to their business
decision-making. Computer vision has been utilized to acquire
images from objects such as fruit products on canopy trees,
which images are then processed to extract particular features,
allowing their use for diverse purposes. The purpose is to use
remote sensing data collected from satellite sources to forecast
yield estimation [1]; however, there is a generally large area
that is a non-specific geographical region. In addition, the fruit
yield estimation by tree sampling [2] is a method to collect
images from a specific field, which detects several stone fruits
present in trees and then counts the total quantity of fruit
discovered; there are methods to provide a promising accuracy
approximately to the outcome of human’s performance.
Recently, there has been much research aimed at employing
computer vision to accurately estimate agricultural product
yields, for example, predicting the crop output of apple groves
[3], and utilising high-angle photography through remote
sensing techniques, the image was taken by an unmanned
aerial vehicle (UAV) at top view, hovering in an aligned row
to collect visual data, using a deep learning model applying
Mask R-CNN that detects fruit, which includes image aug-
mentation, such as adjusting brightness and rotation. Ander-
son et al. [4] developed an autonomous vehicle equipped with
a camera to navigate a garden bed and capture images as a
dual view, and analyze each row of the garden rather than
focus on individual trees. According to the previous research
above, it is necessary to collect historical yield data. Also, the
stems should be cultivated and aligned in a straight pattern in
order to utilize linear regression to achieve the best estimation.
Ma et al. [5] used YOLO-tiny to detect objects, which allows
the multi-view aspect of this research to capture images of
apples from single angles per tree with an optimal point of
view. Moreover, there are several cases of missing detection
due to occlusion caused by tree branches or leaves and a fruit
overlapping. Neupane et al. [6] and Chen et al. [7] proposed
image segmentation using Mask R-CNN along with the ability
to detect overlapping fruit.
There have been studies on mango detection. Devi et al. [8]
and Neethi et al. [9] presented a method for extracting features
from images using image analysis with color thresholding,
image contour and connected components, counting a fruit
with circular fitting algorithm. Many processing parameter
adjustments would be necessary if it were to be worked with
various environments so that less robustness compared to deep
learning [2], [5]. Kestur et el. [10] proposed a MangoNet
which is a deep learning model, to detect a mango on

II. METHODOLOGY
The approach of recognizing and counting the number of
mangoes on a tree canopy uses deep learning techniques to
identify and classify objects as shown in Figure 1. An image
was taken from the mango orchard via digital camera and
smartphone with a resolution of 4032 x 3021, a total of 308
images. The ”Nam Dok Mai” mango cultivar is cultivated in
the ”Farm AOR.ACE” in Chainat Province, Thailand. They
are in unripe states that have been through a cultivation period
of around 50 days from the mangoes bloom, then they will
shield the mangoes for pets and disease control, improved
quality and appearance. During training augmentation tech-
nique is included, such as resizing, and flipping along with
Random Erasing then compare two deep learning models are
compare and capable of identifying objects by utilizing two
instance segmentation: Mask R-CNN and Cascade R-CNN.
A. Image Augmentation
Regarding the issue of overfitting in model deep learning
caused by a small dataset, an option is to augment the
dataset by including various data in different scenarios, thus
enhancing the robustness of the model, which is implemented
in process training. In the field of computer vision, we
utilize geometric transforms and image normalization [12] to
modify images, such as resizing, and flipping. This research
proposes an approach of baseline augmentation by resizing
the image to a dimensions of 1333x800 and flipping it with a
random probability of 0.5. Furthermore, there are alternative
approaches specifically designed for solving specific issues.
1) Random Erasing: This technique aims to enhance oc-
clusion problems through image augmentation [13]; a random
square box is generated to randomly occlude an area in the
image. The defined parameter for random to generated erase
with probability of 0.5, the quantity of boxes generated in a
range of [3,10], the area ratio in a range of [0.1,0.5], the color
of the box generated is 128. Figure 2 shows image annotation
Random Erasing which has generated a total 4 square boxes
occluding the labelled objects and determine that the object
was originally labeled, and Figure 3 show its original image.
B. Model Construction
Convolutional neural network (CNN) is a technique used
to extract features from an image; it uses a matched filter to
perform cross-correlation on the images in order to extract
patterns that are of relevance for data to training a model. In
order to achieve precise feature extraction, it is necessary to
have multiple filter layers.
1) ResNeXt Block Backbones: Backbone, in the context of
CNN architecture, is a repeating unit that combines a group of
cardinality with the same structure; each group is structured
in a hierarchy to offer a diverse range for adjusting weight
learning; the last layer has been combined with the identity
layer for prevent vanishing gradient problem [14]. RestNext-
101-32x8d and RestNext-101-32x4d are the most commonly
used backbones and different amounts of feature extraction
in group cardinality.
2) Feature Pyramid Network (FPN): Methods for extract-
ing features of images to attain optimal object detection at
different scales. Therefore, this approach splits the extraction
process into two separate parts: Bottom-up are derived by
using cascading convolutions, which will possess exceptional
characteristics of significant value. Bottom-down, the layer on
top will expand resolution, and a convolution filter is included
to keep the unique characteristics of each scale [15]. The
model includes the Feature Pyramid Network into the Neck
section of the CNN architecture. It is positioned after the
Backbone to improve the extraction of features and enhance
performance.
3) Mask R-CNN Architecture [16]: It is a development of
the Faster R-CNN algorithm, capable of generating binary
masks on regions of interest at the pixel level for image seg-
mentation in the block of Region of Interesting (RoI), which
implements with RoI Align in order to enable upsampling
of the mask on mangoes area with convolution. The model
utilizes a Region Proposal Neural Network (RPN) to generate
anchor boxes for identifying candidate regions that have an
area overlap with annotations over a specific threshold using
a Non-maximum suppression (NMS) algorithm to eliminate
candidate objects with lower overlap until only the contender
with the highest overlap remains. The pipeline involves pass-
ing the output from the Region of Interest (RoI) to a fully
convolutional network. Cross-entropy loss function is used to
classify mangoes and an L1 loss function for regression to
determine the mangoes’ bounding box.
4) Cascade R-CNN Architecture [17]: It is a developed
version of the Mask R-CNN algorithm, which also generates a
mask on the region of interest. The model reduced overfitting
occurs due to a decrease in the amount of positive data used

A. Evaluation metrics
Object detection research evaluates the performance by
comparing ground truths with predicted areas [18], consid-
ering the area overlapping of both areas is Intersection over
Union (IoU), and the calculation can be performed using
an equation (1). The prediction result is positive (detected
mango) when IoU value is more than 0.5 experimentally.

The confidence threshold is the minimum probability
threshold necessary for classifying an object as positive.
Therefore, we select the confidence threshold corresponding
with the highest F1-score on the validation dataset, and then
we employ that confidence threshold to assess the testing
dataset. The IoU threshold will be used to evaluate the follow-
ing metric for detection performance. Precision is a capability
to classify objects as either positive or negative. Recall is a
capability to identify and recognize objects positively in an
image. F1-score is calculated using the harmonic mean of
precision and recall. mAP@50 is obtained by computing the
average of the accuracy and recall curves for every confidence
value using IoU above 50%. MAE is a mean absolute error
obtained by computing the error between manual count and
model inference true positive.
B. Tools and environment setting experimental
OpenMMLab Detection [19] is used as a framework tool
specifically designed for creating object detection with deep-
learning models; it can easily modify parameters as configu-
ration files, which contain various kinds of model baselines.
This case the positive class represents mangoes, while the
negative class represents the background. In the implementa-
tion, the dataset is split into three parts: training, testing, and
validation, with a ratio of 70:15:15; 221 images for testing
with 1339 annotations, 40 images for validation with 224
annotations, and 47 images for testing with 260 annotations,
the dataset was label images using polygon annotations.
Throughout the training process, configure the parameter of
the Mask R-CNN model to use Stochastic Gradient Descent
(SGD) as the optimizer, with a learning rate of 0.0025. The

C. Comparing the performance of different backbone archi-
tectures
This experiment aims to investigate the backbone, which
performs an important part in extracting features image input.
The main difference between ResNext-101-32x4d-FPN and
ResNext-101-32x8d-FPN is the width per group, determining
the total number of feature channels in the bottleneck layer.
The ResNext-101-32x8d-FPN backbone, along with the Mask
R-CNN and Cascade RCNN model architectures, have better
performance than the ResNext-101-32x4d-FPN backbone in
overall, as shown in the result in Table I. For Mask R-CNN,
the F1-score exhibits a slight enhancement by 1.1%, while
mAP@50 exhibits an impressive improvement by 5.2%. For
Cascade R-CNN, the F1-score shows an impressive rise of
2.7%, whereas the mAP@50 has decreased by 0.2, which
indicates an area under the graph precision-recall curve has
decreased. Nevertheless, if focusing strictly on the best areas
in the graph, it becomes obvious that the model has enhanced
potential, which is precision and recall to increase.
The experimental results indicate that increasing the dimen-
sion of the feature data expands its ability to recognize varied
image patterns effectively.

D. Performance in detecting mango using Random Erasing
augmentation with model Mask R-CNN and Cascade R-CNN
architecture.
This experiment investigates the results obtained by uti-
lizing two deep-learning object detection models, including
Mask R-CNN and Cascade R-CNN with backbone ResNext-
101-32x8d-FPN, to detect mangoes. Table II presents the
results obtained from testing the dataset with baseline aug-
mentation and Random Erasing techniques to improve the
performance specifically in cases of mango occlusion.
For the Random Erasing augmentation method, Mask R-
CNN experimentally selects a confidence threshold of 0.5,
while Cascade R-CNN employs a threshold of 0.4. According
to the obtained result, Mask R-CNN demonstrates a higher
F1-score and mAP@50 than Cascade R-CNN, with an in-
crease of 1.9% in F1-score and 0.9% in mAP@50. In addition,
we considered the cascade R-CNN with Random Erasing,
which improved the mAP@50 score with an increase of 2.5%
compared to the baseline method. However, there was a drop
of 0.7% in the F1-score, indicating that Random Erasing leads
to overfitting due to the architecture including a three-stage
cascade component, which leads to over-optimization loss
bounding box, there is an excessive abundance of positive in-
stances. Consequently, the performance for classifying objects
decreases. However, Random Erasing demonstrates efficient
performance with Mask R-CNN; compared to the baseline,
it obtained an increase of 2.3% in precision, 2% in recall,
1% in f1-score, and 1.3% in mAP@50. We investigate our
best experimented model in solving the occlusion problem,
as shown in Figure 4a, which illustrates two overlapping
mangoes. Figure 5a displays a mango, which was hidden
partially behind the leaves. Figure 4b and 5b show our model
correctly detected by Random Erasing added in the model.
We compare the results of the true positive obtained from
the model’s inference and the manual count in the counting
performance. The Mask R-CNN model with Random Erasing
demonstrates superior performance compared to other models,
and the model attained a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for
counting mangoes on a mango tree of 1.106 fruits per image
as a result of its precise detection and effective classification
of mangoes. The Cascade R-CNN reported an increase in the
detection of true positives, but a loss in the detection of false
positives, resulting in overall poor performance.

IV. CONCLUSION
Mango object detection can be employed to recognize man-
goes on the canopy, giving an estimation for the subsequent
downstream preparation. We developed the model based on
its predicted scenario and conducted an experiment utilizing
deep learning models, particularly Mask R-CNN and Cascade
R-CNN, to detect mangoes on a canopy tree. ResNext-
101-32x8d-FPN, used as the backbone layer demonstrates
better performance compared to ResNext-101-32x4d-FPN.
Data augmentation techniques is implemented to enhance ro-
bustness, especially for occlusion problems, through Random
Erasing technique. The comparison of the models discovered
that Mask R-CNN, including Random Erasing, is superior
across all metrics, achieving a precision of 0.922, recall of
0.786, F1-score of 0.849, mAP@50 of 0.883 and counting
