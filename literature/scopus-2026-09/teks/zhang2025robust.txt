A B S T R A C T
Accurate counting is essential for enabling automated blueberry harvesting and logistics decision-making in 
greenhouse environments. However, the features of small size, dense distribution, and frequent occlusion of 
blueberries result in severe missed detections and duplicate counting, making accurate counting highly chal­
lenging for yield estimation. To address these challenges, we propose an effective technical framework for real- 
time accurate counting of ripe blueberries in greenhouse environments, including specific deep-learning algo­
rithms of lightweight small-object detection and mamba-based trajectory completion. First we introduce a hybrid 
loss function and a multi-scale feature fusion module, both integrated into the YOLOv12n model to enhance 
small object detection performance. Then in the trajectory prediction phase, we design encoders based on KAN 
and decoders based on Mamba, integrating multi-target positions and trajectories to achieve efficient and ac­
curate position prediction. Thereafter we put forward a prediction-driven two-stage matching strategy based on 
ByteTrack, which effectively reduces duplicate counting caused by missed detections. Experimental results 
demonstrate the validity and robustness of the framework, and also highlight the algorithms’ performance ad­
vantages. The proposed lightweight small-object detection method increases precision by 8.3% and mAP@50 by 
2.9% compared to the baseline on the public PEST24 dataset, and achieves an object detection mAP@50 of 0.877 
on the real blueberry greenhouse dataset, The trajectory prediction based on our mamba-based trajectory 
completion algorithm results in an ADE of 15.4 pixels and an FDE of 24.8 pixels over 6 frames, and a single-frame 
ADE of 3.68 pixels. For blueberry greenhouse counting task, the overall pipeline achieves an accuracy of 92.5% 
and a processing speed of 35 FPS on the edge device. It effectively addresses repeated counting caused by oc­
clusion and small target size, thereby achieving reliable fruit detection and accurate counting.
1. Introduction
In facility agriculture, accurate fruit counting forms the foundation 
for intelligent decision-making, harvest management, and yield pre­
diction. Small berry crops, such as blueberries, are characterized by 
small fruit size, dense distribution, overlapping ripening periods, and 
frequent occlusion. These features render traditional manual counting 
methods inefficient and error-prone, failing to meet the accuracy and 
scalability requirements of modern automated greenhouse production.
Existing fruit counting methods primarily fall into two categories: 
traditional image processing techniques and deep learning-based 
detection algorithms. Representative approaches include threshold 
segmentation combined with the watershed algorithm [1], image 
enhancement [2], image segmentation [3], YOLO-based detection [4,5], 
and methods integrating Transformer and Mask R-CNN [6]. Although 
these methods improve the accuracy of frame-by-frame detection, most 
systems estimate the total count by accumulating frame-wise detections. 
This approach can easily result in duplicate counting in video-based fruit 
counting tasks [7]. To address this issue, multi-object tracking (MOT) 
was introduced to the detection framework to ensure cross-frame con­
sistency by assigning unique identities to each fruit. However, chal­
lenges such as trajectory drift and identity switching still persist [8,9].
In real-world scenarios, factors such as camera shake and uneven 
ground surfaces often result in nonlinear trajectories, leading to com­
mon issues such as trajectory drift, identity switches, and duplicate 
counting [10]. To overcome these limitations, deep learning–based 
trajectory prediction methods have been proposed, aiming to forecast 
future object positions based on historical motion patterns. For instance, 
Trajectory Former [11] and PECNet [12] are capable of modeling 
complex trajectory dynamics and have demonstrated high-precision

motion forecasting performance. Nevertheless, relying solely on an in­
dividual target’s historical trajectory often overlooks the dynamic in­
teractions between agents, and fails to exploit the motion information of 
neighboring targets. Therefore approaches such as Social LSTM [13] and 
Social GAN [14] attempt to incorporate the behavior of nearby agents by 
modeling within-frame interactions. These methods introduce social 
behavior modeling mechanisms to trajectory prediction, enabling the 
network to better capture inter-agent motion dependencies. However, 
such methods focus on local neighborhood relationships but ignore the 
temporal dynamics of neighboring targets and their influence on the 
main trajectory. As a result, these approaches struggle to provide 
effective performance under occlusion conditions. This limitation is 
particularly critical in small fruit counting, where occlusions are 
frequent and severe. Currently, mainstream trajectory modeling 
methods are primarily based on Transformers, LSTMs, and MLPs. 
However, each has significant limitations in practical applications. MLPs 
struggle to capture complex nonlinear interactions [15]; LSTMs have 
limited capability in modeling long-term dependencies [16]; and while 
Transformers offer strong performance, they require excessive compu­
tational resources, making them unsuitable for edge deployment [17]. 
Therefore, achieving efficient and robust trajectory modeling for small 
fruit counting under dense occlusion remains a critical and unsolved 
challenge.
As discussed previously, trajectory modeling using MLPs suffers from 
limited fitting ability, while self-attention mechanisms incur excessive 
computational overhead. These drawbacks motivate us to consider more 
efficient and expressive alternatives. The Kolmogorov-Arnold Network 
(KAN) [18] can fit high-order nonlinear functions with low computa­
tional overhead and minimal dependence on dataset size, making it 
effective for modeling complex interactions and nonlinear motion pat­
terns between fruits. Mamba [19], a new generation of efficient 
state-space sequence modeling frameworks, accurately captures 
long-term temporal dependencies while maintaining linear growth in 
parameter size and computational complexity. It offers greater repre­
sentational power than models such as LSTM and incurs significantly 
lower computational overhead than Transformer architectures. These 
features are crucial for applications such as greenhouse environments, 
where edge computing power is limited and target motion patterns 
change nonlinearly over extended periods.
To address the challenge of accurately counting densely packed 
small fruits in greenhouse environments, this paper proposes a 
comprehensive framework integrating enhanced small-object detection, 
interactive perception-based trajectory prediction, and multi-target 
tracking. This framework effectively models complex motion relation­
ships and long-term dependencies among fruits. The proposed pipeline 
is given as Fig. 1. And our main contributions of this paper are as follows: 
(1) We propose a lightweight small-object detection model based on 
YOLOv12 [20], with a hybrid loss function (HL) to address the 
challenges of small-object detection, together with a lightweight 
module named MCSPF. The proposed method significantly im­
proves blueberry detection accuracy in real-world scenarios and 
consistently outperforms the baseline on the public small-object 
detection benchmark PEST24 [21].
(2) We propose a lightweight GamaKAN encoder that jointly models 
the historical trajectories and spatial positions of both primary 
and neighboring auxiliary targets via spatio-temporal in­
teractions. This approach significantly improves trajectory pre­
diction accuracy and robustness under occlusion and complex 
motion conditions. The framework achieves top performance on 
our dataset and demonstrates strong generalizability on the 
MOT17 [22] dataset.
(3) We put forward a real-time deep-learning based blueberry 
counting framework that integrates small object detection, 
spatio-temporal interactive trajectory prediction, and a two-stage 
trajectory completion strategy. This system achieves high- 
precision counting in greenhouse environments by effectively 
reducing duplicate counts caused by occlusion and missed 
detections.

2.2. Object detection model
Given the dynamic nature of the detection task, the dataset 
frequently contains challenging samples such as motion-blurred objects, 
heavy occlusions, and small targets. To address these issues, we adopt 
the latest YOLOv12 model as the baseline. We propose a hybrid loss 
function to replace the IoU loss in the box loss of YOLOv12, and intro­
duce the Small-object Perception Fusion (MCSPF) module to replace the 
A2C2f module in the head. The enhanced YOLOv12 architecture pro­
posed in this study is illustrated in Fig. 3.
2.2.1. Hybrid loss
Our blueberry dataset contains a large number of small objects, with 
the smallest target as small as 16 pixels. In small object detection tasks, 
challenges such as limited target size, blurred boundaries, and amplified 
localization errors are particularly pronounced. To address these issues, 
we design a stage-wise hybrid loss strategy that leverages the strengths 
of different loss functions at different training stages. This method 
effectively balances global localization accuracy and boundary refine­
ment throughout the optimization process.

we employ the Small-Object Focal Wasserstein Loss as the primary 
regression objective. This loss is formulated based on the Wasserstein 
distance, where the center-point error is normalized by the target’s 
width and height. A focal mechanism is introduced to emphasize 
regression gradients for small objects and samples with small spatial 
deviations. In addition, logarithmic smoothing is applied to width and 
height errors to suppress the influence of extreme scale deviations on 
gradient magnitude. This stage enables the network to rapidly establish 
a coarse spatial localization capability and learn scale-aware represen­

2.2.2. MCSPF (multi-branch cross-stage pyramid fusion)
To improve the detection of small and densely packed objects, a 
Multi-branch Cross-stage Pyramid Fusion (MCSPF) module is proposed. 
Based on the Cross Stage Partial (CSP) architecture, the module divides 
the input features into two parallel branches: one serves as a direct re­
sidual connection to retain low-level semantic information, while the 
other passes through multiple deep residual blocks followed by a Spatial 
Pyramid Pooling (SPP) module to achieve multi-scale and multi-level 
feature fusion.
Specifically, the MCSPF module first applies a 1 × 1 convolution to 
divide the input features into two branches. One branch bypasses further 
processing to retain shallow semantic information, while the other 
stacks multiple inverted residual blocks for deep feature extraction. An 
spp block is inserted within this path to significantly enhance the net­
work’s ability to capture objects at varying scales. The two branches are 
concatenated along the channel axis and then fused through another 1 ×
1 convolution to produce the final output. 
O = Conv1×1(Concat[Conv1×1(X1), SPP(ResBlock(X2))])

Here, X denotes the input feature map, ResBlock represents the 
stacked residual block sequence, and SPP refers to the Spatial Pyramid 
Pooling module. By leveraging multi-branch fusion and multi-scale 
enhancement, the MCSPF module effectively maintains a trade-off be­
tween computational efficiency and feature representation quality. It 
significantly improves detection accuracy for small objects and in 
complex scenarios. Moreover, the module demonstrates high inference 
speed and strong portability, making it well-suited for real-world 
deployment.

2.3.1. OTAM (other trajectory aggregation module)
To achieve robust modeling of the primary target in complex scenes, 
relying solely on its own trajectory is often insufficient to handle oc­
clusions and interactions. Therefore, we introduce an “other-target- 
aware” mechanism into the trajectory prediction framework by aggre­
gating information from surrounding trajectories to enhance the spatial 
understanding of the primary target. To this end, we design a light­
weight module named Other Trajectory Aggregation Module (OTAM), 
which models inter-object spatial relationships using attention pooling.
The module takes as input the center-position tensor of all auxiliary 
targets, where each entry represents the 2D center coordinate of a 
neighboring object at a given frame. The position of the primary target 
at each frame is mapped as the query vector, and it interacts with the key 
and value vectors constructed from the same-frame auxiliary targets via 
scaled dot-product attention. The output is a spatial context represen­
tation that encodes the local environment as perceived by the primary 
target.
Within the overall framework, the spatial features generated by 
OTAM are used in two separate paths: (1) they are concatenated with the 
primary trajectory and fed into the primary trajectory encoder to sup­
plement environmental semantics, and (2) they are aggregated into a 
global motion representation of auxiliary targets, which serves as input 
to the auxiliary trajectory encoder. This modular design enables spatial 
and temporal decoupling and aggregation of primary and auxiliary 
trajectory information, substantially enhancing the model’s robustness 
under occlusion and interference.
2.3.2. GamaKAN: lightweight trajectory fusion encoding module
To effectively model the temporal trajectory of the primary target 
while incorporating its spatial context at each frame, we design a 
GamaKAN module as the core component of the primary trajectory 
modeling path. The input to this module is a concatenated sequence 
composed of the primary target’s historical positions and the context- 
aware spatial features aggregated from the OTAM module. The output 
is a dynamic feature representation of the primary target across the 
entire historical window.
Compared to conventional structures such as MLPs or Transformers, 
KAN achieves a better trade-off between representational capacity and 
computational efficiency, making it well-suited for deployment in 
resource-limited agricultural scenarios. The overall architecture of 
GamaKAN is illustrated in Fig. 5.
The GamaKAN module first applies a depth-wise separable convo­
lution to encode the fused trajectory sequence. This structure uses 
channel-wise convolutions to capture intra-channel temporal patterns, 
followed by pointwise (1 × 1) convolutions to integrate information 
across channels. This step generates a feature representation that is both 
locally sensitive and adaptively responsive to temporal variations, 
forming a stable foundation for subsequent nonlinear modeling in the 
temporal dimension.
To enhance the model’s capacity to model complex trajectory evo­
lution patterns, GamaKAN incorporates a nonlinear transformation 
block based on KAN. According to the Kolmogorov–Arnold representa­
tion theorem, any multivariate continuous function can be expressed as 
a superposition of univariate functions, which theoretically provides

trainability of neural networks while providing strong structural inter­
pretability and numerical stability.
To further enhance the stability and robustness of feature represen­
tations, GamaKAN introduces a channel-wise gating mechanism after 
nonlinear modeling. This gating unit learns the relative importance of 
each trajectory channel for the primary target and adjusts the fusion 
weight between the nonlinear output and the original input accordingly.
The final output of the GamaKAN module is a high-dimensional 
temporal representation of the primary trajectory over the historical 
window. This output is passed to the subsequent primary–auxiliary 
fusion module, where it is combined with spatial features generated by 
OTAM. This integration effectively fuses environmental context with 
dynamic motion cues, forming the foundation for downstream multi- 
object interaction modeling and trajectory forecasting.
2.3.3. OTEM (other trackers encode model)
In multi-object trajectory prediction, explicitly modeling the motion 
trends and spatial distribution of auxiliary agents is crucial for 
enhancing the prediction accuracy of the primary target. To better 
capture group behavior patterns, we propose a dedicated auxiliary tra­
jectory encoding module that extracts dynamic semantic cues from the 
surrounding agent clusters and provides rich contextual information for 
primary target forecasting. The module takes as input a tensor con­
taining multiple auxiliary trajectories, from which n representative 
auxiliary agents are selected per frame. Their center-point sequences 
serve as the basis for temporal modeling, balancing computational ef­
ficiency and representational completeness.
Structurally, the module adopts an attention pooling mechanism to 
aggregate the spatial motion features of the selected auxiliary trajec­
tories, where the primary target serves as the query and the auxiliary 
targets as keys and values. By computing attention weights, the module 
adaptively fuses auxiliary features, yielding a compact behavioral rep­
resentation highly relevant to the primary target. The aggregated fea­
tures are then passed through a lightweight temporal encoder based on 
the GamaKAN architecture, which integrates linear projection, depth­
wise separable convolutions, and KAN-based nonlinear units to extract 
temporally coherent semantic representations. The design maintains 
structural symmetry between the primary and auxiliary encoding 
pathways, facilitating unified modeling and effective feature fusion in 
subsequent stages.
The output of the auxiliary trajectory encoding module is integrated 
with the primary target encoding in the gated fusion module to achieve 
feature-level alignment and unified interaction modeling. This design 
enables efficient and robust multi-agent behavior modeling, signifi­

2.3.4. Gated fusion module (KG)
To mitigate semantic discrepancies between the encoded features of 
primary and auxiliary targets—which can cause instability in trajectory 
prediction when directly concatenated or merged—we introduce a 
Gated Fusion Module to enable feature alignment and dynamic inte­
gration across the two streams.
The inputs to this module are the trajectory encoding features of both 
the primary and auxiliary targets. Each stream is first projected into a 
unified representation space via separate linear transformation layers to 
ensure consistent dimensionality. The projected features are then 
concatenated along the feature dimension and fed into a gating sub­
module. This submodule comprises a linear layer followed by a Sigmoid 
activation, which learns adaptive fusion weights to balance the contri­
butions of each target type.
The fused representation retains the key dynamic patterns of the 
primary target while incorporating contextual information from the 
auxiliary group behavior. This mechanism enhances the model’s 
robustness in scenarios involving dense interactions or partial target 
disappearance, ensuring more stable and accurate trajectory forecasting.
To further enhance the nonlinear expressiveness of the fused repre­
sentation, we introduce a gated channel-wise nonlinear transformation 
layer (KG), constructed based on Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks (KAN). 
This component applies a set of one-dimensional spline-based nonlinear 
mappings across individual channels, enabling flexible, learnable 
transformations on the fused feature space. The output of this layer is 
then passed to the decoder module for final position prediction.
The KG module achieves semantic alignment and integration be­
tween the primary and auxiliary encoding pathways. Through its gating 
mechanism, it provides dynamic control over the contribution of 
different feature channels, ensuring consistent multi-target modeling 
and stable trajectory forecasting. The resulting unified semantic repre­
sentation is forwarded to the decoder to generate multi-frame future 
trajectory predictions.

2.4. Multi-object tracking framework based on trajectory prediction and 
two-stage track completion mechanism
In crowded and heavily occluded scenarios, conventional tracking 
methods that rely on detection and association frequently experience 
trajectory interruptions and identity switches. To address these prob­
lems, we propose a trajectory prediction-centered counting framework 
consisting of four key components: trajectory state encoding, spatial 
interaction reasoning, state forecasting, and track maintenance. First, 
the GamaKAN encoder encodes the historical trajectory of the primary 
target to generate a dynamic temporal feature representation. Second, 
the OTAM network models the spatial interactions among auxiliary 
targets, providing contextual environmental cues for primary trajectory 
prediction. Third, the primary and auxiliary features are fused using the 
gated fusion module and passed to the Mamba decoder, which predicts 
the target’s next position. The output not only guides future motion 
estimation but also serves as a fallback state reference when detection 
fails during tracking.
To enhance the robustness of trajectory maintenance, we propose a 
two-stage trajectory completion mechanism built upon the ByteTrack 
method, which incorporates a trajectory prediction module. In the first 
stage, the current detection box is matched with all active trajectories 
using a standard matching approach. During matching, the trajectory 
state estimate is provided by the linear extrapolation from the trajectory 
prediction module, and the cost matrix is calculated using the pixel 
distance between center points. For unmatched trajectories, if their 
history length meets the minimum requirement of the prediction mod­
ule, the extrapolated box is activated and participates in the second- 
stage matching.
In the second stage, the predicted extrapolated boxes are matched 
with the remaining unmatched detection boxes. If a match is found, the 
track is updated with the predicted position and continues to be main­
tained. If a track fails to match any detection for a specified number of 
consecutive frames, it is considered terminated and removed.
Compared with traditional detection-based multi-object tracking 
methods, the proposed two-stage completion mechanism incorporates 
trajectory prediction into the association process. This integration en­
ables the algorithm to utilize both detection and prediction information, 
which significantly reduces ID switching and trajectory fragmentation 
caused by short-term occlusion or detection failure, and effectively 
mitigates repeated counting resulting from frame loss.
As shown in Fig. 6, the area within the yellow box experienced 
missed detections due to small object size and motion blur. However, 
when the object was re-detected, the ID remained consistent, demon­
strating the robustness of the dual-stage trajectory recovery method 
proposed in this study.
2.5. Loss supervision
To enhance the detection performance for small objects, we reuse the 
Wasserstein distance-based loss function described in Section 2.2.1, and 
employ it to supervise trajectory prediction with the aim of improving 
small-object trajectory prediction accuracy. In addition, ADE loss, FDE 
loss, and Velocity Loss (VEL Loss) are combined in a weighted manner to 
jointly supervise bounding box regression. Experimental results 
demonstrate that this combination of losses achieves superior perfor­
mance on small-object scenarios in our greenhouse dataset.

3.3. Performance evaluation of multi-object tracking algorithms
Ablation and comparative experiments are conducted to evaluate the 
effectiveness of the proposed trajectory prediction module and multi- 
object tracking framework. The experimental setup is carefully 
designed to isolate and verify the contribution of each module, 
demonstrating the superiority of our approach through comprehensive 
evaluation. As shown in the Fig. 8, the schematic diagram of multi- 
object tracking shows that our method in this study performs stably in 
the real blueberry greenhouse scenario, and the repeated counting 
caused by ID switching and other factors is significantly reduced.
3.3.1. Ablation study on multi-object tracking algorithm
To systematically assess the effectiveness of each component in our 
framework, we conducted seven groups of ablation experiments as fol­
lows. Group A investigates the impact of the KAN structure and gating 
mechanism within the GamaKAN module by replacing KAN with MLP, 
multi-head attention, and linear attention, as well as comparing 
different gating strategies. Group B replaces the GamaKAN encoder with 
a Mamba encoder to evaluate the effect of the encoder architecture. 
Group C compares various pooling methods. Group D examines the in­
fluence of removing information from other trajectories and auxiliary 
target positions, respectively. Group E evaluates the KG module by 
replacing KAN with MLP and attention mechanisms, and by ablating the 
gating mechanism. Group F investigates different decoder structures by 
comparing Mamba, GRU, and LSTM. Finally, Group G represents the 
complete method proposed in this work, integrating all optimized 
modules and achieving the best overall performance. Table 3 reports the 
results in terms of Average Displacement Error (ADE), Final Displace­
ment Error (FDE), and Frames Per Second (FPS).
Results indicate that while baseline methods such as MLP and 
average pooling achieve high inference speeds, they exhibit large pre­
diction errors, rendering them unsuitable for real-world deployment. 
Although multi-head attention improves prediction accuracy, it sub­
stantially increases computational cost, thereby limiting practical 
applicability. Removing either the cooperative target modeling or 
tracking information improves speed but leads to a notable drop in ac­
curacy and generalization ability.
Only the full proposed architecture (GamaKAN + APR + OT + KG) 
achieves the best overall balance, outperforming all other configurations 
in both ADE and FDE while maintaining a high FPS. These results 
confirm the necessity and effectiveness of our collaborative module 
design for robust and efficient multi-object trajectory prediction.

4. Discussion
Results 
demonstrate 
that 
the 
proposed 
unified 
detec­
tion–interaction–prediction framework effectively addresses the chal­
lenges 
of 
high-density, 
small-object 
scenarios 
in 
greenhouse 
environments. By leveraging semantically-aware pooling, the method 
recovers from severe occlusions by aggregating contextual cues from 
neighboring trajectories. The GamaKAN encoder captures complex, 
nonlinear motion patterns without compromising inference speed. The 
multi-layer Mamba decoder then yields smooth, long-range trajectory 
forecasts, enabling stable object counting even under rapid motion 
variability. Quantitatively, the proposed method achieves a detection 
mAP@50 of 0.877 on the in-house blueberry dataset, and improves 
Precision by 8.3 % and mAP@50 by 2.9 % on the PEST24 small-object 
benchmark. Furthermore, in real-world scenarios, our method ach­
ieves a counting accuracy of 92.5 %, with an edge device deployment 
efficiency of 35 fps. These results validate both the accuracy and real- 
time efficiency of our method.
Despite the encouraging results, this study still presents certain 
limitations and outlines several promising directions for future research. 
Most publicly available agricultural image datasets focus on specific 
scenarios or a single object scale, and there remains a lack of high- 
quality datasets that simultaneously cover both large and small blue­
berry instances. To better evaluate model robustness under extreme 
occlusion and small-object conditions, we adopted the PEST24 small- 
object detection benchmark as a public validation dataset to assess 
generalization on challenging samples. While the proposed framework 
demonstrates strong adaptability across various fruit types and agri­
cultural environments, it has not yet been validated in certain deploy­
ment scenarios, such as UAV-based fruit counting.
In addition, the current system primarily relies on RGB imagery for 
blueberry ripeness estimation, which makes it vulnerable to interference 
from strong illumination, specular reflection, and occlusion. This limi­
tation particularly affects the ability to distinguish between unripe and 
discolored ripe fruits, thereby reducing the reliability of maturity 
assessment. To enhance robustness under such complex lighting condi­
tions, we plan to incorporate multimodal sensing, such as near-infrared 
(NIR) imaging, to enable more stable fruit state recognition. Moreover, 
in high-density scenes with frequent occlusions and complex motion 
trajectories, long-term identity preservation accuracy tends to decline. 
To address this issue, we propose to fuse static visual cues of fruits (e.g., 
shape and texture) with dynamic trajectory information, in order to 
improve trajectory continuity and identity consistency, especially under 
extended occlusion or intermittent detection loss.
5. Conclusions
This paper presents an effective technical framework for accurate 
blueberries counting in greenhouse scenarios, featuring in deep learning 
algorithms of lightweight object detection for small size blueberries and 
mamba-based trajectory completion for target position prediction in 
continuous multi-frame scenes. detection, trajectory prediction, and 
tracking-based counting. For object detection, a hybrid loss function and 
the MCSPF module are introduced to address the challenges posed by 
the small size and low detectability of greenhouse blueberries. For tra­
jectory prediction, a Mamba-driven position prediction model is 
employed to accurately model complex nonlinear movement patterns. 
And a two-stage tracking and track-completion strategy is implemented 
to effectively reduce repeated counting for accurate counting task. 
Extensive experiments on real greenhouse datasets and public bench­
marks demonstrate that the proposed method achieves real-time per­
formance and surpasses existing methods in accuracy. These results 
confirm the framework’s applicability for practical deployment in 
agricultural environments and its strong cross-scenario generalization. 
Future work will explore cross-modal fusion and multi-task perception 
to further improve robustness in diverse practical applications.
