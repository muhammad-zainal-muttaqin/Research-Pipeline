Abstract
Grading of oil palm fresh fruit bunches (FFB) plays a vital role in the postharvest operation as it directly inﬂuences the
extraction rate of oil palm, thereby ensuring quality control in the estate and mill. Currently, manual grading based on
visual assessment is employed, but it has limitations mainly due to subjective judgment-inﬂuenced factors such as visual
resemblance, light intensities, and differences in colors across ripeness categories. Hence, an automated oil palm fruit
grading system in postharvest technology is proposed to enhance the grading process and improve productivity while
maintaining operational cost efﬁciency. This work involves developing an improved object detection model based on the
You Only Look Once model to accurately identify four grades of oil palm FFB, namely, ripe, unripe, underripe, and
overripe. The proposed model incorporated several improvements, including a hybrid feature extractor comprising mobile
inverted bottleneck module and densely connected neural network. Additionally, it employs a spatial pyramid pooling
structure to expand the receptive ﬁeld and utilizes the complete intersection over union function for bounding box
regression. The results indicate that the proposed model obtains a remarkable mAP of 94.37% and an F1-score of 0.89.
Besides, the model performs real-time detection at a faster rate of 4.8 FPS on a limited-capacity embedded device,
NVIDIA Jetson Nano. The comprehensive experimental results conﬁrm the superiority of the proposed model over various
detection models.

1 Introduction
Generally, oil palm can be considered a high-value plan-
tation crop. Each fruit can produce approximately 50% of
oil, making it a highly efﬁcient oil producer [1]. Palm oil
has proven to be useful in numerous applications, including
edible and non-edible products [2, 3]. In addition, biofuels
are becoming increasingly popular as a way to minimize
dependency
on
fossil
fuels.
This
development
has
increased the demand for palm oil as a feedstock element
in biodiesel manufacturing [3].
Oil palm fresh fruit bunch (FFB) is composed of four
ripeness levels, including ripe, underripe, unripe, and
overripe. The relationship between the FFB ripeness level
and its oil content can be described as follows; ripe fruit
contains optimal oil content, underripe fruit contains less
than optimal oil content, unripe fruit contains low oil
content, and the quality of oil content degrades for overripe

detached fruits are the two parameters that can be used to
assess the maturity stage of the fruit. The FFB is classiﬁed
as ripe and overripe if the mesocarp color is reddish–or-
ange and orange, while the mesocarp color for unripe or
underripe is yellowish or reddish–purple. In addition, the
quantity of detached fruits is determined using two distinct
criteria: the number of detached fruit sockets on the bunch
and the number of loose fruits on the ground for uncut FFB
[6]. Evaluating the ripeness level of FFB will directly
impact the oil palm extraction rate (OER) [7, 8]. Therefore,
the FFBs must be harvested at the optimal ripening stage to
obtain high quality and optimum oil yield.
Presently, the grading of harvested FFB is carried out
manually at the mill by expert graders. The expert graders
will assess the FFB quality based on their vision and
subjective judgment. The manual inspection can be biased
due to different human perceptions, limited skills, and
experiences. Therefore, implementing the computer vision
approach is essential to avoid biases in assessing the
maturity level of FFB. The computer vision technique
highly relies on the color and texture features of FFBs,
which may have certain limitations, especially if the input
image is captured at different light intensities. The visual
resemblance poses a great challenge for conventional
computer vision techniques, particularly given the slight
change in hue between the ripeness of the FFB. In addition,
the color of the FFB varies from different angles. These
features have increased the level of complexity in grading
FFB compared to the grading of other fruits.
In order to resolve the abovementioned limitations, an
accurate and lightweight object detection method for FFB
grading is proposed. The proposed model focused on two
primary criteria: improving the detection performance of
FFB grading over the current state-of-the-art model and
developing a lightweight detection model that is feasible
for a limited-capacity embedded device. The contributions
of this paper are as follows:
•
A novel object detection algorithm is developed using
the YOLOv3 tiny model as its baseline. The model
employed various enhancements to strengthen the
feature extraction of the FFB at different ripeness.
First, a hybrid feature extractor that integrates a mobile
inverted bottleneck (MBConv) module and densely
connected neural network (DenseNet). Then, the mul-
tiscale local features are concatenated in the same
convolutional layer using an improved spatial pyramid
pooling (SPP) module. This structure helps in increas-
ing the network’s receptive ﬁeld. Next, a localization
loss function called a complete intersection over union
(CIoU) is adopted.
•
The experimental results demonstrate a remarkable
improvement in detection accuracy, time, and model
size, which has great potential for inﬁeld FFB grading
implementation.
•
This work is focused on the oil palm dataset. However,
the developed object detection algorithm can be applied
to any type of fruit dataset or different object datasets.
As for the hypothesis of this work, the utilization of
hybrid
feature
extractor
architectures
integrating
the
MBConv module and DenseNet is expected to aid the
feature extraction process by effectively captures both
global and local features. This hybrid architecture will
enhance the ability of the developed model to learn intri-
cate patterns within the input data. Additionally, it is
expected to have a less complex network with a lower
number of parameters. Furthermore, with the implemen-
tation of an improved SPP module for concatenating
multiscale local features in the same convolutional layer, it
is hypothesized that this architectural modiﬁcation will
contribute to an increased receptive ﬁeld, which helps to
improve the model’s understanding of spatial relationships
in the data. Additionally, the adoption of the CIoU loss
function is expected to improve the accuracy of bounding
box predictions by considering both bounding box overlap
and geometric information. In summary, the proposed
hybrid feature extractor, combined with an improved SPP
module and CIoU loss function, will collectively contribute
to improving detection performance and developing a less
complex model for oil palm fruit grading.

work and provides suggestions for future work.
2 Related works
Computer vision usage has grown in recent years to meet
the growing demand for fast and accurate methods in
various artiﬁcial intelligence applications. It is performed
to capture images, process and analyze images to replicate
the effect of human vision for computers or devices to
understand images in various applications such as damage
detection [9, 10], face detection [11, 12], remote sensing
[13, 14], medical application [15], vehicle detection [16],
and others. Moreover, computer vision has been widely
applied in precision agriculture applications, including fruit
classiﬁcation [17], fruit detection and counting [18–20],
estimation of fruit load [21], crop disease detection
[22, 23], grading [24–26], etc.

To date, many researchers have developed non-de-
structive grading systems for evaluating oil palm FFB
using image processing approaches. This can be achieved
by adopting two critical stages: feature extraction and
classiﬁcation. Color and texture feature extractors were the
most often employed feature extraction methods in the
previous study. Numerous methods have been developed to
classify the ripeness of FFB. In recent research, color
extractors methods have been used to investigate the fea-
sibility of utilizing multiple color spaces to determine the
ripeness of FFB, such as the hue, saturation, and intensity
(HSI) [27], L*a*b* [28], and color moment [29]. This color
analysis can be employed during the pre-processing step as
well as to determine the classiﬁcation features. Further-
more, various classiﬁcation approaches have been adopted
to measure the ripeness level of FFB, such as support
vector machine (SVM) [29], K-means clustering [30],
linear discriminant analysis (LDA) [28], artiﬁcial neural
networks (ANN) [25, 27, 31], and convolutional neural
network (CNN) method [32]. Aside from that, some
researchers proposed using a camera or multispectral
device to acquire the FFB images in speciﬁc wavelengths
[33–35]. Multispectral image analysis mainly examines the
image intensity in several speciﬁc wavelengths, such as
infrared (IR), visible light, and ultraviolet (UV). However,
these works adopted machine learning techniques which
can be tedious and time-consuming. In addition, limited
studies have been reported on the application of the deep
learning approach for grading FFB on limited-capacity
embedded devices. Therefore, some of the current works
for fruit grading using deep convolutional neural networks
(DCNNs) are reviewed.
DCNNs have been frequently utilized for challenging
computer vision problems due to their robust automatic
feature representation ability instead of hand-crafted fea-
ture-based approaches. Numerous CNN-based architec-
tures such as AlexNet [36], VGGNet [37], ResNet [39],
DenseNet [39], and others have been applied for grading
different types of fruits. ResNets are renowned for
addressing the vanishing gradient problem through identity
mapping, whereas the VGG network employs a consider-
ably smaller receptive ﬁeld instead of a massive one. In
recent studies, the ResNet50 and VGG16 network was used
to grade and classify the maturity of various fruits [40–42].
Despite proving powerful in numerous classiﬁcation tasks,
ResNet and VGG16 are huge networks and require
extensive computation resources. Consequently, this ren-
ders them practically infeasible in real-world applications.
A stacking ensemble deep learning approach was proposed
using EfﬁcientNet for fruit grading based on their freshness
and outer appearance [43]. EfﬁcientNet has limitations in
terms of training requirements, as it still demands sub-
stantial
computational
resources.
Although
these
requirements are lower compared to previous state-of-the-
art models, they remain signiﬁcant, potentially restricting
accessibility for certain applications.
Meanwhile, a novel multi-input deep learning model
with two modalities (HSI and RGB) was employed for
feature extraction in order to classify bananas [44]. CNN
approach was used to process the hyperspectral and RGB
images, followed by multilayer perceptron (MLP) pro-
cessing of extracted features. Additionally, the CNN uti-
lizes a pre-trained VGG16 network from ImageNet. In a
recent study, a CNN algorithm was enhanced by adopting a
stochastic pooling mechanism based on sparsity for grading
sour lemon fruit into healthy and defective groups [45].
This pooling structure balances max-pooling and average
pooling strengths and weaknesses while reducing the net-
work parameters. Another study improved the convolu-
tional neural network by adopting a hybrid pooling method
to classify irregular- and regular-shaped cherries [46]. The
proposed method combined the max-pooling and average
pooling techniques to enhance the generalization of the
CNN. It can be observed that the classiﬁcation models
proposed above are based on CNN architecture and are
only suitable for small class problems and less complex
features.
Despite the classiﬁcation methods mentioned above,
object detection methods have gained much attention in
classifying fruit ripeness. Object detection is a computer
vision technique that works to identify and locate objects
within an image. It combines the tasks of image classiﬁ-
cation and object localization and can be categorized into
the region-based method (two-stage) and regression-based
method (one-stage). A two-stage method, Mask R-CNN,
was implemented for the classiﬁcation of bananas into
normal and rejected categories [47]. In another study, the
Mask R-CNN model weight was used to train the combi-
nation of YOLO and Swim transformer module which
represents the attention mechanism module [48]. Region-
based methods have limitations as they require higher
computational resources, and the detection speed is slower
compared to the proposed model. Hence, they are not
suitable for real-time applications. Another research used a
one-stage method, namely, the improved YOLO-based
model for real-time grading of apples [24, 49]. The pro-
posed YOLO models adopted the DenseNet and BiSeNet
network which has proven to increase the detection accu-
racy. The YOLOv4 model also was used for grading oil
palm fresh fruit bunch [50, 51]. Both works obtained a
higher detection accuracy which demonstrated the advan-
tages of employing a YOLO-based model. YOLO network
is known for its simultaneous prediction of bounding boxes
and class probabilities, which simpliﬁes the object detec-
tion pipeline and eliminates the need for separate region
proposal networks as in the two-stage detection model. The

3 Materials and methods
3.1 Dataset
A 2 MP Hikvision outdoor WDR ﬁxed mini dome network
camera is used to capture the FFB images. The images
were taken at the Sime Darby Plantation oil palm estate in
Selangor, Malaysia, during the harvest season. Collection
periods were done from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. in cloudy and
sunny weather conditions. Four classes of bunch ripeness
were included: ripe, underripe, overripe, and unripe. The
FFB grades were labeled manually based on the knowledge
provided by the expert graders by using a LabelImg tool.
The dataset of 1281 images consists of different image
resolutions between 272 9 194 and 11,339 9 3780 pixels.
In this work, 70% of the dataset is used for training,
whereas 30% is used for validation.
3.2 The proposed model architecture
The
proposed
model
is
developed
through
several
improvements made to the conventional YOLOv3 tiny
model. Several modiﬁcations were made to the backbone,
neck, and prediction section to improve the model’s
accuracy and efﬁciency for embedded device applications.
First, the backbone network adopted a hybrid feature
extractor that integrates an MBConv and DenseNet module
replacing the original backbone of the YOLOv3 tiny
model. Then, the neck network used four detection scales
utilizing the feature pyramid network (FPN) method to
concatenate the multiscale local features from the feature
extractor. An SPP module is employed to produce an
output of ﬁxed length, independent of the input size.
Finally, the CIoU localization loss function is employed in
the prediction network.

transfer from the input to the output layer becomes more
comprehensive, causing the loss of feature information.

each layer, facilitating robust and maximal gradient ﬂow
[39]. In addition, DenseNet works by reusing features to
create more diversiﬁed features and richer patterns. As a
result, the recovered features may be employed efﬁciently,
particularly for the subsequent convolution layers. Feature
maps from all preceding layers are obtained by every layer,

MBConv works well in devices with limited resources
as it efﬁciently handles interactions between features in
different channels and utilizes parameters effectively
through its depthwise separable convolutions and inverted
bottleneck structure. The previous study has adopted the
MBConv module and demonstrated its effectiveness in
reducing
the
model
weight
[14].
Hence,
this
work
employed the optimized mobile inverted bottleneck mod-
ule (MBConv) module presented in EfﬁcientNet [52]. The
MBConv is an efﬁcient type of residual block with an
inverted structure [53]. It performs three separate convo-
lutions using a narrow–wide–narrow approach. The opti-
mized MBConv incorporated both the Swish activation
function and a squeeze and excitation network (SENet) into
its architecture. Moreover, the residual connection is
removed to simplify the network. The initial layer uses a
1 9 1 convolutional layer to expand the number of chan-
nels in the low-dimensional input. The next layer performs
a 3 9 3 depthwise convolution, reducing the number of
parameters. Both layers adopted a Swish function and
batch normalization (BN). Finally, the compression layer
utilizes a 1 9 1 BN-linear function convolutional layer to
decrease the number of channels.
The MBConv module uses an attention mechanism to
conduct the compression and excitation operation. Firstly,
a depthwise convolution produced a compressed feature
map. The global feature is extracted using the global
average pooling. Then, a squeeze operation is employed for
each feature map channel to get a single numeric value.
Later, the per-channel weights are derived by performing
an excitation operation on the global feature. The sigmoid
function gives each channel a smooth gating function [54],
where smoothness refers to how quickly or slowly the
function transitions from its minimum value to its maxi-
mum value. The smoothness of the sigmoid function is
primarily ensured by its mathematical continuity and dif-
ferentiability, as deﬁned by the standard sigmoid function
in Eq. (3) and its derivative in Eq. (4). The derivative is
continuous and lacks abrupt changes contributing to the
overall smoothness of the sigmoid function, as illustrated in
Fig. 2. Lastly, the feature map is rescaled to obtain the
output map. Figure 3 depicts the structure of the MBConv
module.

3.2.2 The neck network
Four prediction scales are introduced using the FPN to
obtain the location information for targets of various sizes.
The FPN integrates the feature maps generated by multiple
convolutional layers in the feature extractor, each corre-
sponding to distinct feature scales [55]. An upsample
operation followed by 1 9 1 and 3 9 3 and convolutional
layers are adopted to efﬁciently fuse these feature maps
from different layers as shown in Fig. 4. The combination
of 1 9 1 and 3 9 3 convolutions in the neck section
facilitates feature fusion and information aggregation. The
1 9 1 convolution is performed for dimensionality reduc-
tion by reducing the number of channels in the feature
maps while maintaining the spatial dimensions. The sub-
sequent 3 9 3 convolution has a larger receptive ﬁeld
which enhances the model’s ability to capture complex
patterns and allows the network to combine low-level and
high-level features effectively. As a result, four prediction
scales are obtained with varying map sizes: 104 9 104,
52 9 52, 26 9 26, and 13 9 13, corresponding to the
smallest, small, medium, and bigger scales, respectively.
The multiscale prediction focused on concatenating the
global features from various convolutional layers while
ignoring the inclusion of features from a multiscale local
region in the same convolutional layer. Moreover, the FPN
structure improves the detection performance on small
objects; however, it performs poorly on medium- and
large-size objects. Therefore, the SPP module is adopted to
concatenate and pool the features from the multiscale local
region [56]. Consequently, the SPP increases the receptive
ﬁeld while highlighting the most signiﬁcant features. The
conventional SPP module mapped the feature vectors of
variable size to a ﬁxed-size output. The proposed model
used an improved SPP module with multiscale max-pool-
ing layers to extract the features from the multiscale local
region as illustrated in Fig. 5.
The SPP module linked the backbone to the YOLO
detection layers. Before applying the SPP layer, 3 9 3 and
1 9 1 CBL layers with 512 and 256 ﬁlters are performed,
resulting in a 13 9 13 9 256 input feature map. The
combination of 3 9 3 and 1 9 1 convolutions enables the
network to capture multiscale features, control computa-
tional complexity, and build effective hierarchies of fea-
tures. The SPP structure comprises three scales of max-
pooling layer; each has 13 9 13, 9 9 9, and 5 9 5 kernel
sizes with a stride of 1. The local region feature maps are
concatenated with input feature maps to obtain the ﬁnal
13 9 13 9 1024 feature maps. The SPP structure is
employed for the 13 9 13 YOLO prediction.
3.2.3 The prediction network
The 1 9 1 convolutional layer produces the ultimate pre-
diction feature map, which is subsequently utilized by the
detection layers to identify and detect the targets. The
1 9 1 convolution reduces the number of channels in the
feature maps before they are fed into the YOLO head,
serving as a form of dimensionality reduction. Despite its
small receptive ﬁeld, the 1 9 1 convolution introduces
nonlinearity through activation functions. The combination
of dimensionality reduction and nonlinearity achieved
through the 1 9 1 convolution is effective in preparing the
feature maps before they reach the YOLO head. The ﬁnal
prediction is presented in the form of a vector that includes
the class label, conﬁdence score, and coordinates of the
predicted box. The CIoU function is used to calculate the
localization loss for the regression box. It considers the
distance between center points, the overlap area, and the
aspect ratio for bounding box regression. These three
geometric factors are adopted into CIoU loss for faster
convergence and better prediction results. In addition, the
function improved the average precision and recall without
sacriﬁcing inference efﬁciency. Equation (5) expressed the

3.3 Evaluation metrics
The performance of the developed object detection model
is evaluated based on the detection and computational
performance. These metrics are generally computed to
evaluate the performance of object detection problems
[57]. The detection performance is evaluated using preci-
sion, recall, F1-score, and mAP. The correctly detected
objects are indicated by the true positive (TP), while the
false negative (FN) and false positive (FP) refer to the
missed and incorrectly detected objects. The precision and
recall curve provides a thorough perspective on the per-
formance of an object detection model. Equations (6–8) are
used to determine the precision, recall, and F1-score. The
mean IoU value calculated over all images is described as
the average IoU.
The choice of IoU threshold is crucial in balancing
recall and precision in object detection. Generally, in object
detection, the standard Intersection over Union (IoU)
threshold between the ground truth and predicted bounding
box is set at 0.5 [58]. For a predicted bounding box to be
considered a true-positive detection, it must have an IoU of
at least 0.5 with a ground truth bounding box. Anything
less than 0.5 is classiﬁed as a false positive. Setting a low
IoU threshold means that the model is less strict in
requiring the predicted bounding box to closely align with
the ground truth bounding box for a true-positive detection.
This leniency may lead to an increase in false positives, as
the model might detect objects even with minimal overlap
or poor alignment, resulting in low overall detection
accuracy.
Therefore, in this work, the IoU value is set at 0.5 to
calculate the evaluation metrics. The mAP is also evaluated
at an IoU threshold of 0.75, representing a relatively strict
criterion for considering a true-positive detection. Addi-
tionally, the mAP at an average IoU value from 0.5 to 0.95
with an interval of 0.5 is used to measure the model’s
performance across a range of IoU thresholds. This will
provide insights into how well the model generalizes and
performs across different levels of overlap between pre-
dicted and ground truth bounding boxes.

Overall, the proposed model demonstrates superior
performance in terms of accuracy and efﬁciency compared
to the state-of-the-art model. The proposed model achieved
high accuracy while maintaining a smaller model size,
making it suitable for real-time applications. The addition
of four detection scales has contributed to the increase in
accuracy, enabling the detection of targets of different
sizes. Additionally, the SPP module and CIoU loss function
contribute to the overall improvement in detection accu-
racy. Moreover, the model requires fewer computational
resources by incorporating the MBConv module in the
backbone, making it suitable for deployment on low-cost
embedded devices such as NVIDIA Jetson Nano. The
detection performance of the proposed model is also better
than the previous works that performed oil palm grading
using machine and deep learning approaches. This work
utilized more training and validation images, highlighting
the superiority of deep learning over the machine learning
method.
This proposed model is particularly suitable for small
multi-class problems, which, in this case, is the classiﬁ-
cation of FFB ripeness into four classes (ripe, unripe,
underripe, and overripe). However, the developed object
detection algorithm can also be implemented on various
fruit datasets or datasets containing different objects.
Additionally, the robustness of the developed model was
demonstrated on a public dataset (VisDrone dataset) that
consists of a higher number of classes. Results show that
the developed model outperforms two YOLO-tiny models
and several previous works.
The proposed model has a limitation in its architecture
consisting of 291 layers, which include numerous max-
pooling and convolutional layers. This design leads to a
slight increment in the number of parameters and BFLOPs
value compared to the YOLOv3 tiny and YOLOv4 tiny
models, consequently impacting the detection time and
FPS. Although the proposed model achieved high accuracy
in grading the ripeness of the palm oil, the architecture of
the proposed model can be further improved in the future to
enhance the detection time for real-time detection.
4.2.6 Limitations
The obtained results reveal a trade-off between accuracy
and efﬁciency performance. Despite achieving higher
detection accuracy, the model exhibited lower efﬁciency in
terms of model complexity, corresponding to the detection
speed. The proposed model resulted in a larger model size,
a greater number of trainable parameters, and a higher
BFLOPs value compared to the YOLOv4 tiny and
YOLOv3 tiny models. This is primarily attributed to the
use of the DenseNet architecture. The DenseNet employs
feature reuse leading to parameter efﬁciency. However, the
DenseNet architecture used in the backbone network of the
proposed model has a complex network with excessive
parameters, resulting in a signiﬁcant demand for compu-
tational and storage resources. This is illustrated in
Table 7, where almost 87% of the FLOPs value is con-
tributed by the backbone network. The inclusion of 78
dense layers, each with a ﬁlter size of 128 and 32, sub-
stantially inﬂuences the complexity of the structure.
Additionally, the detection speed achieved by the proposed
model is notably slow on both GeForce GTX 1650 and
NVIDIA Jetson Nano. This slowdown is due to the
detection process having to pass the complex structure
consisting of 291 layers.
The developed model demonstrated excellent detection
accuracy when tested on the palm dataset, which comprises
only four classes. Furthermore, the proposed model was
evaluated on the public dataset of VisDrone, consisting of
ten object classes. However, testing on a dataset with a
higher number of classes is necessary to assess the
robustness of the model. This would provide a more thor-
ough understanding of the model’s generalization capa-
bilities across a broader range of object categories and
ensure its suitability for diverse and complex scenarios.

5 Conclusion
This paper proposed an improved model for the automatic
grading of fresh fruit bunch at palm oil plantations. Three
signiﬁcant improvements have been made to the structure
of the YOLOv3 tiny model. Firstly, the backbone structure
incorporated a hybrid network of DenseNet161 and mobile
inverted bottleneck modules, removing the original back-
bone in the YOLOv3 tiny model. Next, the backbone and
the YOLO prediction layer were connected using an
enhanced SPP block. Lastly, the network utilized the
complete intersection over union function as the localiza-
tion loss function. The developed model was validated and
compared to YOLO variant models using the FFB dataset,
containing four ripeness categories. The proposed model
performed well in detection performance based on the
experimental results, with an mAP of 94.37% and an F1-
score of 0.89. The proposed model demonstrated superior
performance compared to the YOLOv3 and YOLOv4
models in detection time when tested on the GeForce GTX
1650 and Jetson Nano. In addition, the proposed model
achieves a substantial enhancement in computational per-
formance as it developed a lightweight size of 65.6 MB
with BFLOPs of 28.353. These beneﬁts may result in a
large cost reduction in hardware implementation. In sum-
mary, the results indicate that the proposed model is fea-
sible for the automatic classiﬁcation of FFB ripeness in
palm oil plantations.
Despite its excellent detection accuracy, the proposed
model is still considered to have a larger number of
trainable parameters, weight size, and BFLOPs value
compared to other lightweight models. Additionally, the
detection speed achieved by the proposed model is also
considerably slow. In the future, the architecture of the
proposed model can be further optimized to reduce the
complexity and enhance the detection speed while main-
taining or even improving detection performance. This can
be achieved through various improvements. Firstly, the
kernel and ﬁlter sizes in certain convolutional layers can be
reduced. The size of the kernel and ﬁlter has a direct impact
on the model’s complexity. Reducing these sizes is
expected to have a minimal effect on detection perfor-
mance, especially for object detection on datasets with a
small number of classes. Additionally, pruning some of the
convolutional layers can further simplify the model’s
structure. Finally, consider exploring the use of alternative
activation functions, such as the Mish function, which may
help reduce loss during the training process.
