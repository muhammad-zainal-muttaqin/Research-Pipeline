Abstract—Image 
background 
subtraction 
is 
an
important and essential process in many computer vision 
applications as allows for a more effective processing of the 
foreground objects. Various methods have been proposed 
for performing background subtraction in the literature. 
In this study, we investigated various background 
subtraction to automatically identify the correct class of the 
foreground objects.  There are only a few major producers 
of palm oil and Malaysia is the world’s second-largest 
producer and exporter of palm oil in terms of volume.  In 
2019, the gross domestic product (GDP) contribution from 
palm oil in Malaysia was estimated to be around 37.6 
billion ringgit to Malaysia’s economy or at 2.7 percent of 
the country’s GDP.  Among the many major industries, it is 
one of Malaysia’s primary industries, and a main 
agricultural export.  There are various studies to 
automatically identify fruit ripeness, ranging from mangos 
to strawberries, etc. In addition, there have been some 
work in recent years to identify the maturity of the palm oil 
fruit bunches, and the use of Raman spectroscopy on 
individual fruitlets, etc.  This study investigates the effect of 
background subtraction on the performance of a deep neural 
network to accurately identify the ripeness of palm oil fruitlets 
i.e. ripe, unripe and over ripe.  This was compared with a 
feature based probabilistic approach.

I. 
INTRODUCTION 
Palm oil extracted from the fruits of the oil palm is used in 
a variety of consumer products, from lipstick and toothpaste 
to cooking oil and many other household items[2]. 
Malaysia is the second-largest palm oil producer in the 
world and hence it is not surprising that palm oil production 
is the main export income generator for the country[3].  It 
contributed USD 3.4 billion in export earnings to the 
nation’s gross domestic product (GDP) in 1997.  Planting 
of the palm started in the 1960’s after the successful 
exploration of oil palm as a major commercial crop by the 
Federal Land Development of Authority (FELDA) [9]. 
Nevertheless, quality evaluation and classification of oil 
palm fruits in Malaysia’s oil palm industry are still 
manually inspected by humans.  Fruit colour is an 
important trait in terms of fruit harvesting and, therefore, oil 
yield. Although it is commonly known that the ripeness of 
the oil palm fruit is tied to the colour, nevertheless this is 
purely based on the judgement call of the humans, trained 
as they may be.  Harvest the fruits too early and oil yields 
are significantly decreased. Harvest the fruits when they are 
over ripe and you end up with lower quality oil.  At the 
moment, fruit ripeness identification is either through 
looking at the fruit colour or observing the number of fallen 
ripe fruits under the tree - all done manually. Various 
colour gradients of oil palm fruitlets will also indicate the 
ripeness level of the fruit to proceed with the classification 
process.  According to Palm Oil Research Institute of 
Malaysia, the unripe oil palm fruitlet would have a black to 
reddish colour while the ripe one will develop reddish 
orange colour[4]. 
Hence it is important to enhance the traditional approach to 
identify the oil palm fruitlets ripeness level in order to 
maximise the quantity and quality of extracted palm oil. 
Moreover, with the world's population expanding every 
year, there is an immense need to be able to utilise 
whatever available agricultural land efficiently to meet 
increasing demands for food worldwide. Hence an 
automated system can help to further improve the oil palm 
fruitlets ripeness grading, since there will now be quality 
palm oil extracted from the fruit at the optimum ripeness 
level.

The banana is known to be the most consumed main food 
source after rice due to its high nutrition content. There are 
over 150 countries which produce millions of tons of 
bananas per year but the banana exports amount is not 
proportional with the production due to the lack of 
appropriate technology which can efficiently ensure that the 
quality of bananas meet the international fruit exports 
standard [10]. Traditionally, the ripeness level of banana 
fruit is usually inspected individually by the colour of the 
peel and diameter of the fruit by trained human inspectors, 
Even though this can consume a significantly huge amount 
of time, unfortunately it does not guarantee or ensures good 
classification accuracy or consistency. There are three 
stages for banana ripeness level which are un-mature, 
mature and over mature, Nevertheless the maturity level 
changes proportionally with the banana peel colour from 
green to brownish yellow [11]. Time constraint is the main 
factor in the banana classification process since any delay 
post-harvest may affect the flavour of the bananas or even 
causing splitting of the fruit itself. In this case, it is 
important to produce an efficient automated banana 
maturity level system to solve the problem faced in manual 
classification. Prabha and Kumar had proposed a banana 
fruit maturity classification system whereby the maturity 
level of extracted banana features acquired by vision 
system will be classified using the Mean Colour Intensity 
and Area algorithm [12]. For each maturity group, 40 
fingers of banana data were captured with a calibrated 
camera, followed by image background removal and then 
RGB-binary image conversion. The size of banana fruit will 
then be measured by regional descriptors before these were 
analysed by Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) with Duncan’s 
Multiple Range Test (DMRT) repeatedly for 20 times. 
Lastly these will be processed for the colour mean intensity 
and classified based on the area of the fruits. The results 
showed a classification accuracy of 95% to 100% for colour 
mean intensity while only 15% to 100% for area density. In 
conclusion, the colour mean intensity algorithm can be 
combined with area algorithm to achieve a better banana 
detection accuracy. 
Mangos harvested during the summer days are well known 
for their flavourful taste and nutritious content. The number 
of days before the mango becomes rotten should be 
calculated precisely to reduce the occurrence of rotten 
mangos during the fruit transportation process which can 
cause huge economic losses to the farmers. The traditional 
process to estimate the days to rot of the mango can be very 
tedious and time consuming so an automated machine 
vision system was proposed to enhance the sorting process 
based on the mango’s maturity level [13]. In this work, a 
total of 4 different species of mango fruits with a total of 
600 samples were collected in two phases on different days. 
Firstly, the feature of the mangos such as size, colour, 
shapes and surface defects were extracted by machine 
vision after the background removal process. Then, the 
extracted colour features of the mangos were processed 
with Support Vector Regression (SVR) so that the actual 
days to rot can be estimated while the other extracted 
features will be passed to a Multi Attributes Decision 
Making (MADM) algorithm to evaluate the fruit quality. 
Lastly, the data outputs from SVR and MADM were 
combined and processed by a Fuzzy Incremental Learning 
algorithm to grade the fruits. Based on the classification 
results, the average performance accuracy of the automated 
classification system is around 87% which was deemed 
satisfactory and hence will be able to improve the banana 
grading system.  Abu Bakar et al also investigated the 
relationship between the colour of Harumanis mango to 
one of four ripeness level by image analysis through a 
series of image processing techniques and the L*a*b 
colour space[4]. In this study, 247 mangos were tested 
and the results were compared with the measured sugar 
content of the mango juice, and they were able to 
achieve an accuracy of 92% with their approach. 
Other than bananas and mangos another fruit that has been 
auto-graded is strawberries.  A novel maturity identiﬁcation 
system for strawberries based on three levels, namely 
mature, nearly-mature and immature using smartphones 
was proposed [5]. The smart phone was equipped an optical 
filters of 535 nm and another of 670 nm to capture the 
characteristic wavelengths of 535 nm and 670 nm.  The 
samples were first inspected and manually graded by the 
farmers to separate them into the three levels of mature, 
nearly-mature and immature as well as the ground truths. 
As each maturity level consists of 60 samples, hence the 
total number of strawberries used to train the maturity 
models is 180.  Three maturity recognition models i.e., 
multiple nonlinear regression discriminant (MNR), multiple 
linear regression discriminant (MLR) and SoftMax 
regression classiﬁers were investigated.  Once trained, the 
smart phone system was tested in the field with a new set of 
90 strawberries collected from the farm. Based on the 
results the MNR was chosen to be the recognition model 
used in the smart phone. They reported an accuracy of 
94.44%. 
Palm oil which is widely used in various area is produced 
from oil palm fruitlets, which can be found from fresh fruit 
bunches (FFB) of oil palm trees. Due to the oil palm’s 
hydrolysis process characteristic which converts fat into 
fatty acids, only certain degree of oil palm fruit ripeness can 
produce the optimum amount of palm oil to be extracted. 
So, a good practice in the industry is to monitor the quality 
of oil palm fruitlets and ensure only fruitlets with optimum 
ripeness will be selected and processed. According to the 
Palm Oil Research Institute of Malaysia (PORIM), the 
ripeness level of oil palm fruits can be categorized visually. 
For example, the unripe fruitlets would have over 90% 
coverage of purplish black colour and colourless at the

There have been some work in recent years to identify the 
maturity of the palm oil fruit bunches[6,7].  The latest study 
on individual fruitlets by Tan et al [8] used Raman 
spectroscopy on the individual fruitlets where a total of 50 
samples were collected with the combination of 13 under 
ripe, 20 ripe and 17 over ripe fruitlets. Half of these were 
used to train the system with the remaining for testing. The 
extracted Raman features serve as a predictor for training 
the ANN model and they reported 100% recognition 
accuracies for the 1 ripe, 1 unripe and 2 overripe fruitlets. 
Abdullah et al. developed an inspection system integrated 
with machine vision to investigate the grading consistency 
and accuracy of machine vision to compare those obtained 
manually from human inspectors [9]. Furthermore they also 
wanted to determine whether automated fruits inspection 
system can ever replace human inspectors.  A total of 500 
sets of oil palm fruit samples were sourced from a local oil 
palm plantation.  From this, 100 samples were allocated as 
the training set while the remaining were used to test the 
system and the results compared with those obtained from 
the human inspectors. The fruit samples were analyzed 
based on 11 principle hues of Wilk’s analysis and 
Discriminant analysis. Their results showed that the 
machine achieved an average classification accuracy of 
around 91.5%. They concluded that machine vision 
classification has the potential to identify as well as humans 
in such a task. 
In order to make local fruits to be more competitive against 
imported fruits, a mobile application named Fruitylicious 
which can determine the ripeness level of various fruits 
without damaging the fruit itself was developed[14]. 
Various fruits like melons, bananas and apples were 
investigated with datasets created from Fuji apples. Photos 
of the apples were taken from different angles before the 
images were passed to an instrument called refractometer 
to determine the sweetness level of the fruit given as a Brix 
value.  When the apple is ripe it will have a Brix level 
above 14 and unripe otherwise. The extracted features of 
apple image are based on the colours of red and yellow as 
well as any imperfections such as dots on the apple skin. 
The output data will then be collected and passed to a K-
Nearest Neighbour classifier to be trained. It is found that 
using only the sweetness level of apple is insufficient to 
accurately determine the ripeness of apple since there are 
some other factors to be considered, for example the acidity 
of the apple and the different preferable apple taste of 
consumers. While Iswari et al were satisfied with the 
results, nevertheless they have also suggested various 
improvements in order for it to be more. 
Septiarini et al investigated an image processing approach 
involving 
segmentation, 
features 
extraction 
and 
classification to classify fruit ripeness [15].  Two methods 
were used for the segmentation process, which involves a 
combination of thresholding with Otsu’s method and the 
HSV (hue, saturation and value) colour space. Next, the 
colour features were extracted together with the shape, and 
texture of the fruits even though colour is the main 
discriminating feature. The red, green, blue (RGB) and 
grayscale values of each image contributes to the mean and 
standard deviation feature data, thereby resulting in a total 
of 8 variables for each image. The output datasets were then 
classified by the Support Vector Machine (SVM) to 
identify the fruit ripeness into the correct classes, namely 
raw, under ripe and ripe. Although the background of the 
image captured was also being computed in the 
classification process, but a satisfactory classification 
accuracy of 92.5% for 160 samples was achieved.  It is 
believed that the classification accuracy can be further 
improved if the fruit image background is eliminated. 
Even most fruits shows changes in their appearance 
according to the ripeness level, there are still some fruits 
which shows no visual changes when ripening. In this case, 
the changes involve the fruit’s internal content affecting the 
fruit’s heat capacity. Heat capacity is the maximum total 
heat energy that can be absorbed by a material in any state 
regardless of surrounding temperature. In this case, the 
measuring unit for specific heat capacity will be calorie per 
degree Celsius [16].  An example of such an application 
that investigated the use of a heat capacity using thermal 
imaging in fruit ripeness detection was conducted by 
Sumriddetchkajorn and Intaravanne.  In their study,, they 
utilized two-dimension mango ripeness detection under 
different environment of different surrounding temperature 
[17]. The process starts by placing the mango in a 
refrigerator with temperature of 5 - 6 degrees Celsuis for 
around 15 minutes.  Then the mango is transferred to 
another warmer area with a surrounding temperature of 25 
degrees Celsius.  The overripe mango will be left longer in 
this warmer area before the thermal camera is used to 
record the thermal image of the mango. The images 
captured under both areas will first be processed by image 
and temperature thresholding processes with a minimum 
temperature threshold of 10 degrees Celsius. Then this is 
continued by blob filtering in order to determine the ripe 
and unripe areas on the mango. The images from each area 
are recorded and then compared.  The degree of ripeness of 
the mango can be identified due to the changes of heat 
capacity in the ripe area of the fruit. Although the heat 
capacity method is accurate in determining the ripeness 
level of mango, but it can be quite troublesome to set up the 
preparation work for the detection process. 
III.
METHODOLOGY
In this section, the methodology of the investigation will be 
described in more detail in the following sections.  The 
samples of palm oil fruitlets were harvested from Genting 
Plantation’s palm oil estate at Tebong, Melaka - a distance 
of approximately 130 km from our processing facility. 
Once the fruitlets were harvested from the palm tree, it was 
quickly rushed to our laboratory for processing within 8 
hours as the window to process this is only 48 hours 
after harvesting.  Anything later than this would severely 
affect the oil content.  42 samples of each class, namely 
unripe, ripe and overripe were obtained.

will develop reddish orange colour and this is the best time 
to harvest and process the fruits. Another popular method

V. 
CONCLUSIONS 
Several 
background 
subtraction 
techniques 
were 
investigated and used together with CNN and the Naïve 
Bayesian classifier to help identify the ripeness of oil palm 
fruitlets. With the Naïve Bayesian Classifier, the best 
classification accuracy was obtained from OTSU’s 
thresholding background modelling approach.  While 
humans have heavily relied on the colour of the palm oil 
fruitlets as a measure of ripeness, the results here have 
shown that it is not reliable on its own when used with the 
Naïve Bayesian Classifier.  However when coupled with 
the mass and volume of the fruitlet, there was an increase of 
nearly 23% in terms of the system’s ability to reliably 
identify the ripeness.  On the other hand, with addition 
effort to perform data augmentation to increase the size of 
the datasets, the best CNN model was able to identify the 
ripeness at 96.93% with the default dataset without any 
background processing followed by the semi-manual 
background subtraction model at 90.79%.
