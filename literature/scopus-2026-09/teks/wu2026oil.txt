Abstract
Accurate classification of oil palm fruit maturity is critical for optimizing harvest sched-
ules and maximizing yield. To address the limited discrimination accuracy caused by
subtle color variations, this paper proposes an LAB dual-branch feature fusion method for
five-class oil palm fruit maturity classification. A classification model named CMF-Net is
constructed, in which the luminance (L) and chrominance (ab) components are decoupled
into two parallel branches to separately extract structural and color information. An input
adaptation module is introduced to perform channel mapping and feature enhancement.
During feature fusion, a gated fusion mechanism is employed for adaptive integration of
the dual-branch features, followed by a BiFPN module for multi-scale feature interaction.
A multi-level attention enhancement module composed of coordinate attention and self-
attention is designed to strengthen key-region perception and global contextual modeling.
An auxiliary color regression constraint and ordinal supervision are incorporated to im-
prove the model’s sensitivity to subtle chromatic changes and the progressive relationship
among maturity stages. The proposed algorithm provides a color-decoupled and order-
aware representation framework for gradual fruit maturity classification, offering a useful
reference for fine-grained agricultural visual classification tasks involving continuous color
transitions. Experimental results on a public dataset show that CMF-Net achieves a valida-
tion accuracy of 94.66%, outperforming mainstream models by 1.3–6.1 percentage points,
while also yielding superior recall and F1-score.
Keywords: oil palm fruit; maturity classification; LAB color space; dual-branch network;
feature fusion; attention mechanism; ordinal learning
1. Introduction
As one of the most productive oil-bearing crops globally, the oil palm fruit features
an oil content that correlates strongly with its ripeness. Across Southeast Asia, Africa, and
Latin America, it stands as a vital economic pillar, supporting local agricultural livelihoods
while ensuring the stability of global edible oil supplies. In palm oil processing, maturity
control mainly takes place during the raw material handling stage before sterilization.

Mixing fruit bunches with different maturity levels can affect oil yield and product quality.
Therefore, it is important to assess fruit maturity before sterilization. By equipping auto-
mated production lines with intelligent vision systems, real-time maturity recognition and
quality monitoring of fruit bunches can be achieved, providing support for process control.
Without changing the existing workflow, this approach can improve oil yield and product
consistency, and support the intelligent development of the palm oil industry [1].
In practical palm oil production, oil palm fruits are commonly categorized into differ-
ent maturity stages according to external visual characteristics, especially exocarp color
and the degree of fruit detachment [2]. Accurate identification of these maturity stages
is important because adjacent stages often exhibit gradual color transitions and similar
surface appearances. Early research efforts largely relied on handcrafted features; for
instance, some studies utilized color features combined with Canny edge detection to
achieve recognition [3], while others employed Principal Component Analysis (PCA) or
Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) for feature dimensionality reduction, subsequently
training Support Vector Machine (SVM) classifiers using Red, Green, and Blue (RGB) or
grayscale images [4]. More recently, image-based maturity assessment has been extended
from single-source visual features to multimodal sensing. Lin et al. [5] integrated RGB and
multispectral images for tomato maturity classification and fruit counting, demonstrating
the potential of complementary visual and spectral information for automated maturity
monitoring in greenhouse environments.
With the rapid development of deep learning, its application in maturity classification
tasks has become increasingly widespread and has gradually emerged as the dominant
approach in this field. Thus, the analysis and design of maturity classification can be
mainly organized into three branches. The first branch relies on CNN approaches, such
as AlexNet [6,7], RetinaNet [8], DenseNet [9], MobileNetV2 [10], MnasNet [11], Efficient-
Net [12], and CNN models combined with transfer learning and data augmentation strate-
gies [13]. These methods mainly focus on extracting discriminative visual features for
image-level maturity classification. Owing to real-time performance and high accuracy
of YOLO, the second branch relies on YOLO methods, such as YOLO-ELS [14], MTD-
YOLO [15], and DCS-YOLO [16], which are commonly used when fruit localization and
maturity recognition need to be performed simultaneously. The third category is based
on vision transformers (ViTs), which represent an important shift in visual recognition by
enabling long-range dependency modeling and improved scalability for complex visual
tasks [17]. However, training Transformer-based models with strong performance and good
generalization ability usually requires higher computational resources than conventional
CNNs. Therefore, in practical agricultural vision applications, Transformer-related models
can be broadly divided into pure Transformer-based models and CNN–Transformer hybrid
models. For example, Rai et al. [18] proposed FruitProm, a Transformer-based maturity
estimation and detection framework for fruits and vegetables by introducing a proba-
bilistic maturity prediction head into RT-DETRv2. Amad et al. [19] presented a two-step
semi-supervised approach that leverages knowledge distillation and transfer learning for
citrus maturity detection in uncrewed aerial vehicle images. Khan et al. [20] proposed a
convolutional Transformer framework for tomato maturity recognition, combining con-
volutional feature extraction with Transformer-based contextual modeling. In addition,
Yu et al. [21] proposed a quality-driven Transformer–CNN hybrid model for the fusion
of external and internal fingerprint features, further demonstrating the effectiveness of
hybrid Transformer–CNN feature fusion in complex visual recognition tasks. These studies
indicate that Transformer-based architectures have shown promising potential in fruit
maturity and quality assessment tasks, but their computational cost and data requirements
should also be considered in practical agricultural deployment.

More recently, several novel approaches have been proposed for maturity classifica-
tion, such as LAB dual-branch network. Dual-branch neural networks employ decoupled
modeling of different features, achieving promising results in remote sensing image anal-
ysis [22], medical image segmentation [23], and industrial defect detection [24]. These
networks can effectively extract key features in complex backgrounds and improve the
model’s discriminative capability. In maturity recognition tasks, color information serves
as an important basis for distinguishing different growth stages. Relevant studies have
demonstrated the critical role of color features in maturity determination [25]. For exam-
ple, Zhao et al. [26] focused on different wheat varieties, converted RGB images to the
LAB color space, and extracted the a-component to analyze color variation characteristics
across different maturity stages. The results demonstrated that LAB color components can
effectively reflect crop maturity variation patterns. Sabri et al. [27] addressed the maturity
classification of fresh oil palm fruit bunches, comparing multiple color models including
RGB, HSV, and LAB, and combined color moment features with support vector machines
for classification. The results indicated that the LAB model achieved better classification
performance than the RGB model, demonstrating its superior discriminative capability in
maturity representation. Compared with the RGB space, LAB can separate luminance and
chrominance information and exhibits stronger robustness to illumination variations. The
research by Saifullah et al. [28] further validated its superiority in accuracy and stability
over traditional RGB methods.
On another research frontier, attention mechanisms have been widely employed to
enhance the model’s capability in modeling fine-grained information. These mechanisms
apply weighted attention to key regions or channel features, improving the model’s re-
sponse to important features. For example, Herman et al. [29] introduced a residual
attention mechanism in oil palm fruit maturity classification to enhance the capability of
identifying subtle differences. Zhao et al. [30] employed a self-attention mechanism based
on a lightweight Transformer to achieve global feature modeling, maintaining high recog-
nition performance while reducing computational complexity. Additionally, Ji et al. [31]
proposed the FMA-YOLO model, combining CBAM, coordinate attention, and EMA mech-
anisms to improve detection performance in complex environments.
The aforementioned methods have achieved great progress in maturity classification
and detection tasks; however they still face challenges in complex scenarios. For example,
fruit maturity shows continuous color changes, and adjacent maturity levels only differ
slightly in color ratio, spot distribution, and texture roughness, which can cause confusion
between nearby classes, and in the RGB color space, brightness and color information are
mixed together, making it hard to highlight subtle color differences related to maturity
changes. Furthermore, existing methods typically focus on extracting overall features
but ignore the collaborative modeling of local color changes and brightness information.
Effectively separating brightness and color information while enhancing collaborative
modeling of local key regions remains a significant challenge, which is a key motivation for
our research.
Therefore, how to effectively separate brightness and color information, and improve
the ability to model key local regions together, remains a key problem in maturity classifi-
cation. To address this issue, this paper proposes an oil palm fruit maturity classification
model based on LAB dual-branch feature fusion with multi-level attention enhancement,
namely the Color-Matrix Fusion Network (CMF-Net). The model converts input images
to the LAB color space and employs an input adapter to perform channel mapping for
the L luminance branch and ab chrominance branch, adapting them to the input structure
of the pre-trained network. A dual-branch feature extraction network is utilized to learn
luminance texture information and chrominance discriminative information separately, and

a gated fusion mechanism adaptively adjusts the contribution weights of the two feature
types, achieving complementary luminance and color feature representation. The model
further introduces BiFPN for multi-scale feature interaction, combining coordinate attention

modeling capabilities, thereby improving the discriminative ability for fine-grained matu-
rity differences. Additionally, an ordinal supervision strategy is designed to incorporate the
ordinal relationships between maturity levels into the training process, enabling the model
to better learn the continuous variation patterns of adjacent maturity stages. Experimental
results on the main Oil palm fruit ripeness dataset and supplementary external datasets
demonstrate that the proposed CMF-Net achieves promising performance across multiple
evaluation metrics and shows good generalization potential.
2. Materials and Methods
2.1. Dataset Description
The main Oil Palm Fruit Ripeness Dataset used in this study consists of oil palm
fruit images collected under a specific acquisition scenario. The images are categorized
into five maturity levels, Immature, Partially ripe, Fully ripe, Overripe, and Decayed, as
shown in Figure 1. These five categories represent a fine-grained maturity grading task,
where adjacent maturity stages often exhibit subtle differences in color, texture, and surface
appearance. In particular, the transition from partially ripe to fully ripe and from overripe
to decayed can be difficult to distinguish because of gradual changes in fruit color and
surface degradation.

2.2. Data Preprocessing and Augmentation
For all datasets, images were first organized according to their mapped maturity
labels, and the same train–validation split was used for CMF-Net and all baseline models
on each dataset to ensure a fair comparison. All images were resized to 224 × 224 pixels
before being fed into the models. For CMF-Net, each RGB image was converted into the

CIELAB color space, where the luminance channel L was used as one input branch and the
chromatic channels a and b were concatenated as the second input branch. The L, a, and b
channels were normalized to the range [0, 1], allowing the model to learn luminance-related
texture information and chromatic maturity cues separately. This separation is particularly
important for adjacent maturity stages, where the visual differences are often reflected
in gradual chromatic shifts rather than obvious shape changes. For the baseline models,
including ResNet152V2, MobileViT-CBAM, Swin-T, ConvNeXt-Tiny, and EfficientNetV2-S,
RGB images were resized to 224 × 224 pixels and normalized according to the preprocessing
strategy used by each model implementation. For models initialized with ImageNet
pretrained weights, ImageNet-style normalization or the corresponding framework-specific
preprocessing was adopted. The data preprocessing pipeline is shown in Figure 2.
Figure 2. Data preprocessing pipeline for CMF-Net.
Data augmentation was applied only to the training set. For CMF-Net, augmentation
was performed in the LAB space, including random horizontal flipping, brightness pertur-
bation on the L channel, small-angle rotation, and Gaussian noise injection. The brightness
factor was randomly sampled from [0.5, 1.5], the brightness bias from [−0.1, 0.1], the ro-
tation angle from [−15◦, 15◦], and Gaussian noise with a standard deviation of 0.03 was
added to both the luminance and chromatic channels. Label smoothing with a factor of
0.02 was applied to the classification labels during training. For the RGB-based baseline
models, random horizontal flipping, random rotation, random resized cropping, and color
jittering were used during training. No data augmentation was applied to the validation set,
ensuring that all models were evaluated on unmodified validation images while improving
robustness during training. The augmentation procedures are shown in Figure 3.

2.3. Overall System Architecture
In this section, we present the proposed model of CMF-Net in detail. As shown
in Figure 4, the model CMF-Net consists of six components: Data Pre-processing and
LAB Decomposition, Dual-Branch Feature Extraction, Gated Feature Fusion, BiFPN-based
Multi-scale Feature Interaction, Attention Enhancement, and Ordinal Classification. The
Data Pre-processing and LAB Decomposition part transforms the original RGB images into
luminance and chrominance maps. The Dual-Branch Feature Extraction part is shown in
Figure 4a,b, which extracts luminance-structural features and chrominance-discriminative
features through two parallel branches. The Gated Feature Fusion part is shown in Figure 4c,
which adaptively integrates the features from the two branches. Then, the fused features
are processed by BiFPN, coordinate attention, and self-attention modules. Finally, the
Ordinal Classification part obtains the final ripeness category through sigmoid prediction
and threshold decoding.

In the Data Pre-processing and LAB Decomposition part, the input RGB images are
first resized and normalized. Then, the pre-processed RGB images are converted into
the LAB color space. Based on the LAB representation, the image is decomposed into
the L map and the ab map. The L map contains one luminance channel, which mainly
describes brightness-related structure and texture information. The ab map contains two
chrominance channels, which mainly represent color distribution information related to oil
palm fruit ripeness.
The Dual-Branch Feature Extraction part includes the L branch and the ab branch.
As shown in Figure 4a, in the L branch, the one-channel L input is first projected into a
three-channel representation by an input adapter. As shown in Figure 4b, in the ab branch,
the two-channel ab input is also projected into a three-channel representation. The input
adapter is composed of a 1 × 1 convolution, batch normalization, and ReLU activation.
After channel projection, the two inputs are fed into two parallel InceptionV3 backbone
networks. The backbone extracts multi-scale feature maps from different stages, denoted
as L3, L4, and L5, corresponding to feature resolutions of 1/8, 1/16, and 1/32, respectively.
In the Gated Feature Fusion part, the features from the L branch and the ab branch
are fused adaptively. As shown in Figure 4c, the gated fusion module first concatenates
the two branch features along the channel dimension. Then, global average pooling, a
fully connected layer, and a sigmoid function are used to generate the gate weight. By
using this learned weight, the model can adjust the contributions of luminance features
and chrominance features, obtaining fused features for the following modules.
In the BiFPN-based Multi-scale Feature Interaction part, the fused features are further
processed by the BiFPN module. The BiFPN module uses bidirectional feature paths to
exchange information among multi-scale feature maps. In this way, low-level texture details
and high-level semantic features can be combined more effectively, improving the feature
representation for adjacent ripeness stages.
In the Attention Enhancement part, the fused multi-scale features are enhanced by co-
ordinate attention and self-attention. The coordinate attention module is used to strengthen
the spatial location information of key ripeness-related regions. The self-attention mod-
ule is then used to model global relationships among different feature regions, obtaining
enhanced feature maps with stronger discriminative ability.
In the Ordinal Classification part, the enhanced feature maps are first transformed
into feature vectors by global average pooling. Then, the feature vectors are processed
by fully connected layers and dropout. Considering the ordinal relationship among oil
palm fruit ripeness levels, an ordinal prediction head is used to output K −1 ordered

For the five-level classification task, the model outputs immature, partially ripe, fully ripe,
overripe, and decayed categories.
Remark 1. Figure 4 describes the novel CMF-Net framework proposed in this paper, including data
preprocessing and LAB decomposition, LAB dual-branch feature extraction, gated fusion mecha-
nism, BiFPN-based multi-scale feature interaction, multi-level attention enhancement, and ordinal
classification. The merit of this design is that CMF-Net can more accurately classify oil palm fruit
ripeness when adjacent maturity levels exhibit subtle color transitions and when luminance and
chrominance information are coupled in the RGB space. By introducing the LAB color space to sepa-
rate luminance and chrominance information into two branches, and by integrating the gated fusion
mechanism with multi-level attention enhancement, the proposed model strengthens the synergistic
representation of luminance-structural information and chromaticity-discriminative information.
2.4. Dual-Branch Feature Fusion and Enhancement Mechanism
The dual-branch InceptionV3 architecture effectively extracts image luminance struc-
tural information and chromaticity distribution features. Building upon this foundation,
this paper introduces an input adaptation module to achieve a unified representation
of features from different modalities, and constructs a gated fusion mechanism follow-
ing the backbone network to perform adaptive weighted integration of luminance and
chromaticity features. Subsequently, the fused features are fed into a cross-branch BiFPN
module for multi-scale feature interaction, incorporating both Coordinate Attention (CA)
and Self-Attention (SA) to further enhance the representational power of key regions. Si-
multaneously, a hierarchically ordered supervision mechanism is introduced to optimize
the model’s discriminative capability regarding continuous variations in maturity levels.
The specific implementation details for each core module are outlined below.

chrominance-driven maturity variations in oil palm fruit images, this study constructs an
LAB dual-branch gated fusion module combined with an improved Cross-Branch BiFPN.
As shown in Figure 5, the proposed module consists of three main stages: input adaptation
and dual-branch feature extraction, scale-wise gated fusion, and improved cross-branch
multi-scale feature interaction.

Compared with conventional BiFPN structures that mainly aggregate multi-scale
features from a single backbone, the proposed module is specifically designed for the LAB
dual-branch architecture. First, the input adapter performs learnable channel mapping
for the L and ab components, enabling different LAB components to better adapt to the

representations for fine-grained oil palm fruit maturity classification.
Remark 2. Existing LAB dual-branch methods typically separate luminance and chrominance
information before feature extraction, but they often lack effective channel adaptation and adaptive
fusion between the two branches. It is worth noting that the proposed Input Adaptation module
introduces learnable channel mapping for the L-channel and ab-channels, enabling different LAB
components to better adapt to the backbone network. Furthermore, the Gated Fusion module
incorporates the relative contributions of luminance-structural and chromaticity-discriminative
features into the fusion process, thereby improving the complementarity of dual-branch features. This
is further combined with a cross-branch BiFPN module to promote multi-scale feature interaction and
enhance the discriminative representation of subtle maturity-related color and structural variations.
2.4.2. Multi-Level Attention Enhancement Mechanism
This subsection introduces the Coordinate Attention (CA) module and the Self-
Attention (SA) module, which are applied sequentially for local spatial enhancement
and global contextual modeling.
(1) Coordinate Attention Module In this section, the multi-level attention enhance-
ment mechanism consists of two sequential modules: the Coordinate Attention (CA)

In terms of computational complexity, CMF-Net still has certain limitations. Owing to
the use of dual-branch feature extraction and multi-level feature fusion, its parameter size
and FLOPs are higher than those of lightweight models such as MobileNetV2, MobileViT-

before deployment on highly resource-constrained devices. Nevertheless, compared with
heavier CNN architectures such as VGG16 and ResNet152V2, CMF-Net requires fewer
FLOPs and achieves a shorter inference time, while obtaining higher accuracy, recall, and
F1-score. More importantly, CMF-Net is designed for fine-grained oil palm fruit maturity

challenges. Therefore, the increased computational cost is accompanied by improved
maturity-discriminative capability. In future work, lightweight optimization strategies,
such as model pruning, knowledge distillation, quantization, and lightweight backbone
replacement, will be explored to further reduce the computational burden and improve the
suitability of CMF-Net for mobile terminals, embedded devices, and field-side agricultural
inspection systems. The performance gain of CMF-Net can be attributed to its collaborative
design. First, the LAB color space separates luminance and chrominance information,
allowing the model to learn structural and color-related cues through different branches.
Second, the gated fusion and BiFPN modules promote adaptive cross-branch and multi-
scale feature interaction. Third, the coordinate attention and self-attention modules enhance
local key-region perception and global contextual modeling. Finally, the auxiliary color
regression constraint and ordinal supervision further improve the model’s sensitivity
to continuous maturity changes. These components jointly enable CMF-Net to achieve
stronger discriminative ability for oil palm fruit maturity classification.

were selected: ResNet152V2 as a deep CNN baseline, MobileViT-CBAM as a lightweight
attention-enhanced model, Swin-T as a Transformer-based model, EfficientNetV2-S as an
efficient convolutional network, and ConvNeXt-Tiny as a recent high-performing ConvNet
architecture. These models cover different mainstream visual recognition paradigms and
provide a balanced comparison with CMF-Net.
For each model, five-fold cross-validation was performed under the same preprocess-
ing strategy, input size, training settings, and evaluation metrics. The results are reported as
mean ± standard deviation, and 95% confidence intervals were calculated to describe the
stability of model performance. The p-values indicate paired comparisons with CMF-Net
based on accuracy. Paired statistical tests were further conducted between CMF-Net and
each representative baseline using fold-wise results.
As shown in Table 7, CMF-Net achieves the highest mean accuracy and F1-score,
reaching 95.88% ± 0.32% and 96.20% ± 0.35%, respectively. The corresponding 95%
confidence intervals are [95.48, 96.28] for accuracy and [95.77, 96.63] for F1-score, indicating
stable performance across different folds. Compared with ResNet152V2, MobileViT-CBAM,
Swin-T, and EfficientNetV2-S, CMF-Net shows statistically significant improvements. For
ConvNeXt-Tiny, the p-value is 0.087, indicating that the difference is not significant at the
0.05 level, although CMF-Net still obtains slightly higher mean accuracy and F1-score.
Overall, these results indicate that CMF-Net performs consistently across different data
partitions and remains competitive against representative CNN-based, Transformer-based,
and modern ConvNet models.

Decayed, where the classification accuracies reach 98.68% and 98.78%, respectively. Most
misclassifications occur between adjacent categories, such as Partially Ripe and Fully Ripe,
which is consistent with the continuous color transition during oil palm fruit ripening.
These results indicate that subtle color differences between adjacent stages remain the main
source of classification difficulty. Nevertheless, the concentration of most samples along the
diagonal suggests that the proposed LAB dual-branch representation and auxiliary color
supervision help alleviate adjacent-stage confusion.

3.9.2. t-SNE Feature Visualization Analysis
To further analyze the discriminative ability of the learned feature representations,
this study employed t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) to visualize the
high-dimensional features extracted from the penultimate layer of the validation set. t-SNE

a low-dimensional space while preserving local neighborhood relationships as much as
possible [41]. Therefore, it is widely used for visualizing feature separability in classification
tasks. In this study, the extracted deep features were projected into a two-dimensional
space using t-SNE, allowing the distribution and separation of different maturity categories
to be intuitively observed.
The results of the t-SNE feature visualization are presented in Figure 12. Figure 12a
shows that the baseline model produces noticeable overlap among several maturity cate-
gories, indicating that its feature representation is less discriminative. In contrast, CMF-Net,
as shown in Figure 12b, presents more compact intra-class distributions and clearer inter-
class separation. This indicates that the proposed feature fusion and attention enhancement
strategy improves the representation of maturity-related visual information. The clearer
separation between neighboring maturity categories further supports the effectiveness of
CMF-Net in modeling subtle color and texture transitions.

4. Discussion
The experimental results show that CMF-Net achieves competitive performance in oil
palm fruit maturity classification compared with both conventional CNN-based models
and recent advanced visual recognition models. Traditional CNN architectures, such as
VGG16, DenseNet121, MobileNetV2, and ResNet152V2, mainly learn features from RGB
images in a single-stream manner, which may limit their ability to distinguish adjacent
maturity stages with gradual color variations. In contrast, CMF-Net introduces LAB-based
luminance–chrominance decomposition, allowing structural information and color-related
maturity cues to be learned through different branches.
Compared with recent models such as MobileViT-CBAM, Swin-T, ConvNeXt-Tiny, and
EfficientNetV2-S, CMF-Net also shows strong overall performance. Although these mod-
els benefit from attention mechanisms, Transformer-style representation, or modernized
convolutional structures, CMF-Net obtains the best accuracy, recall, and F1-score, reaching
95.88%, 96.66%, and 96.20%, respectively. This indicates that a task-specific design based on
color-space decomposition and feature fusion is effective for fruit maturity classification.
The advantage of CMF-Net mainly comes from the coordinated modeling of lumi-
nance, chrominance, multi-scale features, and ordinal maturity relationships. The LAB
dual-branch structure separates brightness-related structural information and color-related
maturity cues, while the gated fusion and BiFPN modules enhance cross-branch and multi-
scale feature interaction. In addition, the CA and SA modules improve regional feature
perception and global contextual modeling. The auxiliary color regression and ordinal su-
pervision further help the model preserve subtle chromatic variations and reduce confusion
between neighboring maturity categories.

The cross-dataset experiments further demonstrate the generalization potential of
the proposed method. CMF-Net achieves strong performance on external oil palm fruit
datasets and also shows promising results on the Date Fruit Dataset. These results sug-
gest that the proposed LAB-based feature representation has potential transferability to
related fruit maturity classification tasks with different sample sizes, fruit appearances, and
imaging conditions.
Despite these advantages, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, publicly
available oil palm fruit maturity datasets are still limited, and the current experiments
cannot fully cover variations caused by different regions, cultivars, seasons, imaging
devices, and field environments. Second, the dual-branch structure of CMF-Net increases
computational cost compared with lightweight models. Although CMF-Net achieves a
favorable accuracy–complexity trade-off, further optimization is still needed for mobile or
low-power embedded deployment.
In practical applications, CMF-Net is more suitable for scenarios where recognition
accuracy is prioritized, such as automated grading systems, quality inspection terminals,
and harvesting decision-support platforms. Future work will focus on collecting larger-
scale multi-region and multi-device datasets, improving model efficiency through pruning,
knowledge distillation, backbone replacement, and quantization, and extending the frame-
work from image-level classification to real-time fruit detection and maturity assessment in
field scenes.
5. Conclusions
This study proposed CMF-Net, an LAB dual-branch feature fusion network for five-
class oil palm fruit maturity classification. By decomposing input images into luminance
and chrominance components, the model separately extracts structural information and
color-related maturity cues. The input adaptation module, gated feature fusion, Cross-
Branch BiFPN, and multi-level attention mechanism enhance cross-branch complementarity
and multi-scale feature interaction. In addition, ordinal supervision and color-assisted
regression are introduced to model the progressive relationship among maturity stages.
From a methodological perspective, CMF-Net provides a task-oriented framework that in-
tegrates color-space decomposition, adaptive feature fusion, and maturity-order modeling
for image-based fruit maturity recognition.
Experimental results on the main Oil Palm Fruit Ripeness Dataset show that CMF-Net
achieves an accuracy of 95.88%, a precision of 96.02%, a recall of 96.66%, a specificity of
98.91%, and an F1-score of 96.20%. Compared with classical CNN models and recent ad-
vanced visual recognition models, including Swin-T, ConvNeXt-Tiny, and EfficientNetV2-S,
CMF-Net achieves the best accuracy, recall, and F1-score. The ablation experiments further
verify the contribution of the main components, including LAB decomposition, input
adaptation, gated fusion, multi-scale interaction, attention enhancement, and ordinal su-
pervision. Cross-dataset experiments and five-fold cross-validation further demonstrate
the generalization potential and stability of CMF-Net. These results indicate that the pro-
posed method has practical potential for non-destructive maturity assessment, automated
grading, harvesting decision support, and quality control in oil palm production.
At its current computational scale, CMF-Net is more suitable for GPU-equipped
workstations, edge servers, and industrial inspection terminals where recognition accuracy
is prioritized. For deployment on embedded platforms, mobile inspection devices, and field-
side agricultural equipment, further lightweight optimization is still needed. In the future,
we will establish independent datasets or evaluate the proposed model under real-world
production conditions. We will also focus on collecting larger-scale multi-region and multi-
device oil palm fruit datasets, improving model efficiency through backbone replacement,
