Abstract— Smart Agriculture is a part of Humanitarian 
Technology. Oil palm fruit is one of the leading agricultural 
product exports by Malaysia. At present, the general methods 
used to determine the ripeness of oil palm fresh fruit bunch are 
using human vision, computer vision and laser-based imaging 
techniques. The objectives of this research are to design and 
build a scanning system based on a LiDAR sensor and servo 
motors and to obtain point cloud data from oil palm fresh fruit 
bunch (FFB). The proposed project consists of LiDAR Lite V3, 
Arduino UNO and two servo motors as its main component. 
LiDAR sensor is used to collect the intensity value that reflects 
from the Virescens oil palm FFB, and the data collected are 
saved in a CSV file for further analysis. The methodology used 
in this research is the Iterative Waterfall model. This model 
supports redesign if there are any improvements needed in this 
project and the phase can be looped back to the previous 
iteration if the process faces any errors. The system proposed 
works successfully to produce point clouds from oil palm fresh 
fruit bunch and it is found that ripe oil palm fruit has lower 
mean intensity value compared to unripe oil palm fresh fruit 
bunch.  
Keywords—oil palm, LiDAR, fresh fruit bunch, Arduino 
UNO, Virescens 
I. INTRODUCTION (HEADING 1) 
Humanitarian Technology is the creation of technologies 
that help people. Agricultural technologies and methods are 
some of the examples of humanitarian technologies for 
developing 
communities, 
while 
Information 
and 
Communication Technology (ICT) includes any device, tool, 
or application that permits the exchange or collection of data 
through interaction or transmission. Thus, this paper explores 
a possibility to create a prediction method for Oil Palm fresh 
fruit bunches (FFB) by using ICT in agricultural sector which 
contributed 7.1 per cent (RM101.5 billion) to Malaysia Gross 
Domestic Product (GDP) in 2019. Oil palm was the major 
contributor to the value added of agriculture sector at 37.7 per 
cent [1].  
The Malaysian crude palm oil (CPO) recent performance 
of 3.47 t/ha/year and fresh fruit bunches (FFB) of 17.19 
t/ha/year in 2019 indicated that there was not much progress 
in yield improvement since the last four decades (Parveez et 
al., 2020). The stagnation demands a holistic effort involving 
various disciplines in tackling this dilemma. The expansion of 
oil palm hectarage is the most straightforward approach but 
this raises a major concern on sustainability (Qaim et al., 
2020).  With the current planted area of 5.9 million hectares 
and mature palms covering 88.5% (Parveez et al., 2020), close 
to reaching the targeted limit of 6.5 million hectares 
introduced by the Malaysian government in committing to 
sustainability (Jaafar et al., 2020), the demand to escalate yield 
has never been more urgent. Therefore, moving forward the 
oil palm industry needs to actively integrate new research 
developments and innovations while adopting the most 
current good planting materials coupled with the best 
management in the plantations.  
The commercial oil palm planting material known as 
tenera (dura x pisifera) is generally derived from the fruit 
variety, nigrescens. Another variety, the virescens fruit type is 
not commonly cultivated in a mass scale. The unripe 
nigrescens fruit character which features hues of deep violet 
to black and only changes to slightly red with purple tinges 
upon ripening (Rao et al., 2020), may cause a hurdle for 
harvesters in the field in identifying ripened fruits especially 
for tall palms. If harvested late, the fully to over-ripe bunch 
would largely become loose fruits. This situation contributes 
to yield loss if the loose fruits are left uncollected as they 
possess optimum oil content, which will significantly reduce 
the total oil extraction rate (OER) (Mohd Ramadhan et al., 
2021).   
Meanwhile, despite representing a dominant trait, the 
distribution of virescens palm is far lower than the common 
variety of nigrescens at about less than 1% in Nigeria and 
Angola and close to 50% in Congo area (Rajanaidu, 1986). 
The virescens variety can facilitate the identification of bunch 
ripeness due to its apparent exocarp (skin) colour changes 

from unripe to ripe. The distinct fruit colour of green when 
unripe to gradually becoming orange upon ripening is 
controlled by the VIRESCENS gene, known as VIR which 
offers efficiency for bunch harvesting (Singh et al., 2014). 
This finding has allowed the utilization of the oil palm fruit 
skin colour as a cue for bunch ripening, which could further 
enhance improvements in field management and operation. 
Although possessing similar oil physical properties, the 
virescens variety has yet to gain commercial status as large 
scale cultivation is still rare brought about by a lack of interest 
in its breeding. In addition to this, assessment on the 
potentially varying chlorophyll content and other pigments 
prior to further downstream processes such as in the 
processing of CPO is required to ensure similar refining 
procedures used for nigrescens can be applied for oils from the 
virescens variety. Realizing the prospect in virescens 
cultivation, MPOB cloned a selected virescens ortet sampled 
from the MPOB/UKM Research Station, Bangi and its ramets 
are currently planted in several field plantation trials for 
comprehensive evaluation.  
Generally, the palm oil industry in Malaysia still practices 
a traditional way of harvesting oil palm fruit which is assessed 
by a trained inspector using human vision and counting the 
number of fruits loose from the bunches. The quality of the oil 
palm fresh fruit bunches can be determined by their condition 
such as ripeness, bruise or damage. The purchasing value of 
FFBs is a measure based on the bunch appearance and 
prediction of the quantity and quality of oil that can be 
extracted from the bunch (Makky et al., 2014). LiDAR is a 
remote sensing technology that uses light in the form of a 
pulsed laser to calculate the distance or another quantity from 
a remote targeted object. In this project, a LiDAR Lite V3 
from Garmin will be used to measure the reflected intensity 
value and to produce point cloud from the oil palm fresh fruit 
bunch. Point clouds are a group of the points that show a 3D 
shape or feature. Each of these points has its own set of X, Y 
and Z coordinates, and in this project, it also includes the 
reflected intensity values. This project aims to study and 
verify that the sensors reflected intensity values can determine 
the ripeness level of the fresh palm fruit bunches. Besides, the 
project will provide a rapid and precise mechanism in 
determining the maturity of oil palm FFB, and it can ensure 
the palm oil production increases over the years.  
Recently, many researchers are researching to find a non-
destructive and rapid method to determine the ripeness of oil 
palm FFB. The previous study includes light detection and 
ranging (LiDAR) technology (Mohd Zulkifli et al., 2018), 
imaging technique (Shabdin et al., 2016) and laser-based 
imaging technique (Shiddiq et al., 2017). The imaging 
technique using computer vision is based on the fruit colours 
that are influenced by the background image. Besides, the 
quality of the image captured by the camera and the laser light 
instrument is affected by environment lighting. So, it can only 
be used for close-range detection in the laboratory. 
Hence, this paper is the study of a development of 
virescens fresh fruit bunch ripeness prediction using LiDAR 
technology for realising smart agriculture.   
II. METHODOLOGY 
A. Methodology Model 
In this project, the Iterative Waterfall Model is chosen to 
develop the scanning system using LiDAR Lite V3. The 
global block diagram in Fig.1 shows that this project consists 
of three major parts. As shown in Fig. 2.  The first part is to 
collect the intensity value reflected from the oil palm fresh 
fruit bunch using LiDAR Lite V3 sensor. The second part 
focused on transferring the data that has been recorded using 
the Lora sensor. The last part will plot a graphical 
representation of point cloud data in R Studio software and 
also develop a web application to show the classification of oil 
palm fresh fruit bunch. This paper only will focus more on the 
first part, which is to collect the reflected intensity of oil palm 
FFB using a Lidar Lite V3 sensor. Next, the project flow in 
Fig. 3 was designed to evaluate the flow of each operation and 
how the system will operate.

B. Hardware Implementation 
In this project, a LiDAR sensor is embedded with two small 
hobby servo motors to create a scanning mechanism as shown 
in Fig. 5. Servo motors have been utilised as it can rotate up 
to 180 degrees and have a capability to rotate the motor shaft 
precisely. The mechanical setup of the scanner consists of a 
LiDAR Lite V3 sensor, a long "U" bracket, two multipurpose 
servo brackets and two servo motors. The mechanical design 
was created using an online application named Sketchup and 
Adobe Photoshop software. First servo motor at the base level 
controls the horizontal movement, and another servo motor 
controls the vertical movement of the system. LiDAR 
sensors, servo motors and brackets were assembled using 
screws, bolts and nuts provided by the manufacturer. 
 
 
 
Fig. 5 Assemble LiDAR sensor and servo motor together with 
the bracket 
 
In this project, Inter-Integrated Circuit (I2C) interface 
configurations are used to communicate between the LiDAR 
sensor and Arduino. The sensor has two wires, serial data 
(SDA) and serial clock (SCL), which are used to carry 
information between the devices connected to the bus in Fig. 
6. The I2C bus operates internally at 3.3 V and can run up to 
5 V with an internal level shifter. It also supports the data 
transmission of 400 kHz Fast Mode. Fig. 7 shows the 
connection between the I2C interface and the Arduino. 
 
 
 
Fig. 6 Standard Arduino I2C Wiring 
 
 
 
 
Fig. 7 I2C wiring between LiDAR Lite V3 and Arduino 
UNO 
 
 

Servo motors can be attached directly to the Arduino. As 
mentioned before, this project uses two servo motors to 
control the horizontal and vertical movement of the system. 
The servo has three connections which are red wire connected 
to 5V pin Arduino, a brown or black wire connected to 
ground and yellow wire connected to Arduino's PWM pin. 
Arduino's PWM pin 9 is used for servo Y which controls the 
vertical direction while pin 10 is used for servo X to control 
horizontal direction. The initial position for servo X and servo 
Y is set to 45 degrees and 30 degrees as shown in Fig. 8.

V. CONCLUSION 
In conclusion, the project is a development of a scanning 
system using Arduino, LiDAR Lite sensor and servo motor as 
a ripeness prediction scanning system to realised smart 
agriculture in Oil Palm Plantation. The prototype has fulfilled 
the objective to design and develop a scanning system based 
on LiDAR sensor and servo motors. The prototype is also able 
to obtain point cloud data or return intensity value from oil 
palm fresh fruit bunch. Based on the results and findings, the 
point cloud is successfully produced from LiDAR Lite V3 
sensor. This project can be considered as the pioneering phase 
to combine range sensor and material visualisation for the 
harvesting of oil palm fresh fruits bunch. 
For future study, this project can be developed with a 
drone so that the time for moving from a tree to another tree 
in an oil palm field can be reduced. It also can do the scanning 
for oil palm FFB at a high tree. Next, the analysis of point 
cloud data can be done using MATLAB software. . MATLAB 
has an-add on package for Arduino and Garmin LiDAR that 
would be useful for the project. The testing process for the 
prototype is limited due to conditional movement control 
order, CMCO. Therefore, to improve the results, more test 
needs to be carried out so that more data can be collected for 
further analysis of oil palm   
FFB ripeness. Lastly, it is recommended to add a 
component that can save the data from the prototype directly.
