Abstract—In agriculture, detecting and counting fruits are
prominent tasks that facilitate harvesting and yield estimation.
Since traditional methods such as manual counting are tedious
and error-prone, developing a real time system to automatically
and accurately detect, classify, and count fruits in an agricultural
environment is required. The aim of this paper is to propose
a system based on cutting-edge technologies, YOLOv8 and
ByteTrack, to accurately detect and count fruits in real time.
Consequentially, farmers will have access to precise information
about the quantity and variety of fruits in their ﬁelds, enabling
them to make informed decisions and maximize the proﬁtability
of their agricultural operations. Furthermore, the system will
enable them to enhance the efﬁciency of agricultural work
and reduce the required workforce. In this paper, a dataset
containing 20 classes of fruits with diverse backgrounds and
lighting conditions is constructed to train and assess the model’s
performance. The validation results of the detection and counting
model showed an mAP 0.5 value of 94.2%, a recall of 87.6%,
and a precision of 91.5%. The comparison between the manual
counting and the model showed an accuracy of 75.33%.
Index Terms—Object detection, deep learning, multi-object
tracking, smart agriculture, object counting.
I. INTRODUCTION
Agriculture represents one of the most crucial sectors of the
economy, providing food and materials for numerous indus-
tries. This sector is constantly evolving and actively seeking
innovative means to enhance production efﬁciency in order to
meet the dietary demands of a growing population, estimated
to surpass 9.6 billion by the year 2050 [1]. Consequently, the
implementation of intelligent agricultural practices has become
an indispensable necessity to achieve these food security
objectives [2].
Fruit recognition and classiﬁcation, as well as accurate
counting, play an essential role in farm management. Accurate
fruit recognition and precise counting of fruit will enable
yields to be assessed, i.e., to obtain valuable information
on the expected yields of different types of fruits. This will
enable farmers to make informed decisions about maximizing
farm proﬁtability. However, traditional methods are often time-
consuming and prone to human error. This is why the inte-
gration of cutting-edge technologies such as Deep Learning
(DL) opens up new prospects for automating and improving
these tasks. Fruit counting can be classiﬁed into two main
types: regression-based fruit counting and detection-based fruit
counting. According to [3], the latter is considered to be more
effective. In the case of fruit counting based on detection
techniques, the accuracy of fruit detection and localization
plays an important role in determining model performance.
Detecting fruit in agricultural settings has presented a
signiﬁcant challenge due to the intricate nature of growing
environments. Initially, attempts have focused on extracting
characteristics such as color, shape, or dominant features,
followed by techniques like wavelet transform-based analysis
and manual observation factors [2]- [4]. However, these meth-
ods are still inadequate for dealing with difﬁcult situations
like complex backgrounds, changes in lighting, and weather
variations.
DL has shown signiﬁcant progress in different ﬁelds, lead-
ing to the development of object detection models. These
models are categorized into one-stage and two-stage models.
The latter, such as Faster-RCNN [5], R-CNN [6], Fast-RCNN
[7], and Mask R-CNN [8], show better accuracy and recall,
while one-stage models, like SSD [9] and YOLO series [10],
exhibit faster inference speed. YOLO is one of the latest
advancements in DL algorithms used in object detection,
which stands for You Only Look Once [10]. YOLO has
proven to be highly efﬁcient in detecting and recognizing
objects across different contexts, making it an ideal choice for
fruit detection applications. Various articles discuss the use of
these algorithms for fruit detection, speciﬁcally focusing on
tomatoes and apples in different conditions [11], [12]. New
models like YOLO-Tomato [13], YOLO with DSConv, DPAG,
and FEM [14], and TDPPL-Net [15] have been proposed to
improve tomato detection precision in agriculture applications.

On the other hand, the progress of DL in the past few years
has also greatly inﬂuenced the ﬁeld of Multi-Object Tracking
(MOT), which serves as a subsequent task to object detection.
Multi-object tracking represents a crucial task in the ﬁeld
of computer vision. It focuses on the ability to locate and
track objects in videos or image sequences over a period of
time. This task holds immense signiﬁcance for various real-
world applications, such as robotics and autonomous driving.
However, object tracking encounters several obstacles, such
as dealing with changes in the appearance of objects, camera
movement, occlusion, and alterations in lighting conditions. To
overcome these challenges, advancements in DL have played
a crucial role. These advancements have resulted in more
robust and accurate tracking algorithms, enabling us to track
objects with greater precision and reliability. Notable MOT
algorithms include the Simple Online and Real-time Tracking
(SORT) algorithm [16], the Deep-SORT algorithm, which is
an extension of SORT [17], FairMOT [18], and ByteTrack
[19]. ByteTrack, being one of the latest MOT algorithms,
has demonstrated superior performance in soccer videos and
satellite imagery compared to other advanced multi-object
trackers [20], [21].
In the ﬁeld of fruit counting, Egi et al. [22] focused on
the use of YOLOv5 and Deep Sort algorithms, alongside
a dataset of greenhouse tomatoes captured by drones. The
dataset comprised three distinct categories, namely ﬂowers,
green tomatoes, and red tomatoes. This dataset was used to
train and assess the counting model. At epoch 96, the optimal
model for all classes is found with an accuracy of 0.618 at
mAP 0.5. The average F1 score for the three classes was 0.63.
The count results obtained manually and through the AI-Drone
indicated that the accuracy rates for the three classes varied
from 50% to 99%. Zhang et al. [23] presented a deep-learning-
based algorithm designed for counting citrus fruits using video
sequences. The algorithm comprised two sub-algorithms: Or-
angeYolo, used for detecting fruits, and OrangeSort, employed
for tracking fruits. OrangeYolo achieved higher mean average
precision values compared to other algorithms, while Orange-
Sort mitigated the issue of double-counting occluded fruits.
Parico and Ahamed [24] developed a pear fruit counter using
YOLOv4 models and the Deep SORT algorithm for mobile
applications. YOLOv4-CSP was found to be the optimal model
in terms of accuracy, while YOLOv4-tiny was ideal in terms
of speed and computational cost. Hu et al. [25] introduced
a new model that combined the self-attentive mechanism of
vision transform with Yolov7 to overcome the difﬁculties
encountered in accurately counting fruit yield within apple
orchards. This model incorporated two attention mechanisms,
CA and CBAM, and was trained and evaluated using apple
images. The results showed an mAP of 91.3% and an F1 score
of 0.85. Furthermore, the paper integrated two multi-objective
tracking methods, namely SORT and Cascade-SORT, to allow
for the counting of fruits in a variety of complicated situations
across video frames. The results showed that the presented
model had the potential to enhance fruit yield assessment
techniques and decision-making processes in the fruit industry.
To the best of our knowledge, there are only a few studies
in the literature that combine multi-object tracking algorithms
with DL object detection algorithms for fruit detection and
counting [22]- [25]. This area of research is still relatively
unexplored and primarily focuses on a single type of fruit.
The aim of this paper is to combine YOLOv8 and ByteTrack,
which are currently recognized as the most advanced object
detection models and MOT algorithms, respectively, to detect
and count 20 different types of fruits. This research presents
two key contributions. Firstly, a dataset consisting of fruit
images under various conditions, including illumination varia-
tions and complex backgrounds, was collected and annotated.
Secondly, the cutting-edge YOLOv8 object detection model
and the ByteTrack multi-object tracking model were combined
to accurately detect and count 20 different types of fruits.
II. METHODOLOGY
A. General Methodological Overview
Our proposed system involves six successive stages (Fig. 1).
In the ﬁrst stage, we collect fruit images, which are used
to train and evaluate the YOLO model. Then, in the second
stage, we subject the dataset to a treatment process involving
annotation, augmentation and pre-processing, which increases
the number of samples available in the dataset. The third step
is to train the YOLOv8 object detection model on our dataset.
Using the split dataset for validation purposes, we evaluate the
reﬁned model’s detection performance and analyze the results
obtained. Finally, in the last phase, we use the developed
model to build a system capable of detecting and counting
fruits in the ﬁeld.
Fig. 1: Diagram of the proposed system.
B. Data collection
In order to train and validate the fruit detection and counting
system illustrated in Fig. 1, we used a dataset representing
various types of fruits, collected from gardens and fruit and
vegetable markets. We also used the Internet as a source for

C. Data Annotation and preprocessing
Image annotation is an important step in the process of
preparing the YOLO model for training. This annotation
involves extracting features from an image and associating
them with labeled inputs. During training, the machine can
thus learn to recognize the features present in the labeled
images. Consequently, the accuracy of the labeling has a major
impact on the model accuracy. The YOLO model must be able
to learn distinctive features for different types of fruits. In
the annotation process, the type and location of fruits present
in the labelled image are speciﬁed. To this end, Roboﬂow’s
Annotate tool was used to label the images. Information on
the location and category of fruits in the image was retrieved
and saved as a text ﬁle in YOLO format.
To improve model performance, Roboﬂow offers a data pre-
processing process, which was applied to the dataset. For the
sake of uniformity, the images were resized to 640 640 pixels.
Automatic orientation was also used to ensure that image
orientation has no undesirable impact on the model.
D. Data augmentation and splitting
In general, Deep Learning models tend to perform better
when more data are available. Besides, to counteract over
ﬁtting and enable the model to generalize better, increasing
the number of training samples can be an effective strategy. In
this study, additional training data were generated by applying
various image enhancement techniques, including ﬂipping,
horizontal and vertical rotation, brightness modiﬁcation, gray-
scale conversion and blurring. Fig. 2 shows the application of
data augmentation techniques to fruit images.
After the data augmentation stage, the dataset reached a
total of 1792 samples, divided into 20 different fruit classes.
To train the fruit detection model efﬁciently, the dataset was
split into training, validation, and test sets. Speciﬁcally, the
dataset was split into 1569 images for training, 153 images
for validation, and 70 images for testing.
E. Description of the YOLO Model
YOLOv8 is the latest version of the YOLO family, launched
by Ultralytics on 10 January 2023 [26]. It is an advanced and
cutting-edge model that outperforms its predecessors in the
YOLO series in terms of both detection accuracy and speed
[26]- [28]. Unlike its predecessors, YOLOv8 implements an
anchor free detection technique that predicts the center of an
object, rather than predicting its distance from a known anchor
box. This technique reduces the number of box predictions,
thus accelerating the post-processing process.
The YOLOv8 architecture comprises three key components:
the backbone, the neck, and the head. This architecture is

illustrated in Fig. 3. The backbone of YOLOv8 is in charge
of extracting feature maps from input images. It is based on
a modiﬁed CSPDarknet-53 that uses a series of convolutional
layers enabling the network to learn complex representations
[28]. The neck acts as a bridge between the backbone and
the head, facilitating the integration of multi-scale features.
YOLOv8 uses a Path Aggregation Network - Feature Pyramid
Network (PAN-FPN) structure as its neck module, allowing the
model to merge features from different levels of the backbone
network [28]. The head of YOLOv8 processes the feature
maps extracted to generate bounding box predictions and class
probabilities.

To count fruit, we used the ByteTrack algorithm and a
counting tool provided by the Supervision library. ByteTrack
enabled us to track each fruit across several frames of a video,
guaranteeing accurate counting. It is used to track fruits across
different frames of a video. Tracking fruits is essential for
accurate fruit counting. This tool associates each fruit detected
in one frame with its position in the next frame, enabling fruit
movement to be tracked over time. This ensures that each fruit
is counted only once, even if it moves from one image to the
next.
The supervision library created by the Roboﬂow team
provides a ”line counter” counting tool that we used to count
the fruits. This tool is responsible for counting how many
individual detections have passed a virtual reference line on
the video. The line consists of two counters: In, which counts
the detected objects that cross the line on the way in, and
Out, which counts the detected objects that cross the line on
the way out. The in and out line counters analyze information
on object detections made and adjust the count accordingly.
In this work, if a fruit is detected crossing the line, one of
the counters increments the total number of fruits counted
according to their direction of travel.

Fig. 10 shows that the system is able to track four fruits

incremented until it reaches the number 4, which corresponds

our future work.

IV. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we presented a real-time system that relies
on computer vision and artiﬁcial intelligence to detect, clas-
sify, and count fruit on a farm automatically and accurately,
representing a crucial step in the development of automated
information harvesting. As a result, farmers can obtain precise
