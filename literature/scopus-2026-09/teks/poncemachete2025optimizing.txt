Abstract—Accurate fruit yield prediction is necessary for 
enhancing agricultural methods and providing quality output. 
This work analyzes the efficacy of multi-object tracking (MOT) 
algorithms in detecting calamansi fruits from drone-captured 
video footage, using the YOLOv8m object detection model. Data 
were gathered from a commercial calamansi farm in Naujan, 
Philippines, and prepared for model training. Three MOT 
algorithms—SORT, DeepSORT, and ByteTrack—were evaluated 
using performance metrics such as Multiple Object Tracking 
Accuracy (MOTA), Higher Order Tracking Accuracy (HOTA), 
and ID F1 Score (IDF1). ByteTrack achieved exceptional 
performance with MOTA, HOTA, and IDF1 ratings of 59.67%, 
65.42%, and 73.72% respectively. The integration of YOLOv8m 
for detection with ByteTrack for tracking significantly reduced 
double-counting while ensuring good computational efficiency. 
The results underscore ByteTrack's capability in real-time fruit 
monitoring and its significance for improving yield estimate and 
agricultural production.

I. INTRODUCTION
Sustainable yield in agriculture is crucial, as inappropriate 
use of agricultural inputs and environmental factors such as 
weather and soil conditions have adversely affected crop output 
[1]. Numerous researchers have undertaken efforts to establish 
accurate agricultural yield assessment methodologies needed for 
farm expansion and meeting market demands [2][3][4]. 
Currently, the emergence of advanced cameras and robust 
processors enables artificial intelligence methodologies, such as 
deep learning to provide a range of opportunities for real-time 
object detection [5].  
Calamansi, 
scientifically 
known 
as 
Citrofortunella 
microcarpa, is a citrus fruit cultivated in the Philippines. The 
production of calamansi is economically vital for regional 
welfare and the livelihood of local farmers, due to its adaptability 
to many ecological conditions and its substantial demand in both 
local and international markets [6]. Autonomous technologies 
that identify and measure fruits pre-harvest present opportunities 
for enhancing harvesting procedures [7]. The standard yield 
measurement methods are manual counting and vision 
identification. These procedures are, however, subjective, 
complicated, and open to human error [8]. Assessing yield 
accurately is essential for the effective crop management of 
calamansi producers when evaluating its economic value [9].  
Recent 
technological 
breakthroughs 
provide 
unique 
instruments that utilize their capabilities for many agricultural 
purposes. Deep Learning (DL) has demonstrated significant 
progress in agriculture by executing essential tasks such as yield 
estimation, crop classification, and fruit detection [6].  
Fruit detection provides a specific instance of object 
detection. In recent years, the majority of methodologies employ 
end-to-end trained Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for 
detection purposes [10]. Numerous architectures exist, although 
the majority of approaches may be categorized into two kinds: 
single-stage and two-stage models [11]. In recent years, the 
single stage You Only Look Once (YOLO) family has become 
a highly favored option in numerous applications owing to its 
exceptional accuracy, real-time performance, and ease of use 
[12]. YOLO algorithm has attained an exceptional balance 
between speed and precision which is needed for accurate 
detection of objects.  
The YOLO series, as a prominent deep learning network, is 
extensively used for the detection and counting of fruits and 
vegetables [13]. The average detection accuracy of 92.23% was 
achieved by YOLOv4 for apple detection [14]. YOLOv5 was 
applied to autonomously differentiate between fruits that are 
graspable and those that are ungraspable in apple tree images for 
a harvesting robot. There are also performance disparities among 
YOLO models of varying versions. YOLOv5 significantly 
enhanced the model's mean Average Precision (mAP) by 
14.95% and 4.74% compared to YOLOv3 and YOLOv4, 
respectively, as revealed by comparative analysis [15]. In order 
to enhance the accuracy of detection, a blueberry recognition 
method that has been developed from YOLOv8 was applied. 
The approach achieved a precision of 84.6%, a recall of 91.3%, 
and a mAP of 94.1% [16]. YOLOv8's competitiveness in 
mAP@50 scores suggests its potential as a viable option in the 
object identification domain, despite the fact that YOLOv5 is 
superior in quantitative measurements. Due to its superior 
inference times, YOLOv5 is advantageous for real-time 
applications. Nevertheless, YOLOv8's commendable inference 
times are also substantial, indicating that it is suitable for

The YOLOv8 architecture's improvements were also 
illustrated by other YOLOv8 variants, which outperformed 
traditional models. Mask R-CNN, FCIS, and Faster R-CNN 
were conventional models that demonstrated lower efficacy, 
particularly in the mAP50-95 metric. The models were less

The YOLOv8 variants, in general, exhibited significant

architecture's adaptability and advancements [18].  
Counting fruits poses a challenge, especially because of fruit 
occlusions [19]. Fruits may appear markedly different due to 
variations in lighting and storage conditions, complicating the 
ability of algorithms to reliably identify them in diverse 
environments. One strategy to address this difficulty entails 
obtaining data from many views, positing that calamansi fruits 
obscured from one viewpoint will be observable from another 
[20]. However, this method may result in an overestimated fruit 
count due to double counting from various image angles. 
Multi-object tracking (MOT) is a computer vision challenge 
that seeks to recognize and locate objects of interest within a 
video sequence, associating them across frames to monitor their 
motions over time [21]. Different trackers adhere to the tracking-
by-detection paradigm, which initially localizes and classifies all 
targets, then associating detections of the same object by 
appearance, motion, or a combination of both [22][23]. Yield 
estimates necessitate monitoring methodologies to ensure that 
objects are counted only once in every frame [24]. 
An advanced information system for automated fruit 
detection and counting is important to enhance the productivity 
of the farm. This study proposes the utilization of multi-view 
sensing through the acquisition of continuous motion films using 
a drone in capturing the tree. Thereafter, fruits are identified in 
every video frame, and Multi-Object Tracking (MOT) methods 
are employed to avoid double-counting of fruits. 
This study compares the SORT, DeepSORT and ByteTrack 
algorithms for counting fruit in recorded videos. The paper 
contrasts the three leading tracking methodologies for fruit 
counting. The following portions of this manuscript are 
structured as follows: Section II highlights the Materials and 
Methodology utilized for this research and provides a synopsis 
of the algorithms applied in the methodological framework. 
Section III outlines and analyzes the outcomes achieved in fruit 
detection, tracking, and counting tasks. Finally, in Section IV, 
the conclusions were defined based on the results of the study. 
II. MATERIALS AND METHODOLOGY 
A. Dataset 
The data was gathered from calamansi farms in Naujan, 
Oriental Mindoro, particularly at Calangi Farm in Aurora, 
Naujan. The farm engages in commercial production aimed at 
broader markets, including exports. It is 5 hectares and has more 
than 3000 calamansi trees. The researchers gathered data from 
300 trees on the land, which were shot from January to February 
2024 between 8 AM-11 AM, and 2 PM-4 PM. The fruit trees 
and flowers were photographed from many angles and under 
varying lighting conditions to collect sample data for research 
purposes. 
RGB video frames were recorded with a DJI Air 25 drone 
mounted with a 20 MP camera, attaining a video resolution of 
5472×3078 at 25fps in calamansi fields during daylight hours. 
The video clips were stored on an SD card and transferred to 
the local computer for image segmentation. The video frames 
were converted into pictures via Roboflow. Fig. 1 illustrates the 
topographic and lateral perspectives of the tree acquired by the 
drone. 
The drone maintained a distance of 1 to 1.5 meters from the 
target, with the camera positioned vertically upward or below 
while either hovering or gradually navigating around the tree. 
This dynamic movement reveals the tree's connection with its 
surroundings. Considering the typical height of Calamansi 
trees, which ranges from 3 to 6 meters, a minimum altitude of 4 
to 8 meters over the tallest tree in the orchard was maintained 
to guarantee safe flying and avert any harm to both the drone 
and the trees. This offered detailed views of the Calamansi 
trees, including close-ups of the fruits and flowers.

A comparative analysis of three MOT trackers—SORT, 
DeepSORT, and ByteTrack—for the fruit tracking was 
conducted. SORT depends significantly on precise object 
detections for data association. It employs many association 
techniques, including the Kalman Filter and Hungarian 
Algorithm, which depend on a motion model of identified 
objects. DeepSORT enhances SORT by integrating appearance 
data derived from a re-identification (Re-ID) network. 
ByteTrack uses both high- and low-confidence detections to 
proficiently manage densely clustered objects or complex 
situations. 
C. Object Counting
The YOLOv8m model detects objects in each video frame
and provides the bounding box coordinate and class ID to 
indicate the type of object (flower or fruit). After extracting the 
bounding box coordinates for each detection, each detected 
object is passed to the tracker. The tracker assigns a unique ID 
to each object that it detects and tracks across frames. This 
process helps maintain the ID of each object across frames, even 
if it moves. 
Each detected object now has an assigned unique ID 
provided by the tracker. For each tracked object in the frame, 
the system extracts the object’s unique ID and class label. It 
checks if the object’s ID has already been counted for that class. 
If the ID is new (not yet counted), the count of the object will 
be added. Each unique object is counted only once for each 
class across all frames. Fig. 2 shows the example tracked 
objects and their unique IDs.

D. Evaluation Metrics
To evaluate the performance of the tracking algorithms, a
set of standard metrics was used in multi-object tracking (MOT) 
tasks. These metrics help assess how well the tracker follows 
objects over time, handles object re-identification, and manages 
various tracking challenges like occlusions. 
To measure the overall accuracy of the tracking system by 
considering the effects of false positives, missed detections, and 
mismatches, the study used Multiple Object Tracking Accuracy 
(MOTA).  
To identify the harmonic mean of precision and recall, based 
on unique identities rather than bounding boxes, the ID F1 
Score (IDF1) was used. 
Higher Order Tracking Accuracy (HOTA) is an expansion 
of the MOTA that integrates many metrics to assess numerous 
error kinds, including detection recall and accuracy, association 
recall and precision, and localization.  
These metrics (MOTA, HOTA, IDF1) evaluate the tracker’s 
ability to maintain consistent IDs, detect and follow objects 
accurately, and process frames efficiently.

IV. CONCLUSION 
This study used computer vision to develop and evaluate a 
fruit counting system intended to predict orchard productivity. 
The technology was assessed in an experimental calamansi 
orchard. The fruit count in recorded videos was primarily 
compared to the efficacy of three advanced tracking methods: 
SORT, DeepSORT, and ByteTrack. Across performance 
metrics, ByteTrack consistently excels achieving superior 
results in MOTA, IDF1, and HOTA relative to DeeSORT, 
however SORT had less effective performance. Nonetheless, 
these findings suggest that the appearance characteristics of the 
SORT algorithm do not significantly assist fruit tracking.  
This research is a vital phase in the process of converting 
theoretical developments in MOT and deep learning into 
practical agricultural solutions. By utilizing drone-based data 
collection and processing, the research illustrates the potential 
of cutting-edge technology to predict fruit yields in a scalable 
and efficient manner, thereby bridging the divide between 
computational theory and real-world applications. 
This research is a pioneer in the incorporation of 
sophisticated tracking paradigms that are specifically designed 
for agricultural environments by utilizing MOT algorithms—
SORT, DeepSORT, and ByteTrack. The research demonstrates 
that the integration of sophisticated tracking mechanisms with 
high-precision object detection can result in more accurate 
predictions of fruit yield, even in visually complex and dynamic 
environments such as orchards.
