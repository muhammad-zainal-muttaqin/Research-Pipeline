Oil Palm Fresh Fruit Bunch Ripeness Classiﬁcation
Using Artiﬁcial Neural Network
Norasyikin Fadilah∗, Junita Mohamad Saleh∗, Haidi Ibrahim∗, Zaini Abdul Halim†
∗School of Electrical & Electronic Engineering, Universiti Sains Malaysia , 14300 Nibong Tebal, Pulau Pinang,
Malaysia
†Collaborative Microelectronic Design Excellent Centre, Universiti Sains Malaysia, 14300 Nibong Tebal, Pulau Pinang,
Malaysia
Abstract—This paper presents the ripeness classiﬁcation of oil
palm fresh fruit bunch (FFB) using artiﬁcial neural network
(ANN). ANN method was used to automate the decision of grading
oil plam FFBs, replacing the manual human grading method. A
total of 80 oil palm FFB samples from unripe, underripe, ripe
and overripe categories were collected. Images of oil palm FFB
were obtained using a color digital camera and their color was
analyzed using digital image processing techniques. Then the color
features were extracted from those images. These features were
used as the input parameters for ANN learning. The performance
of ANN was measured by testing the network with independent
test data. Results show that ANN was able to generalize four
ripeness categories of oil palm FFB.
I. INTRODUCTION
Oil palm fruit is one of the major agricultural products
particularly in Malaysia. The most common species is Elais
guineensis of nigrescens type. An oil palm fresh fruit bunch
(FFB) may contain up to 2000 fruits with an individual weight
of 3-30g and 2-5cm in size [1]. Its surface color varies from
deep violet to orange depending on its gene and ripeness stage.
The oil content for different stages of FFB ripeness also varies,
and it is generally stated as oil-to-bunch ratio [2]. As the oil
content of FFB is a function of its degree of ripeness [3], it is
crucial that the FFBs are harvested at the optimum ripeness.
Currently, the oil palm FFB is harvested manually by ob-
serving number of loose fruits. It is by counting either the
number of loose fruits on the ground before the FFB is cut
or the number of loose fruit sockets on the FFB [3]. The latter
is feasible for short trees since the harvester could clearly see
the FFB, while the former method is often used for tall trees.
This harvesting method may be inaccurate because loose fruits
might fall under a different tree, being stuck in the fronds,
washed away by heavy rain, or taken by animals in the oil
palm estate. The probability of harvesting FFBs which are not
ripe will increase. Moreover, this method is time consuming and
laborious, which could lead to higher harvesting and production
costs.
Besides loose fruits, the surface color of the fruits has been an
important indication of oil palm FFB ripeness. Malaysian Palm
Oil Board (MPOB) identiﬁed purplish black fruits as unripe,
reddish black as underripe, red as ripe, and reddish orange
as overripe [4]. Studies on correlation between the color of
oil palm fruits and their oil content reported that there was
a positive correlation between both attributes [5]–[7].Unripe
fruit has the lowest oil content, followed by underripe fruit.
Whereas, ripe fruit has the highest oil content and the oil
content deteriorates when the fruit reached overripe stage.
In detecting ripeness of oil palm FFBs using color vision,
color space selection is important to ensure accurate analysis.
RGB color space has been used widely in oil palm FFB
classiﬁcation [8]. However, this color space might not be the
best when analyzing images in a natural light environment
because as light intensity becomes higher, the RGB pixel values
increase [5]. Thus, another color space which is hue, saturation,
and intensity (HSI) color space was used in some studies.
Hue value was found to have no or less effect on variances
of lighting intensity [9]. Therefore, hue value is an important
attribute for detecting FFB color in any light intensity.
In developing automated ripeness classiﬁer, there were stud-
ies on application of intelligent systems. Neuro-fuzzy and fuzzy
logic were used previously to classify the ripeness of oil palm
FFB. Jamil et al. [10] trained RGB values of 45 FFB images
by using Hebb algorithm and fuzzy logic. This neuro-fuzzy
technique yielded 73.33% correct classiﬁcation. Meanwhile,
May and Amaran [11] developed an automated oil palm fruit
grading system using fuzzy logic algorithm, which resulted to
86.67% correct classiﬁcation. This fuzzy logic system could
only classify individual fruits in controlled lighting environ-
ment. More studies on other intelligent classiﬁcation techniques
could be investigated on oil palm FFB. Artiﬁcial neural network
(ANN) system for classiﬁcation is one of them.
ANN has been widely used to map input patterns with their
desired outputs. Its application is wide, ranging from data
classiﬁcation to data prediction to data visualization. No pre-
deﬁned rules needed to be set for an ANN, as it is able to
learn and generalize from ’experience’ or a set of presented
examples. In agricultural industry, ANN has been used to detect
ripeness, bruise or shape of agricultural products.
The objective of this work is to develop an algorithm
for automatic intelligent grading of oil palm FFB based on
color vision in a natural light environment. Hue distribution
was obtained from the image of oil palm FFB to indicate
the ripeness parameter. The application of ANN for ripeness
classiﬁcation based on hue distribution were investigated. The
aim was to ﬁnd the most optimum ANN architecture that could
classify four ripeness categories of oil palm FFB.

II. METHODOLOGY
The intelligent grading system consisted of a camera for
image acquisition, and a computer for data storage, image
processing and ANN system for classiﬁcation task. Image
processing and classiﬁcation tasks were developed using Matlab
R2011a. The methods involved in this work are discussed in
the following subsections.
A. Image Acquisition
Oil palm FFBs of DxP Yangambi planting material were
sourced from Felda Agricultural Services Sdn. Bhd. (FASSB).
A total of 80 FFBs were harvested and directly gathered in
an open space. A FASSB’s trained grade inspector segregated
the fruits into four ripeness categories: unripe, underripe, ripe
and overripe. A digital color camera was set 35cm away from
the FFB to capture an image. At most, four color images were
captured for each FFB at different areas of the bunch. Each
image was at the size of 480x640 in a 24-bit RGB format.
All the captured images were stored in a computer for further
processing. Then the images were randomly divided into 3 sets;
160 images were categorized as the training set, 28 images
were grouped as the validation set, and 120 images formed the
independent test set.
Four sample images from different ripeness categories are as
shown in Fig. 1. It can be seen that the unripe fruits are in deep
violet. As the fruits ripen, they turn red. The overripe oil palm
FBB shows that most of the outer fruits are gone, and the inner
fruits are orange in color.

hue values. After obtaining the hue values, a hue histogram was

D. Development of Ripeness Classiﬁer
In this work, the application of multilayer perceptron (MLP)
neural network, a commonly used ANN architecture was in-
vestigated. It is a three-layer ANN consisting of input layer,
hidden layer and output layer. Each layer comprises a number
of neurons, which are also known as processing elements (PE).
Fig. 3 illustrates the structure of a three-layer MLP architecture.
The number of inputs were represented by 59 hue measure-
ments obtained from features extraction task. All 59 values
were buffered through input layer of the MLP to be distributed
to the hidden layer. In the hidden layer, the number of hid-
den neurons was determined experimentally from the training
process of MLP classiﬁers. In the training process, a MLP
network repetitively updated the trained-weights from the input
and hidden layers after each training cycle to improve its
performance. In this work, a validation set was used to validate
MLP performance and terminate a training process when there
was no improvement in the validation performance.
In selecting an optimum MLP model, four combinations
of the selected transfer functions as listed in Table 1 were
applied to determine the suitable transfer functions for hidden
neurons and various numbers of output neurons. Logistic sig-
moid (logsig) and hyperbolic tangent sigmoid (tansig) transfer
functions were used. For all combinations, each output neuron
value was represented in binary, ’1’ or ’0’. The best-performed
MLP model was selected based on the highest classiﬁcation
accuracy of the independent test set.

III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The results of classiﬁcation accuracies for all CA-CD com-
binations are shown in Fig. 4. The MLP was trained with
one hidden neuron and added one by one untill 15 hidden
neurons. For the ﬁrst hidden neuron, the classiﬁcation accuracy
for each combination was the lowest. Moreover, CA and CB
combinations which used 2 output neurons had the lowest
accuracies than CC and CD combinations which used 2 output
neurons. It showed that by using only one hidden neuron, the
MLP did not generalize well. When the number of hidden
neurons increased, especially after the second and third hidden
neurons, the classiﬁcation accuracies increased. It showed that
MLP classiﬁer performed better with more than 3 hidden
neurons in the hidden layer.
Table II shows the results for MLP highest classiﬁcation
performance for each combination. CD combination produced
the highest classiﬁcation accuracy of 86.67%. This combina-
tion which implemented tangent sigmoid function for hidden
neurons and logistic sigmoid function for two output neurons
managed to classify the ripeness of oil palm FFB.
Fig. 5 illustrates the classiﬁcation accuracy for each ripeness
category for CD combination. Overripe FFBs has the highest
accuracy, whereas underripe FFBs has the lowest accuracy.
The underripe FFBs produced the lowest accuracy because
in the real situation, the human grader sometimes misclassify
underripe with either unripe or ripe. This intermediate ripeness
stage is hard to grade because the color can be close to either
unripe or ripe.
IV. CONCLUSION
In this work, the algorithm for the ripeness classiﬁcation of
oil palm FFB had been successfully developed. The perfor-
mance of MLP had been investigated for classiﬁcation purpose
by using hue measurements of the oil palm FFB’s images. 59
hue measurements were obtained for each image and these
values were used to characterize the ripeness of oil palm FFB.
