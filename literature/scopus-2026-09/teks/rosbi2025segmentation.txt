Abstract. The agriculture industries are switching to computerized crop recognition where robots or machines can 
execute crop sorting and harvesting procedures. The process of crop sorting could be performed faster by machines and 
computers compared to limited human labor. In the palm oil industry, an efficient palm fruit harvesting process is 
essential to ensure the high quality of oil, which can be achieved by harvesting the ripe fruit. The recognition of palm 
fruit ripeness using image processing needs a reliable segmentation technique to process only fruit image features with 
less noise. A segmentation technique using YOLO and Otsu method was explored in this work and the segmentation 
performance was assessed using an image classification algorithm. Color moments of the segmented images were 
inserted into a Gaussian Naïve Bayes for ripeness classification. The result shows that accuracy of up to 93% was 
achieved for the classification of 16 palm fruit images.  
INTRODUCTION 
A crop recognition technology is essential in current agriculture practice because it could perform a structured 
harvesting process. In a conventional oil palm Fresh Fruit Bunch (FFB) process, harvesters observe the external 
features of the FFB to determine the maturity of the fruit for oil extraction [1]. Currently, there is approximately a 
30% loss in yielding palm oil due to the unripe fruits harvested in the field [2]. The harvesting practices by humans 
also cause a loss in unharvested ripe bunches and uncollected loose fruits [3]. Due to that, the involvement of a 
computer vision system is important to reduce the yielding loss and increase the productivity in harvesting FFB. It is 
important to develop a machine vision system that could provide an automatic maturity recognition based on the 
fruit images.  
 
The processing of large and colored images could increase the computational size, hence, the image pre-
processing step is crucial in producing an efficient machine vision system. Segmentation is an image pre-processing 
step that is being applied to segment the images so that it is easier for the image analysis process. An unwanted part 
such as the background will be removed from the image to prevent unnecessary information. There are several 
studies performing threshold methods for removing the background in agriculture applications. The conservative 
method used by the scholars was histogram thresholding where the background could be observed by the 
distribution of the grayscale intensity in the image. A study in [4] develops an algorithm for thresholding in 3-D 
YCbCr color space using an elliptical boundary model to distinguish between pomelo fruit and the tree background. 
This is because the 2-D YCbCr Gaussian distribution does not show the obvious difference between foreground and 
background. A work [5] presented a segmentation method using improved segmentation in separating red tomatoes

The segmented image makes a huge change in the color detection process for FFB due to the complexity of the 
fruit’s shape. As stated by Jaffar et.al, there are distinct differences in red-green-blue (RGB) values for each 
maturity class for the segmented FFB image. In contrast, for the unsegmented image, the discrepancy in RGB value 
is not visible [6]. The threshold method is being utilized by Fahmi et.al by choosing a threshold of 0.2 to 0.7 for the 
saturation value of RGB in the FFB images [7]. Other than that, Septiarini et.al implemented the Otsu method and 
morphological process to segment the FFB fruitlets image after it was localized using Adobe Photoshop CS6. The 
data were then tested using a Support Vector Machine (SVM) and an accuracy of more than 90% was achieved. 
 
Thresholding is a simple and traditional technique that has been applied for various image applications, either 
indoor or outdoor images. As the FFB has spikes that are not needed during color extraction, the threshold method 
could remove the spikes using color observation. The thresholding methods were applied by scholars in segmenting 
a simple fruit shape with no spikes. Therefore, the proposed experiment could assess the performance of 
thresholding segmentation on complex-shaped fruit.  
 
In this paper, we proposed a fusion of classic and recent methods for image segmentation by combining You-
Only-Look-Once (YOLO) algorithm and the Otsu method. The rest of this paper is organized as follows: Section 2 
describes the methodology used in FFB image segmentation and Section 3 presents the result of our experiment. The 
performance evaluation of the proposed method also being presented in Section 3. Finally, Section 4 concludes the 
works that have been proposed in this paper. 
 
METHODOLOGY 
124 images of Tenera palm fruit were captured from a palm oil plantation in Malaysia, half of them were ripe 
and the rest were unripe fruits. MATLAB R2019b was utilized for this experiment. There are two steps for image 
segmentation using the proposed method, (a) fruit localization and (b) background removal. The proposed 
segmentation procedure were presented in Figure 1.  
 
FIGURE 1, Segmentation procedure 
 
(a) Fruit Localization 
The fruit localization process could ensure that the image has as minimal background as possible to reduce the 
errors during the thresholding process. YOLOv2 algorithm [8] was applied to detect the location of the FFB in an 
image. YOLO was first presented by Redmon et.al in 2015 [9] and it has been widely used in many image 
localization applications. A pre-trained ResNet-50 model was utilized for feature extraction while a Convolutional 
Neural Network (CNN) was applied for the object detection network. Figure 2 shows the architecture of the fruit 
localization process using the YOLOv2 detector.

For data training in the YOLO algorithm, 108 images were labeled with a region of interest (ROI) to annotate the 
ground truth location of the FFB in the image. The box label information was used to estimate the size of the anchor 
box. The anchor box is a default box that was used to predict the size of bounding boxes for palm fruit [10]. The 
image size was reduced to 224 × 224 × 3 for a faster processing time and the data augmentation was performed on 
the training data. Throughout the data augmentation process, the training images were randomly flipped on the x-
axis and scaled, while the annotated box also underwent the same process. Other than that, the color of the image 
was altered, therefore, three images could be generated from an FFB image in this stage. The training data were 
increased up to 432 images by applying the image augmentation procedure. During the testing stage, the input image 
was split into grids where each grid predicted the probability of the object contained in the bounding box. After the 
detection process, a final bounding box info was used for the cropping process and the FFB was cropped from the 
image to reduce some of the backgrounds.  
 (b) Background Removal 
In this stage, some part of the background has been removed, but the leaves, ground and fruit spikes could be 
observed in the image (Figure 3). Therefore, the color thresholding using the combination of two color spaces was 
tested. 8 images of ripe and unripe FFB were used for the segmentation process and further evaluated by the end of 
the experiment. The Otsu method [11] was implemented by iterating through all possible threshold values and 
finding a threshold value that could give the best separation between foreground and background. There will be a 
new value assigned for the new segmented image for every pixel on the original image. Pixel values that are higher 
than the threshold will be detected as foreground (Equation 1), thus it gives results in the binary image.

RESULT AND ANALYSIS 
Evaluation of FFB Localization Using YOLOv2 
From the results, the YOLOv2 algorithm could detect all FFB images and it shows good performance even 
though there were 432 training images. We evaluated the localization performance using the Intersection over Union 
(IoU) method. The predicted bounding box (𝐵) was compared against the ground truth bounding box (𝐴) created 
during the image labeling session. The IoU computation that approaches 1 indicates an excellent detection. The 
calculation of IoU could be described as the intersection area between 𝐴 and 𝐵 divided by the area of union between 
both (Equation 2). From the calculation, the average IoU for 16 images is 0.77 with 0.91 as the highest IoU value. 
The high IoU value supports that the proposed method using YOLOv2 is reliable for localizing FFB in the image.

The performance of the segmentation method could also be assessed by inserting the color features into a 
classifier. The fruits were classified into two classes, unripe and ripe. The first and second color moments were 
extracted from the segmented image and the Gaussian Naïve Bayes classifier was chosen for the segmentation 
analysis, as it is a simple statistical Bayesian technique. The same 16 images were trained and random testing was 
done by choosing the features and determining the probability of likelihood. An accuracy of 93.8% was achieved by 
using the proposed method on 16 test images. Only one unripe image was predicted as a ripe image and the others 
were detected correctly. We compared the proposed technique with a k-means clustering segmentation with 3 
clusters and the result was 68.8% accuracy. This is due to the presence of the ground and leaves in the image after 
being segmented using the clustering method. The proposed method results in lower error compared to the clustering 
method as presented in Figure 5.

CONCLUSION 
A segmentation method for FFB images was developed by combining the YOLO localization algorithm and the 
Otsu method. 432 images were trained for YOLO detection while 16 images consisting of ripe and unripe fruits 
were tested. The implementation of a two-stage threshold method could reduce the leaves and the background in the 
images. By extracting the first and second order of color moments, a classification accuracy of 93.8% could be 
achieved using a Gaussian Naïve Bayes. This method could be potentially extended by testing more datasets with 
varying lighting and background for an outdoor application. Other than that, the maturity class could be expanded 
with the addition of the under-ripe and over-ripe classes to ensure an accurate FFB recognition and classification. 
Finally, other possible work could be done by detecting the shape structure of the FFB using edge detection 
techniques such as Randomized Hough Transform (RHT) to increase the precision of the segmentation and reduce 
the missing important color features.
