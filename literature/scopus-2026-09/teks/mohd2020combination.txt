A B S T R A C T
The classiﬁcation of oil palm fresh fruit bunches (FFB) in relation to the maturity level is an important aspect to
determine the quality and productivity of the fruit. This study evaluated the utilization of combined computer
vision and laser-light backscattering imaging in determining the oil content and color changes of oil palm FFB at
diﬀerent maturity levels i.e. unripe, ripe, and overripe. Red-green-blue (RGB) images referring to the computer
vision and backscattering images of 90 oil palm FFB samples were acquired with 30 samples per each maturity
level. Standard reference methods for oil content and color values (L*, a*, and b*) were determined in relation to
the quality attributes of the oil palm FFB samples. Partial least squares (PLS) and principal component analysis
(PCA) were used to analyze the quality changes of oil palm FFB based on its maturity levels. Multivariate
algorithms such as linear discriminant analysis and quadratic discriminant analysis were applied to evaluate the
classiﬁcation performance based on the combined RGB and backscattering parameters. The combined optical
techniques showed a good coeﬃcient of determination (R2) of greater than 0.80 for both oil content and color
values. The average classiﬁcation accuracies were also higher than 85% in classifying oil palm FFB maturity.
Hence, this work has demonstrated that combined computer vision and backscattering imaging systems could be
useful as a non-destructive device for evaluating the classiﬁcation of oil palm FFB.
1. Introduction
Oil palm is one of the main agricultural crops in Malaysia that
produces various products including foodstuﬀs, cosmetics, biodiesels,
pharmaceutical products, etc. Malaysia was regarded as the second
biggest oil palm fruits producers worldwide with 100 million tons of
production in 2017 (FAOSTAT, 2019). Speciﬁcally, each oil palm fruit
is known as fresh fruit bunches (FFB) that has roughly around 2000
fruitlets which varies from dark purple to orange along with the ma-
turity levels. Despite having a large demand for the yield of oil palm
production, the maturity grading of oil palm FFB is still heavily rely on
the human assessment. Besides human assessment, the maturity level is
also being evaluated based on the standard laboratory analysis with
respect to the oil content and color changes of oil palm FFB. These
conventional methods are time-consuming and providing inconsistent
results that could aﬀect its quality. Previous studies demonstrated the
maturity of oil palm FFB was associated with the yield of oil palm
products by which the maximum FFB oil content can be obtained from
the ripe bunch (Ishak and Hudzari, 2010; Tan et al., 2010). Thus, it is
essential to evaluate the maturity level of oil palm FFB accordingly once
it arrived at the oil palm mill to maintain the quality and obtain the
maximum yield of palm oil production (Makky and Soni, 2014).
The surface color of oil palm FFB steadily changed along with ma-
turity levels as the fruit ripen because of the presence of carotenoids
and chlorophyll contents (Bensaeed et al., 2014). Numerous works have
been done towards the development of optical techniques for detecting
the maturity of oil palm FFB. It was possible to determine the FFB
maturity by evaluating the biochemical reaction since it was associated
with the color changes of the fruit. Besides, the unripe bunches tend to
have a high amount of chlorophyll whereas the overripe bunches had
higher carotenoid contents (Harun et al., 2013). The color changes and
biochemical reaction of the oil palm FFB can be detected by means of
non-destructive optical imaging techniques as well as machine vision-
based systems. These non-destructive techniques have shown the po-
tential of using optical imaging devices in the detection of oil palm FFB
maturity. Nevertheless, most of the current works were conducted
based on conventional methods by following standard reference ana-
lysis. An optical imaging technique oﬀers a rapid detection mechanism

that enables time-saving and reduction in production cost. Moreover,
the detection of oil palm FFB for the optimum maturity can avoid the
unnecessary harvest for the unripe FFB, thus allowing it to mature in

Laser-light backscattering imaging is one of the novel optical ima-
ging approaches that uses a laser diode as a light source for monitoring
food quality (Adebayo et al., 2016a; Lorente et al., 2015). The funda-
mental of laser-light backscattering imaging uses the principle gov-
erning light interactions with biological substances based on the se-
lected wavelength region. In a similar manner, the relationship between
the light properties and sample tissue delivers adequate information
about the structure and chemical content of the material. Several re-
searchers had studied the application of laser-light backscattering
imaging in monitoring the quality attributes of agricultural produce
including watermelons (Mohd Ali et al., 2017), potatoes (Babazadeh
et al., 2016; Geng et al., 2019), bananas (Adebayo et al., 2016b; Hashim
et al., 2018; Zulkiﬂi et al., 2019), pears (Zude-Sasse et al., 2019), and
plums (Rezaei Kalaj et al., 2016). According to Mollazade et al. (2012),
this technique was more rapid and cheaper compared to other optical
imaging methods since it does not need any spectral or correction
analysis despite dealing with wavelength selection for the laser beam.
The data processing using various multivariate algorithms only took a
few seconds to be completed for a single sample and very few minutes
for batch-wise. Apart from that, the laser-light backscattering imaging
system has also been recognized to be very practical and delivered a
fundamental study on establishing an automated system for the de-
termination of oil palm FFB maturity.
Computer vision referring to the Red-green-blue (RGB) imaging is a
non-destructive and rapid tool which was widely used in food quality
detection and grading operation of agricultural products (Taghizadeh
et al., 2011). Computer vision had been utilized in various tasks and
inspection including defect detection, variety classiﬁcation, ripeness,
grading, as well as quality attributes of diﬀerent agricultural crops
(Brosnan and Sun, 2004; Hashim et al., 2012; Hussain et al., 2018; Wu
and Sun, 2013; Zhang et al., 2018). The main advantage of computer
vision is the fast processing of image acquisition and the low cost of the
instrumentation system. For example, Garrido-Novell et al. (2012) in-
vestigated the utilization of RGB imaging in evaluating the color evo-
lution of apples according to the diﬀerent storage conditions. In the
same vein, Hashim et al. (2013) applied the RGB imaging for the de-
tection of chilling injury in bananas which gave a signiﬁcant correlation
between the color space and ripening stages of the fruit. The ﬁndings
had proven that the computer vision oﬀered a prospective future to
overcome the problems arise in the conventional method, speciﬁcally in
terms of monitoring quality attributes and maturity detection of oil
palm FFB.
Although there are several studies have been investigated on the
utilization of computer vision and laser-light backscattering imaging,
the combination between those systems have not been thoroughly ex-
amined. Exclusively, computer vision incorporated with the laser-light
backscattering imaging provided a robust assessment and enhanced the
system competence (Mohd Ali et al., 2019). Additionally, the combined
system is cost-eﬀective due to the involvement of light scattering and
RGB images which are acquired from the same imaging unit without
extra sampling preparation. There are several optical parameters in-
cluded for the combination of the system in the model development and
food quality detection that must be considered in order to meet the
commercial needs. To date, no adequate research has been conducted
on the utilization of combined computer vision and laser-light back-
scattering imaging to determine the oil palm FFB maturity. As oil palm
is one of the important agricultural crops in the world, the ﬁndings
from this study could help the palm oil mill industries to improve their
production in-line with industrial revolution 4.0. Hence, the aim of this
work was to assess the combination of using computer vision in-
corporated with the laser-light backscattering imaging for determining
the oil content and color values of oil palm FFB in relation to the
maturity levels.
2. Materials and methods
2.1. Samples preparation
The oil palm FFB can be classiﬁed into three maturity levels; unripe,
ripe, and overripe based on the standards established by the Malaysian
Palm Oil Board (MPOB). The maturity of oil palm FFB is basically
evaluated by the number of loose fruits in a bunch. Unripe bunch was
identiﬁed when neither of the fruitlets detached, 10 or more fruitlets
detached from the bunch is a ripe bunch, and overripe is deﬁned when
40 or more fruitlets detached from the bunch. A total of 90 oil palm FFB
samples of DXP GH 500 Golden Hope comprised of 30 FFB from each

2.2. Oil content and color measurements
The oil content of each oil palm FFB samples was measured after
conducting the image acquisition process. After harvest, the FFB sam-
ples were boiled to deactivate the reaction of lipase for 24 h. The
fruitlets were detached from the bunch and sliced to separate from the
mesocarp. For this study, three fruitlets were used from the same bunch
with three replications in order to determine the oil content. The oil
content of oil palm FFB was determined using Soxhlet method on a wet
weight basis at each maturity level. The FFB samples were then
weighed and dried to remove excess water. After that, Soxhlet extractor
was used to extract the mesocarp oil from the FFB samples with hexane
as the chemical solvent. The residue of ﬁber and oil solution in the
thimble was dehydrated and cooled in the desiccator. The mesocarp oil
content (% oil content) of oil palm FFB was calculated using Eq. (1):

Color values (L*, a*, and b*) of oil palm FFB were measured by
using a handheld colorimeter (NR20XE, Shenzhen 3nh Technology Co.
Ltd., China). The colorimeter was calibrated with a standard white re-
ference before conducting the color measurements. Each FFB sample
was cleaned and placed on the sample holder to get a ﬁxed position.
The aperture of the colorimeter was placed perpendicular to the surface
of the FFB sample at the same location as the image acquisition spot.
Since the color of oil palm FFB was not uniform, the color measure-
ments were performed at three diﬀerent locations from the top, middle,
and bottom of each bunch. The color values were then determined
based on the average values of three replications.
2.3. Combination of computer vision and laser-light backscattering imaging
system
An optical imaging system comprised of a charge-coupled device
(CCD) camera (QICAM Colour Fast 1394, QImaging, Surrey, BC,
Canada) with zooming lens of 5.6 and focal length of 18 mm, two
ﬂuorescent lamps (HFB RB 218, Natural Daylight, TC – L18W,
Germany), a laser diode emitting at 658 nm with 30 mW maximal
power, and a computer equipped with an imaging software (Image-Pro
Insight 9 software, Media Cybernetics, Inc., USA) for capturing both
RGB and backscattering images (Fig. 1). For the RGB images, the

acquired for each FFB samples, creating in 270 of RGB and back-
scattering images, respectively.
2.4. RGB image analysis
The RGB images were segmented using an image processing method
developed in MATLAB software (Version R2016a, The Mathworks Inc.,
Natick, MA, USA). Image segmentation and pre-processing are im-
portant in enhancing the quality image and separating the background
from the region of interest. The RGB images of oil palm FFB samples
were segmented using a thresholding method in which the optimal
threshold value was determined for the image background removal in
order to obtain region of interest. Filtering process was applied to
eliminate the background noise. Mask operation was performed to re-
trieve the original color of segmented image which then converted to a
binary image for the feature extraction based on the RGB parameters.
The RGB parameters extracted from the images were red color space
(Red), green color space (Green), and blue color space (Blue).
2.5. Backscattering image analysis
The backscattering images were pre-processed using image proces-
sing method developed in MATLAB software (Version R2016a, The
Mathworks Inc., Natick, MA, USA). After the backscattering images
were converted to grayscale images, a Gaussian ﬁlter was applied to
obtain a smooth image. The backscattering algorithm was developed to
measure the backscattering parameters based on the image regions as
described by Lockman et al. (2019). The average values from the se-
lected backscattering parameters were calculated by performing image
segmentation to select the region of interest. From the image histogram,
the inﬂection point was obtained and used to convert the backscattering
image to a binary image. A threshold value was determined from the
histogram of backscattering images in order to separate the background
from the region of interest. It was assumed that no correction method
was needed for the backscattering images since the size of the light
beam is relatively small. The feature extraction was extracted from the
region of interest of backscattering images based on the pixel intensities
known as backscattering parameters. The selected backscattering
parameters were major axis length, minor axis length, mean intensity,
and perimeter. The major axis length and the minor axis length in-
dicated the length of the major axis and minor axis within the ellipse
which has similar normalized second central moments as the image,
respectively. The mean intensity indicated the mean intensity value,
while the perimeter calculated the distance between each adjoining pair
of pixels around the backscattering image region. The RGB and back-
scattering images of oil palm FFB at diﬀerent maturity levels are shown
in Fig. 2.

Analysis of variance (ANOVA) was carried out to calculate the sig-

predict the quality attributes of oil palm FFB were evaluated based on
the coeﬃcient of determination (R2), root mean square error of cali-
bration (RMSEC), and root mean square error of prediction (RMSEP).

Principal component analysis (PCA) was used to obtain the classi-
ﬁcation representation between the maturity levels of oil palm FFB. The
clustering performance was determined using the Unscrambler software
(Version 10.5, CAMO AS, Oslo, Norway) with the ratio of training to
testing dataset (70:30). Linear discriminant analysis (LDA) and quad-
ratic discriminant analysis (QDA) were used to classify the oil palm FFB
maturity levels based on both RGB and backscattering parameters. The
discrimination functions were determined from the input variables that
were used to evaluate the classiﬁcation accuracies between the groups.
The groups of classiﬁcation accuracies were then calculated from a
confusion matrix. Both LDA and QDA were determined in the SAS
software (Version 9.4, SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA) in which the
principal component scores were randomized using the combined da-
tasets. The average classiﬁcation accuracies were determined by cal-
culating the average of ﬁve readings from each maturity level.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Quality changes of FFB at diﬀerent maturity
Table 1 shows the quality attributes of color values (L*, a*, and b*)
and oil content of oil palm FFB at three diﬀerent maturity levels. A
signiﬁcant diﬀerent (p < 0.05) was obtained in color values of oil
palm FFB between the diﬀerent maturity levels. The value of L* in-
creased from unripe to ripe stages before declining to 40.20 at the
overripe group. A rise in a* value was also found from 16.76 to 32.37
for unripe and ripe groups, respectively. The a* value for the overripe
group was slightly decreased (32.10). Similarly, the value of b* in-
creased from unripe to ripe stages before decreasing to 31.06 at the
overripe stage. The red color on the surface of oil palm FFB was asso-
ciated with carotenoid contents which is known as red pigmentation.
Besides, the color of oil palm FFB changes to light color as it ripens
because of the decrease in the chlorophyll content. As mentioned by
Utom et al. (2018), they found that the unripe group had the highest
content of chlorophyll followed by ripe and overripe groups which
associated with the absorption of light during the maturity process of
the fruit. Theoretically, chlorophyll can be denoted as a photoreceptor
since it is capable to absorb light at the wavelength in the visible region
of 400 to 700 nm. Hence, it can be seen that carotenoids and chlor-
ophyll contents were considered as the major role in the determination
of oil palm FFB maturity.
On the other hand, the oil content of oil palm FFB gradually in-
creased from unripe (15.21%), followed by ripe (38.98%) and overripe
(41.22%) groups. A similar trend was observed for the oil content with
respect to L*a*b* values ranged from 6.46 to 68.13% (Sunilkumar and
Sparjan Babu, 2013). In another study, Tan et al. (2010) investigated
the correlation between the oil content of oil palm FFB according to the
maturity which was represented in terms of hue peak from the RGB
images. The study indicated that the determination of oil content in FFB
might be viable using color imaging if it was exposed with the similar
sunlight condition due to the high carotenoid pigment at an early stage
of the oil palm maturity. Likewise, the oil content was determined ac-
cordingly to the three diﬀerent maturity levels by means of data fea-
tures extracted from the RGB images including several parameters (hue,
saturation, intensity) which correlated with the laboratory analysis
(Makky, 2016).
The ANOVA results showed the means of RGB and backscattering
parameters of oil palm FFB; Red, Green, Blue, major axis length, minor
axis length, mean intensity, and area between three diﬀerent maturity
levels (unripe, ripe, and overripe) as tabulated in Table 2. All para-
meters were greatly inﬂuenced by maturity levels at P < 0.05. The
highest means recorded for Red and Green with the values of 0.70 and
0.59 were inﬂuenced by the overripe group, respectively. Blue para-
meter (0.45) was found to be inﬂuenced by the ripe group. Likewise,
the parameters that were most aﬀected within the ripe group were
major axis length (156.99) and minor axis length (141.47), respec-
tively. On the contrary, mean intensity was aﬀected by the unripe group
with the mean values of 106.23, whereas perimeter (481.68) was af-
fected by the ripe group. Generally, it was observed that the RGB and
backscattering parameters (Red, Green, Blue, major axis length, minor
axis length, mean intensity, and area) were signiﬁcantly aﬀected by the
diﬀerent maturity levels of oil palm FFB.

Regression models of oil content and color values of oil palm FFB
based on the combination of the backscattering parameter are tabulated
in Table 4. Practically, both oil content and color values of oil palm FFB
indicated that the increment in R2 values as the combination of back-
scattering parameters in the regression models increased. The best
grouping of backscattering parameters was obtained by the combina-
tion of major axis length, minor axis length, mean intensity, and peri-
meter for oil content, L*, and b* values. The oil content, a*, and b*
values achieved R2 values of 0.82, 0.92, and 0.93, respectively. In
contrast, the combination of the major axis length, minor axis length,
and mean intensity values gave the highest R2 of 0.88 for the L* values.
The high R2 values indicated that these quality attributes of oil palm
FFB were inﬂuenced by the light scattering upon photon transmittance
within the fruit tissues using the combination of backscattering para-
meters. The results were compared with those obtained by Zulkiﬂi et al.
(2019) who presented a high correlation (R2 > 0.70) between the
color values in bananas based on the selected combination of back-
scattering parameters. From those results, the best single parameter for
determining color pigments of bananas was the mean intensity which
presumed that the formation of new pigments including carotenoids
and anthocyanins in the fruit.
3.3. Correlation between FFB quality and optical properties
The correlation between the combined imaging parameters and
quality attributes of oil palm FFB is presented in Table 5. The results
demonstrated the strong correlation of oil content and color values of
oil palm FFB that can be used to determine the features based on the
RGB and backscattering images. A high correlation of r > 0.50 was
achieved for all backscattering parameters; major axis length, minor
axis length, mean intensity, and area with respect to both oil content
and color values of oil palm FFB. However, L* values were not sig-
niﬁcant with all RGB parameters; Red, Green, and Blue. The oil content
of oil palm FFB was positively correlated to the mean intensity
(r = 0.822). This trend was in agreement with the results of Tan et al.
(2010) in which the correlation with oil content and RGB parameter
obtained a correlation coeﬃcient of 0.793. In a similar trend, L* values
also positively correlated to the mean intensity (r = 0.798). Among the
oil content and color values, only a* values showed a strong correlation
to the RGB parameter, i.e. Green (r = 0.776). Meanwhile, b* values
recorded the highest correlation with the Blue (r = 0.953). These ob-
servations showed the discrepancy in both RGB and backscattering
parameters that could be explained by the formation of pigmentation
throughout the maturity process of oil palm FFB. In this case, the
combined imaging parameters were feasible in determining the quality
attributes of oil palm FFB.

3.5. Prediction of FFB quality
The calibration and prediction models of oil content and color va-
lues of oil palm FFB based on the computer vision parameters, back-
scattering imaging parameters, and the combination of both imaging
parameters are shown in Table 6. The PLS statistical indicators con-
sisting of RMSEC, RMSEP, and R2 were calculated to evaluate the
predictive performance of the imaging system. The calibration models
obtained R2 values of 0.56, 0.78, 0.87, and 0.88 as well as RMSEC
values of 2.56, 1.12, 3.78, and 2.59 for predicting the oil content of oil
palm FFB using RGB parameters, respectively. Conversely, the predic-
tion models obtained R2 values of 0.57, 0.81, 0.88, and 0.90 as well as
RMSEP values of 4.28, 1.17, 3.74, and 2.75 for predicting the oil con-
tent of oil palm FFB using RGB parameters, respectively. Based on the
backscattering parameters, the calibration models achieved the values
for oil content (R2 = 0.59, RMSEC = 8.48), L* (R2 = 0.82,
RMSEC = 3.92), a* (R2 = 0.85, RMSEC = 3.29), and b* (R2 = 0.98,
RMSEC = 2.51), respectively. Meanwhile, the prediction models ob-
tained the values for oil content (R2 = 0.79, RMSEP = 6.58), L*
(R2 = 0.86, RMSEP = 3.15), a* (R2 = 0.86, RMSEP = 3.18), and b*
(R2 = 0.99, RMSEP = 0.46), respectively.
The combination of computer vision and laser-backscattering
parameters were used to improve the robustness and accuracy of the
PLS models, generating the calibration and prediction models of quality
attributes of oil palm FFB. The calibration models achieved the values
for oil content (R2 = 0.75, RMSEC = 2.56), L* (R2 = 0.89,
RMSEC = 4.23), a* (R2 = 0.92, RMSEC = 0.85), and b* (R2 = 0.95,
RMSEC = 1.47), respectively. The prediction models obtained the va-
lues for oil content (R2 = 0.80, RMSEP = 4.37), L* (R2 = 0.91,
RMSEP = 2.68), a* (R2 = 0.90, RMSEP = 1.53), and b* (R2 = 0.93,
RMSEP = 3.48), respectively. The combination of computer vision and
laser-light backscattering imaging presented good results in predicting
the oil content and color values of oil palm FFB as compared to the
prediction results using computer vision and backscattering parameters,
individually. In other words, the combined imaging parameters were
found to be reliable to predict the oil content and color values of oil
palm FFB with R2 > 0.80.
In perspective, the accumulation of oil content inside the oil palm
FFB stopped upon the fruit is cut from the tree. The prediction models
observed that there was a signiﬁcant correlation between the oil con-
tent with the chlorophyll and carotenoid pigments in the fruits. Makky
and Soni (2014) revealed that the variation of carotenoids in the oil
palm FFB resulted in the oil formation during the ripening of the fruit.

a good correlation of color evaluation and moisture content of sweet
potatoes with R2 values higher than 0.70 in which the models showed a

imaging to classify apple maturity under diﬀerent storage conditions.
Similar behavior was obtained by Romano et al. (2012) in predicting
changes in moisture using digital camera combined with the laser
diodes during bell pepper drying. The implementation of the combi-
nation of these techniques could deliver a strong fundamental for the
recent trends in the non-destructive application of other agricultural
crops. The current technology towards providing on-line monitoring in
the ﬁeld is recommended considering the fact that the technique is
promoting fast and low processing time. Moreover, it is also proposed
that the eﬃciency of this method to be tested over a wide range of crops
to enhance the processing rate using a real-time system. Apart from
several internal properties including moisture content, acidity, and
soluble solids content, the internal defect detection at an early stage is
necessary for evaluating the overall quality inspection of agricultural
crops. Hence, further work in enhancing the model development are
considered for the new alternative in combining computer vision and
laser-light backscattering imaging to be recognized in the commercial
line.
4. Conclusions
The combined optical techniques of computer vision and laser-light
backscattering imaging system signiﬁes a novel invention for mon-
itoring quality changes of oil palm FFB in relation to the maturity le-
vels. The prediction results showed R2 > 0.80 for oil content and color
changes (L*, a*, and b*) of oil palm FFB using the combined optical
methods. Based on the classiﬁcation results, the utilization of computer
vision and laser-light backscattering imaging system oﬀered a pro-
mising tool to classify the oil palm FFB quality according to the ma-
turity levels with the average classiﬁcation accuracies greater than
85%. Nevertheless, the ﬁndings presented a great promise of laser-light
backscattering imaging system compared to the computer vision in such
a way that it demonstrated much better prediction and classiﬁcation
accuracies. In conclusion, the combined optical methods could deliver
robust information in the invention of the non-destructive tool, espe-
cially in the postharvest technology and food processing industries.
Further research in assessing discriminative potential from diﬀerent
cultivars and conditions of oil palm FFB as well as improving the ro-
bustness of the predictive model is also needed for future investment
towards implementing the system into a real-time situation.
