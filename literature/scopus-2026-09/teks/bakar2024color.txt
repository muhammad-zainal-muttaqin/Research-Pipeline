Abstract— Proper ripeness categorization of oil palm fresh 
fruit bunches is essential to ensure they are harvested at the 
optimal stage to maximize oil output. The current manual method 
of ripeness assessment, which relies on harvesters visually 
inspecting the fruit’s color, is both labor-intensive and prone to 
human error. To address these limitations, there is a growing need 
for an automated classification system that leverages artificial 
intelligence. The manual classification process is cumbersome and 
susceptible to inaccuracies due to its reliance on subjective visual 
inspection. This labor-intensive approach increases the likelihood 
of human error, underscoring the necessity for a more reliable and 
efficient automated solution. Thus, this study aims to evaluate the 
feasibility of using Convolutional Neural Networks for automating 
the grading based on color. A dataset was compiled from various 
sources and augmented using a data generator. The model was 
constructed incorporating a transfer learning approach with an 
Xception model. The model achieved an overall accuracy of 
80.4%. Visualization of the model using activation maps 
confirmed that the model can effectively classify the color, 
indicating its potential viability for the task.

I. INTRODUCTION 
Ripeness classification of oil palm fresh fruit bunches 
(FFBs) is essential for optimal oil yield, achievable by 
harvesting at the right time [1]. Palm oil demand is anticipated 
to grow due to its use in various products, though much of the 
harvesting still relies on manual labor [2][3]. As the largest 
global producer, Malaysia has unique technologies for high-
quality palm oil production, with research focusing on 
cultivation, harvesting methods, and therapeutic properties [4]. 
Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are advanced tools 
in computer vision, particularly for object identification due to 
their high recognition accuracy. Although CNNs require 
extensive training data, transfer learning with pre-trained 
models helps address this need [5]. Key steps in CNN-based 
image classification include determining a classification system, 
feature extraction, selecting training samples, image pre-
processing, choosing an appropriate classification method, post-
classification processing, and accuracy assessment [6]. CNNs 
are the preferred model for image categorization, making them 
suitable for classifying FFB ripeness. 
The oil palm industry is a major contributor to Malaysia's 
GDP, supported by high exports and production, as shown in Fig 
1. Initially, the Malaysian government promoted oil palm 
cultivation to alleviate poverty, with the Federal Land

plantations in the 1960s. Current research in the oil palm sector 
emphasizes innovative approaches to cultivation, revenue 
generation, and high-quality product development from palm 
oil.

II. RELATED WORKS 
A. A Simple and Efficient Deep Learning-Based Framework 
for Automatic Fruit Recognition 
This study presents a straightforward deep convolutional 
neural network (CNN) for fruit and vegetable classification, 
targeting 20 categories with data augmentation techniques [7]. 
The model, with three convolutional and two fully connected 
layers, processes input without pre-processing and achieved 
high accuracy on real-world images under varied conditions like 
lighting and pose. Training lasted 20 epochs, with dropout 
layers, data augmentation, and hyper parameter tuning to 
combat overfitting, reaching a 96% detection accuracy. 
However, the model performs sub optimally when trained on 
one dataset and tested on another, which future research aims to 
address through dataset expansion and domain adaptation. 
B. Improved Classification Approach for Fruits and Vegetable 
Freshness Based on Deep Learning 
This research enhances fruit and vegetable classification 
using a deep learning (DL) model with spatial pyramid pooling 
(SPP), feature pyramid networks (FPN), and path aggregation 
network (PAN) modules, promoting healthy eating and reducing 
misclassification [8]. The CNN was trained on a high-
performance PC with an extensive dataset to distinguish fresh 
from decayed produce. Designed for use in the food industry and 
as an aid for visually impaired individuals, the model classifies 
produce under varied conditions, though it struggles with small, 
overlapping objects and similar textures among fruits and 
vegetables. 
C. Identification, Classification & Grading of Fruits Using 
Machine Learning & Computer Intelligence: A Review 
A review of machine learning techniques for fruit 
classification highlights applications like retail checkouts and 
quality inspections, utilizing preprocessing, feature extraction, 
and dimension reduction with PCA [9]. Techniques include 
Support Vector Machines (SVMs) and Random Forests, 
achieving accuracies over 91% for guava and lemon 
classification. This research underscores the potential of CNNs 
with data augmentation to automate grading based on color, 
specifically for oil palm fresh fruit bunches. 
Thus, this study aims to integrate data augmentation and 
evaluate the feasibility of using Convolutional Neural Networks 
to automate the grading of oil palm fresh fruit bunches based on 
color. 
III. METHODOLOGY 
In the following section, the essential steps to process the 
images and build the model using neural networks are being 
explained in addition to the deployment of the model. 
A. Computational Platform 
This work utilizes Google Colab and TensorFlow as primary 
platforms. Google Colab, a collaborative tool from Google 
Research, enables users to run Python code remotely, making it 
suitable for machine learning and data analysis, with free access 
to resources like GPUs. TensorFlow, integrated within Colab, 
uses data flow graphs and tensors for efficient data management 
and distribution across GPU clusters, supporting model design 
and training [10]. 
B. Database Acquisition 
The data, which consists of pictures of FFB, was collected 
from various internet sources such as Kaggle [11], along with 
self-taken photos. The collected data was divided into three 
classes: Class 0 (unripe), Class 1 (under ripe), and Class 2 (ripe). 
Classification was based on the FFB color: black (Fig. 3, left) 
indicates unripe, major redness (Fig. 3, middle) indicates under-
ripe, and major orange (Fig. 3, right) indicates ripe. Experts were 
asked to classify the self-taken images into these three 
categories. 
 
Fig. 3. Unripe FFB, Under ripe FFB and Ripe FFB 
C. Image Pre-Processing  
 
The images of the fresh fruit bunches were gathered, and 
various pre-processing techniques were applied to create a 
dataset with high accuracy. One technique used is image 
augmentation, which involves expanding the data by adding 
slightly altered copies of existing data or generating synthetic 
data from it. This technique acts as a regularizer during machine 
learning model training and helps to minimize overfitting. 
Additionally, the data was divided into two subsets: one for

D. Model Architecture 
 
To create the model architecture, transfer learning was 
utilized, beginning with the first input layer, which includes 
information about the input size. Next, the layers of the Xception 
model are incorporated, indicating that this is a transfer learning 
model because a previously trained model is being used. Finally, 
additional layers are added to accommodate the number of 
classes—in this case, three. Initially, a flatten layer was included 
because the layers following the convolutional neural network 
are classifiers that accept only one-dimensional data. 
E. Model Training and Testing 
 
To train the model, the Adam optimizer was used because it 
generally produces superior results compared to other 
optimization techniques, as it has a shorter computation time and 
requires the adjustment of fewer parameters [12]. Additionally, 
categorical cross-entropy was used as the loss function, which is 
suitable for multi-class classification. The number of epochs was 
set to 150, and the batch size was set to 32. 
F. Model Visualization 
 
The first method used for visually representing the outputs 
of intermediate convolution layers involves processing training 
images through several convolutional layers. During the training 
of a deep network, the network is continually provided with 
images of various objects. These images are further processed 
by multiple convolutional layers, each applying the convolution 
operator to generate new images as output. The second method 
to be used is Grad-CAM. Grad-CAM creates a coarse 
localization map that highlights key areas in an image for 
concept prediction by using the gradients of a target concept (in 
this case, palm fruit FFB in a classification network) flowing 
into the final convolutional layer. 
G. GUI creation 
 
The final step was to create a GUI that provided the needed 
data. The GUI was developed using the PYQT5 library in 
PyCharm. This was convenient for the project, as PYQT5 is a 
Python library, eliminating the need for an additional program 
to test the model evaluation.

V. CONCLUSION 
The research aimed to develop a method for segregating oil 
palm fresh fruit bunches (FFB) based on ripeness. The study had 
three main objectives: to identify ripeness characteristics of 
palm fruit, to build a machine learning model for classification 
based on these characteristics, and to evaluate the model's 
accuracy. 
Color was chosen as the key classification characteristic, as 
it varies noticeably between ripeness stages: unripe FFBs are 
predominantly black, underripe FFBs are red, and ripe FFBs are 
orange. The dataset included images from various online sources 
and self-taken photos, classified into these three stages. 
The model was built using TensorFlow, with data pre-
processing and subclassing completed before constructing the 
model architecture. Transfer learning was applied, utilizing the 
pre-trained Xception model. The model was then trained on the 
classified dataset. 
Evaluation involved plotting accuracy and loss functions 
during training, along with a confusion matrix to assess class-
specific accuracy. Additional visualization methods were used 
to analyze the model, and comparisons were made between the 
optimized and original models for inference speed and accuracy. 
A graphical user interface (GUI) was developed to allow 
users to classify oil palm FFBs and view classification results. 
Despite limitations in data, the project was deemed successful, 
with satisfactory results. For future work, more data will be 
collected in cooperation with relevant agencies, and alternative 
models to Xception, as well as image enhancement methods 
such as Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization 
(CLAHE), will be explored to improve model performance.
