Automated Ripeness Detection of Oil Palm Fruit  
Using a Hybrid GLCM-HSV-KNN Model 
Mirfan1, Billy Eden William Asrul2, Mila Jumarlis3, Juliani4 
1,2,4 Department of Computer Science, Faculty of Computer Science, Handayani University Makassar, Makassar, Indonesia

Abstract   
Accurately determining the ripeness of oil palm fruit is crucial for ensuring the quality of palm oil. However, traditional manual 
methods are often time-consuming and less accurate. This study aimed to develop an automated system for detecting the 
ripeness of oil palm fruit by combining the Hue Saturation Value (HSV) model, Gray Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM), 
and K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN) algorithms. This system utilizes K-Nearest Neighbors to classify the relationship between color 
features extracted using the HSV model and texture features derived from GLCM analysis to categorize fruit ripeness. The 
color features represent the fruit's chromatic characteristics associated with ripeness, while the texture features provide 
information regarding surface patterns related to ripeness. The color features represent the fruit's color characteristics 
associated with ripeness, whereas the texture features provide information about the surface patterns related to ripeness. The 
results indicate that the system can classify oil palm fruit into four distinct categories: Over-Ripe, Ripe, Half-Ripe, and Raw. 
The dataset was divided with an 80:20 ratio, where 80% was allocated for training data and the remaining 20% for test data. 
An accuracy rate of 85% was achieved. The results of this study demonstrate that the developed system effectively classifies oil 
palm fruit images based on ripeness levels. This system supports a sustainable automated palm oil production model through 
accurate ripeness detection, thereby reducing reliance on manual methods and enhancing consistency and productivity in palm 
oil processing. These findings indicate that the proposed hybrid method is feasible for integration into an automated 
classification system to support decision-making in oil palm harvesting.  
Keywords: HSV: GLCM; KNN; image processing; oil palm fruit ripeness

1. Introduction  
Oil palm (Elaeis guineensis Jacq) is one of Indonesia's 
leading commodities and plays a strategic role in the 
national economy, particularly in supplying raw 
materials for the palm oil industry (CPO – Crude Palm 
Oil). The quality of the produced palm oil is influenced 
by several factors, including moisture content, impurity 
levels, free fatty acid content, and the ripeness level of 
the oil palm fruit at the time of harvest. Among these 
factors, the ripeness level is one of the most crucial 
variables that determine the quality and quantity of the 
oil produced [1]. 
Several companies in the palm oil industry apply a 
selection or sorting process for fresh fruit bunches 
(FFB) as an initial stage before processing [2]. This 
sorting is generally performed manually, relying on 
visual inspection of color and texture by field workers 
to determine fruit ripeness. Although widely practiced, 
this manual method has notable limitations. It depends 
heavily on the subjective judgment of workers, is prone 
to fatigue-related errors due to repetitive tasks, and is 
time-consuming, especially across large plantation 
areas[3]. Misjudging the ripeness stage—whether 
unripe, under-ripe, ripe, or overripe—can significantly 
affect both the quality and quantity of the palm oil 
produced. Such inaccuracies reduce oil yield and 
impact the chemical and physical characteristics of the 
final product [4]. As a result, there is a growing need for 
more objective and efficient sorting methods. 
Technological solutions involving image processing 
and artificial intelligence offer promising alternatives, 
enabling faster, more consistent, and more accurate 
classification of fruit maturity levels  [5]. As technology

Several studies have explored the classification of oil 
palm fruit ripeness using digital image analysis, 
employing various approaches for both feature 
extraction and classification [8] - [10]. One such study 
was conducted, which detected the ripeness level of 
Fresh Fruit Bunches (FFB) of oil palm based on color 
composition using the K-Nearest Neighbor (K-NN) 
algorithm. The system achieved an accuracy rate of 
92% under controlled indoor lighting conditions but 
saw a decrease in accuracy to 84% when images were 
taken outdoors under unstable natural lighting. This 
indicates that lighting plays a critical role in 
determining the accuracy of classification systems.

In previous studies, a color-based approach was 
commonly employed, such as feature extraction using 
RGB or HSV combined with classification methods like 
Naïve Bayes, Decision Tree, and Support Vector 
Machine (SVM). While color-based approaches are 
generally effective in differentiating ripeness stages 
based on visible surface characteristics, they are often 
limited in robustness when faced with variations in 
lighting conditions or inconsistent image backgrounds, 
which 
can 
significantly 
affect 
classification 
accuracy.[13] -  [15]. 
To address these limitations, recent studies have begun 
incorporating texture features as additional parameters 
to enhance classification performance. Methods such as 
the Gray Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM), Local 
Binary Pattern (LBP), and Haralick Texture Features 
have been utilized to extract surface information that is 
not captured by color features alone. Research 
combining both texture and color features has 
demonstrated improved performance compared to 
single-feature approaches, as this integration enables a 
more comprehensive representation of the complex 
visual changes that occur during the ripening process of 
oil palm fruits [16]. 
In terms of classification algorithms, SVM and 
Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) have been widely 
adopted. SVM is particularly effective in handling high-
dimensional and non-linear data but requires intensive 
parameter tuning and a relatively complex training 
process. ANN, on the other hand, offers the ability to 
learn deep, non-linear representations but typically 
demands large training datasets and considerable 
computational resources[17]. In contrast, this study 
adopts the K-Nearest Neighbors (K-NN) algorithm as 
the classification method due to its simplicity, 
efficiency with medium-sized datasets, and its ability to 
achieve competitive performance without the need for 
complex training procedures. K-NN operates based on 
the proximity of features in a multidimensional space, 
making it well-suited for the simultaneous integration 
of both color and texture information.[18], [19]. 
HSV (Hue, Saturation, Value) color model is a color 
representation that separates hue, color saturation, and 
brightness components, making it more aligned with 
human visual perception compared to the RGB model. 
In the context of oil palm fruit ripeness identification, 
the skin color changes during the ripening process from 
green, yellow, orange, to deep red can be quantitatively 
captured through the Hue (H) component[20]. Hue 
reflects the dominant color present on the fruit’s surface 
and serves as a primary indicator of ripeness level. 
Meanwhile, Saturation (S) represents the color 
intensity, where ripe fruits typically exhibit more vivid 
and saturated colors than unripe ones. [21]The Value 
(V) component denotes brightness, with ripe fruits 
generally appearing brighter due to surface changes that 
influence light reflection. For feature extraction 
purposes, the palm fruit images are converted from the 
RGB color space to HSV, and the average values or 
histograms of each HSV component are computed to 
serve as numerical features in the classification process. 
In addition to color transformation, ripeness level can 
also be characterized by surface texture variations. 
Therefore, texture analysis plays a significant role in 
digital 
image 
processing 
for 
fruit 
maturity 
classification. One of the most widely used statistical 
methods for texture feature extraction is the Gray Level 
Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM) [22]. GLCM calculates 
the frequency of pixel pairs with specific gray levels at 
defined distances and orientations within a grayscale 
image, generating a matrix that captures the spatial 
distribution of texture patterns. Several statistical 
descriptors can be derived from the GLCM, including: 
Contrast, which measures the local intensity variation 
and highlights differences between adjacent pixels; 
Correlation, which quantifies the linear dependency 
between neighboring pixels and reflects textural 
regularity; Energy (Angular Second Moment), which 
indicates image uniformity and compactness of texture; 
and Homogeneity, which reflects how close the 
distribution of elements is to the GLCM diagonal, 
indicating local smoothness of the image. Feature 
extraction is performed across multiple directions (0°, 
45°, 90°, and 135°) and pixel distances to 
comprehensively capture textural characteristics. These 
features are then utilized as input for the classification 
of oil palm fruit ripeness levels [23], [24]

extraction using Hue, Saturation, Value (HSV) and 
texture features using the Gray Level Co-occurrence

This study proposes a novel approach to oil palm fruit 
ripeness classification through a multi-feature digital 
image processing system that integrates both color and 
texture characteristics. The main novelty lies in the 
system's ability to classify fruits into four ripeness 
categories: unripe, half-ripe, ripe, and overripe—unlike 
previous studies that were typically limited to only two 
or three categories [28]. The inclusion of half-ripe and 
overripe categories is a significant distinguishing aspect 
as it enables more accurate detection of the ripeness 
transition phase. This is crucial in selective harvesting 
practices, 
where 
misclassification 
during 
the

prematurely or too late. 
The system employs the HSV color model to capture 
more representative and lighting-robust color gradients, 
and extracts texture features using the GLCM method 
to identify visual differences such as surface wrinkling 
or roughness. These combined features are classified 
using the K-Nearest Neighbor (K-NN) algorithm, 
which demonstrates a high capability in handling 
multidimensional features with strong performance on 
limited data [29]With this approach, the study makes a 
tangible contribution to the development of a more 
precise, objective, and applicable oil palm fruit ripeness 
classification system, supporting optimized harvesting 
automation in plantation industries. 
Accurate classification of oil palm fruit ripeness brings 
significant benefits in enhancing the effectiveness and 
productivity of the palm oil industry. Proper ripeness 
identification 
allows 
for 
selective 
and 
timely 
harvesting, which can increase oil yield and reduce 
losses due to harvesting fruits that are either unripe or 
overripe.  Fruits harvested at optimal ripeness produce 
higher oil content with better quality and lower free 
fatty acid (FFA) levels, which negatively impact oil 
quality.  Furthermore, the implementation of an image-
processing-based automated classification system can 
reduce reliance on manual labor, increase assessment 
objectivity, and accelerate field inspection processes. 
Therefore, this technology plays a vital role in realizing 
sustainable precision agriculture practices in the oil 
palm plantation sector. 
2. Methods 
The research method aims to develop a classification 
system for the ripeness level of oil palm fruit based on 
digital image processing, utilizing a combination of 
color and texture feature extraction techniques as the 
primary approach in image analysis. This system is 
developed through several integrated stages, including 
data 
acquisition, 
image 
preprocessing, 
feature 
extraction, classification using the K-Nearest Neighbor 
(K-NN) algorithm, and system performance evaluation. 
2.1 Data Acquisition 
The dataset used in this study comprises oil palm fruit 
images collected directly from plantations located in 
West Sulawesi, Indonesia. Image acquisition was 
carried out using a high-resolution digital camera, 
specifically the Canon EOS 700D with an 18-megapixel 
sensor, under semi-controlled lighting conditions to 
ensure consistent image quality. The shooting distance 
and angle were standardized to minimize inter-sample 
variability, and all images were captured against a 
neutral background to facilitate segmentation and 
subsequent preprocessing steps. Dataset was divided 
with an 80:20 ratio, where 80% was allocated for 
training data and the remaining 20% for test data. The 
labeling process was conducted manually through 
visual observation by experienced plantation experts, 
who possess practical knowledge in assessing oil palm 
fruit ripeness. The ripeness levels were categorized into 
four distinct classes: unripe, half-ripe, ripe, and 
overripe, based on visual features such as skin color and 
surface texture. Labeling was performed in accordance 
with 
commonly 
accepted 
industrial 
harvesting 
standards, and to ensure label consistency, each image 
was reviewed by at least two experts, with discrepancies 
resolved through discussion and consensus. In total, the 
dataset consists of 600 images, evenly distributed 
across the four ripeness categories, with 150 images per 
class. The dataset was further split into 80% for training 
and 20% for testing to maintain class balance and 
prevent bias during the classification process. To ensure 
the reliability and reproducibility of the labeling 
process, each sample was cross-validated by multiple 
experts. 
This 
expert-driven 
and 
standard-based 
approach ensures that the dataset annotations are 
consistent, traceable, and applicable to real-world 
agricultural practices. This approach ensures the 
reliability and reproducibility of the labeling process.

2.2 Image Preprocessing 
After the images are obtained, preprocessing is 
performed to enhance image quality and standardize the 
format before the feature extraction process. The 
preprocessing steps include cropping, resizing, and 
color conversion. Image cropping process is intended to 
isolate and extract only the region of interest (ROI) that 
contains the primary object—in this case, the oil palm 
fruit—so that it becomes the sole focus of subsequent 
analysis. This step helps eliminate irrelevant elements 
such as background, leaves, stems, or even the 
harvester’s hand, thereby allowing the system to 
concentrate on the most significant visual features. 
Cropping can be performed either manually or 
automatically. In manual cropping, the operator selects 
and trims the fruit region directly using software tools 
such as LabelImg or OpenCV-based applications. This 
approach is suitable for small datasets or during the 
initial labeling phase. In contrast, automatic cropping 
typically involves image segmentation techniques such 
as background subtraction, color thresholding (e.g., 
targeting reddish or orange hues indicative of ripeness), 
or contour detection to identify object boundaries. Once 
the fruit region is detected, a bounding box is generated 
to define the cropping area. The resulting cropped 
image is then resized to a fixed dimension, typically 
128×128 pixels, to ensure uniformity across all dataset 
samples. This process not only enhances computational 
efficiency and processing speed but also significantly 
improves the accuracy of feature extraction—both in 
terms of color (HSV) and texture (GLCM)—as it 
focuses exclusively on the relevant region. Consistent 
and precise cropping contributes to the overall 
robustness 
and 
optimal 
performance 
of 
the 
classification system by minimizing visual disturbances 
from the surrounding environment.  
The resizing process of oil palm fruit images, which 
involves altering the original image dimensions, must 
be conducted carefully, as it can result in the loss of 
critical visual information that directly affects the 
accuracy of image-based classification systems. When 
images are resized to smaller dimensions, fine visual 
details such as surface texture, color gradients, and 
small object contours may be lost due to pixel merging 
or elimination. This issue is particularly crucial in the 
classification of oil palm fruit ripeness, where both 
color and texture serve as primary indicators.

2.3 Feature Extraction 
Feature extraction is performed to obtain the color and 
texture characteristics of oil palm fruit images. HSV 
(Hue, Saturation, Value) and GLCM (Gray Level Co-
occurrence Matrix) are two widely used feature 
extraction methods in digital image processing, each 
capturing distinct aspects of an object’s visual 
representation. HSV is a color model that separates 
visual information into three components: Hue, which 
represents the dominant color (e.g., red or green); 
Saturation, which measures the intensity or purity of the 
color; and Value, which indicates brightness. In the 
context of fruit ripeness analysis, HSV is particularly 
useful, as changes in the skin color of oil palm fruits 
throughout the ripening process—from green to yellow, 
orange, 
and 
deep 
red—can 
be 
quantitatively 
represented through these components. HSV features 
offer a clear visual indicator of maturity stages but are 
sensitive to lighting variations and background 
inconsistencies. 
On the other hand, GLCM is a statistical method for 
extracting texture features from grayscale images by 
calculating the frequency of co-occurring pixel 
intensity pairs at specific distances and orientations. 
From the resulting co-occurrence matrix, several 
texture descriptors can be derived, including contrast 
(measuring local intensity variation), correlation 
(assessing the linear relationship between neighboring 
pixels), 
energy 
or 
Angular 
Second 
Moment 
(representing texture uniformity), and homogeneity 
(indicating the closeness of the pixel distribution to the 
diagonal of the matrix).[30] Unlike HSV, which focuses 
on color-based visual appearance, GLCM provides 
insight into the surface structure and patterns of the 
object—such as roughness or smoothness—which also 
evolve as the fruit matures. 
The fundamental difference between HSV and GLCM 
lies in the type of information they extract. HSV 
emphasizes color and visual cues that align with human 
perception, while GLCM captures spatial patterns in 
pixel 
intensity 
that 
reflect 
surface 
texture 
characteristics. HSV is generally more sensitive to 
lighting conditions, whereas GLCM tends to be more 
robust in such environments. The integration of these 
two feature types allows a classification system to 
comprehensively represent fruit characteristics, as both 
color and texture serve as crucial indicators in 
determining the ripeness level of oil palm fruits. which 
are used to recognize the texture patterns of oil palm 
fruit at various ripeness levels.  
2.4 K-Nearest 
Neighbor 
(K-NN) 
Classification 
Algorithm 
The data obtained from feature extraction is classified 
using the K-Nearest Neighbor (K-NN) algorithm, 
which works by comparing the similarity between test 
data and training data based on Euclidean distance. This 
study uses variations of K = 3, K = 5, and K = 7 to 
determine the optimal parameter. The distance 
calculation between features is performed using the 
Euclidean Distance formula.  
K-Nearest Neighbor (K-NN) was selected in this study 
for its simplicity and efficiency in handling medium-
sized 
datasets. 
It 
can 
effectively 
combine 
multidimensional features from HSV and GLCM 
without requiring complex training. However, its 
performance depends heavily on the choice of K, where 
too small a value may cause sensitivity to noise, while 
too large a value may reduce class distinction. 
2.5 System Performance Evaluation 
The classification system evaluation is performed using 
a Confusion Matrix, which measures the accuracy, 
precision, and recall of the system's predictions [29]. 
Accuracy is calculated by comparing the number of 
correct predictions to the total test data, while precision 
and recall are used to assess the system's accuracy and 
sensitivity in classifying the ripeness levels of oil palm 
fruit. Figure 6.  illustrates the system architecture of this 
study, which consists of two main phases: the training 
phase and the testing phase, designed to extract and 
classify visual information from palm fruit images 
using a combination of color and texture features. In the 
training phase, the system receives a collection of palm 
fruit images as input. These images undergo a 
preprocessing stage that includes resizing, color 
normalization, noise removal, and conversion to HSV 
or grayscale color spaces to ensure consistent quality 
and 
format 
for 
feature 
extraction. 
Following 
preprocessing, the system performs two parallel feature 
extraction processes. The first utilizes the HSV (Hue, 
Saturation, Value) method to extract dominant color 
attributes, including hue, saturation, and brightness. 
Hue captures the transition in skin color during ripening 
(e.g., from green to deep red), saturation measures the 
intensity of the color—which typically increases with 
maturity—and value represents brightness, which may 
vary depending on surface texture and reflectance. The 
second extraction applies the Gray Level Co-
occurrence Matrix (GLCM) method to obtain texture 
features from grayscale images. GLCM analyzes the 
spatial relationships of pixel intensities by computing 
the frequency of gray-level co-occurrence at specified 
distances and orientations, producing statistical 
descriptors such as contrast, correlation, energy, and 
homogeneity, which represent surface roughness, 
texture regularity, and uniformity.

The extracted color and texture features are then fused

classification model to be used in the testing phase. 
In the testing phase, the system receives new images as 
test data. Identical preprocessing steps are applied to 
maintain feature consistency. The HSV and GLCM

a fruit by identifying the majority class among the k

The strength of this approach lies in the integration of 
complementary color and texture features and the use of 
K-NN, a lightweight, non-parametric classifier that  
does not require intensive training. Since K-NN relies 
solely on distance-based similarity, it is well-suited for 
numerical data derived from combined feature sets.

4. Conclusions 
Based on the results of the research conducted, it can be 
concluded that the hybrid model method combining 
Gray Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM), Hue 
Saturation Value (HSV), and K-Nearest Neighbor (K-
NN) algorithms is effective in classifying the ripeness 
levels of oil palm fruit. The classification results 
indicate that the K=3 parameter in the K-NN algorithm 
yields the best performance, achieving an accuracy of 
85%, precision of 85%, and recall of 88%, 
outperforming other values of K (K=5 and K=7). The 
HSV method successfully extracts informative color 
features (Hue, Saturation, and Value), which are critical 
in determining the fruit’s ripeness. In parallel, the 
GLCM method provides texture features (Contrast, 
Correlation, Energy, Homogeneity) that effectively 
differentiate each ripeness category. The use of the K-
NN algorithm as the classification method has proven 
to be reliable in categorizing oil palm fruit into four 
ripeness levels: Overripe, Ripe, Half-Ripe, and Unripe. 
The results of the conducted tests demonstrate that this 
system enhances harvesting precision and supports 
sustainable palm oil production by automating ripeness 
detection with high accuracy, thereby reducing 
dependence on manual methods and improving 
consistency and productivity in palm oil processing. 
These findings suggest that the proposed hybrid method
