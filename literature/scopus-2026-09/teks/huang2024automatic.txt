A B S T R A C T  
Automatic tracking of litchi fruit abscission provides important information for the orchard management. 
Currently, the prevalent method for tracking litchi fruit abscission involves manual selection of branches fol­
lowed by daily fruit counting, a process deemed both inefficient and subjective. Therefore, this paper proposed a 
novel approach that integrated an improved image matching and object detection algorithms to intelligently 
analyze litchi fruit abscission of branches in the orchard. The method consisted of three steps. Firstly, an 
automatic fixed-point focus system was developed to capture long-term image sequences of selected branch 
areas. From April 27th to June 21st, 2022, the system autonomously navigated to specified locations and 
automatically adjusted the focal length to capture daily images of selected branches, thereby forming image time 
series is crucial for analysis. Secondly, to reduce fruit counting errors due to interferences from other branches, 
an improved SuperGlue algorithm named SE2-SuperGlue was developed to match branches and extract the re­
gion of interest of the target branch (ROI-B) from the image time series. The SE2-SuperGlue algorithm introduced 
an equivariant steerable CNN that is equivariant to translations and image rotations, rendering it robust to ro­
tations and ensuring accurate matching amid changes in branch angles. Thirdly, the ROI-B sequences were input 
into the YOLOv8n to detect litchi fruits, yielding tracking results of litchi fruit abscission on the image time 
series. Experimental results showed the good performance of the proposed method. The SE2-SuperGlue network 
achieved higher matching score (0.53) and a speed of 177 ms/frame compared to conventional matching al­
gorithms, successfully extracting the ROI-B sequence. Additionally, YOLOv8n achieved an mAP@0.5 of 98.7 %, 
and the correlation coefficient R2 between prediction and ground truth for automatic tracking of daily fruit count 
over a 56-day image time series of selected branches was 0.98. Furthermore, the study analyzed a continuous 56- 
day image sequence depicting relative fruit abscission for 80 branches of three varieties (Guiwei, Nuomici, and 
Huaizhi), along with the peaks of fruit abscission.   
1. Introduction 
China boasts the largest cultivation area and highest litchi output 
globally, making it a pivotal player in the litchi industry. Litchi serves as 
a significant economic crop in southern China, where it enjoys wide­
spread cultivation (Qi et al., 2019). Despite its prominence, litchi yields 
remain suboptimal, largely due to the prevalent occurrence of litchi fruit 
abscission. It has been widely documented that litchi fruits undergo 
three distinct peak periods of abscission prior to maturation, each 
attributed to varying factors, which contributes to the inconsistent and 
often low litchi yield, as well as the occurrence of alternating “on” and 
“off” years (Li et al, 2019; Zhao et al., 2020). The study of litchi fruit 
abscission is crucial for industry management and market forecasting, 
providing essential information for farmers, businesses, and the gov­
ernment. This, in turn, promotes the stable development of the litchi 
market. Moreover, it holds significant implications for regulating seed 
and fruit production, as well as enhancing breeding and harvesting 
practices (Estornell et al., 2013; Yi et al., 2023). The fast and accurate 
monitoring of litchi fruit abscission dynamics facilitates the timely 
implementation of fruit preservation strategies, thereby contributing to

branches, which are then tagged. Subsequently, from the third week

tagged branches is manually counted every seven days to analyze the 
relative fruit abscission rate (Fan et al., 2015; Yan et al., 2019). This is

constrained by factors such as orchard environment, canopy structure,

Currently, deep learning has been widely applied in the field of fruit 
detection and counting (Gao et al, 2020; Wu et al, 2021; Wang et al, 
2022). These detection algorithms can be broadly categorized into two 
types: region proposal-based target detection methods, such as Mask R- 
CNN (He et al., 2017) and Faster R-CNN (Ren et al., 2017), and 
regression-based target detection, such as SDD (Liu et al., 2016) and the 
YOLO series (Redmon et al., 2018; Jocher et al., 2021; Wang et al., 
2023). In contrast, regression-based target detection algorithms have 
faster detection speeds while ensuring detection accuracy, and are more 
suitable for agricultural applications. Among them, single-stage net­
works, represented by YOLO (You Only Look Once), hold a significant 
advantage in agricultural detection scenarios due to their rapid detec­
tion speed and superior accuracy. Liu et al. (2020) replaced the tradi­
tional rectangular bounding box of the YOLOv3 model with a circular 
one for tomato detection, achieving a 96 % mean average precision 
(mAP) and a detection speed of 0.054 s on a 3648 × 2056 pixel image. 
Lyu et al. (2022) proposed a lightweight citrus detection network, 
YOLOv5-CS (Citrus Sort), to achieve accurate detection and counting of 
green citrus in natural environments, with a mAP of 98.23 %. Wang et al. 
(2022) detected ripe litchi in the orchard based on an improved YOLOv5 
model, and the improved model’s mAP presented a 3.5 % improvement. 
In summary, existing studies on fruit detection and counting have 
mainly focused on targets with colors that are highly distinguishable 
from the background such as tomatoes (Das et al., 2020), oil palms (Ali 
et al., 2020) and citrus fruits (Chen et al., 2022). 
However, the precise counting and tracking of litchi fruit abscission 
of branches still encounter two major challenges. Firstly, while 
capturing images on a large scale and counting can be used for statistical 
analysis of litchi fruit abscission, the relatively small size of litchi targets 
in the large-scale images, coupled with the similarity in color between 
the green litchi fruit and the surrounding area, often lead to lower

handcrafted descriptors, leading to high computational requirements

Overall, this study introduces an innovative method for the auto­
mated tracking of litchi fruit abscission rates on target branches in or­
chards, through the integration of object detection and image matching 
techniques. Firstly, a fixed-point and fixed-focus capture system was 
developed for autonomous long-term (56 days) tracking and imaging of 
target branches. Secondly, the improved SuperGlue algorithm was 
employed for image matching of branches to obtain the sequences of 
ROI-B from the image time series of target branches. To address the 
matching algorithm’s sensitivity to angle changes, an equivariant 
steerable CNN, invariant to translations and rotations, was introduced to 
enhance the SuperGlue algorithm’s robustness to rotations. Finally, the 
sequence of ROI-B were input into the YOLOv8 object detection network 
to generate long-term sequential statistics on litchi fruit abscission. This 
research can provide reliable foundational data for fruit preservation, 
fertilization, and other aspects of orchard management.

2.1. Image acquisition using fixed-point focus system 
The RGB images used in this study were captured at the SCAU Main 
Campus Teaching & Research Base located in Guangzhou, Guangdong 
Province, China (23◦1′58.29′′N, 113◦3′59.93′′E) (See Fig. 2). The pano­

the experimental orchard, the spacing between rows of fruit trees

Nuomici, and Huaizhi, with Guiwei trees approximately double in 
number compared to Nuomici and Huaizhi varieties, respectively. The 
height and canopy size of each fruit tree within the orchard exhibit 
variation. To monitor the growth of litchi and obtain experimental im­

30 m at the farthest. For automated fixed-point and fixed-focus image

2.4. Improved image matching network for ROI-B sequence extraction 
The flowchart of the SuperGlue algorithm for litchi branch image 
matching is shown in Fig. 8. The algorithm primarily comprises two 
components: Inputting a pair of images with certain similarity, utilizing 
an improved SuperPoint network for feature extraction (detection and

the homography matrix is calculated, and finally, the ROI-B is obtained.

The equivariant steerable CNN composes of a number of steerable 
convolutional layers consisting of multiple steerable filters, as illustrated 
in Fig. 9 (Li et al., 2020). This steerable CNN has a steering parameter θ 
that can be used to make it sensitive to the parts that have orientation θ. 
The steerable filters are obtained as a linear combination from a basis B1, 
B2, …, Bk, C1, C2, …, Ck and F, and can be rotated to any angle θ by a 
simple re-weighting of the basis. The response maps of each layer for any 
angle θ can be obtained by convolution with linear combination of all 
filters and weight vector w(θ). The Steerable CNN for this task consists of 
4 steerable convolutional layers. The third steerable convolutional layer 
is followed by ReLU activation, and the last one returns the response. We 
used the feature extraction method of e2cnn in backbone to solve the 
problem that SuperGlue is sensitive to angle changes. 
2.4.2. SuperPoint features extraction 
The growth of litchi branches, along with factors like rain and wind, 
causes changes in branch morphology and angle. SuperPoint’s feature 
extraction relies on convolutional operations, which are highly sensitive 
to angle changes. This sensitivity significantly impacts the accuracy of 
image matching and poses challenges in accurately extracting the ROI-B 
from the branch image time series. To address this issue, this research 
proposes an improved SuperGlue algorithm using equivariant steerable 
CNNs to enhance its robustness to rotations.

firstly uses a VGG-Style (Simonyan and Zisserman, 2014) encoder 
downsize the image and extract features. This encoder section comprises 
a convolutional layer, a Max-Pooling layer and a non-linear activation 
layer. We substituted the CNN backbone of the encoder with equivariant 
steerable CNNs (Weiler et al., 2019), and called the modified models

2.4.3. SuperGlue features matching 
SuperGlue is a network designed to execute feature matching and 
outlier removal simultaneously. Feature matching is achieved by solving 
a differentiable optimal transport problem, and the loss function is 
constructed using a Graph Neural Network (GNN). The SuperGlue 
network, depicted in Fig. 11, consists of two main modules: the 
attention-based GNN (AGNN) and the optimal matching layer (Sarlin 
et al., 2020). AGNN maps the coordinates of the keypoints p and the 
visual descriptors d obtained through the SuperPoint network into a 
vector f (feature matching vector) using a keypoint encoder, which 
utilizes convolution to obtain local feature points. The feature matching 
performance of vector f is enhanced through Self-attention and Cross- 
attention (repeated L = 7 times). 
Subsequently, the feature matching descriptors fA

matrix Sij, which is used to calculate the similarity between the de­

As designed, both the GNN and the optimal matching layer in Su­
perGlue are differentiable, enabling backpropagation from matching to 
visual descriptors. SuperGlue is trained in a supervised manner from the 
ground truth matches M = {(i, j)} ⊂ A × B, which are estimated using 
real relative transformations. If the keypoint I ⊆A and J ⊆B have no 
reprojections in each other’s neighborhoods, they are labeled as non- 
matches and the negative logarithmic likelihood of the assignment 
matrix P is minimized. 
2.4.4. ROI-B acquisition 
Once the position information of matched keypoints is obtained from 
the SuperGlue network, a robust RANSAC-based method is employed to 
calculate the projective transformation matrix H between imgA and 
imgB. The input, m1 and m2 for imgA and imgB, respectively, are two 
corresponding sequences of keypoint coordinates, where each pair of 
data corresponds to one another. These sequences contain both correct 
matches, referred to as inliers, and incorrect matches, referred to as 
outliers. The RANSAC method aims to separate the correct matches from 
the data that includes incorrect matches and estimate the homography 
matrix H. Subsequently, the matrix H is used to extract ROI-B based on 
Perspective Transformation. 
2.4.5. ROI sequence of target branches 
Based on the survey, on April 27th, 2022, the Guiwei, Nuomici, and 
Huaizhi varieties were at 17th, 17th, and 18th day after flowering, 
respectively. This paper selected April 27th as the first day of fruit 
abscission. The high variability of the litchi growth process leaded to 
more pronounced differences in branch morphology over a 56-day 
period. According to calculations, the ROI-B area reached around 
31.6 %~70.2 % of original image area. For each branch, images of 
branches on the 10th, 28th, and 46th days were selected and manually 
cropped out of the ROI-B for image matching, respectively. Specifically, 
the algorithm matches the image from the 10th day with the images 
captured on days 1–19, the image from the 28th day with the images 
captured on days 20–35, and the image from the 46th day with the 
images captured on days 36–56. 
2.5. Litchi fruit detection network 
As the latest version of the YOLO series, YOLOv8 inherits the ad­
vantages of previous versions and further optimizes speed and accuracy. 
It performs well on various benchmark tests and datasets. YOLOv8 
provides a user-friendly API and documentation, making it easier for 
developers to integrate and use the model. In addition, the YOLO series 
has always been popular as it is easy to train and tune features. The 
YOLOv8 model consists of four parts: Input, Backbone, Neck, and Head. 
The input layer adopts the mosaic data augmentation method and in­
corporates adaptive adjustment functions for anchors and images. In the 
backbone section, YOLOv8 largely mirrors YOLOv5, but replaces the C3 
module with the C2f module. Drawing inspiration from the ELAN 
concept in YOLOv7, the C2f module combines C3 and ELAN, enhancing 
YOLOv8′s ability to capture comprehensive gradient flow information.

bottom-up and top-down pathways, it facilitates information aggrega­
tion, allowing each level to capture more detailed features and 
improving the model’s object detection capability across various scales. 
Significant changes in the Head component include transitioning from 
the original decoupled head to the coupled head, and from Anchor Based 
in YOLOv5 to Anchor Free. YOLOv8 adopts CIOU and Distribution Focal 
Loss as the loss function for bounding box regression.

speed, YOLOv8n is chosen in this study. The YOLOv8 network is shown

3. Experimental results and analysis 
3.1. Experiment platform 
The training environment used in this study includs Ubuntu 18.04, 
64-bit Linux operating system, 32 GB RAM, and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 
TITAN graphics card. The PyTorch-based code was employed to train 
the SE2-SuperGlue and YOLOv8 models from scratch using pre-trained 
weights provided by the SuperGlue and YOLOv8 official repositories. 
The SE2-SuperGlue network was trained and its effectiveness was 
verified on the test set. An input image size of 640 × 480 was chosen for 
the model, with Adam serving as the optimizer and an initial learning 
rate set to 0.0001. The keypoint confidence threshold was set to 0.01, 
with an NMS radius of 8, a matching threshold of 0.3, and 50 Sinkhorn 
iterations. The model was trained for 10 epochs, with a default batch 
size of 1. The weights of convolutional layers of the equivariant Steer­
able CNN were initialized from the normal distribution with standard 
deviation 0.01. For the equivariant Steerable CNN, we started with a 
learning rate of 1 ⋅ 10−6. After every epoch the learning rate was 
multiplied by 0.8. Pretrained weights were used to train the model from 
scratch, which took approximately 120 h. Codes are available at https 
://github.com/sunset326/SE2-SuperGlue. 
This study trained the YOLOv8 network, utilizing a 640 × 640 input 
size for the model. Adam was employed as the optimizer with an initial 
learning rate of 0.01 and a weight decay of 0.0005. The model under­
went training for 300 epochs with a batch size of 16.

4. Discussion 
A novel and robust method for automatic tracking the litchi fruit 
abscission rate in complex natural orchard environments was proposed 
in this study. An image matching method based on SuperGlue was 
employed to determine the region of a single branch and extract the ROI- 
B from the time series images. The morphology of the same branch 
exhibited similarities over a period of growth, which justified the use of 
image matching. However, branches may undergo movements and ro­
tations due to factors such as wind or growth-induced bending, neces­
sitating enhancements to the SuperGlue algorithm. We tackled this 
problem by replacing the backbone CNN with a steerable CNN, ensuring 
equivariance to translations and rotations, contributing to a more robust 
model named SE2-SuperGlue. Considering the significant morphological 
changes of branches over the 56-day period, we divided the process into 
three distinct time periods for image matching. Although image 
matching successfully identified the ROI-B, instances occurred where 
other branches entered into this ROI-B, causing errors (See Fig. 20 a). 
This research employed YOLOv8 to detect litchi fruits, achieving 
optimal accuracy, inference speed, and model size compared to existing 
object detection algorithms. However, there are also limitations (See 
Fig. 19). One limitation is misidentifying leaves as litchi due to their 
similar shapes, as is indicated by the yellow circle in Fig. 19 (a). The 
second limitation arises from the dense clustering of litchi fruits on the 
tree, which leads to occlusion of some fruit, as is highlighted by the 
yellow circle in Fig. 19 (b). 
Utilizing the aforementioned methods, the automatic tracking and 
statistical analysis of litchi fruit abscission were conducted. As are 
depicted in Fig. 17 and Fig. 18, the fruit abscission rate of the three 
varieties is severe, with an increasing trend during fruit development, 
consistent with previous research on litchi fruit abscission (Fan et al., 
2015; Cai et al., 2017). During the fruit abscission peak period (See 
Fig. 18), monitoring the phenological status of litchi fruits can guide the 
implementation of specific fruit preservation measures to stabilize litchi 
yield. The mean value of daily fruit set for the three varieties does not 
exhibit a continuous decrease over time (See Fig. 17), which can be 
attributed to the factors illustrated in Fig. 20. The first scenario arises 
from the close interconnections between branches during growth, 
leading to the inclusion of adjacent branches during image matching, as 
is depicted in the yellow circle in Fig. 20 (a). The second scenario is due 
to the substantial morphological changes in leaves and branches 
throughout growth, which may lead to fruit occlusion by leaves, as is 
shown in the yellow circle in Fig. 20 (b). 
5. Conclusions 
This paper introduced a novel approach that integrated our 
improved image matching and object detection for the automated 
tracking litchi fruit abscission in natural environments. A fixed-point 
and fixed-focus data acquisition system was developed for long-term 
tracking and imaging of litchi branch in the orchard. The improved 
SuperGlue network was employed to match branches and extract the 
ROI-B of the target branch from the time series images. Experimental 
results demonstrated that the SE2-SuperGlue network exhibited greater 
robustness to rotations and outperformed traditional feature matching 
algorithms in terms of matching scores, while also maintaining a 
powerful inference speed, with a score of 0.53 and an inference speed of 
177 ms/frame, respectively. YOLOv8 achieved an mAP@0.5 score of 
98.7 % on the test set. The mAP results suggested that YOLOv8 model 
surpassed YOLOv3-tiny, YOLOXs, Faster R-CNN, and YOLOv5s. More­
over, the fruit abscission on a total of 80 branches for three litchi
