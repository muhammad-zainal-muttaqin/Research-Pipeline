harvesting operation

Abstract 
Oil palm fresh fruit bunches (FFB) need to be harvested at the optimum maturity stage to optimise the quality of palm oil. Currently the oil palm 
harvester manually determines the FFB maturity based natural indicators such as FFB color appearance and number of FFB loose fruit drops under 
the tree. During executing the harvesting operation the harvester need to search for a ripe FFB and at the same time carrying the harvesting pole. 
Tedious harvesting operation degrades the consistency of their judgment. The harvested FFB must be screened at the oil palm mill to separate into 
groups of maturity level according to the standard. A computer application called Maturity Table was developed to manage the FFB maturity 
information in form of digital images. The development of FFB from the anthesis to harvesting stage was monitored by using a handy digital camera. 
The digital images of the FFB were processed by using digital image processing techniques to extract the color that represent the maturity stages. As 
a result from the analysed image information and tabulated data in Maturity Table, a relationship of FFB color changing and maturity stages were 
investigated. The processed information also enables the development of a map displaying the location of FFB and the maturity stages. The 
developed map can be used as a support system in harvesting operation. This support system enables site specific harvesting at optimum maturity 
stage, overcome losses due uncollected loose fruit, potential application to eliminate FFB screening process at oil palm mill level and potential tool 
to predict FFB yield as well as plantation resources management. 
Key words: Harvesting support system, site specific harvesting, Maturity Table, oil palm yield. 
Introduction 
Oil palm (Elaeis guineensis Jacq var. Tenera) is one of the most 
important plantation crops in Malaysia. Oil palm fresh fruit bunch 
(FFB)  harvesting and collection form a single largest direct cost 
in the production of oil palm 1, 2. FFB yield is a common parameter 
to measure the productivity and mostly result verified and 
confirmed at post harvesting level. Harvesting oil palm FFB at 
right stage of ripeness is critical to ensuring optimum quality and 
quantity of oil production and thus profitability of the industry 3. 
In the standard operating procedure, matured FFB is highly 
related with numbers of loose fruits drops under the oil palm tree. 
This is used as a sign to harvest the FFB and has a drawback 
because uncollected loose fruits are one of the factors contributed 
to the losses in Malaysian oil palm industries. Sime Darby 
Plantation, one of the biggest oil palm plantation in Malaysia, 
reported for every uncollected loose fruit, the company will 
potentially lose almost RM11 million in annually income 5. A new 
kind of monitoring system is required to determine maturity stages 
of oil palm to avoid losses due to loose fruit 6. 
Various techniques have been developed to measure the 
maturity stages of FFB. Two parameters commonly used to 
measure the maturity of FFB are color, fruitlets moisture and oil 
content. FFB maturity stage and location are very important 
information. Farmer or plantation manager can use this information 
to manage their workers, machines and other agricultural resources 
to increase productivity. This information is also useful for yield 
forecasting and crop management. 
    Each oil palm tree potentially produced 10-12 FFB/tree annually6. 
Female inflorescence takes between 4.5 to 6 months to develop 
from anthesis to ripeness. The synthesis of the oil in mesocarp 
occurs from 15 to 16 weeks after anthesis and completed in about 
20 to 22 weeks. In order to maximise the oil extraction rate, the FFB 
should be harvested at its peak of ripeness 7. The harvesting 
frequency was scheduled within 10-12 days interval. In every 
cycle of harvesting operation, farmer does not have any information 
on how many bunches and which oil palm trees will be harvested. 
During executing the harvesting operation the harvester will 
carry a sickle attached to bamboo or aluminium pole for palm tree 
over 12 m height and  chisel  for shorter palm. They will move to 
each tree searching for ripe fruit (including tree without ripe FFB) 
and used their own experience to guess the maturity stage based 
on the natural indicators. Tedious harvesting task force them to 
lose their judgment consistency and concentration. Their task 
will be easy if they know the exact location of ripe FFB. A reliable 
FFB maturity stage determination system and its specific location 
will be very useful towards better oil palm crop management 
system. Farmer can schedule and execute targeted harvesting 
effectively if they know the location of ripe FFB. Worker salary 
per harvested bunch and total count of bunches can be easily

Khalid and Abbas 8 and Abbas et al. 9 utilize the FFB mesocarp 
moisture and oil content relationship to develop a microwave based 
sensor to measure the maturity stages. This sensor only suit for 
laboratory condition. Razali et al. 10, 11 and Wan Ishak et al. 12 
developed a model to determine the FFB maturity, hue value of 
the FFB color image was correlated with the FFB oil content and 
the coefficient of determination  is  R2 = 0.884. This will help farmer 
to confirm the maturity stage of the FFB at field during executing 
the harvesting operation. 
Wan Ishak et al. 13 and Alfatni et al. 14 used RGB color camera to 
capture the image of harvested FFB. The intensity values of R (red), 
G (green) and B (blue) were analyzed to differentiate the FFB into 
categories namely black, hard, ripe, over ripe, empty and rotten. The 
experiment was conducted under controlled environment in the lab 
condition. Result has shown that average value of red color has 
highest difference value among other colors in the categories. Wan 
Ishak et al. 13 suggested to extent the capability of the developed 
vision system to deal with real plantation environment. 
According to Arokiasamy 15,  color of matured FFB was not a constant 
feature and varies between individual palms and between geographic 
areas. To have an accurate estimation of FFB ripeness based on RGB 
color index required more than 100 samples per class, and the 
percentage of accuracy was around 90 percent 16. Junkwon et al. 17 
developed a system to estimate the weight and ripeness of FFB 
using color and hyper spectral imaging. The RGB color images 
were transformed into HSI (hue, saturation and intensity) color 
space. In hyper spectral imaging, FFB image was acquired within 
the wavelength of 400 - 1000 nm. Junkwon et al. 17  concluded that 
ripeness estimation using hyper spectral imaging achieved 
acceptable accuracy and possible for a practical use to estimate 
FFB ripeness, but the cost for the equipment is not affordable, even 
for bigger plantation.  As stated by Osborne et al. 5, a new kind of 
maturity determination is required that is simple and affordable. 
Accurate yield estimation will be very useful to plan labor and 
machinery requirement, monetary budget, oil palm mill capacity 
and various oil palm plantation management aspect. Ghani et al. 6 
described a method to estimate the FFB yield, termed as “Black 
Bunch Count”. Black bunch count is a process of counting a 
bunch of age 1.5 months after anthesis including ripe and unripe 
bunch at a selected oil palm tree. Recommended amount of tree sample 
is 5 to 10 percent from the total tree in a block of  0.3 to 0.5 km2 area. 
Estimation of FFB yield is a result of multiplying the total counted 
bunches with the estimated bunch weight. 
This practice motivates us to develop a software based system 
to support this task. The objectives of this research were to develop 
a FFB growth monitoring system to determine the harvesting date 
of particular FFB at particular oil palm tree and the projection of 
the expected FFB yield of the particular oil palm tree for the whole 
of its economic life. 
Methodology 
Image acquisition: This research was carried out at the Universiti 
Putra Malaysia oil palm field at the coordinate of 2°58’47.97"N 
and 101°43’44.77"E. Sixty oil palm trees with the age of five years 
were selected to be monitored. In this research a fruiting cycle of 
oil palm FFBs was observed within the period of fruit development 
starting from the early FFB development until the day of 
harvesting.  Oil palm FFB takes 17 to 24 weeks to ripen from the 
anthesis stage to maximum oil content synthesis. According to 
Corley and Tinker 18, anthesis of normal FFB inflorescence starts 
when the trilobes flower open and after anthesis the trilobes flower 
change to purplish color. Anthesis in normal inflorescence usually 
lasts for 36-48 hour but may extend to a week. Based on this 
information image acquisition was carried out in three days interval 
to acquire the images of FFB spanning from the inflorescence 
stage to harvesting stage. 
The process started in the middle of May 2010 extended to 8 
months until end of December 2010. Panasonic DMC-TZ10 camera 
with embedded global positioning system (GPS) receiver was used 
to acquire the FFB images. Each oil palm trees, inflorescences and 
FFBs under study were tagged and given a unique identity for 
data recording and retrieval purposes. The images of FFB were 
acquired independently without specific camera or FFB orientation 
and time of acquisition. By the end of December 2010, a total 809 
FFB at various maturity stages were monitored, resulted at nearly 
30000 FFB images. 
Image data management: Data management is a very important 
activity in precision agricultural practice. A systematic data 
management was developed. Each image was named according to 
the given tag and stored in computer using JPEG* digital format. 
A tabulated form of image information, so called Maturity Table, 
was developed to ease the data management. 
FFB images grouping based on maturity stages: A complete 
FFB development from anthesis to harvesting was named as   ‘Full 
Set FFB’. The images contain a physical appearance of FFB from 
early development until the days of harvesting. Information from 
the Full Set FFB can be utilised to determine the maturity stages 
of the FFB in the acquired images. Determination of maturity stages 
was measured from anthesis stage to harvesting stage. The date 
of image acquisition of opened trilobes flower was recorded as 
the date of maturity stage in week 0 to indicate the beginning of 
the FFB development. The last image in the Full Set FFB images 
will be the image of FFB before harvesting. It can be on the same 
day of harvest or within three days before harvesting (three days 
interval image acquisition). 
A simple equation to calculate harvesting date was embedded 
in the each cell of the Maturity Table. An estimated age of the FFB 
during first image being captured was recorded in the cell 
accordingly with the date of acquired image. The equation 
calculates the maturity stages of the FFB in week, the result 
automatically appeared in Maturity Table. To rectify the accuracy 
of the estimated age, the processed images data from Full Set FFB 
were used because in the Full Set FFB the date of anthesis and 
harvested bunches were known. 
 Fig. 1(a) is the image of FFB 12E, one of the Full Set FFB. It was 
at the beginning of anthesis stage (week 0) on 15 May 2010. A 
zero week was inserted in the cell of the Maturity Table and it 
automatically added the number. Termination of the addition was 
on the date of the last image before harvesting which was on 6 
October 2010. Maturity Table determined the FFB 12E was 
harvested  on 9 October 2010, according to the value in the table 
it was in the first day in week 21. 
   Each maturity stages have an interval of seven days. The maturity 
table calculates the maturity stages of the Full Set FFB based on

Non-full set FFB: In the monitoring process not all FFBs were 
monitored at their early development from anthesis. This set of 
images was termed as Non – Full Set FFB.  There were two types 
of Non-Full Set FFB, Type A and Type B. Non-Full Set FFB type A 
was the set of FFB in which the images were acquired after anthesis 
stage to harvesting stage. FFB images acquired before anthesis 
stage but not complete to harvesting stage were categorized as 
Non-Full Set FFB type B. The difference between type A and B 
was their maturity stage determination approach. Maturity stages for 
type A need to be determined by the harvester and then rectified by 
using maturity table. While maturity stages type B can be determined 
directly from Maturity Table since the anthesis stage 
is known. 
The maturity stages of the non-Full Set FFB 
were determined by visual comparison of  the color 
appearance in the images between non-Full Set 
FFB type A and Full Set FFB of particular oil palm 
tree. A similar color appearance of the Non-Full 
Set FFB type A with Full Set FFB from the same 
tree was assumed to be within the same week of 
maturity stage. Based on this assumption the 
estimated age of Non Full Set FFB type A was 
rectified using the Maturity Table. For example, 
Fig. 2 shows a Non-Full set FFB Type A for FFB 
14A (34*) dated 18 September 2010. This bunch 
was harvested on 21 September 2010 and no image 
was available on that date. 
Full set FFB 14F was chosen as an image comparator. Images 
from FFB 14A were visually compared with images from FFB 14F. 
This process only considers similar color appearance. It was found 
that image 14A (32) dated 9 September 2010 has a similar color 
appearance with image 14F (37) dated 27 October 2010. By referring 
to the Maturity Table, FFB 14F on the 27 October 2010 have a 
maturity stage of week 21. Then in column 14A at the date of 9 
September 2010 was inserted with number 21. It was found that on 
21 September 2010 FFB 14A was probably harvested at maturity 
stage of week 22

Results and Discussion 
Maturity Table: The developed Maturity Table was a very useful 
tool in recording the data. It was very helpful in the process of 
sorting and grouping the digital images in their respective maturity 
stages. Table 2 shows the recorded data. A total of 809 FFB were 
monitored, 217(24%) of Full Set FFB, 351(44%) of Non Full Set 
Type A, 237(31%) Non Full Set Type B and 4(1%) of undeveloped 
FFB. These data can easily be transformed into graphs, charts or 
tables for further interpretation.

351 Non Full Set Type A FFB were harvested from May to 
December 2010.  Fig. 4 shows the bar chart of the harvested FFB 
from May to December 2010. The bar chart shows the fluctuation 
in the recorded yield from May to December 2010. This fluctuation 
pattern might have relation with external uncontrolled parameter 
such as weather. This is not the scope of study of this research,

on the information from a digital map proper action can be carried 
out to rectify the yield variability. 
Harvesting schedule: Since FFB were harvested at different 
optimum maturity stage, it is important to determine best optimum 
maturity stage of each oil palm tree. User can choose the best 
optimum maturity stage based on the highest oil content and 
respective FFB color appearance. In this study the mean of the 
harvested FFB age of Full Set FFB was taken as the best optimum 
maturity stage. This approach considers the judgment of harvester 
during selecting the best FFB to harvest. Table 3 shows the mean 
of the optimum maturity stage of oil palm trees in this study. 
This mean value was used by the Maturity Table to calculate 
the optimum maturity stage of each oil palm tree and generate the 
harvesting date to harvest the future FFB. For example oil palm 
tree P1 has an optimum maturity stage at week 21. Maturity Table 
will calculate the FFB development in tree P1 from the opened 
trilobes flower and terminate the calculation in week 21. The date

Conclusions 
This research produces a simple solution in form of data recording 
and management system called Maturity Table. By using Maturity 
Table the digital images were categorized into 4 groups namely 
Full Set FFB, Non-Full Set FFB Type A, Non-Full Set FFB Type B 
and Undeveloped FFB . The images in these groups will be further 
processed using image processing technique to determine the 
maturity stages. This process will not be reported in this paper, 
but the output from the processed images must be verified with 
the result produced by Maturity Table to correctly determine the 
maturity stages. The data shows that the FFB development from 
anthesis to ripeness can be calculated by using Maturity Table. 
FFB yield can be projected monthly and yield per tree can also be 
projected. By using this information user can produce the 
harvesting table consisting of the specific location of the mature 
FFB and the number of FFB available to be harvested, this helps 
them to manage the harvesting operation properly. The harvester

The reliability of this support system depends on the availability 
of the data. The data must constantly deliver to the computer

within a cycle of six month. Application to bigger scale plantation 
will increase this burden. An automated system for data collection 
process needs to be develop in the form of robotized equipment 
carrying the tools to collect the data for bigger size plantation.
