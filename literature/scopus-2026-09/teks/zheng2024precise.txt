a Guangdong Eco-Engineering Polytechnic, Guangzhou 510520, China

A B S T R A C T
This paper presents an innovative method for predicting grape yield using a modified deep convolutional neural network (M−DCNN). This approach integrates
advanced image processing techniques with deep learning algorithms to analyze high-resolution images of vineyards, enabling precise yield quantification. The
M−DCNN model incorporates novel convolutional layers and activation functions to enhance accuracy and generalization capabilities. Extensive experiments were
conducted using a varied dataset of grape varieties and vineyard conditions. The method involves image processing to identify grape clusters and monitor their
growth at different stages. We evaluated the performance of several algorithms, including K-nearest neighbors (KNN), multinomial logistic regression (MLR), support
vector machines (SVM), random forests (RF), deep convolutional neural network (DCNN), and M−DCNN, throughout the training and testing phases. The M−DCNN
achieved the highest training accuracy at 97.35%, which represents a 0.85% improvement over the standard DCNN. Furthermore, in detecting grape berries, our
method attained an average F1 score of 73.32% and coefficients of determination exceeding 0.98, showcasing high accuracy and precision. The results indicate that
the M−DCNN model surpasses traditional yield estimation methods and existing machine learning models, affirming its potential to significantly enhance precision
agriculture. This method offers a robust tool for accurate grape yield forecasting, facilitating informed decision-making in viticulture.
1. Introduction
Grape production is essential in many countries, serving as both a
significant source of nutrition and a raw material for products such as
wine and juice. The prosperity of the grape industry substantially en-
hances local economic development and influences social employment
and cultural heritage (Olenskyj et al., 2022; Rafique et al., 2024; Cov-
iello et al., 2020). Therefore, accurate prediction of grape yield is critical
for optimizing agricultural production, maintaining market balance, and
ensuring price stability.
As technology has advanced, continuous efforts have been made to
improve the accuracy of grape yield prediction. Initially, predictions
relied on farmers’ experience and basic statistical methods, which had
an average error rate exceeding 20 %. These approaches struggled to
capture the complex nonlinear relationships in grape growth, limiting
their predictive accuracy (Leolini et al., 2023; Arab et al., 2021).
Moreover, as environmental conditions change, traditional methods
face increasing challenges in adaptability and flexibility.
With progress in computer science and statistics, more sophisticated
models such as linear regression and time series analysis were intro-
duced, these methods improved prediction accuracy to 15 %-20 %
(Barriguinha et al., 2021; Palacios et al., 2022; Maheswari et al., 2021).
However, they still encountered limitations when dealing with large
datasets and complex nonlinear relationships.
In the 21st century, machine learning techniques such as support
vector machines (SVMs) and random forests (RF) have surpassed
traditional methods in agricultural prediction tasks, improving accuracy
by 10 % to 15 % (Rahim et al., 2022; Sneha et al., 2024; Upadhyaya
et al., 2024; Parr et al., 2023; Chintalapudi et al., 2023; Peanusaha et al.,
2024). However, these methods still face challenges with large-scale
datasets and complex feature extraction.
Deep learning algorithms have shown exceptional ability in identi-
fying crop growth characteristics from extensive historical data and
predicting future yields. For instance, a hybrid model combining con-
volutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks
(RNNs) achieved 85 % accuracy in predicting grape yields in a region of
China (Ballesteros et al., 2020). The capacity of deep learning methods
to process high-dimensional data and capture nonlinear relationships
makes them superior to traditional approaches (Meng et al., 2023).
Recently, deep learning has achieved significant success in various
fields, including image recognition, speech processing, and natural
language processing. In agriculture, deep learning algorithms have
made substantial progress. These models can process diverse data types
(Kamangir et al., 2024; Laurent et al., 2021; Maimaitiyiming et al.,
2019; Wan et al., 2023), such as remote sensing imagery, meteorological
data, soil characteristics, and crop growth information, enhancing grape

2. Materials and methods
2.1. Data collection
2.1.1. Description of data sources
This study was conducted in a 60-acre grape vineyard located within
the campus of Guangdong Ecological Engineering Vocational College.
Image collection took place from April 2023 to June 2024, resulting in
10,000 image samples. We gathered data from three diverse vineyards
representing early, mid, and late stages of crop growth (Dange et al.,
2023). The data collection method was designed to be cost-effective,
focusing on developing a system that accurately estimates grape yield
despite varying image quality.
Using equipment such as tractors equipped with a SONY Alpha 7R IV
high-definition camera (9504 × 6336 pixels), we collected a sequence of
individual grape images along the field. Approximately 50 GB of image
data was gathered, with poor-quality and non-informative images
removed. Each image was captured from a frontal perspective aimed
directly at the grapevines, excluding other angles and unique vine for-
mations to ensure model scalability.
A multistep combinatorial approach using feature-based detection
was adopted due to the high number of objects involved. A state-of-the-
art image detection network was designed to minimize the feature se-
lection process, reducing the physical labor associated with vineyard
management (Tang Y, et al., 2024). The output, conceptual training
data, and count types were derived from images collected throughout
the grape growth process to construct and validate a grape yield pre-
diction model based on deep learning algorithms.
The collected data underwent extensive preprocessing to enhance
the model’s performance and robustness (Palacios et al., 2023; Li et al.,
2024). This included removing shadows from images and computing
vegetation indices. Outlier detection and interpolation were performed
to address missing data issues. Historical yield data were normalized to
eliminate potential biases between different years. Data augmentation
techniques, random forest interpolation, and time series analysis
methods were used to improve data quality and model training effec-
tiveness (Coll-Ribes et al., 2023).

system equipped with state-of-the-art hardware and software compo-

running at 2.59 GHz, providing the computational power necessary for
intensive model training tasks. Complementing the CPU, the system is
enhanced with an NVIDIA GTX 3090 graphics processing unit (GPU)
with 24 GB of memory, enabling efficient parallel processing and ac-
celeration of deep learning algorithms.

To comprehensively evaluate the performance of the proposed deep

Cross-validation involves assessing the generalization ability of sta-

2.3. Experimental setup
This study aims to develop a deep learning-based grape yield esti-
mation model to improve the accuracy and efficiency of yield prediction.
The experimental design was as follows:
Imagery data from five growing seasons (2019 to 2024) were
collected, comprising 1000 satellite images and 2000 high-resolution
drone-captured images. The dataset included five grape varieties and
covered various growth conditions, including drought, moist, high
temperature, and low temperature scenarios.
Data preprocessing involved applying median filtering to remove
noise from the 3000 collected images. The images were then cropped to
include only grape bunch regions and standardized using Z-score
normalization, ensuring input data had a mean of 0 and standard de-
viation of 1.
To minimize the impact of external factors, all images were captured
between 10 a.m. and 2p.m. The shooting angle was maintained between
45◦and 60◦, and the shooting distance was controlled within a range of
1 to 2 m.
For model training and optimization, the initial learning rate was set
at 0.001, with a decay strategy reducing the rate by 10 % every 10
epochs. Hyperparameters were adjusted through grid search, including
learning rates (0.1, 0.01, 0.001, and 0.0001), batch sizes (16, 32, 64, and
128), and the number of M−DCNN units (64, 128, and 256).

Several factors influence the performance of the model, including
data quality. The resolution and conditions of image capture directly
impact the model’s learning effectiveness. Image resolution significantly
affects the performance of image recognition tasks. For example, using
images with a resolution of at least 256 × 256 pixels can yield better
feature extraction results, where as images with resolutions lower than
128 × 128 pixels may lead to loss of detail, there by affecting the
model’s accuracy. Additionally, variations in lighting, background, and
angle during image capture can cause fluctuations in image quality.
Experiments have demonstrated that models trained with images
captured under uniform lighting conditions achieve approximately a
5 % higher accuracy rate than those trained with images taken under
varying lighting conditions.

enhance the training dataset of the SegNet model by incorporating a

high level of accuracy and precision in estimating the number of visible
berries compared to the actual count.
This study introduces a novel grape quality prediction method uti-
lizing an M−DCNN based model to accurately assess the quality of
grapes at different growth stages and sizes, incorporating color as an
additional metric. The model leverages M−DCNN to extract features
from grape images, recognizing that grape characteristics may vary
across different grape types. Additionally, the study evaluates the
model’s performance across five grape varieties and compares various
classifiers to achieve the highest accuracy. The proposed method pre-
sents a promising solution for the advancement of smart agricultural
technology systems in intelligent vineyards, enabling accurate and
efficient prediction of grape quality based on growth and size.
4. Discussion
The study presents a modified DCNN architecture tailored for grape
yield estimation, contributing to advancements in this field. However,
certain limitations exist. The dataset primarily focusing on specific re-
gions and grape varieties, thus lacking diversity and volume. Moreover,
the infrequent occurrence of extreme weather events and rare pests and
diseases in the dataset hampers the model’s predictive capabilities in
such scenarios. Additionally, the complexity of the model results in
limited interpretability, posing challenges for practical applications.
Despite these limitations, the study underscores the significant potential
of deep learning methods in precision agriculture and grape yield esti-
mation. Notably, the model enhances estimation accuracy and opens
avenues for future research, including exploring the integration of
different deep learning algorithms and testing the model on larger
datasets. Furthermore, practical applications such as integrating the
model with drone technology for field monitoring could lead to more
efficient yield management and optimization. Continuous technological
innovation and application promotion in deep learning are poised to
revolutionize agricultural production, particularly in crop yield esti-
mation and resource optimization.
5. Conclusions
In this study, a deep learning model based on DCNN was developed
to predict grape yield using a dataset comprising samples from vineyards
across five distinct regions. These samples encompassed grape growth
data throughout the entire lifecycle. Each sample point initially con-
tained over 100 features, which were subsequently narrowed down to
30 features with the highest predictive value through feature selection
and engineering. After 100 epochs of iteration, the model demonstrated
its best performance on the validation set, which was then evaluated on
the test set. Key contributions of the study include:
The proposal of an M−DCNN model tailored for grape yield esti-
mation, capable of effectively extracting crucial features from vineyard
images for yield prediction. The model’s robustness across diverse en-
vironments was validated through extensive experimentation, offering a
novel data-driven approach for precision agriculture to enhance crop
management and decision-making efficiency.
Comparative experiments highlighted significant performance en-
hancements of the deep learning model over traditional machine
learning models. Specifically, the improved M−DCNN algorithm
exhibited the highest training accuracy of 97.35 %, representing a
notable increase of 0.85 % compared to the original DCNN algorithm.
