ABSTRACT 
 
Unlike RGB images, depth images are robust to complex 
scenes of densely planted orchards. In this paper, we 
propose a fruit detection method using a multimodal feature 
fusion module (MMFF) of RGB and depth images. A dual-
stream convolutional neural network is adopted in our 
method for feature extraction to capture multi-scale 
information of RGB images and depth images based on 
feature pyramids. The multimodal feature fusion module can 
filter similar and different features between modalities to 
suppress the same features and fuse different features. In 
addition, we use a multi-scale feature fusion method to fuse 
more information and improve the accuracy of fruit 
detection. To validate the effectiveness of our method, 
experimental research is conducted on a self-created pear 
dataset with multiple modalities. Extensive experiments 
demonstrate that our proposed approach can achieve state-
of-the-art performance at low computation cost. 
 
Index Terms— Multimodality, Depth image, RGB-D, 
Feature fusion, Object detection 
 
1. INTRODUCTION 
 
Image fusion is a key technology for information acquisition 
and processing and has been widely used in computer 
vision[1], 
remote 
sensing 
imaging[2], 
agricultural 
automation[3], and many other fields[4]. In recent years, 
with the popularity of depth sensors, the fusion of depth 
images and color (RGB) images has been widely used in 
automatic fruit picking[5,6,7,8]. Different ambient light and 
shooting angles for RGB images produce different image 
characteristics. Since a single RGB sensor has certain 
limitations in representing a scene, combining a depth 
sensor and an RGB sensor can provide a more accurate and 
informative scene description[9]. 
With the popularity of consumer-grade depth cameras 
from companies such as Microsoft and Intel[10], the fusion

of depth and RGB images is becoming increasingly popular 
in the field of image fusion[11]. The depth image records 
the distance information from each pixel to the camera, 
which can endow the spatial relationship between the scene 
and the object, helping distinguish the fruit target from the 
background, and offering better environmental perception 
ability[12]. Since the imaging principles of the depth and 
RGB sensors differ, the images obtained are often 
complementary[13]. Figure 1 shows some RGB-Depth 
image pairs captured in the same scene. It can be observed 
that compared with the traditional single visible light visual 
data, the use of multimodal visual data significantly 
contributes to mining more visual features and semantic 
details[14]. Consequently, the detection accuracy of fruits is 
improved. 
In this paper, we propose a novel, multi-scale RGB-
Depth image fusion network with common modality feature 
shuffling reconstruction and differential modality feature 
attention selection for fruit object detection in complex 
scenes. Both RGB and Depth modalities have their own 
inherent features, which are mixed with noise[15], and it is 
not advisable enough to capture multimodal complementary 
representations through direct concatenation or summation 
schemes[16]. Whereas simple fusion schemes, linear 
combinations, or concatenation, lack the mining and sorting 
out of modality-shared and modality-specific features[17]. 
When the network learns multimodal complementarity, it 
needs to learn the differences between RGB and Depth 
modalities and their commonalities and consistency[18].

2. MATERIALS AND METHODS 
 
2.1.  Bagging Pear RGB-D Dataset 
 
Bagged pear images were obtained from commercial-
intensive orchards during the hardcore stage. The orchard is 
located in Xuanmiao Town, Dangshan County, Suzhou City, 
Anhui Province, China. The depth sensor used in this 
bagged pear shooting adopts a narrow field of view mode, 
with a field of view of 75°×65°, a working range of 0.5-3.86 
meters, and a random error of less than 17mm. The 
resolutions of RGB and depth images are 1280×720 and 
640×576. According to the imaging principle of the Azure 
Kinect camera and using the SDK provided by the camera, 
the RGB image is aligned with the depth image based on the 
camera's internal and external parameters. A total of 2544 
color images and depth images were collected in different 
scenarios. As shown in Figure 2, pears are marked into four 
categories (no occlusion, leaf occlusion, fruit occlusion, and 
branch occlusion) based on the robot's picking strategy and 
the spatial conditions of fruits in densely planted 
commercial orchards[21,22].

two modal complementary feature channels based on 
differential modal features and fuse the selected features

introducing a large number of redundant features and thus 
focus on the more reliable modality-complementary features.

2.3. Network architecture 
 
The RGB-D fusion network proposed in this paper is a 
unified end-to-end multimodal detection network, and its 
architecture details are shown in Figure 4. Our methods 
build a dual-stream convolutional network backbone to 
extract RGB and Depth features separately. Then, our 
proposed MMFF module proceeds in the fusion stage, 
capable of merging mid-level multimodal feature maps of 
RGB and Depth. The fusion based on the MMFF module 
occurs in three stages, allowing the fused features to contain 
multi-scale semantic and fine spatial information.

The deep learning hardware platform in this work consists 
of a desktop computer equipped with an Intel Core i5-12400 
(2.5 GHz) 6-core CPU and 32GB memory and an Nvidia 
Tesla P40 24GB GPU (3840 CUDA cores). The running 
software mainly includes python 3.8.8, PyTorch 1.9.0,

3.2. Ablation Study 
 
In this section, an ablation study is performed on the self-
made multi-modal bagged pear dataset, and a detailed 
analysis is conducted. Our fusion method is compared with 
other methods in Table 1 and provides a baseline for single-
modality (RGB-only and Depth-only) detectors. Among 
them, the pixel sum fusion strategy (Addition) adds the two 
feature maps pixel by pixel to obtain the fused feature map. 
The channel parallel fusion strategy (Parallel) obtains the 
fused feature map by splicing two feature maps along one 
dimension. It can be observed that the proposed fusion 
method outperforms other strategies in five metrics. This is 
mainly because "Additive" and "Parallel" only perform 
simple operations on the feature map and cannot adjust the 
fusion of adaptive inputs. Our proposed fusion strategy 
employs an attention mechanism and space and channel 
selection. The attention mechanism enhances the important 
features in the feature map, and the convolution operation 
makes the feature fusion process learnable. Therefore, the 
proposed fusion strategy can improve the generalization 
ability with good fusion performance. At the same time, the 
proposed MMFF method is also compared with the state-of-
the-art YOLOv5, and YOLOv7 models. 
In Table 1, the AP values for the four categories are 
calculated, and the best AP value for each item is shown in 
bold. It can be seen that under the same settings, the mAP 
values of only RGB and only Depth detection on the multi-
modal bagging pear dataset are 84.5% and 79.2%, and the 
difference between the two is pronounced. Between our 
model and other state-of-the-art models, our model has an 
overwhelming advantage (2%). The mAP50 difference 
between our method and other networks ranges from a 
minimum of 2% to a maximum of 28.6%. Furthermore, our 
method achieves the best AP, including NO (92.6%), OL 
(91.6%), OF (87.5%) and OB (87%).

4. CONCLUSION 
 
In this paper, we propose a method for fruit detection in 
complex orchard scenes using multimodal feature fusion of 
RGB and depth images. We use a two-stream convolutional 
neural network to extract multi-scale features of both 
modalities. We use SK attention in the feature fusion 
module to filter complementary features of the two 
modalities and fuse them. We use the spatial and channel 
selection units in SCConv to select the most informative 
features in the two modalities to generate a fusion result 
with fine details. Experimental results show that our method 
successfully fuses RGB and depth images and outperforms 
other methods in both qualitative and quantitative 
evaluations.
