Detection of Oil Palm Fruit Ripeness through Image Feature 
Optimization using Convolutional Neural Network Algorithm 
Dedy Setiawan a,*, Pradita Eko Prasetyo Utomo a, Muksin Alfalah a 
a Department of Electrical Engineering and Information Systems, University of Jambi, Mendalo, Jambi, Indonesia 
Corresponding author: *dedy_setiawan@unja.ac.id 
Abstract—The increase in the need for raw materials for palm oil products in the form of food and non-food is felt by the people of 
Indonesia and other countries. For this reason, triggering oil palm farmers in Indonesia must be able to maximize their production. 
Currently, oil palm farmers in Indonesia still need help knowing the level of sustainability of oil palm fruit to maintain their production. 
This research was conducted to identify the maturity level of oil palm fruit using practical images for oil palm farmers in Indonesia. 
The Convolutional Neutral Network (CNN) algorithm is the research method used to identify pictures of oil palm fruit. The dataset 
collection comprised 400 images of oil palm fruits divided into three types of classes, namely images of raw, ripe, and rotten oil palm 
fruits. The dataset was taken from various internet sources, and photos were taken directly using a mobile phone camera according to 
a predetermined class. This study found that identifying the maturity level of oil palm fruit using the Convolutional Neural Network 
(CNN) algorithm obtained a high accuracy of 98% in the training process and 76% in the model testing process. The findings of this 
study can also inspire further research in optimizing image features and using the Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) algorithm 
more efficiently. This could include a reduction in model training time, the number of parameters, or the development of other 
techniques that improve algorithm performance.  
Keywords—CNN; palm oil; identification; image; deep learning.

INTRODUCTION
Oil palm is recognized as a crucial plantation crop 
contributing 
significantly 
to 
the 
national 
economy, 
particularly in terms of employment opportunities, income 
generation, and foreign exchange [1],[2],[3]. In 2022, the total 
oil palm plantation area in Indonesia was reported to be 15.34 
million ha [4]. Oil palm is a crop known for producing palm 
oil and palm kernel. Several factors influence the quality of 
palm oil, including water content, impurities, free fatty acid 
content, and, notably, the ripeness level of the palm fruit 
[5],[6],[7],[8],[9]. However, in practice, one of the challenges 
lies in achieving the appropriate ripeness level of the palm 
fruit, necessitating sorting processes to obtain high-quality 
palm fruit with the desired ripeness level. 
Based on interviews conducted with the Manager of PT. 
Rigunas Agri Utama in 2023 that the company consistently 
conducts sorting processes on palm fruit destined for crude oil 
production. 
It 
aims 
to ensure 
quality 
oil 
output. 
Approximately 5% of the palm fruit sorted by the plant is 
deemed unfit for further processing. The process of selecting 
oil palm fruit, conducted by various processing companies 
and palm oil farmers, typically relies solely on manual 
methods 
involving 
human 
decision-making 
[10],[11],[12],[13]. Manual processes are time-consuming, 
prompting the need for mechanized assistance when applied 
on a large industrial scale. Hence, it is necessary to develop a 
system capable of recognizing the ripeness level of oil palm 
fruit according to predetermined categories [14],[15]. 
The findings of this research are expected to contribute and 
provide an alternative for oil palm plantation managers or 
farmers in facilitating the fruit sorting process. Image 
processing serves as a potential solution to address this 
challenge. One of the technological approaches that can be 
utilized is deep learning [16],[17],[18],[19],[20],[21]. Several 
studies have been conducted to classify the maturity levels of 
oil palm fruit, such as the research conducted by [22], and the 
survey conducted by [12]. However, from existing research, 
there has yet to be a similar study utilizing the Convolutional 
Neural Network (CNN) algorithm. Therefore, researchers are 
motivated to conduct a similar survey employing the CNN 
algorithm.

At present, Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) stands 
out as the most significant technology in image recognition 
[23], [24], [25],[26], [27], [28], [29], [30].  This is because the 
CNN method attempts to mimic the image recognition system

process image information like humans [31]. The challenge in 
using the CNN method lies in achieving high accuracy with 
relatively low computational costs. 
Numerous factors can influence this, including the size of 
the training dataset, data dimensions, and the selection of an 
appropriate training rate [32]. Another approach is to employ 
transfer learning architecture, which can emulate and

methodology using CNN to aid in identifying the maturity 
level of oil palm fruit. The research aims to achieve optimal 
accuracy and effectively classify the maturity stages of oil 
palm fruit.

B. Research Data 
In conducting this research, the first step involved 
collecting a dataset of images of oil palm fruit categorized into 
three classes: raw, ripe, and rotten palm fruit. According to 
the predefined classes, the dataset was gathered from various 
online sources and captured directly in the field using a 
mobile phone camera. The dataset consisted of 1000 images 
split into two sections. The Train Test Split method from the 
Sklearn package was employed to split the data, resulting in 
80% allocated for training and 20% for testing. 
C. Data Preprocessing 
At first, the dataset is divided into 80% for training and 
20% for testing. Afterward, each oil palm fruit image is 
resized to 150x150 pixels with a 3-channel size, creating an 
input shape of 150x150x3. A batch size of 16 is chosen, and 
the training is carried out across ten epochs, establishing the 
dataset. Following this, uniform augmentation is applied to 
these images. 
In this study, augmentation involves rescaling to 1/255, 
shearing with a scale of 0.2, zooming images within a range

D. Design of Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) 
After data generation, the subsequent phase involves 
training the CNN model. Generally, CNNs involve two 
phases: feature learning and classification. The CNN model's 
input image is standardized to 128x128x3, where "three" 
denotes an image with three channels: Red, Green, and Blue 
(RGB). The input image undergoes convolution and pooling 
operations during the feature learning phase.  
In this configuration, there are two convolutional layers, 
each with distinct numbers of filters and kernel sizes. Dropout 
is then applied, followed by the flattening process, which 
converts the feature maps from the pooling layer into a vector. 
This step is commonly known as the fully connected layer 
phase. The CNN's architecture in this research is illustrated in 
Figure 3. According to the architecture depicted in Figure 3, 
there are two phases: Feature Learning and Classification.

The initial Convolution operation utilizes a 3x3 kernel with 
128 filters. Convolution involves merging two distinct 
matrices to yield a new matrix value. After convolution, a 
Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) activation function is applied. 
This function is intended to convert negative values to zero, 
effectively eliminating negative values within the convolution 
result matrix. The convolution outcome retains its original 
size, 128x128, due to the utilization of a padding value of 0 
during the convolution operation. 
Pooling is fundamentally a process where a designated size 
filter traverses the entire feature map alternatingly. This 
research uses max-pooling to derive a new matrix value 
following the pooling operation. Through max-pooling, a new 
matrix of dimensions 63x63 is generated using a 2x2 pooling 
kernel. The max-pooling mechanism functions by extracting 
the maximum value as the filter shifts, guided by its stride 
value, set to 2 in this instance. 
The second Convolution operation extends from the output 
of the initial pooling process, with an input matrix size of 
32x32 and 64 filters applied using a 3x3 kernel. Similar to the 
first convolution, this second convolution operation also 
incorporates the RELU activation function. 
The following stage entails the second pooling operation, 
akin to the initial pooling process, albeit with a variance in the 
ultimate output matrix dimensions. Consequently, the 
resulting output yields an image size of 30x30. 
The third Convolution operation proceeds from the output

of 32x32 and employing 32 filters through a 3x3 kernel. This 
third convolution operation incorporates the RELU activation 
function

Subsequently, the third pooling operation is applied,

Moving forward is the flattened or fully connected stage.

aims to mitigate overfitting during training. Thus, in the 
dropout process, neurons that were previously activated are 
randomly omitted, ensuring that only a subset of neurons is 
employed during training to alleviate overfitting and 
streamline the model creation process.

D. Convolution Layer Process 
According to the findings from the network architecture, 
the convolution process can be visualized in Figure 8. 
 
 
Fig. 8  Convolution Process 
 
Convolution entails merging two sets of numerical 
sequences to generate a third sequence. In practical 
implementation, these numerical values in convolution 
manifest as an array matrix. For input images with pixel 
dimensions of 128x128x3, this signifies that the image 
possesses a height and width of 128 pixels and three channels, 
denoted as red, green, and blue, commonly known as RGB.  
Each pixel channel contains distinct matrix values. The 
input undergoes convolution with pre-defined filter values. 
Like another block or cube, the filter possesses smaller height 
and width dimensions but retains the same depth and is 
traversed across the base or original image. The filter serves 
to identify which patterns will be detected, subsequently 
convolving or multiplying with the values in the input matrix. 
The values within each column and row of the matrix heavily 
rely on the detected pattern type. In this convolution process, 
128 filters with a kernel size of (3x3), imply that the resultant 
image from the convolution will comprise 128 feature maps. 
To simplify the convolution process, a sample matrix is 
utilized for the input image in this study. Given that the input 
image has pixel dimensions of 128x128, only a subset of the 
matrix values are used as samples of the convolution process. 
 
 
Fig. 9  Convolution Calculation 
 
Figure 9 showcases the convolution process employing a 
3x3 kernel size with a stride of 1. Stride denotes the number 
of shifts the kernel makes across the input matrix, set to one. 
The outcome of this stride is illustrated in Figure 10 as shown 
below: 
 
Fig. 10  Kernel Position 
 
Figure 10 demonstrates the dot product computation within 
the convolution process, where a 3x3 kernel initiates from the 
left side. This operation, known as the sliding window, 
progresses from the top to the bottom left corner. However, 
this study introduces a padding value of 1, indicating that 
zeros are appended around the input matrix. This ensures that 
both input and output matrices maintain the same size, thereby 
preserving information within the image. For two matrices to 
undergo multiplication, the first matrix must possess the same 
number of columns as the second matrix, which has rows. The 
number of rows determines the resulting matrix's order from 
this multiplication in the first matrix multiplied by the number 
of columns in the second matrix. After the convolution 
process, the ReLU (Rectified Linear Unit) activation is 
employed within the network architecture before advancing 
to the pooling layer. This activation function sets a threshold 
or constrains values between 0 and infinity. This activation 
function transforms negative values within the convolution 
result to zero, while other values remain unaltered. 
E. Pooling Process 
Pooling entails diminishing the matrix size via pooling 
operations, which involve aggregation. In this pooling 
process, the method employed is max pooling, a widely 
adopted technique in deep learning research. Studies, such as 
the one conducted by [34], have shown that the max-pooling 
method outperforms sub-sampling techniques, establishing it 
as one of the most effective approaches in the pooling process. 
Here is an outline of the pooling process: 
 
 
Fig. 11  Pooling Process 
 
In this pooling process, a 2x2 size is utilized with a stride 
of 1, meaning the kernel shifts across the input matrix by one 
position. Max-pooling is employed in this process, where the 
678

window adjusts according to its size and stride to acquire the 
maximum value. As depicted in Figure 11, the output of this 
operation captures the maximum value extracted from the 
feature map matrix resulting from the convolution process. 
The resulting max-pooling matrix has dimensions of 2x2. The 
subsequent process is the Fully Connected Layer, aimed at 
transforming the data dimensions for linear classification. 
 
 
Fig. 12  Fully Connected Process 
 
Figure 12 illustrates the transformation of results from the 
max-pooling feature map into a flattened or vector format. 
The activation map generated from the feature extraction 
layer remains in a multidimensional array structure and 
necessitates reshaping into a vector to function as input for the 
fully connected layer. This process mirrors the Multilayer 
Perceptron (MLP) approach, which typically incorporates 
fully connected layers, treating each pixel as an individual 
neuron. During this process, the 'dropout' technique is 
commonly applied to deactivate certain connections 
associated with each neuron, aiming to mitigate overfitting. 
The final step encompasses classification, where the SoftMax 
activation function is employed. This activation supports the 
MLP in categorizing the input into its three classes: Raw, 
Ripe, and Rotten.  
Before being fed into a fully connected layer, each 
convolution layer's neuron must transform into one-
dimensional data. This conversion leads to the loss of spatial 
information in the data, an irreversible process. Fully 
connected layers can only be applied after the network 
architecture.

IV. CONCLUSION 
This research develops a maturity classification system for 
oil palm fruit using CNN, which is expected to help oil palm 
fruit managers in the sorting process more efficiently and 
accurately. The palm oil fruit image dataset is divided into 
680

three classes: unripe, ripe, and rotten. The designed CNN 
model consists of several convolution and pooling layers, 
followed by a fully connected layer for classification. The test 
results show that the CNN model can accurately classify the 
maturity of oil palm fruit. This CNN implementation shows 
excellent potential in increasing the efficiency and accuracy 
of oil palm fruit sorting, with training accuracy reaching 
99.11% and validation accuracy reaching 100%. This 
technology offers a faster and more accurate alternative to 
manual methods. 
This research can be further improved with experiments on 
various CNN architectures to improve model accuracy. This 
increase in accuracy will directly enhance the performance of 
the palm fruit maturity classification system, resulting in more 
reliable and efficient decisions in the sorting process. In 
addition, testing with more extensive and diverse datasets will 
help the model generalize better to various natural conditions 
in the field, which is essential to ensure system reliability in 
multiple situations. Advanced research could also apply more 
complex data augmentation techniques. Integrating the CNN 
model into an IoT-based automatic sorting system will turn 
research results into practical applications that can be used 
directly by farmers and the palm oil industry. Thus, in the 
future, it is hoped that this research can further develop the 
application of image processing technology and deep learning 
in the palm oil industry, as well as make a significant 
contribution to the efficiency and sustainability of the palm 
oil industry.
