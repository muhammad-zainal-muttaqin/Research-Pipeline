Abstract—Loose fruitlet collection in oil palm plantations 
remains a labor-intensive process that contributes to significant 
yield losses when fruitlets are left uncollected. This work presents 
an AI-driven delta robot system that capable of real-time 
detection, localization, and collection of loose fruitlets. A 
monocular camera captures tray images, which are rectified using 
homography and processed with a convolutional detector trained 
on a curated oil palm fruitlets dataset. Detected centroids are 
transformed into robot coordinates through a zone-based 
calibration scheme, enabling precise targeting within the robot’s 
workspace. The delta robot executes a simplified pick-and-place 
sequence with fixed vertical states for approach and retraction, 
combined with a vacuum end-effector ensure reliable pickup. A 
verification stage provides an accurate fruitlet counting and 
failure handling. The system operates entirely on an embedded 
platform, achieving detection to actuation latencies under 0.5s 
and pick cycles within 2s. Experimental evaluation demonstrates 
practical accuracy more than 85% accuracy, robustness under 
variable lighting, and throughput exceeding 50 fruitlets per 
minute, indicating a strong potential for deployment in plantation 
environments.  
Keywords—Edge AI, delta robot, loose fruitlet collection, 
homography mapping, vacuum end-effector, agricultural robotics 
I. INTRODUCTION 
The palm oil industry contributes significantly to the global 
economy, with Malaysia and Indonesia being the largest 
producers. However, yield losses remain a major challenge due 
to inefficient collection of loose fruitlets that detach from fresh 
fruit bunches during harvesting. These fruitlets often remain 
uncollected on the plantation ground contributing to wastage 
and reduced productivity. Manual fruitlet collection is labor-
intensive, time-consuming, and prone to inefficiency, thereby 
motivating the development of autonomous collection systems. 
Prior studies on suction-based collection have established 
minimum airflow and velocity thresholds required for lifting 
fruitlets, highlighting both the feasibility and the mechanical 
challenges of vacuum-assisted designs [1, 2]. In parallel, 
advances in computer vision and deep learning, particularly the 
You Only Look Once (YOLO) family of object detectors have 
enabled accurate small-object detection in agricultural context, 
including oil palm fruitlet detection in complex field 
environments [3, 4]. Recent work integrating YOLOv4 with 
robotic platforms demonstrated robust detection performance, 
achieving over 90% precision when enhanced with image tilting, 
but real-time deployment remains constrained by processing 
demands [5]. Despite these advances, the integration of AI-
driven perception with robotic manipulation for autonomous 
loose fruitlet collection is still underexplored. Delta robots, with 
their high-speed pick and place capabilities, structural rigidity, 
and precise motion control, present a compelling solution for 
perception and action. In addition to perception, the system 
integrates a planar kinematic study of the delta manipulator, 
covering the reachable X-Y workspace and lifting operation 
along Z-axis to demonstrate coverage and trajectory efficiency 
for loose fruitlet collection [6].  This paper introduces an AI-
driven delta robot system that combines YOLO-based real-time 
detection, homography-based coordinate mapping, and a 
vacuum-assisted-end-effector to achieve autonomous loose 
fruitlet collection. The system is designed to meet field-level 
performance requirements, including more than 85% detection 
accuracy and collection rate exceeding 40 fruitlets per minute.

A. Vision-based Detection for Oil Palm and Small-fruit 
Targets 
The application of computer vision and deep learning in 
agricultural perception has increasingly addressed challenges in 
small-object detection. In oil palm plantations, Junos et al. [7] 
enhanced YOLO by integrating residual connections, Swish 
activation, multi-layer detection heads, and optimized prior 
boxes, significantly improving the recall of small fruitlets from 
UAV imagery while maintaining embedded feasibility. Daud et 
al. [8] demonstrated the use of deep models to detect loose

B. Mechanized and Suction-based Collection Systems 
Mechanized collection has emerged a parallel research 
direction to reduce dependency on manual labor in plantations. 
Fauzan et al. [13] introduced a loose fruit collector that 
integrates suction and mechanical conveyance, offering

Fluid Dynamics (CFD) and experimental validation. Their study, 
established that airflow velocities between 20 - 40 ms-1 are 
critical for fruitlet entrainment while avoiding choking or 
blockages. These findings confirm the feasibility of vacuum-
assisted collection but also highlight operational constraints, 
including efficiency losses from duct design, hose bending, and 
wet debris. Such insights provide important design guidelines 
for suction-based robotic collection and motivate the transition 
from bulk collection machines towards precision end-effectors 
integrated with robotic arms. 
C. Robotic Manipulation in Agriculture and the Case for the 
Delta Robot 
The integration of robotic manipulation with agricultural 
perception has advanced rapidly, with robots now performing 
selective harvesting, weeding, and sorting in dynamic field 
environments. Šlajpah et al. [15] presented a mobile robotic 
platform for asparagus harvesting, showing that perception-
driven trajectory planning can achieve reliable selective picking 
under outdoor conditions. Extending this work, Šlajpah et al. [16] 
developed a delta robot specifically for asparagus harvesting, 
demonstrating the advantages of parallel kinematics such as 
high stiffness, lightweight structure, and fast cycle times. These 
attributes make the delta robot particularly suitable for repetitive 
pick-and-place tasks in constrained workspace near the ground 
plane. When paired with a low-profile vacuum end-effector and 
robust fruitlet detection, delta manipulators present a promising 
solution for loose fruitlet collection in oil palm plantations. 
However, the integration of AI-driven perception with delta 
robot kinematics in cluttered plantation environments remains 
relatively unexplored, which forms the research gap addressed 
by this study.  
III. METHODOLOGY 
The loose fruitlet collector executes on an NVIDIA Jetson 
AGX Orin and integrates three external devices which are an 
industrial camera for image acquisition, a delta robot controller 
for motion execution, and a vacuum controller for suction 
actuation. The camera is connected via a USB 3.0, while the 
delta robot and vacuum controller communicate over USB-
serial link. Power is supplied through 24 V and 48 V rails on the 
platform. The analytics engine is composed of several modules 
which are image warping, loose fruitlets detection, position 
estimation, fruitlets counting, robotic arm coordination and 
collection verification as shown in Fig. 1. The modules 
orchestrated by a main control loop that sequences detection, 
mapping, motion, suction, and verification. The system is 
designed to meet strict performance requirements, including 
sustained detection at ≥15 frames per second (FPS), robot pick 
cycle at ≤10s, enabling the practical throughput of 
approximately 50 fruitlets per minute.

IV. RESULT AND ANALYSIS 
The evaluation of the AI-driven delta robot focuses on the 
accuracy of detection, robot motion performance, and suction 
reliability. In vision trials, the YOLOv8 model sustained an 
average of 15.2 FPS with an inference latency of 85 m∙s per 
frame. The detection accuracy achieved a mean Average 
Precision (mAP@50) of 93.3% exceeding the threshold of 85%. 
The mapping error between camera-warped image coordinates 
and the robot’s planar workspace averaged at ±4.6 mm, which 
remains within the maximum allowable error of ±5 mm. This 
deviation primarily arises from residual lens distortion, 
homography approximations, and calibration tolerances, but is 
sufficiently low to ensure reliable positioning of the vacuum’s 
end-effector. These outcomes confirmed that the vision pipeline 
delivered accurate and real-time localization of loose fruitlets. A 
summary of the vision evaluation is presented in Table I. Vision 
Performance Metrics, listing FPS, inference time, mAP, and 
mapping error.

V. CONCLUSION 
This study presented an AI-driven delta robot system that 
integrates YOLOv8-based vision, planar kinematics, and 
vacuum actuation for automated loose fruitlet collection in oil 
palm plantation. The results show that the system exceeded all 
key design requirements by sustaining detection over 15 FPS, 
maintaining coordinate mapping accuracy within ±5 mm, and 
picking throughput exceeding 50 fruitlets per minute. The 9-
zone calibration strategy effectively maintained consistency 
across the workspace, while the vacuum integration with grace 
interval reduced unnecessary cycling and achieved an average 
of 94.5% collection success rate. 
These findings confirm that combining real-time deep 
learning with planar delta kinematics and suction actuation 
provides a practical and efficient solution for fruitlet 
mechanization. Although the system performs reliably in 
controlled conditions, several challenges remain for field-scale 
deployment. The vision model assumes stable illumination and 
flat tray geometry, conditions that may not hold under variable 
plantation lighting, shadowing, or uneven terrain. The fixed 9-
zone calibration also limits adaptability when the camera or 
robot orientation changes. Future work should include dynamic 
scheduling of multiple simultaneous targets, adaptive suction 
control, and extended field deployment under varying 
illumination and terrain conditions.
