Abstract—Having prior knowledge about the amount of fruit
before harvesting can lead to improved planning and decision-
making in the agriculture sector. In recent years, several
detection-based approaches have been proposed to address the
problem of fruit counting. However, employing a detection
approach for a specific category of fruit requires a large amount
of data, which is very costly. Meanwhile, a trained specific class
detection model cannot handle new unseen categories without
being trained on a large amount of new data. In this paper, we
present a novel approach for class-agnostic fruit counting that
can count the number of fruits in an arbitrary category with just
a few examples. Compared to other fruit detection methods, our
few-shot approach only needs a small amount of data to count
on a new novel class. Specifically, our proposed model takes
advantage of the vision transformer with multi-scale features to
improve the accuracy of the counting result. We evaluated our
proposed approach in the DeepFruit dataset, which contains six
types of fruits (including mango, capsicum, strawberry, apple,
avocado, and orange). We compare our method with several state-
of-the-art object detection approaches. Our approach archives
the MAE of 1.13 and RMSE of 1.48, respectively, on the
DeepFruit dataset. Extensive experiments and qualitative results
demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach compared to other
detection approaches for fruit counting.
Index Terms—Fruit counting, few-shot counting, visual count-

I. INTRODUCTION
The importance of fruit counting in agricultural production
management has increased dramatically with the growth of
deep learning and computer vision. Counting fruits during
plant growth is crucial to minimizing manual work and im-
proving the effectiveness of management and decision-making
related to harvesting through better allocation of labor, storage,
packaging, and harvest scheduling. Counting fruits is difficult
due to the environment and the lack of discriminating features
on fruit surfaces. Despite the potential for improved accuracy
in predicting fruit crop yields through computer vision and
deep learning, fruit counting remains a challenging task.
The most recent fruit counting approaches use detection-
based counting [1, 2, 3]. With the development of the Vision
Transformer model (ViT) model, this model was applied and
is effective in fruit counting compared to the traditional con-
volution model [4, 5]. These detection methods may not work
well in situations where the fruit growth is dense, and the color

In this paper, we propose ViT-FFrCnt, a few-shot fruit-
counting approach using a vision transformer and multi-scale
features. By using the ViT model as well as multi-scale, our
proposed model can count the number of objects of any object
category with a few samples of reference images (also known
as ‘exemplars’) (Figure 1 (bottom)). It is worth mentioning
that the class to be counted does not need to be included in
the training dataset, thereby saving the cost of training a new
class of objects compared to the count-by-detection approach.
We evaluated our proposed approach in the DeepFruit dataset,
which contains six types of fruits (including mango, capsicum,
strawberry, apple, avocado, and orange), and compared it with
other object detection approaches. It is noticed that with a
few given reference images, our proposed model yields more
accurate results on unseen object categories on a training
set compared to fully trained conventional object detection
approaches.
In summary, our contributions are as follows:
1) We propose ViT-FFrCnt, a Few-shot Fruit-Counting
approach with the Vision Transformer and multi-scale
features. Our proposed method can count the fruit quan-
tity of an arbitrary category with a few exemplar images.
2) Our approach to fruit counting is effective compared
to the detection approach on the DeepFruit dataset [6]
through extensive experiments and qualitative results.

II. RELATED WORK
Object Detection Object detection aims to detect the
bounding box of all interest objects in the images. Ren et
al. [7] propose Faster R-CNN, which is an improved version
of Fast R-CNN with the Region Proposal Network (RPN),
showing a superior result on object detection. He et al.
[8] propose Mask R-CNN, which adapts Faster R-CNN for
object segmentation. Lin et al. [9] propose RetinaNet as a
solution to address the issue of the foreground-background
class imbalance in object detection. This method utilizes a
feature pyramid network and introduces a new loss function
called the Focal loss. Redmon et al. introduce the YOLO
algorithm that can predict class probabilities and bounding
boxes directly from full images in a single evaluation. In
further development, models such as YOLOv5 [10] networks
have been proposed, which have higher detection accuracy and
meet real-time prediction requirements.
Vision Transformer In recent years, Vision Transformer
(ViT) has shown potential results in the field of computer
vision compared to traditional convolutional neural network
models. The key component of ViT is the attention mecha-
nism, which was widely used before in natural language pro-
cessing. Xizhou Zhu et al. propose the Deformable Attention
mechanism [11], which calculates the attention of the query
with a selected set of keys instead of all keys. The deformable
attention can be extended with the multi-scale features map,
namely, multi-scale deformable attention. The ViT model is
currently widely applied to downstream tasks such as dense
prediction [12], counting [4, 5]. Several studies have shown
that using multi-scale features makes it possible to increase the
accuracy compared to the vanilla ViT [12]. He et al. introduce
the Masked Auto Encoder (MAE) [13] self-supervised pre-
trained for the ViT, which increases the performance on the
ImageNet dataset of the ViT compared to the original one. The
MAE randomly drops some of ViT’s patch tokens and attempts
to reconstruct the original input image from the remaining
tokens.
Fruit counting In fruit detection, segmentation, and count-
ing in a single image, deep learning techniques have replaced
the traditional computer vision techniques previously con-
sidered the most advanced. The detection of various fruits,
including mangoes, almonds, and apples [14, 6] has been suc-
cessfully accomplished using the Faster R-CNN architecture
[7]. Our approach stands out from previous studies as it applies
few-shot counting to fruit counting. Gao et al. [2] propose
an automated video processing method using a combination
of object tracking and prediction algorithms to improve the
accuracy of counting apples in orchards. Zhang [3] presents a
panoramic method for accurately counting the total number of
fruits on a tree utilizing UAV images and SIFT-based matching
to create a panoramic map of the tree’s surface and then using
YOLO to count the number of fruits. Unlike previous methods
that rely on annotation datasets to count specific classes, our
proposed method requires only a small number of exemplars
to count particular fruits of interest.

After obtaining the multi-scale image features FMS

exemplar features fexp, we performed multi-scale similarity
matching between these features. Specifically, we use the
Mult-scale Deformable Attention [11] with multi-scale image
features FV iT as query and the exemplar features fexp as the
key, value. After performing the similarity matching between
the multi-scale image features FV iT and the exemplar features
fexp, we obtain the multi-scale similarity features map Fsim.
We employ multi-scale regression heads to predict density
maps. We use a progressive upsampling design from UpperNet
[16] for this regression head. Specifically, lower-resolution
features from the multi-scale feature map will be upscaled
using bilinear interpolation and added to the following higher-
level feature map. After that, all feature maps will be scaled
to a resolution of 1/4 and merged using convolution. The

IV. EXPERIMENTS
A. Dataset & Metric
Dataset We use the DeepFruit dataset to evaluate our
approach compared to other fruit detection networks. The
DeepFruit dataset consists of 535 images from 6 classes, in-
cluding ‘mango’, ‘capsicum’, ‘strawberry’, ‘apple’, ‘avocado’,
and ‘orange’ It is noticed that we exclude the ‘rockmelon’
class from the dataset due to the annotation error. For object
detection, we train and test in all classes of the DeepFruit
dataset. For our few-shot approach, we only train in half of
the DeepFruit classes (including ‘mango’, ‘capsicum’, and
‘strawberry’) and evaluate all DeepFruit’s classes. Our initial
step is to gather all the object bounding boxes for each class,
treating them as exemplars of the respective classes. We then
evaluate the results of each class by using the corresponding
class exemplar in the training set. We use only one sample
image as the input of our model one at a time, and the report
result is the average of all training set exemplar results.
Metrics We use two standard metrics for counting problem
evaluation, namely Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Root
Mean Squared Error (RMSE) to evaluate our proposed model
compared to other detection approaches.

B. Implementation
We use the ViT-Base with MAE pre-trained [13] on Ima-
geNet [17] as our backbone architecture. Our proposed method
takes as input the image with a resolution of 384 × 384. The
ViT-Base consists of 12 transformer encoder layers with hid-
den dimensions of 768; the number of attention heads in each
encoder layer is 12. Standard training image augmentation is
applied, including horizontal, vertical flipping, and color jitter.
We use AdamW [18] as an optimizer. Our model is trained
with L2 loss on 250 epochs with a learning rate of 1 × 10−5
and a batch size of 4. Our model is trained and tested on a
single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 24Gb.
C. Results
The results of the DeepFruit test set are shown in Table I,
showing the superiority of our approach over other methods
of counting by detection. Compared to other object detection
approaches evaluated on the DeepFruit dataset, our approach
outperforms Faster R-CNN and RetinaNet regarding Mean
Absolute Error (MAE) and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE)
for multiple fruit classes. Our approach archives a relative
improvement of all classes 10.83% w.r.t. validation MAE
and 17.94% w.r.t to test MAE compared to the YOLOv5.
It is worth noting that, with the ‘Avocado’ and ‘Orange’
classes, which are not in the training set of our approach,
we archive higher results compared with other well-trained
detector methods. This demonstrates the effectiveness of our
approach in accurately counting fruits while minimizing the
need for large training datasets and retraining models for
new fruit categories. Our approach consistently achieves the
lowest MAE and RMSE values across different fruit classes,
indicating its superior accuracy and performance compared to
the other object detection models.

V. CONCLUSION
In conclusion, the proposed few-shot fruit counting ap-
proach, ViT-FFrCnt, addresses the challenges of traditional
fruit counting by detection-based methods and offers an effi-
cient solution for the management of agricultural production.
Our proposed model leveraging the Vision Transformer model
with multi-scale features, ViT-FFrCnt, enables accurate fruit
counting with minimal data requirements. The approach elim-
inates the need for labor-intensive data collection and training
of specific detection models for each fruit category. The effec-
tiveness of ViT-FFrCnt is demonstrated through experiments
on the DeepFruit dataset [6], where it outperforms traditional
object detection methods in terms of accuracy.
