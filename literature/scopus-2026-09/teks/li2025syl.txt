Abstract
Accurate fruit detection is vital significance for estimating the output of orchards. In this paper, an improved deep con­
volutional neural network detection model, SYL-YOLOv8n, suitable for small object detection from the perspective of 
unmanned aerial vehicles based on YOLOv8n is proposed. Firstly, this study designed a PE-C2f module, which founded 
on the partial convolution (PConv) and efficient multi scale attention mechanism (EMA). This design facilitates model 
lightweighting while improving the capacity to extract and integrate peach feature information in complex environments. 
Secondly, the multiple path distance intersection over union (MPDIoU) loss function is used as the new bounding box loss 
function to accelerate the positional fitting between the ground-truth and predicted boxes. Thirdly, the bidirectional feature 
pyramid network (BiFPN) is introduced to improve the information transmission capacity among features of different 
scales through weighted bidirectional feature fusion. Finally, a P2 small target detection layer is added in the neck section, 
thereby better capturing the detailed information of small objects. To verify the validity of the algorithm, experiments 
were carried out on a self-constructed multi-source peach dataset. The Precision (P), Recall (R), mean average precision 
(mAP), and F1-Score (F1) of the SYL-YOLOv8n reached 92.7%, 85.0%, 94.3%, and 88.7%, respectively, which were 
1.4%, 1.9%, 0.7%, and 1.7% higher than the original model. Finally, based on the number of canopy fruits identified by 
SYL-YOLOv8n, a “canopy fruits - LAI - whole tree fruits” yield estimation method incorporating leaf area index (LAI) 
coefficients was proposed. The results showed that the R2 of the yield estimation method reached 0.88, with a RMSE of 
11.16. The accuracy of this estimation method also meets the actual application requirements.

Conventional machine vision detection methods mainly 
depend on manually designed features, such as fruit texture, 
color, and shape for recognition. In complex natural envi­
ronments, however, these approaches are highly vulnerable 
to adverse factors like varying illumination, occlusion by 
branches and foliage, and background clutter, which sub­
stantially degrade detection performance and make them 
unsuitable for practical applications [5]. In contrast, convo­
lutional neural networks (CNNs) offer notable advantages in 
object detection filed. Currently, CNN-based object detec­
tion methods can be broadly divided into two-stage meth­
ods and single-stage methods. Two-stage methods involve 
generating candidate regions likely to contain the target, fol­
lowed by classification and bounding box regression, such 
as RCNN [6] and Faster RCNN [7], offer higher detection 
accuracy but require greater computational resources. Sin­
gle-stage methods skip candidate region generation, directly 
predicting the target’s class and location within the image. 
These algorithms provide faster detection speeds. Examples 
include SSD [8] and YOLO series [9].
In recent years, numerous researchers have made signifi­
cant progress in fruit recognition by applying deep learning 
techniques. Hu et al. [10] addressed the illumination issues 
in samples using a light compensation algorithm and use 
an improved Faster RCNN detect pecan images, achieving 
a 92.21% mean average precision. Agarwal and Bhargava 
[11]. proposed an SSD-based mango detection model that 
employs Darknet-19 as the feature extractor, achieving an 
average detection accuracy of 92.43%. However, the infer­
ence speed was extremely slow. Li et al. [12] proposed an 
improved efficient fresh grape detection model, YOLO-
Grape, for natural complex scenes by incorporating depth 
wise separable convolutions into YOLOv4-tiny. The model 
achieved a FPS of 81 frames per second and a detection 
accuracy of 89.93%. Tong [13]. proposed a novel fruit 
detection architecture, AOD-Net, built on a YOLOv5s back­
bone. Its key enhancements include: integrating a Polarized 
self-attention (PSA) module into the backbone to suppress 
background noise and thus improve feature extraction and 
localization accuracy; replacing certain convolutional lay­
ers with a lightweight CSPC block to reduce parameters and 
accelerate inference while retaining accuracy; incorporat­
ing a receptive-field attention convolution module in the 
neck to strengthen multi-scale feature learning and gener­
alization; and substituting conventional upsampling with a 
Dysample upsampling strategy to optimize feature fusion, 
thereby improving detection accuracy and robustness. On 
the FruitNet dataset, AOD-Net outperformed the YOLOv5s 
baseline, yielding relative gains of 1.30% in precision, 
1.96% in recall, and 3.95% in mAP; meanwhile, model 
memory footprint decreased by 8.97% and FLOPs were 
reduced from 16.0 G to 11.3 G. Zhou et al. [14] introduced 
the RepGhost module and a decoupled detection head into 
YOLOv7 to better extract the target feature information. 
This model, designed for dragon fruit detection in natural 
environments, improved recall and mAP by 2.1% and 1.6%, 
respectively. Wang et al. [15] proposed an NVM-YOLOv8s 
model for detecting tomatoes at various maturity stages, 
by extracting pixels, they increased the number of miss­
ing samples to balance positive and negative samples. This 
model achieved 91.4% mAP and 85.4% F1-Score. Liu et 
al. [16] proposed an improved persimmon-detection model, 
PerD-YOLOv8, which adopts FasterNet as the backbone 
to strengthen feature extraction in complex backgrounds. 
The model additionally incorporates a P2-level detection 
head and replaces the original PAN-FPN with a BiFPN 
for enhanced multi-scale fusion, thereby improving small-
object detection accuracy while controlling computational 
complexity. Finally, a WIoU loss is employed to better 
handle localization under occlusion and object overlap. 
Experimental results report that PerD-YOLOv8 achieves 
precision, recall, and mAP@0.5 of 95.2%, 90.4%, and 
96.3%, respectively.
Although the aforementioned studies have advanced 
fruit detection, several important challenges remain. First, 
most work has focused on ground-level viewpoints, with 
relatively little attention paid to detection from aerial per­
spectives. Second, existing models still exhibit substantial 
variability in performance when confronted with branch/
leaf occlusion, fruit overlap, and small-object scenarios, 
leading to frequent false negatives and false positives. 
Third, achieving a practical trade-off between detection 
accuracy and real-time inference remains an open problem.

YOLOv8n, an enhanced YOLOv8n network optimized 
for UAV-based detection. We also propose a novel yield-
estimation pipeline that combines SYL-YOLOv8n’s canopy 
detections from UAV imagery with leaf area index (LAI) 
measurements to build a corrected regression model for 
tree-level yield prediction. The main contributions of this

Materials and methods
Training model dataset processing
Peach images data required for the model training were 
collected on August 25th, 26th and 27th, 2024, from an 
orchard in Yiyuan County, Zibo City, Shandong Province, 
China. The images collection process was divided into two 
groups: images captured by ground-based cameras and aer­
ial images taken by UAV. The adoption of this multi-source 
data collection approach is mainly attributed to the fact 
that the small target peach in the aerial images captured by 
UAVs contains relatively limited feature information, which 
fails to meet the requirements for model training. Images of 
peaches taken from a low ground perspective contain more 
abundant feature information. The acquisition devices used 
were Canon m6, iPhone 13, IQOO 3 and DJI Phantom 4. 
During the data collection process, factors such as varying 
light intensity, occlusion of fruits by branches and leaves, 
and the overlapping of fruits were carefully considered. 
Both the camera and UAV were adjusted to capture images 
under different lighting conditions, shooting angles, and dis­
tances. This approach further enhanced the diversity of the 
dataset, a total of 2200 images were ultimately obtained.
The original dataset of 2200 peach images were insuf­
ficient for optimal convergence during model training. To 
improve the effectiveness of training and enhance the mod­
el’s ability to generalize, various data augmentation meth­
ods were utilized. These included random rotation, flipping, 
scaling, brightness adjustments and add mosaics as shown in 
Fig. 1, which expanded the dataset to 9450 images, thereby 
improving the model’s robustness in varied scenarios.
To mitigate overfitting caused by mismatches in feature 
distributions between the training and test sets, we evenly 
distributed UAV-acquired aerial images and ground-based

LAI acquisition and actual production count
To ensure measurement consistency, LAI acquisition was 
conducted during afternoon hours when solar illumination 
was relatively uniform, using a LI-COR LAI-2200 C canopy 
analyzer. The procedure (Fig. 2) was as follows. First, a rep­
resentative sampling area was selected in the mid-canopy of 
each sample tree. Second, three measurements were taken 
sequentially within this area along three different directions 
using the LAI-2200 C to collect raw leaf area index data. 
Finally, the arithmetic mean of the three measurements was 
computed and taken as the LAI value for the individual tree. 
This protocol reduces the impact of single-measurement 
noise and improves the reliability and repeatability of the 
LAI estimates.
Model construction
YOLOv8n
Ultralytics released its next-generation object detection 
algorithm, YOLOv8, in January 2023 [17]. YOLOv8 offers 
five versions: n, s, m, l, and x, where accuracy generally 
increases with model size, at the cost of greater computa­
tional demand. Although YOLOv8n attains slightly lower 
detection accuracy than larger variants, it has the fewest 
parameters and the highest inference speed [18]. For this 
study, the YOLOv8n model was selected as the baseline due 
to its ability to balance detection accuracy with speed.
The YOLOv8n architecture is composed of three main 
components: backbone, neck and head. The backbone is 
responsible for feature extraction and comprises five con­
volutional modules, four C2f blocks and one SPPF module. 
The C2f block draws on the ELAN design of YOLOv7 [19], 
by adjusting channel widths and simplifying internal paths 
it produces richer gradient flows, which enhances the learn­
ing of residual features while keeping the structure compact. 
The neck adopts a path-aggregation design based on PANet 
to perform multi-scale feature aggregation and to strengthen 
information propagation from lower to higher feature lay­
ers [20]. Finally, the head contains three detection branches 
at spatial resolutions 20 × 20, 40 × 40 and 80 × 80, which 
improves the model’s ability to detect targets across a range 
of scales.

Pconv
The PConv is an efficient convolutional structure designed 
to optimize computational resources. As shown in Fig. 4, it 
minimizes unnecessary computations and memory accesses, 
leading to better use of the device’s processing capabilities 
and enhanced spatial feature extraction. The PConv dynam­
ically determines the convolutional kernel’s scope based on 
the data’s validity [21], this strategy notably enhances both 
the model’s real-time processing and its efficiency, without 
compromising accuracy.

EMA attention mechanism
The attention mechanisms are frequently used in object 
detection fields to emphasize target regions while minimiz­
ing the influence of non-target areas, such as the background, 
thus enhancing detection precision [22]. In orchard environ­
ments, unremoved bags used by farmers to cover fruit often 
remain, closely resembling peaches, and heavy occlusion 
from branches and leaves further exacerbates background 
interference. To overcome this challenge, this study pro­
poses the EMA module, which adaptively modifies atten­
tion weights across regions at varying scales. This approach 
fosters local cross-channel interactions within each parallel 
subnet, while maintaining the original channel dimensions 
without using conventional convolutions, thus supporting 
both spatial and channel attention prediction. Moreover, 
in cross-spatial learning step, EMA combines the feature 
maps of two parallel subnets, avoiding the need for exces­
sive sequential operations or deep network layers. By using 
matrix dot product operations to assess pixel correlations, 
the model captures global context, which improves its fea­
ture representation capabilities [23]. This enables more pre­
cise attention on critical features in complex scenes, leading 
to enhanced detection and classification performance. The 
architecture of the EMA is illustrated in Fig. 5.

BiFPN
The YOLOv8n model’s neck employs PANet to aggregate 
features of different resolutions [28]. However, PANet is 
a bottom-up unidirectional feature fusion technique that 
mainly improves positional information. When merging 
inputs at different resolutions, it simply adds them together, 
lacking the original features in the fused information, which 
can result in biased learning during training. To overcome 
this limitation, BiFPN is introduced as a replacement for 
PANet. BiFPN is a feature fusion module designed for 
multi-scale processing, which assigns weights to capture 
the significance of input features across varying resolutions. 
BiFPN improves the representation of low-level features 
and mitigates bias caused by the loss of original informa­
tion. In conjunction with the YOLOv8n structure, this paper 
improves BiFPN. The comparison between the PANeT, the 
original BiFPN and the improved BiFPN in this study is 
shown in the Fig. 8.

The improved Bottleneck is referred to as PE-Bottleneck. 
Finally, the PE-C2f structure is used to replace the C2f mod­
ules in both the YOLOv8 head network and the last layer 
of the backbone network. Compared with directly incor­
porating PConv and the EMA module, the integrated PE-
C2f ensures that the input channels and output channels of 
each layer remain unchanged, thereby reducing the increase 
in redundant parameters. This approach integrates a dual-
branch structure, which not only improves the network’s 
feature extraction capabilities but also reduces the model 
size and GFLOPs, resulting in a more lightweight model.
MPDIoU loss function
In natural scenes, peaches often grow densely, leading 
to frequent instances of overlapping fruits in collected 
images. For such targets, the original CIoU loss in YOLOv8 
becomes less effective in optimizing the predicted boxes. To 
address this challenge, this study introduces the MPDIoU 
loss function. By accelerating the model’s fitting of the 
positional relationship between ground-truth and predicted 
boxes, thereby improving localization accuracy, especially 
in cases of overlapping peaches, enabling more accurate 
target localization [26]. The MPDIoU loss increases sensi­
tivity to overlapping regions by considering multiple paths 
to calculate the distance between the predicted bounding 
boxes and the ground truth bounding boxes. As illustrated in 
Fig. 7, MPDIoU integrates and optimizes various positional 
information from both overlapping and exposed regions 
of peaches, thereby enhancing detection accuracy and 
robustness, particularly in complex scenes. This approach 
facilitates more effective optimization of bounding box pre­
dictions during model training.

P2 small target detection layer
Detecting small objects has been a longstanding challenge 
for object detection algorithms, primarily because feature 
maps often lack sufficient spatial and semantic information. 
Shallow feature maps retain abundant spatial information, 
essential for detecting small objects [29], whereas deep fea­

The design of SYL-YOLOv8n follows clear, comple­
mentary principles. First, PConv is combined with an EMA 
channel attention mechanism to form the PE-C2f block, 
which replaces the C2f modules in both the backbone tail 
and the neck. This design reduces redundant computation 
via partial-channel convolution and, at the same time, uses 
channel attention to amplify weak but relevant fruit features, 
thereby improving feature representation while keeping the 
model compact. Second, the MPDIoU regression loss is 
adopted in place of CIoU; MPDIoU provides a more accu­
rate measure of spatial discrepancy between predicted and 
ground-truth boxes and is particularly effective for local­
izing overlapping targets. Third, a weighted bidirectional 
fusion mechanism BiFPN is introduced to strengthen multi-
scale feature interaction and propagation, which benefits 
the detection of small objects. Finally, a P2 small-object 
detection layer is added in the neck to boost responsiveness 
to small-scale peaches commonly present in UAV images. 
Ablation and comparative experiments show that stacking 
these modules yields concurrent improvements in preci­
sion and recall, with a particularly pronounced reduction in 
missed detections.
Based on the improved detector, we further establish a 
canopy-based yield estimation workflow. UAV imagery 
is used to capture tree canopies, the canopy regions are 
cropped, and SYL-YOLOv8n is employed to automatically 
count visible fruits per tree. A regression model is then built 
to map canopy-visible counts to whole-tree fruit counts. 
Considering that foliage occlusion in nadir UAV views sys­
tematically reduces visible fruit ratios, we introduce LAI as 
a correction factor to compensate for differences in canopy 
coverage among trees at different growth stages. Experi­
mental results indicate that incorporating LAI improves 
both the fit quality and the stability of the regression model, 
demonstrating that LAI has practical value for mitigating 
occlusion bias and enhancing yield-estimation accuracy.
Despite the encouraging results, SYL-YOLOv8n still has 
limitations. The trade-off between detection accuracy and 
images has significantly enhanced, with a notable reduction 
in missed canopy fruit detections. After further incorporat­
ing the LAI coefficient into the SYL-YOLOv8n yield esti­
mation model, R² increased 0.07, rose to 0.88, and RMSE 
dropped to just 11.71. This demonstrates that the inclusion 
of the LAI coefficient further mitigates the impact of foliage 
occlusion, validating the feasibility of this approach.

Discussion
In natural environments, achieving accurate and fast detec­
tion of peach fruits is a key technical requirement for 
UAV-based yield estimation and intelligent orchard man­
agement. Conventional methods suffer obvious limitations 
in scenarios with severe occlusion, small-object detection 
and cross-view generalization, whereas deep learning, 
particularly lightweight object detectors offer a practical 
solution. Building on YOLOv8n, this study proposes SYL-
YOLOv8n. Through coordinated improvements in network 
structure and loss function, the proposed method specifi­
cally addresses branch and leaf occlusion, fruit overlap and 
the small-object characteristics of UAV imagery, producing 
substantial performance gains. Moreover, by integrating

Conclusion
This study presents SYL-YOLOv8n, a lightweight and 
robust object detector developed through systematic 
improvements to YOLOv8n, aimed at efficient peach detec­
tion from both ground and UAV viewpoints. By integrating 
the PE-C2f module, the MPDIoU regression loss, BiFPN 
for weighted multi-scale feature fusion, and an additional P2 
small-object detection layer, the proposed method achieved 
excellent results in multiple comparative experiments. Pre­
cision, recall, mAP and F1 reached 92.7%, 85.0%, 94.3% 
and 88.7%, respectively; the model size is only 5.1 MB and 
the computational cost is 11.1 G, yielding superior overall 
performance relative to several mainstream detectors. Abla­
tion studies further confirm the effectiveness and comple­
mentarity of the proposed modules. The yield estimation 
pipeline based on the improved detector, following the 
“canopy fruits - LAI - whole-tree fruits” paradigm, achieved 
R²=0.88 and RMSE = 14.17 on sample validation, indicat­
ing that incorporating LAI as an occlusion-correction factor 
markedly improves UAV-based yield estimation accuracy. 
Overall, SYL-YOLOv8n demonstrates strong accuracy and 
stability when addressing occlusion, overlap and small-
object challenges in complex natural scenes, providing a 
practical technical solution for UAV-based high-throughput 
yield monitoring and intelligent orchard management.
