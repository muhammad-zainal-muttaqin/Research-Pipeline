Abstract. Ripeness is one of important factors for quality sorting of harvested oil palm fresh fruit bunches (FFB). 
Traditional ripeness classifications using FFB color and number of fruit loose for harvesting have some disadvantages 
especially for high oil palm trees. A laser based imaging system is proposed to substitute the traditional method. In this 
study, ripeness detection simulation of oil palm FFBs was performed. The system composed of two diode lasers with 532 
nm and 680 nm in wavelengths and a CMOS camera which was set on a rotating plate for easy adjustment of laser beam 
hitting FFB. The FFB samples were placed on an aluminum platform with 4 height variations, 1.5 m, 2 m, 2.5 m, and 3 
m.  The relations of reflectance intensities represented by Red Green Blue (RGB) values of the FFB images to the height 
variations and ripeness levels of FFBs with and without laser beam were analyzed. The samples were from Tenera variety 
with 4 ripeness levels called F0, F1, F3, and F4. The results showed that the red component of RGB values were 
dominant for FFBs without laser and with red laser. The average RGB values are higher for F3 (ripe) level and F4 
(overripe). Imaging with green laser showed consistency. Imaging methods using laser was able to differentiate ripeness 
levels of oil palm fresh fruit bunch, it could be applied for future remote detection of oil palm FFB ripeness.  
INTRODUCTION   
Ripeness determination or detection of oil palm fruits in recent decades becomes motivating and valuable 
research topics.  Classification of oil palm fresh fruit bunches (FFBs) based on their ripeness is very important for 
estimation of oil content. Traditional methods using human vision to observe color changes during ripening and 
counting number of fruits loose from the FFBs are not efficient methods, lack of objectivity, and high labor cost. 
Detection of FFB ripeness during harvesting has some difficulties especially for high oil palm trees. Many 
researches have been dedicated to assess ripeness and qualities of oil palm FFBs especially using computer vision 
methods. Razali et al. (2012) have reviewed many techniques that have been developed for assessing the ripeness 
and qualities of oil palm FFBs [1]. Most of the researches use computer vision methods which emphasize on image 
processing and . Computer visions has been approved being one of reliable methods for sorting and grading fruits 
[2]. Computer vision methods have been applied for estimating ripeness of oil palm FFBs [3,4]. Researches in this 
field have purposes to obtain a non-destructive method which is safe, low cost, reliable, simple, and could be used 
by farmer or oil palm industries. 
Diode laser and light emitting diode (LD and LED) have been used in agriculture especially for detections and 
measurements.  Laser light is preferred because of its properties which are intense and directional hence it can reach 
long distance. It also has coherence and monochromatic light hence can be applied in many spectroscopy methods.

Recently, Laser has been used for fruit quality assessment using interaction of light with biomatter via reflection, 
scattering, fluorescence process [5]. Laser applications in agriculture become intense because of the availability 
diode laser with wavelength, power, and price variation. Monochromatic and powerful light emitting diodes have 
also been available recently and been used as replacement some of diode lasers due to their lower cost. LD and LED 
have been used in spectroscopic techniques to study tropical fruit ripening [6] and laser used in laser speckle 
imaging for assessment of fruits during shelf-life storage [7].  
Precision agriculture (PA) is a new term defined as agriculture using cutting edge technology. Farmers and 
agricultural industries face many problems that suppress productivity and increase operational cost such as plant 
diseases, manual and traditional methods of harvesting, sorting, and grading agricultural products, and postharvest 
storage. Recently, there are four categories of PA researches, early disease detection, fruit detection for robotic 
harvesters, weed detection, and postharvest activities (ripeness and quality). Detection using electromagnetic wave 
is preferable due to non destructive manner [8].  Researches of fruit detection on tree have been developed to build 
systems or robots which are fast, reliable, and cost effective for harvesting process. Some methods have been 
proposed such as computer vision, hyperspectral imaging, LIDAR, NIR spectroscopy, even magnetic resonance 
imaging [9].  Computer vision method has been used to locate fruit on trees, image processing techniques also 
developed [10]. Computer vision methods relies on image processing techniques to extract information of fruit such 
as color and geometry features that have been used for sorting and grading fruits in industries. Other method such as 
hyperspectral imaging is needed especially if internal attributes of fruits are investigated. Hyperspectral imaging 
techniques is more powerful than computer visions which enable one to obtain spectral and spatial information [9]. 
Hyperspectral reflectance imaging has been used to predict firmness and soluble solids content of blueberries [11]. 
Most hyperspectral imaging methods use lamp light or sunlight as light source.  Laser light has been used in 
Hyperspectral laser-induced fluorescence imaging technique for assessing apple fruit quality [12]. Other 
experiments using diode lasers in detection fruit on tree are using a laser-based computer vision system for 
automatic fruit recognition [13] and using multisensory techniques to detect fruits on trees which used lamp light as 
illumination source [14]. 
Up to now, most researches to assess ripeness stages and maturity of intact oil palms FFB on trees were done 
using computer vision. The ripeness and maturity information are needed in order to harvest the good quality and 
ripe FFB. Lamps with certain wavelength have been used to assess the optical characteristics of FFB with three 
spectrum regions influence for Harvest Decision [15].  
This work was performed to simulate the ripeness detection of oil palm bunches (FFB) on tress with certain 
heights. The experiment was done indoor. The imaging system was made of diode lasers with two different 
wavelengths, a CMOS camera, and some optical components. The platform for placing the FFB samples at certain 
height was built using aluminium corrugated bars. This study was a preliminary study for using hyperspectral 
imaging, laser beam added to an imaging system for oil palm FFB. Diode lasers and a board CMOS camera were 
used to create a portable and compact system in the long run. Oil palm FFB images were acquired by the camera, 
segmented, and pre-processed using ImageJ software to extract feature of Red Green Blue intensity [16]. The 
research has some purposes. First is to study a relation between laser induced reflectance (measured by red green 
blue) intensities and ripeness levels of oil palm FFBs. Second is to study the effect of laser wavelength to the 
relation. Third is to study the effect of height varieties to the intensity profile. The samples were oil palm FFBs from 
Tenera variety with four ripeness levels. The rest of this article will explain about the material and methods, results 
and discussion, conclusion and future works. 
MATERIALS AND METHODOLOGY 
Sample Preparation 
For the present study, the samples were oil palm fresh fruit bunches (FFB) from Tenera variety  above 10 year 
old which consisted of 4 ripeness stages, called fraction based on color and number of loose fruits, F0 and F1 (under 
ripe stage),  F3  (ripe), and F4 (over ripe). Number of fractions found during harvesting depends on situations, only 
4-5 fractions are found harvested. The ripeness fractions were classified by an experience harvester during 
harvesting.  Each level of samples had 2-3 FFB duplicates. After taken from oil palm plantations, the samples were 
cleaned from dirt, and weighted. All samples were marked at basal according to their ripeness level and ready to be 
used.  The samples are stored in an air conditioned room to avoid further ripening. The FFB images wee recorded 
within 24 hours after harvesting.

Research Method 
The laser based imaging system that was proposed for ripeness detection of oil palm FFBs consisted of an 
aluminum platform with stages at height variations of 1.5 m – 3 m (50 cm increment) for FFB samples and a 
detection system as shown in Figure 1a.  The detection system consists of a CMOS camera, two diode lasers with 
wavelength of 650 nm and 532 nm respectively, ND filters, two concave lenses, as shown in Fig. 1b.  The diode 
lasers, concave lenses, Neutral Density filters, were stated on an anodized 3 inch u 3 inch aluminum board equipped 
with rails and post holder for easy alignment. The aluminum board was staged on a small rotating plate for easy 
angle adjustment of laser beam hitting FFBs on a certain height on the platform.  The camera and laser system was 
positioned on a table with 1 m height and 2.2 meter distance from the platform.  The camera used was a 1/3” CMOS 
color USB board camera accompanied by camera lens (DFM 22BUC03-ML Imaging Source), 744 u 480 pixel 
sensors (0.4 MP), 5 fps and 76 fps speed, and 96 dpi.  This camera is accompanied by capture software for recording 
images and saving in many file formats.  A 50 mm concave lens was used to expand the laser beam of each laser 
hence the beam sizes were about 6 cm for red laser and 10 cm for green laser. Both lasers used a set of ND filters to 
decrease the laser power, 2.5 mW for red laser and 2.8 mW for green laser.  The detection using red laser used a 2 
band color filter of blue and red ranges on the camera while the green laser used a green color filter.   
There were 3 experiments performed in this study, using 3 types of illumination on the FFBs. First was without 
laser, using room light only. The room light intensities measured at the height variations were 9, 11, 16, 18  (lx) for 
1.5 m, 2 m, 2.5 m, 3 m, respectively. The second was using a 650 nm laser light. The third was using a 532 laser 
light. The purposes of using laser light with 650 nm and 532 nm were to examine the reflectance light due to 
interaction of these laser lights with pigment molecule such as anthocyanin and chlorophyll content of oil palm fruits 
(Hyperspectral imaging). For each illumination type, images were recorded for each height variation, three times for 
each FFB, front side and back, three FFBs for each ripeness level. The FFBs were placed on the platform which 
surrounded by white sheet to obtain white background. Images of front side of FFB are taken using the accompany 
software of the camera and saved in bitmap files for preprocessing step.  Figure 1c shows the laser beams illuminate 
the FFB.

Image Processing 
ImageJ software is a non-commercial software which is widely used in scientific researches. ImageJ is a Java-
based open source software which was developed for first time to analyze images at National Institute of Health 
(NIH), USA. ImageJ software is widely used in Biology and Medicine for image processing. Recently, it is being 
used in computer vision method for fruits, vegetables, food as available image processing software. ImageJ has been 
used for lemon and tomatoes classification [17].  ImageJ can work at any operating system. The uniqueness of this

software is that Wayne Rasband, the coauthor, only write the program core, many groups have written short -add on 
program (called plugins) intended to increase its functionality for many image processing problems.  
In this study, ImageJ 1.47v software was used to get the average RGB values, There are 2 features in ImageJ 
software were used for image processing.  There were two step image processing has been done, segmentation 
feature extraction [9]. The features extracted were the intensity or value for RGB components using RGB plot 
feature and average RGB Intensity using (R+G+B)/3. The segmentation process was performed first using 
thresholding to reduce background.  Regions of interest (ROI) for the 3 experiments were different. Oval shape 
wand tool was used to draw ROI on the threshold images for each experiment. The threshold images for experiment 
without laser were drawn according to the edge of FFB image excluded background. The size of ROI was 58 u 79 
pixels for red laser and 79 u 102 for green laser.  
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
An imaging system using laser was proposed to simulate the ripeness detection of oil palm fresh fruit bunches 
(FFBs). From this study, images of oil palm FFBs on a platform with height variation were recorded by a CMOS 
camera. From the images, Red Green Blue (RGB) values or intensities were calculated using ImageJ software. 
Relations between Red Green Blue (RGB) values or intensities and ripeness levels, underripe, ripe, and overripe 
were obtained and analyzed for 3 situations where light impinged on the FFB without laser, with red laser, and green 
laser. The camera speed used was 5 frame per second (fps). 
Figure 2 shows the graphs of color component of FFB image of front FFBs for 4 ripeness stages at 1.5 m and 3 
m height for experiment without laser. In Figure 2a, red components more dominated and slightly higher for F4 than 
F3 fractions since the FFB color change from dark red to orange. This is consistent with other researches where the 
red component for ripe FFBs is slightly higher [16]. The intensities decrease as the FFBs get higher for F1 and F2 
fraction as shown on Figure 2b.  The drawback found for higher heights was the ability of the camera to adjust 
intensity automatically due to surrounding light intensity change. The intensity profile for 2 m and 2.5 m height 
were the same as for 1.5 m but lower intensity.

CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORKS 
This study was performed to simulate the ripeness detection of oil palm bunches on tress. This study was a 
preliminary study for using laser beam added to an imaging system. There are some aspects needs to be addressed 
for future research due to the complexities of FFB structures, varieties, and environment lighting. More image 
processing algorithms are needed to obtain relations for ripeness, RGB intensities, spectral intensities, laser-induced 
fluorescence intensity,  and chemical contents of oil palm fruits. Some findings can be summarized from this study. 
The results show that the average RGB intensities of oil palm images are higher for F3 and F4. The intensities 
decrease as the height increase except for without and with red laser experiment. Green laser can differentiate the 
ripeness level better than red laser. This could be due to pigment contents of the FFB which responsible for purple 
colour and orange colour.
