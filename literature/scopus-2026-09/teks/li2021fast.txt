A B S T R A C T  
In agriculture, fruit picking robots on the ground have difficulty adapting to the terrain conditions of mountain 
orchards and cannot pick longan fruit from tall longan trees. In this paper, aiming to allow picking of longan fruit 
by unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), a deep learning-based scheme to quickly and accurately detect and locate 
suitable picking points on fruit branches is proposed. The scheme includes a UAV fuzzy image preprocessing 
method, longan detection based on a convolutional neural network (CNN), red, green, blue and depth (RGB-D) 
information fusion and an accurate target location strategy. First, the UAV is equipped with an Intel Realsense 
D455 camera, which collects longan images from the front for training and testing the model. Second, the 
lightweight MobileNet backbone network is used to improve the performance of the You Only Look Once version 
4 (YOLOv4) model in feature extraction. The results for the test set show that compared with the classical feature 
pyramid network (FPN), YOLOv3 and YOLOv4 models, this model reduces the computation, parameters and 
detection time of the model. Compared with MobileNet single-shot multibox detector (MobileNet-SSD) and 
YOLOv4-tiny, this model exhibits improved detection accuracy. Third, according to the target detection result 
map, a strategy is formulated to accurately determine the suitable picking point on the main branch of the result. 
Finally, the performance of the improved model and picking platform in the harvest scene is evaluated by 
performing picking experiments in a longan orchard. In summary, we fully exploit the advantages of the com­
bination of UAVs, RGB-D cameras and CNNs to improve the speed and accuracy of target detection and location 
for longan picking by UAVs based on vision.   
1. Introduction 
Longan is a famous specialty fruit in tropical and subtropical regions 
that has important economic value and is widely planted in hilly areas of 
Guangxi, Guangdong, Fujian, Hainan and Taiwan Provinces. Due to the 
short fruiting period, picking longan in time is very important for a 
successful harvest. At present, manual harvesting is mainly used. This 
kind of harvesting is laborious and time-consuming, and with the 
transfer of the rural labour force to non-agricultural industries, the 
surplus rural labour force gradually decreases. Therefore, it is of eco­
nomic value to develop an agricultural robot that can automatically pick 
longan. Some researchers have developed fruit harvesting robots 
mounted on ground vehicles (Zhang et al., 2020; Kang and Chen, 2020; 
Li et al., 2020; Loukatos et al., 2021) and designed mechanical damage 
assessment methods for use during harvesting (Beyaz, 2018). On this 
basis, according to the terrain conditions of mountain orchards and the 
growth characteristics of bunches of fruits on tall longan trees, it is 
necessary to develop more suitable harvesting robots. 
In recent years, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have been widely 
used in agricultural production, including plant protection (Tetila et al., 
2020; Zhou et al., 2020), crop monitoring (Fernando et al., 2018; Feng 
et al., 2020a,b) and crop yield evaluation (Feng et al., 2020a,b; Sumesh 
et al., 2021). Compared with ground mobile machinery, UAVs have the 
advantages of good terrain adaptability and high efficiency. Therefore, 
UAVs can be used to perform picking tasks in unstructured orchard

environments. At present, determining how to carry a portable camera 
on a UAV to collect images and how to accurately detect and locate fruits 
based on deep learning is the key to the success of fruit picking by UAVs 
based on vision. 
Researchers have applied monocular cameras to UAVs, which can 
help UAVs detect moving targets in RGB images (Minaeian et al., 2018). 
However, the light-sensitive and time-consuming functions limit their 
performance in outdoor applications. With the development of sensor 
integration and image processing technology, RGB-D cameras are 
becoming less expensive and are suitable for robots to locate targets in 
higher dimensions. Recently, RGB-D cameras (such as the Intel Real­
sense D455 camera) have improved in terms of their lightness, high 
precision and insensitivity to light, and they show great potential to 
become effective tools for collecting orchard image data. In addition to 
the RGB information of the three red, green and blue channels, the RGB- 
D camera also provides a depth information channel, which makes it 
possible to obtain the colour, contour and position characteristics of the 
target at the same time. 
Most longan orchards have rugged terrain. Due to the light weight of 
UAVs, UAVs are easily affected by local circulation and airflow in the 
process of acquiring images, resulting in unstable attitudes of UAVs in 
the air. At this time, the RGB-D camera is likely to collect fuzzy longan 
images, which will lead to inaccurate target detection and positioning. 
Therefore, it is necessary to detect images captured by the RGB-D 
camera in real time to judge whether they are blurred images. At pre­
sent, there are many methods for automatic detection of fuzzy images, 
but most of the algorithms use existing images as a reference, calculate 
the relationship between the images to be detected and reference images 
according to certain mathematical models, and construct an evaluation 
system for fuzzy image detection (Gong et al., 2018; Liu et al., 2019a,b). 
However, the images obtained by UAVs on different fruit trees are 
different, and reference images are lacking, so it is difficult to accurately 
filter out blurred images. 
How to accurately detect and locate the position of longan from the 
collected image data is still a major challenge. Traditional image pro­
cessing algorithms are mainly based on machine learning theory, which 
quantifies RGB, near infrared, thermal infrared and other images; ana­
lyses colour, shape, texture, reflection spectra and other characteristics; 
and carries out classification recognition and regression diagnosis. Its 
recognition and detection accuracy is not high (Patrícioa and Riederb, 
2018; Gewali et al., 2019). With the development of convolutional 
neural networks (CNNs) and object detection algorithms, they are 
increasingly used to process image data collected in agricultural appli­
cations (Kamilaris and Prenafeta-Boldú, 2018), including fruit detection 
and counting (Bargoti and Underwood, 2017; Liu et al., 2019a,b; Fu 
et al., 2020; Xiong et al., 2020; Gao et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2020), plant 
recognition (Dyrmann et al., 2017; Ahmad et al., 2021), pest identifi­
cation and diagnosis (Ghosal et al., 2018; Anagnostis et al., 2021; Singh 
et al., 2021), remote sensing area classification and detection (Ma et al., 
2019; Paoletti et al., 2019), fruit in vivo detection and product classi­
fication (Ak et al., 2019), animal identification and posture detection 
(Norouzzadeh et al., 2018). Compared with traditional image processing 
algorithms, the multilayer structure of a deep learning model can solve 
complex nonlinear problems well by combining the underlying features 
to form abstract high-level representation attribute categories or fea­
tures and has strong robustness in agricultural applications and high 
performance in target detection (Alpaydin, 2021). 
The combination of deep learning algorithm and RGB-D camera to 
accurately detect and locate the position of fruit is an appropriate 
strategy. Many researchers are committed to improving the accuracy of 
object detection by fusing all the information from the four channels 
(Loghmani et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2020a,b). For example, a single 
stream recursive convolutional neural network (SSRCNN) and a deep 
recursive convolutional neural network (DRCNN) have been proposed 
for detecting and rendering salient objects in RGB-D images (Liu et al., 
2019a,b). The evaluation of four datasets in a previous study shows that 
the proposed method is excellent in distinguishing depth features and 
fusing RGB and depth information. Existing studies mainly use depth 
information to improve classification accuracy. However, there is no 
report on implementing deep learning and deep cameras on UAVs to 
accurately detect and locate longan in agriculture. 
To promote the application of UAVs in longan picking, we developed 
a novel solution. This scheme will help drones pick longan based on 
vision by improving the speed and accuracy of target detection and 
location in natural environments. The contributions of this research can 
be summarized as follows: 
a) A dataset of longan images collected by a UAV is constructed to 
train and evaluate the target detection model. 
b) A comprehensive solution is proposed, including a UAV fuzzy 
image preprocessing method, longan detection based on a CNN, RGB-D 
information fusion and an accurate target location strategy. 
c) Adopting a lightweight MobileNet backbone network improves 
the performance of the You Only Look Once version 4 (YOLOv4) model 
in feature extraction and reduces the number of calculations and pa­
rameters in the model, making it more suitable to perform the target 
detection task on a UAV onboard computer. 
d) Taking a cluster of fruits on a longan tree as an example, according 
to the attributes of longan fruiting on the main branches, the precise 
positioning strategy of suitable cutting points on fruit branches is elab­
orated in detail. 
e) The experimental scheme and evaluation index were designed in a 
real longan orchard environment to measure the performance of the 
improved target detection model and picking platform. 
The article is organized as follows: the materials are presented in 
Section 2. The systems and methods are described in Section 3, and the 
results and discussion are detailed in Section 4. Section 5 concludes the 
paper.

2.2. Image preprocessing 
To train the CNN model under supervised learning, aiming at RGB 
images collected by the UAV, fuzzy images are identified and removed, 
clear images are randomly cropped and normalized, and the categories 
and bounding boxes of each sample are manually marked in advance. 
The specific process is shown in Fig. 2.  
(1) Fuzzy image recognition 
To prevent the influence of fuzzy images collected by the UAV on 
longan detection and location, in this paper, the Laplacian of Gaussian 
(LoG) operator is used to extract the edge features of branches, leaves 
and fruits and generate the grey image of edge features. The LoG oper­
ator is an extraction algorithm generated by combining Gaussian 
filtering and Laplace edge detection, so the image edge positioning ac­
curacy is high, and the continuity is good. The grey variance of the edge 
feature grey map reflects the richness of edge features in the image. 
Because the edge features of fuzzy parts cannot be extracted, holes 
appear, and the extracted edge features are sparse, yielding a small grey 
variance. Based on this principle, if the collected image is processed by 
the LoG operator and the calculated grey variance is lower than the 
preset threshold k, it can be automatically judged as a blurred image. It 
is necessary to re-acquire the image before making the judgement. Until 
the grey variance in the acquired image is higher than the threshold 
value k, it can be used as an input image for longan detection and 
location.  
(2) Randomly cropping the image 
To ensure the diversity of training samples, a self-programmed 
random cropping algorithm is used to expand the image data. The spe­
cific steps are as follows: first, a point within 200 pixels from the centre 
of the image is randomly determined, then four points between 200 and 
440 pixels in four directions around the central point are randomly 
selected, and finally, the images are cropped with the largest circum­
scribed rectangle composed of these four points as the cropping 
boundary. After expanding the dataset, 1050 longan images were 
obtained.  
(3) Image normalization 
Because the inconsistency of image size may adversely affect the 
model training process, image normalization technology is used to 
preprocess the amplified images. Image normalization refers to the 
process of transforming images of different sizes into a fixed size by a 
series of standard processing transformations. The standard trans­
formation process is to initially scale the image with a fixed aspect ratio 
until the long side of the image reaches 1280 pixels, and if the remaining 
side is less than 1280 pixels, the two sides are supplemented with 
0 pixels, thus obtaining a standard image with a size of 1280 × 1280 
pixels.  
(4) Image annotation 
Each of the 1050 images were manually annotated. To draw the 
bounding boxes, we followed the guidelines of the reference Pascal VOC 
2010 Challenge (Everingham et al., 2010). Three classification labels are 
defined:  
(1) string_fruit: a cluster of longan fruits from the first branch to the 
last branch on a fruiting parent branch.  
(2) simple_fruit: a single fruit of a string fruit.  
(3) fruit_branch: the main stem of a fruiting main branch suitable for 
cutting. 
LabelImg was used for manual marking, and the marking informa­
tion was saved in an XML file. The annotation information mainly in­
cludes the size of the image, the target category and the coordinate 
information of the specific position of the target area and serves as an 
information file for reading the target features during model training.

3. Systems and methods 
In this section, first, aiming at the colour and depth images collected 
by the RGB-D camera on the UAV, a solution of how to quickly and 
accurately detect and locate longan from these images is proposed. In 
the following four parts, the UAV fuzzy image processing method, object 
detection algorithm and parameter design, evaluation index and accu­
rate target positioning strategy are introduced. 
3.1. Overall solution 
To enable the UAV to detect and locate longan strings quickly and 
accurately, a system solution is developed, as shown in Fig. 3. First, 
colour and depth images of longan are captured from far and close 
distances by the RGB-D camera on the UAV. For colour images, a fuzzy 
image recognition algorithm is used to automatically identify and 
remove fuzzy images, and clear colour images are preprocessed and 
labelled as a dataset. Then, the dataset is divided into a training set and a 
test set at a ratio of 4:1. The training set is used to train the object 
detection algorithm, and the model with three kinds of target weight 
parameters is obtained. The test set is used to test the performance of the 
model. When picking longan, the UAV feeds RGB images into the trained 
CNN model to obtain the classification and boundary box information of 
string_fruit, simple_fruit and fruit_branch. Finally, the results of target 
detection are mapped to the optimized depth image to extract the con­
tours and spatial information of the three kinds of targets. By fusing the 
results of target detection and extracted depth information, the UAV can 
determine the relative position between the UAV and the real fruit_­
branch according to the unique attributes of the three kinds of targets 
(such as contour and position), determine the position of the picking 
point, and calculate the path information with the smallest distance. 
3.2. Fuzzy image processing 
UAVs are easily affected by local circulation and airflow in the 
process of acquiring images, which leads to unstable attitudes of UAVs in 
the air. When this occurs, the collected fruit images are inevitably 
blurred. To improve the accuracy of target detection and positioning of 
fruit picking by UAVs, it is necessary to judge whether the collected 
images are clear in real time. In RGB images collected by UAVs, the edge 
features of branches, leaves and fruits contain much information, which 
is an important indicator of image clarity. If the edge features are 
obvious, then the image is clear. Blurred edge features correspond to 
blurred images. Therefore, the method of extracting edge features can be 
used to judge fuzzy images. 
In this paper, the LoG operator is used to extract edge features of 
branches, leaves and fruits. The LoG operator is an extraction algorithm 
generated by combining Gaussian filtering and Laplace edge detection. 
The main function of the Laplace algorithm is to highlight the edge and 
contour of the grey level in the image and reduce the area where the grey 
level changes slowly. The Gaussian filter is a linear smoothing filter that 
is suitable for eliminating Gaussian noise and widely used in image 
denoising. The Gaussian filter function is defined as:

The LoG operator is used to extract the edge features of branches, 
leaves and fruits of UAV images, the mean grey value of the extracted 
image edge features is calculated, and the total variance value of the 
image is calculated by using the calculated mean grey value and the grey 
value of each pixel. Separate threshold k values are set for the images 
that are 500–800 mm (close shot) and 800–1200 mm (distant shot) from 
longan strings. If the image variance is lower than the predefined 
threshold k, then the image can be regarded as fuzzy, and if it is higher 
than the threshold k, the image can be regarded as clear. Automatic 
fuzzy image recognition is carried out by using the acquired longan 
image. The programming language is Python 3.7.3, the running envi­
ronment is an Ubuntu 16.04 system, the memory is 16.00 GB, and the 
acquired UAV image size is 1280 × 720 pixels. 
In the process of longan image acquisition, due to the strong wind in 
the orchard, the acquired images can be blurred. To let the program 
automatically judge whether an image is blurred, it is necessary to set a 
fuzzy threshold k. Therefore, 100 UAV images in close range and distant 
range are randomly selected, the edge features of the images are 
extracted by the LoG operator, and the grey value of the edge features is 
calculated, with the minimum grey value as the threshold. This method 
is used to process the 550 collected UAV images and calculate their grey 
variance for inspection. When the grey variance is greater than the set 
threshold, and the images are judged to be clear. When the grey variance 
is less than the set threshold, the image is judged to be fuzzy, and the 
fuzzy image is automatically marked. Automatic inspection of 49 blur­
red images, accounting for 8.9% of the total, was confirmed manually, 
and the correct rate of inspection was 100%, which shows that this 
method is reliable for UAV blurred image inspection.

culations and parameters, lightweight MobileNet single-shot multibox 
detector (MobileNet-SSD) and MobileNet-YOLOv4 models are built as

UAV airborne computers are optimized. In this section, the network 
characteristics of the six models are briefly introduced first, and then 
cluster analysis is carried out on the longan dataset to design the training 
parameters of the models. 
FPN (Lin et al., 2017) upsamples the high-level features and connects 
low-level features from top to bottom, which greatly improves the per­
formance of small target detection without increasing the computational 
complexity of the original model. 
YOLOv3 (Redmon and Farhadi, 2018) is a one-stage full convolution 
network model that directly generates the class probability and position 
coordinate values of objects and directly obtains the final detection 
result after a single detection. The first 52 layers of Darknet-53 are used 
(there is no fully connected layer), and a large number of layer-hopping 
connections of residuals are used. To reduce the negative gradient effect 
caused by pooling, convolution with a step size of 2 is used for down­
sampling. To enhance the accuracy of small target detection, YOLOv3 
adopts upsampling similar to FPN and the fusion method (finally, three 
scales are fused, the sizes of which are 13 × 13, 26 × 26 and 52 × 52) 
and performs detection on a multiple scale feature map. YOLOv3 uses 
logistic regression to identify targets. It has been reported that this 
single-stage method can improve the detection speed (Soviany and 
Ionescu, 2019). 
Based on the original YOLO target detection architecture, YOLOv4 
adopts the best optimization strategy in the CNN field in recent years 
and has different degrees of optimization in data processing, backbone 
networks, network training, activation functions, loss functions and 
other aspects (Bochkovskiy et al., 2020). By comparing the parameters 
of three backbone networks, as shown in Table 2, CSPDarknet53 is su­
perior to other backbone networks in terms of parameters and trans­
mission speed. YOLOv4 introduced the spatial pyramid pooling (SPP) 
module in CSPDarknet53 (He et al., 2015), which can significantly 
improve the size of the receptive field and extract the most important 
upper and lower features, and the network processing speed has not 
dropped significantly. In addition, YOLOv4 also uses a path aggregation 
network (PAN) (Liu et al., 2018) instead of FPN for multi-channel 
feature fusion. The feature information below FPN needs to go 
through a multi-layer network to reach the top of the pyramid, which 
may lead to information loss. Bottom-up path enhancement is added to 
the PAN structure, which can prevent the problem of information loss. 
The information obtained after feature map splicing has both bottom 
features and semantics. 
On the basis of YOLOv4, the Mish activation function in the feature 
extraction layer is removed, a feature pyramid is adopted in the feature 
enhancement layer, and downsampling is removed, thus obtaining the 
YOLOv4-tiny lightweight model (Wang et al., 2020a,b). It has ten times 
fewer training parameters than YOLOv4. Therefore, YOLOv4-tiny loads 
the model faster. 
When deploying a CNN on a UAV airborne computer to realize target 
detection, it is necessary to reduce the number of calculations and pa­
rameters while ensuring high accuracy. Therefore, we try to use light­
weight models as feature extraction networks to further improve the 
performance of classical models. 
The MobileNet model is a lightweight deep neural network proposed 
by Google for embedded devices such as mobile phones, and its core idea 
is depthwise separable convolution (Howard et al., 2020). The latest 
MobileNetv3 integrates four characteristics (depthwise separable con­
volutions, the inverted residual with linear bottleneck, lightweight 
attention model and h-swipe instead of swipe function) that greatly 
reduce the computation and parameters of the model. Therefore, the 
main part of the MobileNet model was used to replace the feature 
extraction part of the SSD model and YOLOv4 model, and the 
MobileNet-SSD and MobileNet-YOLOv4 models were constructed, 
which were trained and tested on the longan dataset. 
In the training stage of the model, the image sizes of the six network 
input layers are all 1280 × 1280 pixels, the intersection over union 
(IOU) is uniformly set to 0.5, and 500 epoch iterations are trained. In 
FPN model training, the learning rate is set to 0.001, and the momentum 
is 0.9. In the YOLOv3, YOLOv4, YOLOv4-tiny, MobileNet-SSD and 
MobileNet-YOLOv4 models, the initial learning rate is set to 0.0001, and 
the learning rate at the end of training is set to 0.000001. To verify 
dataset splitting, 90–10 training is used. In addition, to enable the model 
to detect multiple targets in one window, anchor boxes with different 
aspect ratios and sizes need to be set. The scatter diagram in Fig. 6 is 
obtained by k-means clustering. The horizontal and vertical coordinates

Precision is also called accuracy, which means how many of the 
positive examples are true examples, and it is based on the prediction 
results. Recall is also called the recall rate, which indicates how many 
positive examples in the sample are predicted to be correct. It refers to 
the original sample. Precision and recall are a pair of conflicting mea­
sures. In general, when precision is high, recall tends to be low, and 
when recall is high, precision tends to be low. In a classifier, if you want 
a higher precision, then the threshold should be set higher. Only in this 
way can you have a higher degree of certainty that the positive example 
of the prediction is the true example. When the threshold is set high, 
then the number of positive examples is less, the number of real exam­
ples is less, and not all positive examples can be found. Accuracy reflects 
the classifier’s ability to judge the entire sample (that is, it can determine 
positive as positive and negative as negative), and it is the proportion of 
correctly classified samples to the total number of samples. The calcu­

AP and mAP are comprehensive evaluation indicators that are pro­
posed to solve the single-point value limitation of indicators such as 
precision and recall. AP is an indicator that can reflect the global per­
formance of the model. There are two ways to calculate the AP: one is 
the 11-point interpolation method, which sets 11 thresholds for the 
recall rate and then calculates the maximum precision for recall greater 
than each threshold, and the average of the 11 precision values is the AP 
value, which is the calculation method used by the PASCAL VOC chal­
lenge before 2010; the other is the ranking method. The ranking method 
first assumes that there are M positive examples in N samples and M 
recall values (1/M, 2/M…, M/M). For each recall value r, the maximum 
precision corresponding to (r’ >= r) is first calculated, and then the M 
precision values are averaged to obtain the final AP value. The ranking 
method is the calculation method used by the PASCAL VOC challenge 
after 2010. After calculating the AP value, the average value of all APs 
can be obtained to determine the mAP value.

MobileNet backbone network is used to improve the performance of the

detection model and picking platform in the actual harvest scene, a 
picking experiment was performed in a longan orchard. In summary, we 
make full use of the advantages of CNNs to make them more suitable for 
performing target detection tasks on UAV airborne computers,

longan picking by drones based on vision. 
Considering the complexity of dynamic changes in longan orchard 
scenes and the influence of abnormal weather conditions, there are still 
some limitations in this work, and it is necessary to further improve the 
ability of the target detection model to resist environmental interfer­
ence. In future work, we will continue to optimize the details of the 
solution, such as the investigation for hardware (high performance 
image analysis computers) and software architectures (faster image 
analysis models, such as YOLOv5) capable for even faster processing and 
response times, identifying sick or mechanically damaged fruits during 
harvesting (Beyaz et al., 2010) and deciding whether to harvest them, so 
as to obtain better fruit branch cutting accuracy and speed, and promote 
the automation of UAVs for picking longan.
