A B S T R A C T  
In the process of farm automation, fruit detection is the basis and guarantee for yield prediction, automatic 
picking, and other orchard operations. RGB images can only obtain the two-dimensional information of the 
scene, which is not sufficient to effectively distinguish fruits that are dense growth and occlusion by branches and 
leaves. With the development of depth sensors, using RGB-D images with more complementary information can 
boost the performance of fruit detection. However, due to the nature of sensors and scene configurations, the 
quality of outdoor depth images is poor, posing a challenge when fusing RGB-D features. Therefore, this paper 
proposes an end-to-end RGB-D object detection network, termed as noise-tolerant feature fusion network (NT- 
FFN), to utilize the outdoor multi-modal data properly and improve the detection accuracy. Specifically, the NT- 
FFN first uses two structurally identical feature extractors to extract single-modal (color and depth) features, 
which is the base of the subsequent feature fusion. Then, to avoid introducing too much depth noise and focus the 
perception on the important part of the features, an attention-based fusion module is designed to adaptively fuse 
the multi-modal features. Finally, multi-scale features from the color images and the fusion modules are used to 
predict object position, which not only improves the network’s ability to detect multi-scale objects but also 
further enhances the noise immunity of the network. In addition, this paper constructs an RGB-D citrus fruit 
dataset, which contributes to comprehensively evaluating the proposed network. Evaluation metrics on the 
dataset show that the NT-FFN achieves an AP50 of 95.4% with a real-time speed, which outperforms single-modal 
methods, common multi-modal fusion strategies, and advanced multi-modal detection methods. The proposed 
NT-FFN also achieves excellent detection results in other fruit detection tasks, which verifies its generalization 
ability. This study provides the possibility and foundation for performing multi-modal information fusion in 
outdoor fruit detection.   
1. Introduction 
Citrus is one of the most popular fruits in the world. It has the largest 
planted area in China and plays a crucial role in the country’s economic 
development. However, China is not a strong country in citrus produc­
tion. The level of agricultural informatization in China is still at a low 
level in the initial stage. There are many real-world problems such as 
high costs, a shortage of skilled labor, and low efficiency. In recent years, 
the orchard automatic operation has long been a question of great in­
terest in a wide range of agriculture modernization. The automatic 
detection results can not only provide a reliable action basis for fruit 
picking robots but also be the basis of orchard production estimation, 
fruit sorting, and other orchard operations. As a result, whether the 
target fruit can be accurately detected directly affects the orchard 
automation operation. 
Over the years, different methods and techniques have been devel­
oped to tackle automatic fruit detection. Most previous research on fruit 
detection has been explored based on RGB images by using traditional 
methods and accumulated a lot of research achievements (Ji et al., 2012; 
Lu and Sang, 2015; Zhao et al., 2016; Lin et al. 2020). These algorithms 
mainly rely on manually designed feature extractors such as SIFT and 
classification models such as SVM. Lu and Sang (2015) proposed a citrus 
fruit detection method by analyzing the salient edges of the chromatic 
aberration map of R and B channels in RGB images. The proposed 
method detected citrus fruits within tree canopies under variable illu­
mination and different degrees of occlusion. Zhao et al. (2016) devel­
oped an approach to detect ripe tomatoes in the greenhouse, which can 
correctly detect over 96% of ripe tomatoes. Conversely, Lin et al. (2020)

The traditional detection algorithms have great limitations. Hand- 
crafted features are unstable to changes in diversity and sensitive to 
the environment. With the development of software and hardware 
technologies and the rise of deep learning theory, Deep Convolutional 
Neural Networks (DCNNs) have developed rapidly. Compared with 
traditional methods, DCNNs can greatly improve the model robustness 
and increase detection precision, showing great advantages. Inspired by 
this, scholars have applied DCNNs to the field of agricultural production 
to assist in the production of smart orchards (Bargoti and Underwood, 
2017; Koirala et al., 2019; Tian et al., 2019; Yu et al., 2019; Kuznetsova 
et al., 2020; Liang et al., 2020; Wan and Goudos, 2020; Zhang et al., 
2020). The DCNNs-based object detection methods are divided into two 
main frameworks. One is two-stage object detection methods, such as R- 
CNN and its variants. In these methods, region proposals are generated 
in the first stage and classified in the second stage. Bargoti and Under­
wood (2017) presented a fruit detection system for image data captured 
in orchards using the detection framework of Faster R-CNN. They con­
ducted ablation studies over three fruit types: apples, mangoes, and al­
monds. To guide the robot to harvest strawberries automatically, Yu 
et al. (2019) proposed a strawberry fruit detection algorithm based on 
Mask R-CNN (MRSD) in the greenhouse. The proposed strawberry fruit 
detection algorithm has overcome the difficulties of poor universality 
and robustness using traditional machine vision algorithms in a non- 
structural environment. Wan and Goudos (2020) proposed an 
improved Faster R-CNN for multi-class fruit detection to achieve higher 
efficiency, effectiveness, and reliability in outdoor orchard environ­
ments. They optimized the structure of the existing Faster R-CNN model 
in convolutional and pooling layers. Similarly, Zhang et al. (2020) used 
the Faster R-CNN model to realize multi-class object detection. Different 
from Wan and Goudos (2020), they deployed the model to achieve 
simultaneous detection of apples, branches, and trunks and provided a 
foundation and possibility for developing a fully automated shake-and- 
catch apple harvesting system. The two-step object detection methods 
have high accuracy, but the generation of region proposals requires a lot 
of computing resources and a long detection time. The other is one-stage 
object detection methods, such as YOLO and SSD, which predict 
bounding boxes from input images directly without the region proposal 
step. Hence, these methods have the superiority of high detection speed 
and can be used for real-time tasks. An architecture termed ‘Man­
goYOLO’ was developed by Koirala et al. (2019) to detect mango fruit in 
tree canopies. The architecture is based on YOLOv3 and YOLOv2(tiny). 
Tian et al. (2019) used the DenseNet method to deal with the lower 
resolution feature layers in the YOLOv3 network, enabling real-time 
detection of apples in different growth stages and overlap conditions. 
Also using YOLOv3, Liang et al. (2020) implemented the detection of 
litchi at the nighttime environment. With special pre- and post- 
processing, a machine vision system based on the YOLOv3 algorithm 
was used to detect apples and other spherical fruit in orchards (Kuz­
netsova et al., 2020). 
As can be seen from the aforementioned studies, fruit detection based 
on RGB images has accumulated a large number of research achieve­
ments. However, the information provided by a single sensor is limited. 
Dense fruit distribution, occlusion by branches and leaves, overlapping 
fruits, and other factors will bring difficulties and challenges to accurate 
fruit detection based on RGB images in non-structural orchards. One 
way to overcome such difficulties and challenges is to employ multi- 
modal data. There is a certain complementarity and consistency 
among multi-modal information, which can make up for each other’s 
deficiencies and shortcomings. In the field of computer vision, it has 
become a popular way to improve model performance by using multi- 
modal data. For example, in gesture recognition (Miao et al., 2017; Li 
et al., 2018) and saliency detection (Fan et al., 2020; Fan et al., 2021; 
Zhou et al., 2021), researchers integrate RGB and depth data through 
different fusion modules. With the development of image acquisition 
devices, the availability of multi-modal data for orchard object detection 
has increased significantly, such as depth images, infrared images, 
remote sensing images, etc. RGB images only provide two-dimensional 
information. Depth images can provide the physical distance between 
the object and the sensor, aiding better foreground-background differ­
entiation. Near-infrared (NIR) images can obtain a stable image when 
the ambient light changes. Therefore, some recent work has been 
devoted to using multi-modal information to improve the performance 
of fruit detection (Nguyen et al., 2016; Tao and Zhou, 2017; Lin et al., 
2019; Fu et al., 2020; Yu et al., 2021). Fu et al. (2020) discussed the 
advantages and disadvantages of RGB-D cameras with different depth 
measurement principles and application fields, and reviewed various

fruit detection and localization. The fruit detection with the fusion of 
multiple types of information was found to be better than the same with 
only one type of information. Nguyen et al. (2016) developed an algo­
rithm to detect and localize red and bicolored apples based on color and 
shape features. But it requires complex pre-processing of depth and color 
data and has significant detection limitations. An improved 3D

Tao and Zhou (2017). Unlike the above methods, Lin et al. (2019) and 
Yu et al. (2021) used depth information to exclude as many backgrounds 
as possible. 
More research is based on DCNN. Sa et al. (2016) adapted Faster R- 
CNN through transfer learning, for the task of fruit detection using im­
ages obtained from two modalities: color and NIR. Data and decision 
layer fusion strategies were explored for combining the multi-modal 
information. Also to improve Faster R-CNN performance, Gen´e-Mola 
et al. (2019) used data of three modalities: color, depth, and range- 
corrected intensity signal to detect apples. They fused multi-modal 
data at the data layer, making the network suitable for five-channel 
input. By color and depth images, passion fruits were detected using 
Faster R-CNN (Tu et al., 2018). They integrated color-based detection 
with depth-based detection at the decision layer to improve the detec­
tion performance. Similarly, by integrating the detection results of color 
and depth images at the decision layer, Tu et al. (2020) improved the 
Faster R-CNN network structure and proposed MS-FRCNN to deal with 
the detection and counting of small fruits. However, these DCNN-based 
studies only consider the fusion of multi-modal information at the data 
layer or decision layer. Depending on the different multi-modal data 
fusion places, the fusion strategies can be divided into data-layer fusion, 
feature-layer fusion, and decision-layer fusion. The data-layer fusion 
strategy regards multi-modal data as indistinguishable multi-channel 
data, while the decision-layer fusion strategy independently learns the 
characteristics of a single modal. Neither of them fails to consider the 
correlation between different modal information. The feature-layer 
fusion strategy can overcome this problem, which has been proved in 
some object detection research (Xu et al., 2017; Mocanu and Clapon, 
2018; Li et al., 2019a). Therefore, this paper adopts the strategy of 
feature-layer fusion to make full use of the complementarity and con­
sistency between multi-modal data, improving the detection accuracy of 
fruit in complex orchards. 
Due to the limitation of depth sensors and scene configurations, the 
depth images are not perfect. There are pixels without depth values, that 
is, depth holes. Fig. 1 shows example images from the public SUN RGB-D 
dataset (Song et al., 2015) and a citrus orchard dataset. Because of the 
imaging principle and the viewing distance of the depth sensor, some 
depth values in the indoor scene are missing, especially at the object 
boundary and background (see Fig. 1(a)). However, the main objects can 
be easily distinguished from the indoor depth image. In contrast, the 
uncontrolled lighting condition and complex background in the orchard 
lead to a depth image with worse quality and more noise (see Fig. 1(b)). 
Some fruits and leaves are difficult to be distinguished in the outdoor 
depth image. The simple multi-modal fusion methods are used to treat 
the extracted features equally, which not only ignores the fact that 
different modalities and regions contribute differently to the detection 
but also easily causes over-fitting in the noisy areas of the depth images. 
To overcome these problems, several methods (Li et al., 2019a; Li et al., 
2021) introduced attention mechanisms to weight the importance of 
different regions or modalities. In fact, the attention mechanism has 
been widely used in a variety of tasks (Mnih et al., 2014; Woo et al., 
2018; Fu et al., 2019) to help networks extract powerful and discrimi­
native features. In 2014, Google DeepMind applied the attention 
mechanism to the image classification task and proposed a novel 
recurrent neural network model. The model can extract information 
from an image or video by adaptively selecting a sequence of regions or 
locations and only processing the selected regions at high resolution 
(Mnih et al., 2014). To improve the representation ability, Hu et al. 
(2018) proposed the “Squeeze-and-Excitation” (SE) block to focus on the 
channel relationship, which learns the dependency of each channel and 
adaptively recalibrates channel-wise feature responses. SE block only 
considers the channel contribution of feature maps, but it ignores the 
spatial location of the object in images. The spatial location of the object 
has a vital role in object detection. For this, Woo et al. (2018) proposed 
the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM). CBAM sequentially 
infers attention maps along two separate dimensions (channel and 
spatial) and then multiplies the attention maps by the input feature maps 
to perform adaptive feature refinement. In contrast to the studies of SE 
block and CBAM that address attention mechanisms of feature maps, Li 
et al. (2019b) addressed attention mechanisms of convolution kernels. 
They proposed a dynamic selection mechanism in CNNs that allows each 
neuron to adaptively adjust its receptive field size based on multiple 
scales of input information. The designed block is called Selective Kernel 
(SK) unit. Therefore, in this paper, RGB-D multi-modal information is 
fused based on an attention mechanism to reduce the negative influence 
of depth noise and make full use of the complementary multi-modal 
features. 
To compensate for the shortage of citrus detection based on RGB 
images, a noise-tolerant RGB-D feature fusion network (NT-FFN) is 
proposed to eliminate the noise interference from depth holes while 
extracting the complementary RGB-D information. More specifically, 
the NT-FFN consists of feature extractors, attention fusion modules, and 
a prediction module. Two structurally identical feature extractors are 
used to extract the features from different modalities, and the attention 
fusion module avoids introducing depth noise in the fusion process and 
making full use of the complementary multi-modal features. Then, 
multi-scale single-modal and fusion features are sent to the prediction 
module to enhance the detection ability and noise immunity of the 
network. The main contributions of this study can be summarized as 
follow:  
(1) This paper proposes an object detection framework integrating 
RGB-D multi-modal information. The complementary informa­
tion between different modalities is learned and fused at the 
feature layer to make up for the shortage of RGB image based 
detection.  
(2) A fusion module based on the attention mechanism is designed. 
By adaptively focusing on the key regions in both spatial and 
channel dimensions of different modalities, the complementary 
information is learned and the noise from depth images is sup­
pressed in the fusion module.  
(3) A prediction module is designed and applied to detect objects 
from multi-scale RGB features and multi-scale fusion features. 
The noise immunity of the network is further enhanced and the 
multi-scale object detection ability is also improved. 
(4) Comparative experiments and ablation experiments are per­
formed on the RGB-D citrus dataset constructed by this paper, the 
proposed NT-FFN shows significant advantages. In addition, it 
can also be widely used in other kinds of fruit detection tasks. 
The remainder of the article is organized as follows. Section 2 shows 
the acquired dataset and describes the details of the NT-FFN. Section 3 
presents the experimental results and evaluation. Finally, conclusions 
are summarized in Section 4.

2.2. Proposed method 
This paper aims to effectively use multi-modal data (color and depth) 
to improve the accuracy of outdoor fruit detection. Thus, an end-to-end 
noise-tolerant feature fusion network is proposed as shown in Fig. 3. 
First, two feature extractors (Extractor-R and Extractor-D) with the same 
structure are used to extract the features of the color image and the 
depth image, respectively. Then, at different scales, multi-modal fea­
tures are successively sent to the attention fusion module for informa­
tion fusion. This module enables the network to focus more on the 
important regions and channels of the input features, which not only 
learns the common and complementary features between different 
modals but also eliminates the negative effects of depth holes. Finally, 
multi-scale features from the Extractor-R and attention fusion modules 
are used in the training stage to predict object position. In the inference 
stage, only the fused multi-scale features are used to predict object po­
sition and the non-maximum suppression (NMS) is used to filter out the 
redundant prediction boxes. 
2.2.1. Single-modal feature extractor 
For single-modal feature extraction, the network design of FaceBoxes 
(Zhang et al., 2017) is adopted. Extractor-R and Extractor-D have the 
same structure and do not share parameters. The detailed structure is 
shown in Table 1, including kernel size, stride, padding, output size, 
output channel, and layer repeat. Taking the Extractor-R as an example, 
the output features of Inception, Conv4, and Conv6 in Table 1 corre­
spond to R1, R2, and R3 in Fig. 3. 
The first four layers of the feature extractor (Conv1, MaxPool1, 
Conv2, MaxPool2) are set with large stride sizes so that the input image 
(with a fixed size of 1024 × 1024) could be quickly reduced to 1/32 of its 
original size. The kernel sizes of these layers are set to appropriate ones: 
7 × 7,3 × 3,5 × 5 and 3 × 3. This is because a too large convolution 
kernel size will lead to a sharp increase in computation, while a too small 
kernel size cannot extract features efficiently. In addition, in Conv1 and 
Conv2, the convolution original output is concatenated with its negative 
output, which improves the feature expression ability without 
increasing the amount of computation. 
After rapidly reducing the image size, the feature extractor enhances 
the NT-FFN’s ability for multi-scale object detection in both width and 
depth dimensions. The inception module idea (Szegedy et al., 2015; 
Szegedy et al., 2016; Szegedy et al., 2017) is an important milestone in 
the development of CNN. It makes the network “wider” rather than 
“deeper”. This avoids the over-fitting and high computational costs 
caused by deep networks. Hence, an inception module as shown in Fig. 4 
is used in this paper. The module extracts more dense features by par­
allel using different kernel sizes. Convolution kernels with different sizes 
mean receptive fields of different sizes, introducing multi-scale infor­
mation and increasing the adaptability of the network to scale. After 
repeated inception layers, the feature map decreases in size gradually 
and forms the multi-scale feature maps for multi-modal feature fusion 
and object prediction. 
2.2.2. Attention fusion module 
RGB-D images contain complementary features that can help 
improve detection accuracy. However, due to the existence of depth 
holes and the different contributions of different modal features to ob­
ject detection, it is unfair to simply fuse RGB images and raw depth 
images. When fusing multi-modal information, the weights should be set 
according to the characteristics of different modalities. To accomplish 
this, this paper introduces the attention mechanism in the fusion mod­
ule. The attention fusion module can adaptively select the features that 
need to be concerned from different modalities in both spatial and 
channel dimensions. With the help of high-quality RGB images, it 
eliminates the harm from depth holes and avoids over-fitting noisy 
areas. And the different dependence on channels makes full use of the 
complementary information of multi-modal, which helps distinguish 
fruits such as overlap and occlusion. The module structure is shown in 
Fig. 5.

not used. By enhancing the learning of reliable features, the noise im­

densification strategy (Zhang et al., 2017) are associated with multi- 
scale and multi-source features. The anchor densification strategy gua­

original image so that objects of various scales can match almost the 
same number of anchors.

(Lossloc) and the confidence loss (Losscls), which can be defined as:

where λ is the weight coefficient. The localization loss is a Smooth L1 
loss between the predicted box and the ground truth box, which mea­

truth ones. The confidence loss measures how confident the network is 
that the computed bounding box is. Categorical cross-entropy is used to 
compute it. 
3. Experiments and results 
This section describes the experimental implementation and evalu­
ation metrics, and analyzes the research results. To evaluate the per­
formance of the proposed network, the results are compared with those 
methods using different image modalities and different fusion strategies 
on the citrus RGB-D dataset, and ablation studies are conducted on the 
attention mechanism and the prediction module. In addition, we also 
verify the generalization performance of NT-FFN. 
3.1. Experimental settings and implementation details 
The experiments in this paper are run on the server with CentOS 7.2 
operating system, GPU Tesla P100, and v10.1 CUDA environment. All 
procedures are coded by Python language under PyTorch 1.4 deep 
learning framework. 
For all convolutional neural network experiments, the batch size is 
32, and an SGD optimizer with 0.9 momentum and 5−4 weight decay is 
adopted. Models are trained with 100 iterations, with the learning rate 
set as 10-4, 10-5, 10-6 at the beginning, 10th, 40th iterations. The pa­
rameters are randomly initialized with the “Xavier” method. In the 
training stage, color distortion, random cropping, scale transformation, 
and flipping are selected for data augmentation. According to the 
network design of the single-modal feature extractor, the input image is 
resized from 1280 × 720 to 1024 × 1024 after data augmentation. The 
weight ratio of localization loss and confidence loss is 2:1. 
In the process of single image inference, first of all, the image size is 
adjusted with a fixed original aspect ratio, and let its width is equal to 
1024. Then, the resized image is fed into NT-FFN to generate candidate 
bounding boxes. Finally, NMS is used to remove the duplicate detection 
boxes with the IoU greater than 0.3 or the confidence less than 0.05.

3.3. Results and analysis 
3.3.1. Comparison with different image modalities and fusion strategies 
In order to demonstrate the superiority of using multi-modal data 
and compare the performance of different fusion strategies, the 
comparative experiments shown in Table 2 are conducted. 
For a fair comparison, we define the network composed of a feature 
extractor described in 2.2.1 and a prediction module described in 2.2.3 
as the baseline detector. On this basis, different modal images are input 
and different multi-modal fusion strategies are implemented. In single-

modal object detection, the input of the baseline detector is color or 
depth images, and the input of the prediction module is multi-scale 
single-modal features. As for the data-layer fusion strategy, the color 
and depth images are concatenated along the channel dimension before 
being fed into the baseline detector. Considering the results of two 
independently trained single-modal detectors, the intersection of two 
modalities detection results whose IoU is larger than 0.5 is regarded as 
the decision-layer fusion result. The proposed NT-FFN uses the feature- 
layer fusion strategy, which performs attention-based fusion at the 
feature level. 
As can be seen from Table 2, the detection result based on color 
images is reliable. The AP50 can reach 94.1% and the detection precision 
is close to 90%. The significant color difference between fruit and 
background is the main reason for its good performance. Most fruits can 
be distinguished by only using two-dimensional information. In the or­
chard scene, the fruits grow densely and the environment is complex. 
The baseline detector with low-quality depth images is difficult to 
extract discriminative features for detection, and the AP50 is only 37.6%. 
Different from prior knowledge, using multi-modal data does not 
improve the detection performance. Compared to detection based on 
color images, the AP50 of the data-layer fusion strategy is reduced from 
94.1% to 90.5%. The main reason is that depth images have numerous 
holes, and the fusion without filtering brings noise to the network, which 
makes learning difficult. Due to many results of the depth image based 
detection being inaccurate, a lot of fruits will be missed in intersection 
decisions. Although the precision of the decision-layer strategy is 
improved compared with the color image based detection by 5.1%, the 
recall is significantly reduced by 23.2%, and the F1-Score is also reduced 
by 11.1%. In other words, the decision-layer strategy sacrifices the recall 
to obtain high precision results. 
Different from the above methods, our NT-FFN shows obvious ad­
vantages. It can select useful information from low-quality depth images 
and adaptively fuse complementary features of different modalities to 
improve detection accuracy. Our method achieves the best results in 
AP50, precision, recall, and F1-Score, outperforming other methods. The 
AP50, precision, recall, and F1-Score achieve 95.4%, 92.6%, 94.1%, and 
93.4%, respectively. 
In terms of speed, due to using the same network structure, theo­
retically, the inference speed of the two single-modal detectors should 
be close. However, because of the poor quality of depth images, the 
features learned by the network are limited. A large number of bounding 
boxes are generated during inference, which causes the need for NMS to 
remove more redundant bounding boxes and takes more time. So that, 
the speed of the baseline detector using depth images (17FPS) is much 
lower than that of using color images (50FPS). The decision-layer fusion 
is to further optimize the detection results after two independent single- 
modal detectors. Therefore, its speed is slower than any single-modal 
detector, only 12FPS. Although the inference speed of the NT-FFN is 
slightly slower than that of the baseline detector with color images, it 
can still achieve the real-time of 39FPS for images with 1280 × 720 
pixels on a Tesla P100 GPU.

3.3.2. Comparison with different feature-layer fusion methods 
In order to prove the advantages of NT-FFN in feature-layer fusion 
methods, a comparative experiment is conducted in this section. The 
results are shown in Table 3. B-DML and R-DML are SSD-based multi- 
modal fusion methods proposed by Kim et al., (2018). R-DML is a 
network with an information fusion module named GIF. The GIF adjusts 
the contribution of the features from each modality adaptively. B-DML 
has the same structure as R-DML except that the GIF module is replaced 
by a 1 × 1 convolution to combine the intermediate features. In addi­
tion, two commonly used feature fusion methods are compared. Based 
on the proposed NT-FFN, S-FFN indicates that the attention fusion 
module is replaced by an adding operation, and C-FFN indicates that the 
attention fusion module is replaced by concatenation. We can see that 
the NT-FFN achieves a high AP50 score, which is the best among all 
methods. 
In the citrus dataset, the outdoor depth images are noisy, which is not 
as good as the indoor depth images or outdoor radar images used in Kim 
et al. (2018). The GIF fusion module in R-DML increases the noise in the 
learning process. Consequently, all evaluation metrics decrease 
compared with B-DML. The AP50 decreases from 87.2% to 74.1%. At the 
same time, due to the limitation of the network structure, the detection 
precision of R-DML is not satisfactory, and the inference speed is slower 
than other methods. Based on the proposed network structure, S-FFN 
and C-FFN show better performance than B-DML and R-DML. Moreover, 
the proposed fusion module can consider the complementary relation­
ship between different modalities and eliminate noise, further 
improving the performance. The AP50 of the NT-FFN is one percentage 
point higher than that of C-FFN and 0.7 percentage points higher than 
that of S-FFN. The F1-Score of the NT-FFN is 1.8% higher than that of C- 
FFN and S-FFN.

whole image. In Fig. 8(b), the positioning of the dense growth fruit is 
inaccurate. In Fig. 8(c) and (d), the false detection caused by yellow 
leaves and dense growth has not been eliminated. However, the pro­
posed NT-FFN can make full use of the color and depth information, 
eliminating false detection and accurately locating dense growth citrus.

3.3.4. Ablation study of prediction module 
In the proposed prediction module, not only the multi-scale fusion 
features but also the multi-scale RGB modality features are used to train 
the model. In order to prove the effectiveness of this design, this section 
conducts an ablation study on the prediction module. The comparisons 
are conducted on four cases: (a) only fusion features are used for 
training; (b) depth and fusion features are used; (c) RGB and fusion 
features are used; (d) RGB, depth and fusion features are used simulta­
neously. Table 5 provides quantitative results on the citrus RGB-D 
dataset. 
As can be seen, the performance of (a) is better than that of (b), and 
the performance of (c) is better than that of (d). Specifically, the AP50 of 
(a) is 0.5% higher than that of (b), and (c) is 0.6% higher than that of (d). 
This is mainly caused by the instability of depth information. Adding 
multi-scale depth features increases a lot of noise, resulting in detection 
performance degradation. In comparison, RGB features are more reli­
able. Therefore, the input of multi-scale RGB features in the prediction 
module is helpful to improve the detection performance. Therefore, the 
result for (c) is better than (a). The simultaneous input of RGB features 
and fusion features method adopted in this paper ensures the best AP50, 
precision, recall, and F1-Score.

3.3.6. Discussion 
Despite the obvious advantages of the proposed NT-FFN, it still has 
limitations in some challenging images. Due to the limitation of depth 
sensors, depth holes exist along the edge of objects. In the top row of 
Fig. 11, the citrus with a yellow bounding box is in the shadow of the left 
edge of the citrus with a blue bounding box, resulting in no depth in­
formation in the yellow bounding box. Therefore, even if multi-modal 
information is fused, the fruit with the yellow bounding box can not 
be detected because there is no depth information in the area. In the 
second row of Fig. 11, the citrus with a yellow bounding box can be 
detected when only the color image is used. Although the boundary of 
the bounding box is imprecise, there is a high probability that the box 
contains citrus. However, NT-FFN could not detect this citrus. The 
reason is that the occlusion of leaves and branches leads to different 
depth values (even zero) in the yellow bounding box. By fusing this 
depth information with color information, NT-FFN decides that there is 
no citrus in the yellow box. Similarly, in the bottom row of Fig. 11, the

4. Conclusion 
To make up for the shortage of citrus detection based on RGB images 
and improve the detection accuracy, a noise-tolerant RGB-D feature 
fusion network is proposed to eliminate the noise interference from 
depth holes while fusing the complementary RGB-D information. The 
following specific conclusions can be drawn from this study: 
Using multi-modal data for detection can make up for the shortage of 
single-modal detection, and the fusion of different modalities in the 
feature layer can better learn the complementary information. The NT- 
FFN can achieve the AP50 of 95.4%, the precision of 92.6%, the recall of 
94.1%, and the F1-Score of 93.4%. Its detection speed is 39FPS, which 
meets the real-time requirements. Compared with the detection using 
only RGB images, data-layer fusion strategy, and decision-layer fusion 
strategy, our NT-FFN that fuses multi-modal information in the feature 
layer improves the AP50 score by 1.3, 4.9, and 24.8 percentage points, 
respectively, which well balances the detection precision and recall. 
The holes in depth images are the critical factors affecting the effect 
of RGB-D feature fusion. Therefore, this paper proposes a multi-modal 
fusion module based on the attention mechanism, and uses multi-scale 
features from the color images and the fusion modules to guide the 
network learning. The attention fusion module can eliminate the depth 
noise interference, make full use of the relationship between different 
modal features, and focus more on the reliable and important regions. 
Compared with R-DML and the feature layer fusion method without 
attention mechanism, the detection AP50 of NT-FFN is increased by 
21.3% and 1%, respectively. Moreover, compared with the popular 
attention mechanism modules SE and CBAM, the attention feature 
fusion module in NT-FFN could better learn the complementary RGB-D 
information, and the detection AP50 is improved by 0.7% and 1.1%, 
respectively. The additional use of multi-scale features from color im­
ages in the prediction module can further improve the noise immunity of 
the network. Compared with only using the fusion features in training, 
the proposed method could improve the AP50 by 1 percentage point. 
NT-FFN achieves excellent detection results in the apple detection 
task with an AP50 of 93.1%, which verifies its generalization ability. This 
proves that the network can be widely used in other kinds of fruit 
detection tasks. 
This study shows promising results for the RGB-D fusion at the 
feature layer and provides a foundation and possibility for RGB-D object 
detection in outdoor scenes. In future research, we intend to introduce 
depth enhancement, depth estimation, and other technologies to 
improve the quality of depth images, which would be helpful in reducing 
the effects of low-quality depth images and boosting object detection 
performance.
