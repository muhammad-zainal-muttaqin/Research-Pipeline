Abstract— Oil palm fruit farming is one of the most leading 
agriculture industries in the South East Asia region. 
Unfortunately, most of the harvesting method is still done 
through manual labor. Multiple research has been conducted to 
help farmers automatically detect the ripeness level of the oil 
palm fruit. We conducted an experiment of detecting the 
ripeness level of oil palm fruit by using one of the state-of-the-
art computer vision approaches, which was deep learning with 
a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN). The dataset used 
consists of 7 levels of ripeness with 400 images of oil palm fruits. 
The models used in this test scenario were both AlexNet and 
DenseNet. The result of this study showed that DenseNet has 
been proven to outperform AlexNet by 8.5% in terms of 
accuracy and 8% in F1 score. 
Keywords— oil palm fruit, deep learning, convolutional 
neural network, computer vision 
I. INTRODUCTION
As a country in the tropics zone, agriculture becomes the 
main industrial sector in Indonesia. Various studies have been 
conducted in Indonesia to enhance its agriculture production 
[1], [2]. Among the agricultural commodities, palm oil can be 
considered the most important commodities in Indonesia. In 
fact, Indonesia is the largest oil palm exporter in the world [3]. 
Considering the importance of palm oil in Indonesia, an 
intensification of palm oil production can significantly boost 
the Indonesian national economy. For that purpose, improving 
the oil palm Fresh Fruit Bunch (FFB) harvesting method is 
one of the most promising approaches. Currently, most of the 
oil palm plantations use manual labor as the harvesting 
method. Therefore, developing an automatic harvesting 
system can significantly improve production productivity. 
The most practical approach for the automatic system is by 
using an Artificial Intelligence (AI) system that can classify 
the ripeness of a palm oil FFB from an image, whose 
feasibility was demonstrated by Harwardana et al. [4]. 
In contrast to its practicality, developing the AI system 
poses an overwhelming challenge. The variability of the oil 
palm FFB shape, structure, and color is not easy to be 
deconstructed as simple instructions that can be understood by 
a computer. Fortunately, this task is currently feasible by 
utilizing deep learning, a sub-category of AI that is able to 
learn from complex data representations such as image [5]–
[9], audio [10]–[14], and text [15]–[17]. In this study, we 
explored the capability of DenseNet, one of the deep learning 
algorithms that showed an astonishing performance for image 
processing tasks, such as in medical image analysis [18], [19]. 
We compared the performance with another deep learning 
algorithm called AlexNet [5], which was already studied by 
Ibrahim et al. [20] for oil palm FFB classification. 
II. RELATED WORKS
Numerous studies have been conducted to develop an oil 
palm FFB image classification system. However, most of the 
studies used classic image processing techniques that rely on 
color feature extraction. For instance, Alfatni et al. [21] used 
the mean value of the red, green, and blue channels of palm 
oil FFB images as features with a simple rule-based algorithm 
for classification. The features were also utilized by Jamil et 
al. [22], but with a neuro-fuzzy algorithm for classification. 
Similarly, Fadilah et al. [23] used simple color features for 
classification, in this case, selected hue values from all pixels 
in the palm oil FFB image. The classification algorithm is a 
standard Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). The hue value was 
also utilized by Ishak and Hudzari [24] in their prediction 
model for palm oil fruit ripeness. A more advanced technique 
was introduced by Bensaeed et al. [25] that extracted the 
reflectance value of palm oil FFB hyperspectral images in 
different wavelengths as features, which can be seen as the 
color of hyperspectral images, and classify it with a standard 
MLP. 
Despite their popularity for palm oil FFB classification, 
techniques that rely on feature extraction are known to be 
brittle. For a reliable result, the camera configuration needs to 
be fixed, which is not practical in a real-world setting. This 
problem can be alleviated by using deep learning, which can 
learn to extract features in various image settings. Deep 
learning was started to be used since the study by Ibrahim et 
al. [26]. In the study, they employed AlexNet [27], a type of 
deep learning architecture, to classify palm oil FFB images. 
Harsawardana et al. [4] also used a deep learning algorithm 
for their smart crane grabber.

III. CNN MODELS 
Because our dataset was relatively small, we utilized 
transfer learning to obtain the most optimal performance of 
the CNN model. Therefore, we need to select models that 
already pre-trained on the ImageNet dataset [28], [29], which 
is the most common practice for transfer learning in computer 
vision. In this section, we briefly elaborate on the selected 
CNN models, which are AlexNet and DenseNet. These two 
models have been pre-trained on the ImageNet dataset. 
A. AlexNet 
AlexNet [27] is a type of Convolutional Neural Networks 
(CNN). As illustrated in Figure 1, the AlexNet architecture has 
five convolution layers and three fully connected layers, 
including the last classification layer. To downsample the 
feature maps, AlexNet used a Max Pooling operation after the 
first, second, and fifth convolution layers. AlexNet is 
considered as the CNN architecture that sparked the 
popularity of deep learning for image processing due to its 
astonishing winning in the ImageNet Large Scale Visual 
Recognition Challenge (ILSVRC) [28], [30] in 2012. 
 
 
Fig. 1. The Illustration of AlexNet Architecture 
 
B. DenseNet 
DenseNet [9] is another CNN architecture that is known to 
have superhuman performance, especially in medical image 
analysis [18], [31], [32]. It extends the formulation of residual 
connection [8] by appending the feature maps of all previous 
layers in a unit called Dense Block, as depicted in Figure 2. 
This novel connection is called dense connection. DenseNet 
has four architecture variants, each with 121, 169, 201, and 
264 layers. The layers of all DenseNet architectures are 
grouped in four DenseBlock as illustrated in Figure 2. 
IV. EXPERIMENT SETTING 
In the experiment, we compared the AlexNet model with 
the 121-layer DenseNet. On the one hand, AlexNet was 
chosen for comparison because it was used for the previous 
study by Ibrahim et al. [26]. On the other hand, DenseNet was 
picked because it is a more powerful CNN than AlexNet. Its 
performance can even achieve expert-level performance [18], 
[19].    
 
 
Fig. 2. The Illustration of AlexNet Architecture 
Both models were trained and tested using a dataset of 400 
palm oil FFB images. Examples of the images are shown in 
Figure 3. The images were classified into 7 ripeness categories 
by experts. The ratio among the number of images in each of 
the seven classes is imbalanced as shown in Table I. The 
dataset was split into training (60%), validation (20%), and 
testing set (20%). These sets were used to train the models 
with the standard train/validation/test strategy. The models 
were trained for 50 epochs using Stochastic Gradient Descent 
(SGD) with a 0.001 learning rate, decayed by tenth every eight 
epochs. The code for the experiment was developed with 
PyTorch [33].

V. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
The results of the experiment in this research are reported 
in Table II. The results show that DenseNet has better 
accuracy than AlexNet for palm oil FFB image classification. 
Not only that, but DenseNet is also better for the classification 
despite the imbalanced ripeness class ratio in the dataset. This 
is proved by its superior performance measured by the F1 
Score compared to AlexNet. Moreover, the DenseNet model 
is also better at the precision and recall metric.  
The reason why DenseNet was better because it has a 
better performance [9] on the ImageNet dataset, which is the 
dataset that the AlexNet and DenseNet were pretrained with. 
This in effect leads to better performance for transfer learning 
[34].  Another reason why DenseNet was better is because of 
its dense connection that keeps the magnitude of the gradients 
during backpropagation. This ultimately alleviates the 
vanishing gradient problem that is known to be harmful to 
deep learning algorithms’ performance. This should be 
contrasted by the AlexNet that has no such connections that 
prevent the vanishing gradient problem.

VI. CONCLUSION 
In this study, we proved that DenseNet with 121 layers 
outperforms AlexNet for palm oil FFB image classification. 
The DenseNet superior performance is also valid for the 
imbalanced dataset case, which is displayed by the better 
precision, recall, and F1 score compared to AlexNet. 
Following this study, future work can explore a more complex 
architecture tailored specifically for palm oil FFB image 
classification. The future work also need to collect more data 
because deep learning is known to have a better performance 
with more data. For that, it will be beneficial to develop a 
system that ease the collection of massive data [35]–[37].
