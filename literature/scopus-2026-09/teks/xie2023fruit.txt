Abstract—This study focuses on the precise localization of
fruits using multiple stereo visual perception units in a multi-arm
collaborative harvesting robot. Fruit localization in overlapping
camera ﬁelds is challenging in complex and unstructured orchard
environments. This is due to the inaccuracies in estimating
the fruit centroid of the same target fruit under different
camera perspectives, making it difﬁcult to determine whether
different centroid positions belong to the same fruit. To address
this issue, a novel fruit localization method based on multiple
visual units is proposed in this work to reduce mislocalization
of fruits in overlapping camera ﬁelds. Firstly, a target-based
multi-camera extrinsic calibration method is employed to obtain
accurate external parameter matrices between multiple cameras
and the robot base coordinate system. Secondly, to enhance the
recognition and localization accuracy of fruits under a single
camera, a multitask fruit detection and segmentation model
is used, along with a frustum-based fruit localization method.
Finally, to reduce the problem of repeated detections of the same
fruit in overlapping camera ﬁelds, a ﬁltering method based on
fruit surface point cloud matching is proposed. Experimental
results demonstrate that the proposed method improves fruit
localization accuracy and reduces the probability of repeated
false detections. Visualized point cloud information generated by
the stereo cameras is provided to showcase the effectiveness of
the proposed method.

I. INTRODUCTION
With the shortage of labor and the rising costs of manpower,
there is a signiﬁcant increase in the pressure on agricultural
labor supply. The development of highly efﬁcient, high-quality,
and cost-effective agricultural robots that can replace manual

In recent years, combining modern standardized agricultural

Recently, there has been a growing interest in multi-arm

increased efﬁciency[8]. These robots typically integrate multi-

equipped with visual acquisition devices corresponding to the
number of mechanical arms to facilitate gripping tasks. These
innovations have contributed to enhancing the efﬁciency of
harvesting robots.

frustum projection, enabling precise fruit localization. Finally,
to reduce the issue of repeated detections of the same fruit in
overlapping ﬁelds of view, a fruit surface point cloud matching
ﬁltering method is proposed.
II. METHODOLOGY
A. Multi-arm harvesting robot system
The multi-arm harvesting robot in this work consists of a
wheeled mobile platform, mechanical arms, grippers, visual
perception system, a fruit conveying system, an illuminating
system and a central control module. The robotic arm system
includes four units, each of which includes a 3-degree-of-
freedom electric-pneumatic hybrid driven Cartesian mechani-
cal arm. The custom-designed 2-DoF gripper has three curved
ﬁngers and is able to perform the motions of rotation and
grasp to detach the target fruits. The multi-vision system
consists of four RGBD cameras that is ﬁxed at the top of
third linear module. The RGBD cameras acquire perceptual
information of target fruits, and the images are transmitted
to the edge computing module to implement detection and
segmentation tasks for fruit localization. The control system
consists of an industrial personal computer to perform central
control and human-machine interaction, two edge computing
devices to fulﬁll image inference task, a CAN bus as the way
of communication of control system, and several embedded
system circuits.

C. Fruit recognition and localization
1) Perception Network: We employed a multi-task deep
convolutional neural network that is proposed in our previous
work [14]. The network is built upon the YOLOv4 architecture
with improvements and consists of a shared encoding network
and two decoding networks that perform different tasks. The
encoding network comprises a backbone network responsible
for feature extraction and a neck network that collects feature
maps at different stages. The decoding networks consist of a
detection head network, which predicts fruit occlusion types
and complete bounding boxes, and an instance segmentation
head network that generates pixel masks for the visible parts
of fruits as shown in Figure 4.
2) Training: The network training is based on a graphic
workstation with Dell Precision 7920 Tower (2 Xeon Silver
4214R @ 2.4 GHz CPU cores, 128 GB RAM, and NVIDIA
GeForce RTX 3070 (8 GB)). To train the proposed multi-task
DCNN, two kinds of datasets were collected: an open-source
MinneApple dataset[15] containing 1000 images with over
41,000 labeled instances of apples, and the dataset manually
collected in two modern standard orchards in Haidian District
and Changping District, Beijing, China, acquired by Realsense
D435i RGBD camera, containing 1000 images with over 12k
labeled instances and bounding boxes of apples with different
kinds of occlusion: non-, leaf-, branch- and fruit-occlusion.
The training and validation sets are distributed into 4:1.
In the training stage, we employ a) photometric distortion
including random contrast, random saturation, random hue,
random brightness, b) random mirror operation, c) random
sample crop, d) random ﬂip, e) random expansion to augment
the dataset.
3) Fruit Localization: Through the object detection branch
of a multi-task network, complete bounding boxes of fruits
are obtained. By incorporating the camera’s internal imaging
parameter matrix, a three-dimensional viewing frustum is
calculated. Due to the estimation of the two-dimensional image
bounding box, which encompasses the complete outer contour
of the fruit, even in cases of partial occlusion, the point cloud
of the target fruit in the generated RGBD image will also be
fully enclosed within the frustum. The symmetric axis of the
frustum can be considered as the central line passing through
the centroid of the fruit. Subsequently, the segmentation
branch of the multi-task network enables the extraction of fruit
masks. Similarly, based on the camera’s internal parameters
and aligned depth map information, a three-dimensional point
cloud of the mask, speciﬁcally the fruit surface point cloud,
can be generated. By applying DBSCAN clustering analysis,
the cluster centroids of the fruit surface point cloud can be
extracted. Compared to clustering methods such as K-Means
and BIRCH, which are generally suitable for convex sample
sets, DBSCAN can be applied to both convex and non-convex
sample sets. Utilizing DBSCAN helps ﬁlter out noise points
within the frustum, including outliers caused by occlusions and
excessively smoothed disturbance surfaces that are not part of
the fruit surface point cloud. After obtaining reference points
on the fruit surface, the radius of the target fruit can be further
determined by utilizing the camera’s intrinsic parameters and
the two-dimensional bounding box information. Subsequently,
by combining this radius information with the known central
line of the fruit, the centroid position of the fruit can be
calculated.
D. Multi-vision perception information fusion
Once the recognition and localization tasks are ﬁnished, it
is necessary to fuse the fruit position information obtained
from different visual units to form a global fruit distribution
map. Due to the fact that different cameras localize the same
fruit from different perspectives, there are differences in the
number of point clouds on the fruit surface and the degree of
occlusion captured from different viewpoints.
Step 1: Utilizing camera extrinsic parameters and the re-
spective ﬁeld of view, objects detected by each camera that do
not reside within the overlapping ﬁeld of view are transferred
from the original list to the global fruit list.
Step 2: For each object detected within the overlapping
regions by i-th camera, if the n-th target, Oi,n, is located
in adjacent overlapping regions between cameras, the position
ˆPi of the center of the detection box Pi in i-th camera is
transformed using the intrinsic and extrinsic matrices of i-
th camera and the adjacent camera. Then iterate through the
objects in the overlapping region of the adjacent camera. If the

veriﬁcation.
Step 5: Transmit the generated global fruit list to the task
planner.
Step 6: Once the global fruit list is picked, the robotic
arms return back to the observing site to start to acquire fruit
distribution again and repeat the above ﬁve steps.

IV. CONCLUSION
This study investigates the problem of acquiring large-
scale fruit distribution information through multi-vision tech-
niques. Firstly, a deep-learning-based network is proposed for
the simultaneous detection and segmentation of fruits. The
positional accuracy is improved through the utilization of
the DBSCAN point-cloud-processing method. Secondly, an
information fusion method is presented for fruit distribution
by employing joint calibration of multiple vision units and
introducing a fusion algorithm to determine the identity of
fruits across different cameras.
Lastly, a complete series of experiments was conducted
to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed method. The
experimental results demonstrate that the proposed approach
effectively integrates fruit distribution information from var-
ious vision units, resulting in a reduction of 20% in the
probability of false positives and the elimination of duplicated
targets during the fusion process.
