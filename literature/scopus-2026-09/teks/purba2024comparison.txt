Abstract—This research explores the capability of the Deep 
Learning models YOLOv8 and YOLOv9 in detecting the 
ripeness level of oil palm fruit. The purpose of this research is to 
compare the accuracy results between YOLOv8 and YOLOv9 
in detecting the ripeness level of oil palm fruit and to find out 
whether there is an improvement in the accuracy of YOLOv9 
compared to the previous version, YOLOv8. This research uses 
datasets sourced from the oil palm plantation repository of 
PTPN IV Medan, North Sumatra, Indonesia. The dataset 
collected for this research consists of 180 images with a size of 
640x640 and a camera resolution of 12 Mega Pixel. The dataset 
was uploaded into Roboflow and underwent pre-processing and 
augmentation. After obtaining the API from Roboflow and 
inputting it into Google Colab for YOLOv8 and YOLOv9 
training, testing was carried out on the provided images. The 
results show that both YOLOv8 and YOLOv9 successfully 
detected ripe, rotten, and unripe oil palm fruit with an accuracy 
of 0.984 (98.4%) and 0.99 (99%). The results show that YOLOv9 
has higher accuracy than YOLOv8 in detecting the ripeness 
level of oil palm fruit. The potential use of the latest version of 
the YOLO model in detecting the ripeness level of oil palm fruit 
in plantations can serve as a basis for developing more effective 
and efficient automatic detection systems in the future. 
Keywords—YOLO, Oil Palm Fruit, Object Detection, Deep 
Learning, PTPN IV 
I. 
INTRODUCTION  
Indonesia is a country with enormous natural resources. 
Agriculture is one of Indonesia's most significant industries. 
Oil palm plantations are one of Indonesia's main agricultural 
industries. Indonesia is one of the world's largest oil palm 
producers [1]. The expansion of the oil palm industry is 
critical for the country's economic growth. Given Indonesia's 
huge area of oil palm plantations, enhancing production in 
order to improve the efficiency and quality of the oil palm 
fruit harvest is challenging. As a result, a company 
specializing in oil palm management was established. 
PT Perkebunan Nusantara IV (State-owned Company in 
Plantation) is a company that operates in agro-industrial 
business, headquartered in Medan, North Sumatra, Indonesia 
(now known as PalmCo). PalmCo Subholding was formed 
through the merger of PT Perkebunan Nusantara V, VI and 
XIII into PTPN IV as the surviving entity and the impure 
separation of PTPN III (Persero) into PTPN IV [2]. PT 
Perkebunan Nusantara IV (PTPN IV) focuses on plantation 
and processing of oil palm and tea commodities, which 
includes the management of plantation areas and plants, seed 
gardens and mature plant maintenance, processing of 
commodities into raw materials for various industries, 
commodity marketing, and other supporting activities. PT 
Perkebunan Nusantara IV has multiple business units, which 
are split into 30 business units managing oil palm cultivation, 
1 business unit managing tea cultivation and 1 unit of oil palm 
plasma plantations, as well as 1 workshop business unit 
spread across several districts [3]. 
Oil palm plantation management is a crucial aspect, 
where one key factor is the automatic detection of the 
ripeness of oil palm fruit. Facing the complexity of a huge 
area of oil palm plantations, the utilization of computer vision 
technology is an excellent solution for automatically 
detecting the ripeness of oil palm fruit. Computer vision is a 
branch of artificial intelligence technology enabling 
computers to extract features from images and other visual 
inputs and perform image classification, object detection and 
other tasks (Szeliski, 2021) [4]. You Only Look Once 
(YOLO) is a deep learning model that is part of computer 
vision. YOLOv1, the first version of YOLO, was developed 
by Joseph Redmon, et al., and was published in 2016. Joseph 
Redmon is one of the significant figures in the field of 
computer vision. YOLOv8 was developed by Ultralytic and 
was released in early 2023. Ultralytic is a company that has 
also developed YOLOv5 [5]. 
The YOLO model has the advantage of being able to 
detect objects with high accuracy and in real-time due to its 
rapid processing technique [6]. YOLOv8 has advantages in 
speed and accuracy of object detection. YOLOv8 was chosen 
as the primary model due to its capability to detect objects 
rapidly and in real-time. YOLOv9 was also chosen as the 
primary model because it is the latest version of the YOLO 
model, which was released in early 2024. 
The primary question of this research is the comparison 
of accuracy levels between YOLOv8 and the latest version, 
YOLOv9, in automatically detecting the ripeness level of oil 
palm fruit. This research aims to compare the accuracy results 
between YOLOv8 and YOLOv9 in automatically detecting 
the ripeness level of oil palm fruit and to find out whether 
there is an improvement in the accuracy of YOLOv9 
compared to the previous version, YOLOv8. It is hoped that 
this research can help maximize the detection results of the 
ripeness of oil palm fruit, provide more accurate information 
about the ripeness of oil palm fruit, and support more 
appropriate decision making in the oil palm fruit harvesting 
process.

II. 
LITERATURE REVIEW 
Many research studies have been undertaken to detect the 
ripeness level of oil palm fruit using various models, 
including YOLO as their model. The following is a research 
paper that uses YOLOv5. In this research [4], the 
performance of three object detection algorithms was 
compared to classify the ripeness level of oil palm fruit, 
namely MobileNetV2 SSD, EfficientDet, and YOLOv5. 
These models were trained using a dataset of 304 images of 
oil palm fruit with 4 different ripeness levels, namely unripe, 
half-ripe, ripe, and over-ripe. YOLOv5 achieved the best 
results with a Mean Average Precision (mAP) of 0.842 
(84.2%), making it the preferred model for these 
classification tasks. YOLOv5 offers faster detection speed, 
good computational efficiency, and can be run on mobile 
devices. The research findings contribute significantly to the 
digitalization of the agricultural industry, especially in the 
context of choosing and harvesting oil palm fruit. 
There are also research studies that use YOLOv4 to detect 
the ripeness of oil palm fruit. Paper [6] discusses a real-time 
detection system for a group of ripe oil palm fruit using 
YOLOv4. The dataset used consists of 490 images, divided 
into 240 positive images containing ripe oil palm fruits and 
250 negative images without ripe oil palm fruits. The research 
shows that the YOLOv4 model achieved mAP above 96% 
during model training. The trained YOLOv4 model then 
tested in the field and it was able to detect ripe oil palm fruits 
in real-time with an average speed of 21 frames per second 
(fps) and achieved a mAP of 87.9%. Paper [11] also uses 
YOLOv4 in its research. This paper discusses the creation 
and use of annotated dataset to determine the ripeness level 
of oil palm fruits using YOLOv4. The dataset consists of 2 
types, video and image datasets. The video dataset contains 
recordings of 45 files for single category and 56 files for multi 
category of oil palm fruit piles. The pre-processing involved 
extracting frames from these videos, which resulted in a total 
of 4160 files with 14559 objects and 7171 images. After that, 
these images were annotated and divided into 2908 images 
for training, 835 images for validation, and 417 images for 
testing. Each image has several object classes, such as unripe, 
under-ripe, ripe, overripe, empty bunch, and abnormal. The 
research findings show that YOLOv4, which has been trained 
using the provided dataset, has good performance with a mAP 
value reaching 99%. This research shows that the use of video 
datasets and deep learning approaches can increase the speed 
in determining the ripeness level of oil palm fruit. However, 
this research also found several challenges, such as class 
imbalances in the dataset and the need for data augmentation 
to improve model performance. These two research studies 
highlight the importance of using deep learning to detect and 
classify the ripeness level of oil palm fruit in real-time. 
Additionally, emphasis is also placed on the process of 
collecting, processing, and annotating datasets, which are 
important steps in developing accurate and reliable models. 
There are also research studies that use YOLOv3. Paper 
[7] discusses the use of YOLOv3 to detect a group of oil palm 
fruit in real-time. The dataset used consists of 1500 images 
for each ripeness class of oil palm fruit, namely unripe, ripe, 
and overripe. The YOLOv3 used in this research was able to 
achieve an accuracy of 95.18% based on the mAP score 
obtained from model testing at the 5000th iteration. However, 
the test results also showed that the model tends to overfit 
after the 5000th iteration. Furthermore, this research found 
that the model had flaws in resistance to light and high color 
disturbances, as well as constraints related to the lack of high-
quality images that can affect detection accuracy. To increase 
model reliability, improvements in dataset collection and 
overfitting handling are needed. Then, paper [10] also uses 
YOLOv3, where the research compares ResNet50 and 
YOLOv3 in classifying the ripeness of oil palm fruit. The 
dataset used consists of 299 images of oil palm fruit with 
three ripeness classes, namely ripe, overripe, and unripe. The 
results show that YOLOv3 is able to outperform ResNet50 in 
extracting oil palm fruit ripeness features from images.

Fig. 1, where the research process begins with identifying the 
problem to be solved. The next step is observing related 
phenomena through direct observations at PTPN IV Medan, 
North Sumatra, Indonesia. Following this, reference sources 
are collected, and then a literature study is carried out related 
to the problem being studied. The next step is collecting the 
dataset, which is directly obtained from PTPN IV Medan,

through pre-processing and augmentation, and this dataset is 
used to train the model. After that, each model is run and the 
results are evaluated, followed by a comparison of the 
accuracy results of both models. Finally, these results are

B. Data Pre-processing and Augmentation 
 
The collected image dataset was uploaded to Roboflow, 
then annotated and labeled for each oil palm fruit object. The 
labeling used is the ripeness of oil palm fruit, which consists 
of 3 classes, namely ripe, rotten, and unripe. After that, each 
image that has been annotated and labeled will be divided into 
3 sets, namely a training set with 124 images, a validation set 
with 37 images, and a testing set with 19 images.  
 
The next step is data pre-processing and augmentation. 
Data pre-processing is carried out to format images before 
being used for model training and inference. Pre-processing 
involves using Auto-Orient to adjust the visual orientation of 
the images to match how they are stored on disk, resizing 
images to 640x640, and using a Null Filter to eliminate 
images marked as null or without objects [12]. Data 
augmentation is carried out to overcome overfitting in the 
trained model and to increase inference accuracy by 
increasing the dataset with different variations [13]. Data 
augmentation uses 4 variations, namely Flip, 90° Rotate, 
Rotation, and Shear. The total dataset obtained after 
augmentation is 428 images, with the number of images in 
the training set increasing to 372 images.  
C. YOLOv8 and YOLOv9 Model 
YOLO is a real-time object detection method in computer 
vision. The YOLOv8 model architecture builds on previous 
YOLO algorithms, incorporating various improvements and 
advanced features. This architecture consists of two major 
components: the backbone and the head. Like previous 
models, YOLOv8 employs a one-stage model, which divides 
the image into regions and makes predictions for bounding 
boxes and probabilities in each region [9]. These probabilities 
are then used to determine if an object is present in each 
bounding box. The model uses the Darknet-53 backbone 
network, known for its speed and accuracy. This backbone 
features partial stage connections across 53 convolutional 
layers, which enhance information flow between layers, 
improve 
feature 
extraction, 
and 
promote 
better 
representation. The head consists of several convolutional 
layers, followed by fully connected layers that make 
predictions related to object detection, including bounding 
boxes, class probabilities, and scores of detected objects. 
YOLOv8 excels at recognizing objects of different sizes. 
Overall, YOLOv8 is a state-of-the-art model that marks a 
significant improvement in real-time object detection. Its 
capabilities include multi-scale object detection in both 
images and videos, utilizing a feature pyramid network 
composed of several layers to detect objects at various scales. 
This pyramid structure allows YOLOv8 to accurately identify 
objects of different sizes. 
YOLOv9 is the latest version of the YOLO series’ real-
time object detection models, designed to increase object 
detection accuracy without increasing computational load 
during inference. YOLOv9 combines two main components, 
namely Generalized Efficient Layer Aggregation Network 
(GELAN) as the backbone and Programmable Gradient 
Information (PGI) as the training method. Combining 
GELAN and PGI allows YOLOv9 to increase its object 
detection accuracy without increasing computational load 
during inference. This makes YOLOv9 the next generation of 
real-time object detection models that are more accurate and 
can be run on limited computational devices [14].

GELAN is a new backbone network developed 
specifically for the real-time object detection model, 
YOLOv9. It is developed based on the previous ELAN 
architecture but with a more generalized structure that allows 
it to integrate different types of computational blocks. This 
increases the architecture's flexibility and portability across 
different tasks. The goal is to increase detection performance 
while remaining lightweight and using fewer computing 
resources. GELAN’s ability to efficiently combine various 
computational modules enables it to extract representative 
features from images while maintaining a good balance 
between accuracy and computational complexity [14]. PGI is 
a new training method implemented in YOLOv9 to address 
the problem of information loss during deep network training. 
PGI consists of two branches, namely the main branch that 
performs inference and the auxiliary branch designed to 
produce reliable gradients in a programmable manner. The 
auxiliary branch has reversible mechanisms for recovering 
information lost during the feedforward process in the main 
branch. Additionally, PGI uses multi-level auxiliary 
information to control the learning of semantic information at 
different levels. Producing more reliable gradients enables 
PGI to improve gradient flow during network optimization, 
thereby increasing the model’s detection performance [14].

C. Discussion 
Based on the results, it was found that the YOLOv8 and 
YOLOv9 models achieved better accuracy compared to 
previous versions of YOLO used by previous researchers in 
studies for detecting the ripeness of oil palm fruit. While no 
research studies have been found using the YOLOv9 model 
for this purpose, YOLOv8 has been used in some, such as 
papers [8] and [9]. When compared to those two research 
studies, the accuracy achieved in this research is higher than 
in research [9], but lower than in research [8]. Although this 
research used a much smaller dataset and fewer epochs for 
model training, the accuracy achieved is not significantly 
lower compared to research studies that used larger datasets 
and more epochs, which can take a long time to train the 
model. By collecting a sufficient and clear dataset to train the 
YOLOv8 and YOLOv9 models with enough epochs, it’s still 
possible to achieve a high accuracy level. There are many 
factors that influence the detection accuracy, one of them is 
the image dataset’s clarity. The clearer the images, the higher 
the accuracy achieved. Another factor is the quantity of the 
image dataset collected, which can affect the trained model’s 
accuracy percentage in detecting an object. Additionally, 
using more epochs in model training can also increase the 
accuracy results, although it takes more training time. 
V. 
CONCLUSIONS 
 
Based on the research, it can be concluded that the latest 
versions of Deep Learning YOLO, YOLOv8 and YOLOv9 
are capable of detecting the ripeness level of oil palm fruit, 
which consists of 3 levels of ripeness, namely ripe, rotten, and 
unripe, with high accuracy. After going through training 
using oil palm fruit datasets collected directly from the oil 
palm plantation of PTPN IV Medan, North Sumatra, 
Indonesia, YOLOv8 and YOLOv9 successfully detected ripe, 
rotten, and unripe oil palm fruit with an accuracy of 0.984 
(98.4%) and 0.99 (99%). This shows great potential for the 
application of computer vision technology to increase 
efficiency and productivity in the oil palm industry. Based on 
this experiment, it can be concluded that YOLOv9 has higher 
accuracy than YOLOv8 in detecting ripe, rotten, and unripe 
oil palm fruit. In applying computer vision technology 
directly in the field, YOLOv9 can be the right choice to use 
in automatically detecting the ripeness level of oil palm fruit 
in oil palm plantations. However, further research is still 
needed to validate and optimize the performance of both 
models under various different environmental conditions. 
The potential use of the latest version of the YOLO model in 
detecting the ripeness level of oil palm fruit in plantations can 
serve as a basis for developing more effective and efficient 
automatic detection systems in the future.
