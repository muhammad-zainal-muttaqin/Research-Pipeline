1. Introduction 
 
Oil palm as the largest vegetable crop in the world, is rich in Southeast Asia because of its vast and profitable 
geographical location [1]. The world palm oil industry contributes around 40% of total vegetable oil production, where 
Malaysia and Indonesia contribute a total of 85% of global production [1]. The oil palm farming, despite its benefits, 
smallholder farmers face lower productivity levels due to limited access to knowledge, funding, agricultural chemicals, 
and markets [2]. The industry also faces challenges in managing large amounts of data and human resources [3]. Until 
now, traditional methods for measuring the maturity of oil palm fruit are still widely used by many industries, such as 
manual observation and the use of simple tools. As a result, inconsistencies can occur during the quality assurance 
process, potentially affecting the final quality, making it impractical and time consuming [4], [5]. Therefore, an

Abstract 
The palm oil industry has always been a pivotal sector on the world's vegetable crop industry, especially for southeast Asian 
countries such as Indonesia and Malaysia. These fast-growing sectors has led to challenges for assessing oil palm fruit maturity. In 
order to overcome these obstacles, this research suggests an automating the detection of oil palm fruit maturity through the use of 
computer vision. The six maturity clusters for oil fruit images are Rotten, Underripe, Unripe, Overripe, and Empty Bunch. Several 
different types of YOLOv8 models, including YOLOv8n, YOLOv8s, YOLOv8m, YOLOv8l, and YOLOv8x, are evaluated in this 
study. The YOLOv8m emerges as the best option, which shows notable gains in accuracy metrics at the same time as processing 
speeds. For example, YOLOv8m processes at an average speed of about 14.68 iterations per second and achieves top-1 and top-5 
accuracy of 0.9885 and 0.998, respectively.

Integrating automation technology and the application of data-based methods, specifically using computer vision 
technology to assess the maturity of palm fruit offers a promising solution to overcome the difficulties in managing 
large oil palm plantations [4], especially in processing large amounts of data and identifying complex patterns. With 
the advent of future technology, measuring the maturity of oil palm fruit can finally be done automatically with good

This research comprehensively reviews previous research on maturity classification of oil palm fruit, exploring 
various methodologies. Existing methods including YOLOv4-Tiny, ANN, SVM, and CNN. Overall, YOLOv4-Tiny 
combined with genetic algorithm optimization and ANN models utilizing various feature extraction techniques appear 
to be the most promising methods for accurately classifying oil palm fruit maturity, offering competitive performance 
and high accuracy levels [6]. 
This research aims to identify the best approach to accurately classify oil palm fruit based on their level of maturity. 
Although YOLOv4-Tiny have been proven effective in previous research [3], this study explores the possibility of a 
more sophisticated object detection model, for oil palm fruit maturity classification [7]. Despite existing research has 
high accuracy in the classification of oil palm fruit maturity, we aim to conduct experiments with YOLOv8. YOLOv8 
has achieved substantially high precision, especially detecting small objects, which is crucial for tasks involving the 
diverse sizes of oil palm fruit [8]. Additionally, YOLOv8's optimized network architecture and advanced segmentation 
capabilities enhance its ability to detect complex shapes and sizes [9]. It is highly scalable and adaptable to larger 
datasets and more intricate tasks [9]. This current study, expected to have better feature extraction, hyperparameter 
tuning capabilities, and enhanced detection performance, this research aims to provide deeper insights into the visual 
characteristics associated with different stages of palm fruit maturity, thereby improving overall efficiency and 
accuracy in managing oil palm plantations. 
2. Related Works 
Previous existing research had been carried out by Abdulrazak et al. [10] in the year 2020, utilizing CNN 
architecture to classify the ripeness of palm oil fruit using the data sourced from Tomanggong Palm Oil Mill in Lahad 
Datu Sabah, comprising 628 images which resulted in high accuracy of 98%, outperformed similar studies conducted 
by Meftah et al. [11] in the same year using an Artificial Neural Network (ANN) classifier which obtain a lower 
accuracy of 94%. 
EfficientNetB0 which is a customized type of CNN was utilized by Wei et al. [12] in the year 2021 for ripeness 
classification with 328 RGB images. By utilizing the model alongside with colored edge maps input, resulted in a 
significantly improved classification accuracy compared to fully colored images. In the same year, a study by Thinal 
Raj et al. [13] aimed to classify palm oil growth using Raman spectroscopy based on carotene content. Various 
classifiers such as Fine KNN and SVM were applied to the data set of 46 oil palm fruit samples. Fine KNN classifier 
achieved good performance with close to perfect accuracy.  
Moving to the year 2022, Suci et al. [14] employed CNN that resulted in the model’s accuracy dropping to 76%. 
Ahmad et al. [15] , in the same year, deployed YOLOv4 for the same purpose using a dataset comprising 309 images 
which resulted in a better accuracy of 97%. Another study conducted by Shahrzad et al. [16], the ANN achieved the 
highest accuracy, 92.5% accuracy in the test phase and 99.1% in the training phase. Mohamed et al. [6] developed a 
reliable classification tool using computer vision to accurately classify fresh oil palm fruits that resulted the 
MobileNetV2 SSD to be a great choice for mobile applications. Furthermore, Meftah et al. [17] developed a real-time 
oil palm fruit bunch (FFB) growth classification system shows the ANN and BGLAM algorithms show the best 
performance. 
Another similar study conducted using K-nearest neighbors (KNN) in the year 2023 by Shoffan et al. [18] that 
effectively increase their accuracy to a high of 97%. In the same year, Stephenie et al. [19] experimented the CNNs 
which utilizes 413 images with a higher epoch of 100 alongside with a higher batch size of 64. This resulted in success 
with mean accuracy over 90%. Similarly, Hamdanzakirin et al. [5] developed a CNN that use 8598 images with 
93.19% accuracy. Mohamed et al. [20] also uses spectroscopy that proves to be highly accurate. Yosua et al. [21]

images from Riau, Indonesia and was categorized into ripe and unripe. However, the accuracy seems to fluctuate 
across the folds, ranging from a low of 63.63% to a high of 90%.

3.3 Model Building & Training 
For model building, we use the YOLOv8 model architecture to enhance the classification of palm fruit oil ripeness; 
to achieve higher accuracy compared to its predecessor. YOLOv8 is our stated-of-the-art classification model which 
incorporates a hybrid backbone network, which in our study uses the newly improved CSPDarknet-AA backbone. 
This backbone network further improves the classification accuracy by the robustness of Convolutional Neural 
Networks or the CNNs alongside with the precision of spatial attention modules. This backbone was chosen for its 
efficiency and performance in object detection tasks, specifically for classification task in this specific research. 
The YOLOv8 model building also includes a significant improvement with the use of ELU (Exponential Linear 
Unit) activation function which has been proven to address the vanishing gradient problem which could lead to a faster 
convergence. For this model, we also use the adoption of GIoU (Generalized Intersection over Union) loss which 
further enhances the precision of object localization. This could result in a better approach on small and overlapping 
objects such as the clustered palm oil fruits.  
YOLOv8 is scalable in various sizes, which are nano, small, medium, large, and extra-large. From the dataset that 
has been prepared, the image resolution resized to 224x224 pixels, which means for this specific study, the model will 
be trained on the architectures YOLOv8n, YOLOv8s, YOLOv8m, YOLOv8l, YOLOv8x. The reason behind this is 
for its capability and for its efficiency between accuracy and computational accuracy which leads to a better model in 
real-time applications. 
In our study, we conducted a series of experiments to determine how varying configurations of training affect the 
performance of YOLOv8 models. We varied parameters such as batch size, learning rate, momentum, weight decay, 
and optimizer choice. For instance, we apply batch sizes 16 and 64 in order to understand the impact of smaller versus 
larger mini-batch updates on how well the model learns and generalizes. We tested the efficiency of initial learning 
rates, at 0.0005 and 0.00075 to reduce the loss function. Momentum values of 0.9 and 0.95 were explored to see how 
different settings affect training stability and convergence speed. Adjusting the weight decay parameter between 
0.0001 and 0.0005 allowed us to study its role in regularization and preventing overfitting. Finally, we compared two 
of the most popular optimization algorithms, Adam and AdamW, for their accuracy and training efficiency. 
For the training of the model, it will be a span of 20 epochs, ensuring that the model has sufficient exposure to the 
dataset to learn and generalize effectively. This approach aims to give a balance between the depth of the model’s 
learning and the practical constraints of computational efficiency. Thus, creating a model that not only achieves high 
accuracy in classifying the ripeness of palm oil fruits but also suitable for real-world applications.

when compared to previous approaches. 
5. Conclusion 
Given the trade-off between accuracy and computational efficiency, YOLOv8m is found to be the best option for 
tasks involving the classification of palm oil when the accuracy and computational efficiency are taken into account 
as the accuracy of the top-1 and top-5 samples both reaching 0.9885 and 0.998, respectively. YOLOv8m uses between 
1.08G and 1.12G of GPU memory, and its average instance processing speed is 14.68 iterations per second. This 
indicates as the most balanced out accuracy and computational efficiency method.
