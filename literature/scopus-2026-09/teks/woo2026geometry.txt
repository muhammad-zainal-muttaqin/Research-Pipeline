Abstract
Berry thinning in table-grape production requires estimating berry
count and bunch structure under severe occlusion. While exist-
ing 3D reconstruction and neural rendering pipelines can recover
dense geometry or enable novel view synthesis, they rarely produce
compact, instance-level models for densely packed grape bunches.
We propose a geometry-aware, three-stage framework that recon-
structs a semantic 3D model of individual berries from handheld
field video. Each berry is represented as an opaque 3D Gaussian
ellipsoid with a 3D center, principal radii, and orientation. Stage
1 applies a berry detection/segmentation network together with
a lightweight structure-from-motion (SfM) COLMAP method run
to obtain multi-view 2D attributes (centers, circle/ellipse parame-
ters, masks, and visibility labels) and initial camera poses; these 2D
observations later act as supervision for optimizing the 3D model.
Stage 2 forms multi-view tracks using epipolar constraints, azimuth-
based neighborhood restriction, and radius consistency (without ap-
pearance descriptors), then triangulates and consolidates 3D berry
centers via geometric gating and 3D non-maximum suppression.
Stage 3 refines the compact semantic model by differentiable ren-
dering under multi-head 2D losses, with conservative clone–prune
topology updates driven by persistent unmatched evidence. On 30
real-field bunches with labelled point-cloud ground truth converted
to 3D ellipsoids, the refined models achieve F1 scores of 78.7–88.5%
at oriented bounding box Intersection of Union (OBB-IoU) thresh-
old (𝜏) at 0.10 and 64.5–74.9% at 𝜏= 0.25, while Stage 3 runs in
1.8–5.1 minutes per bunch for 10–30 selected views. These results

INTRODUCTION
Table-grape quality depends strongly on bunch compactness and
berry size, both of which are regulated during berry thinning. In
practice, workers must estimate the number and spatial arrange-
ment of berries under heavy occlusion and strong self-similarity,
which makes thinning cognitively demanding and leads to variabil-
ity between operators.
Computer-vision tools have been proposed for berry detection
and 2D counting from single images [1–4], but their outputs are in-
herently viewpoint-dependent and do not provide the 3D structure
needed for robust phenotyping or downstream automation. At the
other extreme, general 3D pipelines such as classical structure-from-
motion / multi-view stereo (SfM/MVS) and neural rendering (e.g.
NeRF [5] and 3D Gaussian Splatting [6]) recover dense scene geom-
etry or radiance fields, yet typically lack a compact, instance-level

RELATED WORK
3D reconstruction for agriculture has used classical SfM/MVS
pipelines to recover geometry from multi-view images, but per-
formance in vineyards is often limited by foliage clutter, repeated
textures, motion blur, and occlusion, producing noisy or incomplete
point clouds that require additional segmentation or clustering for
object-level analysis. Neural rendering methods such as NeRF [5],
pose-refining extensions [7], and 3D Gaussian Splatting [6] opti-
mize dense scene representations from posed images and excel
at view synthesis and fine detail, but their outputs are dense and
entangled; obtaining compact, instance-level semantics typically
requires external masks or post-hoc clustering, which is difficult
when many small, similar berries overlap in depth.
For grape bunches specifically, prior work includes laboratory
systems based on laser scanning, stereo, or structured light that
yield accurate point clouds under controlled backgrounds but are
not field-deployable [8, 9], and image-based methods that exploit
spherical priors or simplified geometry to estimate 3D structure
from one or a few views [2, 10]. More recent pipelines combine SfM
with berry segmentation and point-cloud clustering (e.g., K-means)
to initialize per-berry hypotheses and refine them via reprojection
losses [11], but they are sensitive to pose error and point-cloud
quality and depend on external berry-count estimates to set the
number of clusters.

4.2.3
Optimization Schedule and Clone–Prune Topology Mainte-
nance. To avoid instability at the start of training, we adopt a three-
phase schedule:
Phase 1 – Warm-up (pose refinement)
Optimize berry centers 𝑋𝑘and camera extrinsics (𝑅𝑣,𝑡𝑣); keep
radii {𝑠𝑘} and orientations {𝑞𝑘} fixed. Clone–prune is disabled. We
exit warm-up once the average per-view match rate (fraction of
detections matched by the Hungarian step) exceeds a threshold
(e.g., ≥0.40).
Phase 2 – Stable topology with clone–prune.
Freeze camera extrinsics (𝑅𝑣,𝑡𝑣) and continue optimizing 𝑋𝑘(and
optionally a subset of radii). After a short buffer (e.g., 5 epochs), we
enable a conservative topology update:
• Clone: if a 2D detection stays unmatched for at least 3 con-
secutive epochs and lies near a well-supported 3D neighbor,
we spawn a new berry by copying that neighbor’s radii and
orientation and adding a small 3D perturbation. A mini-
mum spacing constraint and a cap on new clones per epoch
prevents duplicates.
• Prune: for each 3D berry we track (i) match-rate and (ii)
high-percentile residuals over a sliding window of epochs.
Berries that are never matched or that have persistently low
match rate and large residuals are removed, again with a
per-epoch cap.
Phase 3 – Scale refinement
Once the loss plateaus, we unfreeze radii {𝑠𝑘} and jointly optimize
(𝑋𝑘,𝑠𝑘) with camera extrinsics fixed. Orientations {𝑞𝑘} remains fixed
in our current implementation.
The final output of Stage 3 is an image-aligned semantic 3D
reconstruction {(𝑋𝑘, 𝑠𝑘, 𝑞𝑘)} whose berry count and geometry are
jointly supported by all selected views.
5
EXPERIMENTAL AND RESULTS
We evaluate the proposed three-stage framework on 30 real-field
table-grape bunch sequences captured with a handheld smartphone
while moving around each bunch to approximate a view ring. The
videos are recorded at a resolution of 1080 × 1920, and all frames
are processed at this native resolution before Stage-1 detection/seg-
mentation. The berry detector/segmentor of [4] is applied to each
frame to obtain per-berry masks and bounding boxes, as described
in Stage 1: Input Processing (2D Supervision and Camera Pose
Estimation).
For each bunch, we first run Stage 2 once to obtain the initial se-
mantic 3D model, denoted INIT. Stage 3 is then executed three times
using view subsets of size 𝑀∈{10, 20, 30}. The corresponding re-
fined models are denoted OPT-10, OPT-20, and OPT-30, respectively.
Unless otherwise stated, quantitative results are reported for both
INIT and the OPT variants.
Stage-3 refinement is implemented in PyTorch and run on a
single NVIDIA RTX 4080 SUPER (16 GB) GPU. We use SGD with
learning rate 10−3 for berry parameters (𝑋𝑘,𝑠𝑘) and a smaller learn-
ing rate 10−5 for camera extrinsics (𝑅𝑣,𝑡𝑣) during the warm-up
phase. Berry orientations 𝑞𝑘are kept fixed throughout refinement
to avoid pose–scale–orientation degeneracies under purely 2D su-
pervision. Reported Stage-3 runtimes correspond to the wall-clock
time from INIT to the converged OPT-M model for each bunch
(Stage-1 and Stage-2 costs are shared across all M and are therefore
not included).

Runtime and Computational Efficiency
Stage 3 runtime increases approximately linearly with the number
of views used in differentiable optimization (Table 1). Using 𝑀= 10
views provide fast refinement (≈1.8 min/bunch), while 𝑀= 30
yields the highest accuracy at increased cost (≈5.1 min/bunch).
These timings are measured from the Stage-2 initialization (INIT)
to the converged refined model (OPT-M); Stage-1 and Stage-2 costs
are shared across all settings and are therefore excluded.
5.4
Counting Accuracy and Topology Behavior
Table 2 reports berry-count MAE for the Stage-2 output (INIT) and
the Stage-3 refined models (OPT-10/20/30). For small and medium
bunches, Stage 2 already achieves low counting error, and Stage
3 largely preserves topology. The main gains appear in dense
bunches (≥60 berries), where refinement significantly reduces
under-counting, particularly at 𝑀= 30(dense MAE: 10.5 →4.8).
Topology updates are conservative: across 30 sequences most
bunches retain the same berry count, and when changes occur, they
are dominated by berry additions rather than deletions, consistent
with the design of the clone–prune mechanism.
5.5
3D Geometric Accuracy Oriented Bounding
Box Intersection of Union (OBB-IoU)
We evaluate 3D geometric quality using OBB-IoU between predicted
and ground-truth ellipsoids. For a given IoU threshold 𝜏, we match
predictions to ground truth by Hungarian assignment on centers
and compute precision, recall, and F1 over matched pairs.
Table 3 shows results for INIT and OPT-M at 𝜏∈{0.10, 0.25, 0.45}.
Differentiable refinement yields substantial gains at 𝜏= 0.10and
𝜏= 0.25, indicating much tighter alignment of berry centres and
scales. Performance at 𝜏= 0.45 remains challenging due to occlu-
sion and noise in the ellipsoid ground truth but still improves over
the Stage-2 initialisation.
Overall, Stage 3 improves F1 by roughly +43–53 points at 𝜏=
0.10 and +54–65 points at 𝜏= 0.25, while keeping refinement time
under 6 minutes per bunch for 𝑀≤30.
5.6
Qualitative Comparison
Figure 4 illustrates a representative dense bunch (ground-truth:
55 berries). The labelled point cloud is converted into a semantic
ground-truth model by fitting Gaussian ellipsoids to each berry (54
berries after minor manual consolidation). The Stage-2 initialisa-
tion already recovers a plausible topology but exhibits noticeable
misalignment in some centres and orientations.
After Stage-3 refinement, models using 𝑉𝑀with 𝑀∈{10, 20, 30}
all converge to the correct berry count (54) and progressively im-
prove geometric alignment, reaching IoU scores of 80.7 (M=10) and
89.9 (M=20/30) with runtimes of about 1.4, 4.2, and 6.2 minutes,
respectively. The refined ellipsoids also correct several erroneous
ground-truth orientations, indicating that the differentiable op-
timisation improves both position and shape under multi-view
supervision.
6
CONCLUSION
We presented a three-stage framework for instance-level 3D seman-
tic reconstruction of grape berries from short handheld multi-view
sequences captured in real vineyards. Stage 1 extracts multi-view
2D supervision (centres, shape/scale, masks, boxes, visibility) and
initial camera poses from a lightweight SfM COLMAP run. Stage 2
builds a compact 3D berry set via geometry-aware matching and
triangulation with conservative validation and 3D consolidation.
