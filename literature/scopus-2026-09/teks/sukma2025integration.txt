Abstract— This research develops a computer vision-based 
system to detect oil palm fruit bunches in low light conditions. 
The main contribution of this study is to integrate two methods 
that can be used to detect oil palm bunches in low light 
conditions caused by cloudy weather conditions and dense 
foliage. The proposed approach is to integrate Single Scale 
Retinex (SSR) to improve the quality of light intensity in the 
image, then the detection process is carried out by Single Shot 
Multibox Detector (SSD). The experiment was carried out 
using images of oil palm fruit bunches taken directly on the 
tree using a resolution of 1920x1080 pixels and a shutter speed 
of 1/400 and ISO 400. The tests were divided into three 
categories based on light intensity level (lux) and data 
acquisition time, each consisting of 104 images for 245 bunches. 
In the first category (1000–1600 lux, daytime), the detection 
results achieved 100% precision, 85.31% recall, and 92.07% 
F1 score. The second category (500–999 lux, morning) 
recorded a precision score of 99.52%, a recall of 84.08%, and 
an F1 score of 91.15%. Meanwhile, the third category (200–499 
lux, afternoon) produced a precision of 98.55%, a recall of 
84.30%, and an F1 score of 90.87%. A comparative analysis 
with ISO 3200 camera settings without an image enhancement 
algorithm shows that the combination of ISO 400 and SSR 
provides a 16.75% performance improvement at 1000–1600 
lux light intensity, by 48.2% at 500–999 lux light intensity and 
69.21% at 200–499 lux and. A comparison was also made with 
SSDs integrated with MSR, the results showed that SSDs 
integrated with SSR had a high F1 Score. These results 
confirm that integrating SSR with the detection model can 
improve the detection performance of the model in low and 
uneven light conditions. 
Keywords— Palm Oil, Single Shot multi-box Detector, Single 
Scale Retinex, Low Light Detection 
I. INTRODUCTION 
Palm oil (Elaeis guineensis) is one of the many plantation 
crops that have high economic value in the world, The 
demand for palm oil keeps increasing because of its broad 
application in various oleochemical industries and its cost-
effectiveness compared to other vegetable oils [1]. Indonesia 
is one of the major palm oil producers, with an annual 
production of 47 million tonnes [2], [3]. Palm oil is a major 
source of oil that is widely used in the food, cosmetics, and 
biofuel industries [1], [3]. One of the important aspects of oil 
palm management is the harvesting process and monitoring 
of quality fresh fruit bunches because the ripeness of the fruit 
greatly affects the resulting production, which is also 
described in the study [1], [4]. The efficiency of the 
harvesting process is a big challenge, especially for large-
scale plantations, so effective and accurate supporting 
technology is needed. Computer vision aims to develop 
algorithms that make it easier for machines to identify and 
understand visual content in their environment, similar to 
human visual recognition [5], [6]. In agriculture, computer 
vision has an important role in the automation of various 
types of tasks that can make it easier for farmers, such as 
detecting pests on plants, counting the number of plants, 
analyzing plant health, and automatic harvesting processes 
[7], [8]. In oil palm farming, computer vision technology can 
help farmers efficiently identify the location of oil palm fruit 
bunches and the harvesting process automatically, thereby 
reducing dependence on human labor and can increase 
productivity in agriculture [7], [9]. Utilizing computer 
vision-based algorithms, the fruit identification process can 
be carried out faster, more accurately and efficiently, even in 
large plantation areas. 
The success of computer vision-based models relies 
heavily on the algorithm's ability to extract relevant features 
from images. Feature extraction is the process of retrieving 
important or characteristic information from data or objects, 
such as images or text, for use in analysis or pattern 
recognition [10], [11]. Features such as texture, color, and 
shape must be well analyzed so that the model can 
distinguish the object it wants to detect from its background 
[10], [12].  
Low light intensity conditions caused by cloudy 
conditions, the position of fruits covered with dense foliage, 
or even harvest time carried out in the afternoon or morning 
are one of the main challenges for computer vision systems. 
Research conducted by [1] developed a YOLOv4-based 
model to detect FFB in images taken under normal lighting 
conditions, but its accuracy decreases in low lighting 
conditions. Previous studies [13], [14], [15] and [16] 
conducted the detection of oil palm fruit bunches using only 
yolo in various versions and faster R-CNN without 
conducting trials in plantation sites with low light intensity 
levels, which can cause the detection model to have 
difficulty in recognizing objects. 
Low light intensity conditions cause deterioration in the 
quality of the images captured by the camera, such as lack of 
contrast, uneven lighting, or loss of visual detail, so the 
detection model has difficulty distinguishing the oil palm 
fruit bunches from the background or other surrounding 
objects. Therefore, special handling, such as lighting 
adjustments or algorithms that are resistant to various 
lighting conditions, is needed to overcome these constraints. 
Unlike previous studies that only used one method, this 
study focuses on solving the problems described in the 
previous paragraph by using an approach that combines two 
methods of image enhancement and detection model. The 
method used is Single-Scale Retinex (SSR) to improve the 
image, then it will detect oil palm fruit bunches using a 
Single Shot Multibox Detector (SSD), which aims to

II. PROPOSED METHOD 
In this study, we developed a detection model integrated 
with an algorithm to increase the light on the image to detect 
oil palm fruit bunches in low light conditions. In developing 
a computer vision system to detect oil palm fruit bunches on 
trees, the algorithm is trained using image data or samples of 
oil palm fruit bunches on trees. The complete process of this 
research, from data preparation to detection of oil palm fruit 
bunches, is illustrated as a flowchart in Figure 1 that will 
provide a comprehensive view of the methodological 
workflow implemented in this study.

A. Data Preparation 
The data preparation phase includes two important steps, 
image acquisition, and selection, to produce a good model 
for detecting oil palm fruit bunches. 
1) 
Image acquisition. 
The oil palm trees selected in this research object are oil 
palm trees that have a height ranging from 2 to 7 meters 
because that is when the tree produces the most FFB and has 
dense leaves.

The hardware used in the image acquisition process is a 
DJI neo drone connected to a smartphone to capture images 
of oil palm fruit bunches at a height of 7 meters. Figure 2

than 1 meter away from the oil palm fruit bunches. Figure 3

that environmental conditions varied and different conditions 
were observed. During the data acquisition process, the 
camera settings were done manually with a shutter speed of 
1/400 to avoid blurring in the image due to drone shocks 
caused by strong winds and an ISO of 400 to avoid high 
noise, considering the sensor used in the 1/2 inc type drone. 
2) 
Image selection 
The image selection process is very important for building a 
model so that the resulting model performs well. In this 
process, we clean one of the images that resemble each 
other and the image with a high level of blur. The images 
that made it through this phase totaled 1385. Figure 3 shows 
a sample image used as training and validation data.

B. Data Preparation 
The preprocessing phase includes several important 
steps, which are as follows. 
1) Image Annotations 
In this stage, the image is annotated or labeled using 
Roboflow and stored in the TensorFlow record (record) 
format. 
2) Data Split 
The dataset is divided into two, of which 785 data are 
used for training and 600 for validation during the model 
training process. 
3) Image Augmentasi 
This stage aims to increase the variety of data in the 
dataset and strengthen the generalization of the model. 
Augmentation is done through techniques such as 90º 
rotation, 45º rotation, and flip. This process produced 2955 
images, of which 2355 were used as training data and 600 as 
model training evaluation data. The dataset consists of one 
class, namely palm oil. 
C. Data Preparation 
This study proposes an advanced learning method a 
single shot multibox detector (SSD) using the EfficientNet 
backbone to detect oil palm fruit bunches. SSDs are one of 
the CNN architectures for object detection designed to 
perform end-to-end detection in a single shot [17], [18]. 
Research [18] explains that SSDs combine object detection 
and classification in a single convolutional network, allowing 
for simultaneously detecting objects at various scales. 
In contrast to previous object detection methods such as 
R-CNN and Faster R-CNN that separated the feature 
extraction and bounding box prediction processes, SSD 
integrate the two processes in a single network[18]. This 
allows additional convolutional layers that produce feature 
maps at various resolutions, allowing for the detection of 
objects of varying sizes without the need for separate feature 
extraction and classification stages. Figure 5 shows the 
architect of a single shot multibox detector using an efficient 
net backbone with an input image size of 512 x 512 pixels.

D. Single Scale Retinex 
This study using the Single Scale Retinex (SSR) method 
as part of image preprocessing. SSR is a method designed to 
improve image visibility by correcting uneven lighting and 
increasing local contrast. SSR works on the principle that 
imagery can be represented as a combination of illumination 
and reflectance. Research conducted. explains that SSR 
algorithms can improve lighting quality and contrast without 
losing detail in images. In the SSR method, the reflection of 
light is considered as relevant information for further 
processing, it can solve the problem of uneven light in the 
image. Mathematically, SSR is formulated as follows. 
 
R(x, y) = log(I(x, y)) - log(F(x, y)) * I(x, y) 
(1) 
Explanation: 
• 
I(x, y) is the pixel intensity at the location (x, y) 
• 
F(x, y) is a Gaussian function defined by the scale 
parameter (σ) 
• 
*  represents convolutional operations. 
Improving the light in the image is carried out by 
comparing the intensity of the original pixels with the 
version of the image that has been flattened using a Gaussian 
filter. Gaussian filters play a very important role in the SSR 
method, where they simulate the global spread of light so 
that the SSR algorithm can separate the light information 
from the details of the image. In this study, the blur spread 
rate or variance parameter used in the Gaussian filter was 
300 σ. 
III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
A. Single Shot Detector Training Performance 
We use the COCO evaluation matrix standard to evaluate 
the training performance of the single-shot detector model. 
The Common Objects in Context (COCO) evaluation metrics 
standard is used to assess model performance in computer 
vision, specifically object detection, segmentation, and 
tracking. Key metrics include Average Precision (AP), which 
measures the accuracy of object detection at various 
Intersection over Union (IoU) thresholds, such as AP@0.5 
(AP50) and AP@0.75, and Average Recall (AR), which 
measures the model's ability to identify all existing objects. 
In addition, Localization Error measures the accuracy in 
detecting the position of objects. The COCO evaluation 
metrics standard provides a comprehensive overview of the 
model's performance during the training process in 
recognizing and mapping objects in images. It is widely used 
in computer vision research and competitions.

B. Detection Testing in Low Light Conditions 
This system was tested using images of oil palm fruit 
bunches with fairly low light intensity conditions. The place 
where the test data is obtained is different from the place 
where the training data is obtained. This aims to find out the 
extent to which the model that has been built can recognize 
new oil palm fruit bunches. The targeted trees are 2 to 7 
meters tall.  
The analysis of the test data is divided into three 
categories based on the level of light intensity (lux) and the 
time of data acquisition. The higher the lux value, the 
brighter the light intensity. The intensity of the light is 
measured using a lux meter; Figure 2 shows the light meter 
used (lux meter). Light intensity measurements were carried 
out around oil palm trees that were covered in the shadow of 
oil palm leaves. The first category is an average of 1000–
1600 lux, which is obtained during the day (11.30 am) when 
the weather is cloudy. The second category is an average of 
500-999 lux, which is obtained in the morning (8.00 am) in 
cloudy light conditions. The third category is an average of 
200-499 lux, which is obtained in the afternoon (at 5.00 pm) 
in cloudy light conditions. The three categories each consist 
of 104 images of 35 different trees, with 245 bunches of oil 
palm fruit. Similar to the acquisition of training data, the 
process of obtaining test data is the distance between the 
camera and the group is no more than 1 meter. The strong 
wind conditions in the oil palm plantation caused the drone 
to shake and cause the resulting image to become blurry, so 
it required manual camera adjustment to keep the shutter 
speed high even in low light conditions, we used a shutter 
speed of 1/400 and ISO 400 to avoid a lot of noise in the 
image.  
The test uses a confusion matrix with a threshold value of 
50%, where the confusion matrix can calculate various 
important metrics such as precision, recall, and F1 score, 
which provides further insight into the model's performance.

C. Comparison of Detection Results 
In this study, we compared several methods of detecting 
oil palm fruit bunches in low light conditions with four main 
approaches: using ISO 400 directly on drone cameras, using 
ISO 3200 directly on drone cameras, ISO 400 combined with 
MSR algorithm and ISO 400 combined with SSR. ISO 400 is 
used to preserve image detail by reducing noise from the 
beginning of the shoot, then digitally increasing the 
brightness and contrast using the SSR algorithm. In contrast, 
ISO 3200 is used to increase the sensitivity of the camera 
sensor without additional processing. As before, the test was 
carried out by dividing the test data into three categories 
based on the level of light intensity (lux) and data acquisition 
time.

i.e., 1000-1600 lux, 500-999 lux, and 200-499 lux, based on

directly, increased sensitivity often leads to significant noise 
and loss of detail, especially in dark areas. Meanwhile, the 
ISO 400+SSR enhancement approach results in images with 
more preserved detail, more controlled noise, and better 
contrast distribution, making it more suitable for image 
analysis and auto-detection applications in low-light 
environments. 
IV. CONCLUSION 
Oil palm is one of the plantation crops with high 
economic value around the world. Efficiency of the 
harvesting process and monitoring of bunch quality is very 
necessary, especially for large-scale plantations, so effective 
and accurate supporting technology is needed. Computer 
vision technology can help identify fruit bunch locations 
efficiently, minimizing reliance on human labor and 
increasing productivity. Low light intensity conditions 
caused by cloudy conditions, the position of fruits covered in 
dense foliage, or even harvest times carried out in the 
afternoon or morning are great challenges to the performance 
of computer vision systems. This study evaluates the 
effectiveness of SSR integrated with SSD in detecting oil 
palm fruit bunches in plantations that experience low light 
during the detection process. The analysis of the detection 
test was divided into three categories based on the level of 
light intensity (lux) and the time of acquisition of test data, 
each consisting of 104 images with a total of 245 bunches. In 
the first category of 1000–1600 lux (daytime), the detection 
results achieved 100% precision, 85.31% recall, and 92.07% 
F1-score. The second category of 500–999 lux (morning) 
recorded a precision of 99.52%, 84.08% recall, and 91.07% 
of F1 scores. Meanwhile, the third category of 200–499 lux 
(afternoon) produced 99.52% precision, 84.30% recall, and 
90.87% F1 score. A comparative analysis with an ISO 3200 
camera setup without an image enhancement algorithm 
showed that the combination of ISO 400 and SSR provided a 
16.75% performance improvement at 1000–1600 lux light 
intensity, by 48.2% at 500–999 lux light intensity and 
69.21% at 200–499 lux light intensity and. These results 
confirm that integrating single-scale retinex in the detection 
model can improve the detection performance of the model 
in low and uneven light conditions. The next work will be to 
develop a system to help classify the ripeness and identify 
the quality of oil palm fruit bunches in low light conditions 
and accelerate the computing time of the system so that it can 
be used in real-time.
