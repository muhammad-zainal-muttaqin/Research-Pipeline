Abstract—Proper counting of fruits on trees is vital for 
proper timing & scheduling of harvesting, labor force 
requirement and making agricultural sector more efficient. 
Manual methods are costly and time-consuming together with 
being inaccurate in most if not all aspects hence the need for 
automation. It also demonstrates how real-time fruit detection 
and counting can be performed using YOLOv5 with potential in 
handling orchards in a more efficient manner. YOLOv5 was 
trained on a dataset of fruits in different settings including clear 
sun lit conditions, in overcast conditions, and in conditions 
where fruits are entangled in large vegetation. The proposed 
model was able to release the highest detection accuracy with 
higher precision, recall, and F1 scores; 97.5%, 96.1%, 
respectively and 96.8% for the F1-score. It also attained a mean 
Average Precision (mAP@0.5) of 98.2%, and further validating 
its endurance in fruit detection regardless of scenarios. Due to 
the YOLOv5’s real-time indexing that provides about 30 FPS on 
edge devices, that makes it ideal to be used on drones and mobile 
autonomous vehicles in large-scale orchards. A comparison with 
other object detection models like Faster R-CNN, SSD, and 
Mask R-CNN, proved that YOLOv5 performs better in 
accuracy as well as in time efficiency. However, there was a 
small decline in the detection accuracy in cases of partial fruit 
occlusion or dense foliage. In conclusion, this particular work 
demonstrates that indeed YOLOv5 can easily solve the problem 
of automated fruit counting and bring multiple enhancements 
to the orchard management and precision agriculture.  
Keywords—YOLOv5, automated fruit counting, orchard 
management, real-time detection. 
I. INTRODUCTION  
A continuity of using more efficient and sustainable ways 
of farming has led to adoption of various technologies in 
farming. One such area that seems to have a lot of prospect is 
the area of automatism of the harvest procedure more 
accentuated in orchards where the precise counting of fruits is 
vital for establishing crop density, yield potentiality, and farm 
planning[1]. Previous approaches of fruit counting are 
tedious, cumbersome and result oriented for the most part 
hence they have higher propensity to result in mistakes and 
therefore poor estimation of the overall yield. These 
limitations can place negative impacts on crop yield 
prediction, transport, and cash flow estimates for the farmers. 
For this reason, there is a current focus on the utilization of 
computer vision and Machine Learning based tools to address 
this type of problem. Out of the many methods of using 
machine learning, object detection models like YOLO have 
emerged as some of the best because they are real-time and 
accurate. YOLOv5, the most recent modification in this 
family, has proved efficient in the speed and precision of 
object detection, perfect for application in farming. The 
YOLOv5 model is built to perform real-time and precise 
object detection which may enable direct fruits counting from 
images or sequential video acquired in orchards. Using the 
YOLOv5 algorithm, accurate and automatic counts of fruits in 
orchard environments can be obtained without much human 
intervention[2]. 
Issues that can be experienced in the orchard include 
occlusion, changes in light and structures that are nearly equal 
in size to fruits like the leaves. The various obstacles faced in 
object detection can be addressed owing to YOLOv5’s feature 
extraction and multi-scale head properties. It can distinguish 
between small fruits as well as large fruits and it can identify 
fruits normally in position and even when there are other 
greens around the fruits. The ability to consume stream data 
also enables the monitoring of the orchard in real-time hence 
being useful in giving information of fruit counts all season. 
Among those, the use of automated fruit counting systems 
based on YOLOv5 is especially helpful for precision 
agriculture. It allows farmers to use their data scientifically 
when it comes to time for harvesting, resources to apply as 
well as estimates yield rates in the future. More so, the detailed 
records of fruits avail an avenue through which farmers can 
plan their harvesting time effectively to capture the market 
best option and high quality fruits. In addition, it can quickly 
identify instances of over or under-production and localize 
production to each tree, thereby limiting wastage[3].

Besides the short-term advantages in yield management, 
mechanical fruit counting also has value in strategic orchard 
cultivation. Information gathered from at least three 
successive seasons gives trends of fruit yield in order to 
address various decisions on plant management and resource 
utilization in the farm. This data can be combined with other 
smart farming technologies such as the irrigation management 
systems to make a comprehensive approach to the required 
orchard management. For instance, accurate fruit count data 
help the farmers establish a relationship between the amounts 
of water used and the favourable conditions for crop growth. 
Another aspect of labour efficiency taken by the use of 
YOLOv5 for fruit counting is also achieved[4]. In most of the 
agricultural production areas the number of employable 
skilled workers to undertake manual operations like counting 
fruits is rapidly declining. This problem is tackled by 
automated systems that take a significant amount of work 
away from human beings in order to have them work on more 
complex tasks in systems that require a human touch. This in 
addition to enhancing the over production, it also cuts down 
the operating expenses for the owners of the orchards. Also, 
YOLOv5 is easily available due to being open-source for 
being utilized by researchers, developers, and farmers. It has 
high versatility and can be responses to local requirements of 
the specific orchards, types of fruits and environmental 
conditions. The model can be effectively fine-tuned and 
trained on these datasets so that it works in the best way 
possible in different orchard layouts and in relation to different 
species of fruits starting from the basic—the apples, 
oranges—through to those that are less known to the average 
person. 
II. RELATED WORKS 
The estimated yield using real-time imaging and artificial 
intelligence-based computer vision has received significant 
interest in the last few years because of the advantages it 
presents for problematic agriculture. Many scholars have 
investigated different approaches to automate the fruit 
counting procedure, and in earlier work, researchers used 
conventional image analysis procedures. For example, to 
count fruits in images especially in structured backgrounds the 
researchers employed color based segmentation and 
thresholding. However these methods were often restricted by 
environmental factors including lighting changes, shadow and 
occlusion which greatly hindered their precision and stability 
in real life orchard environment. To overcome these 
challenges, the researchers have gradually moved to machine 
learning techniques that are more effective in terms of 
modeling orchard setting[5]. 
There are some of the early methods of automated fruit 
detection that used one of these two methods; Support Vector 
Machines (SVMs) and k-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) classifies. 
These methods were applied to sort fruits from non fruits 
objects depending on their color, roughness and size. Albeit 
these types of models seemed to be promising, they generally 
needed a great deal of feature manipulation and were trained 
in a more traditional manner to suit different types of fruits 
and or climate conditions. With the focus on the desire for 
more generalizable solutions deep learning models started to 
evolve as a stronger approach. Specifically, the Convolutional 
neural networks CNN, which became one of the most 
effective tools for object detection and recognition tasks in 
recent years makes it possible to learn desired features from 
data minimizing the need in manual feature extraction[6]. 
Machine learning dominated all the performed fruit 
detection and counting tasks, with high accuracy marks 
brought by the deep learning models such as Faster R-CNN 
and SSD (Single Shot Multibox Detector). These models were 
able to correctly identify fruits especially in complicated real-
world settings in the orchards, challenges such as different 
fruit size, fruits that more than one is grouped together and 
occlusion were dealt with well. Faster R-CNN method for 
example, employs the use of RPN which produces prospective 
object areas and then a classification/retrieval region and 
bounding box regression and because of its high resolution, is 
ideal for fruit detection in high resolution images. Like SSD, 
our approach also does not require region proposals providing 
better detection speed as well as the comparable accuracy. 
These models have been applied to agricultural uses and are 
widely recognized for establishing a new benchmark of 
automatic fruit counting[7]. 
However, the applications of Faster R-CNN and SSD there 
are mentioned, one of the practical problems in the orchards is 
the necessity of real-time computations. This, especially for 
large scale production of fruits, where counting of fruits needs 
to be done at regular intervals across the large extent of the 
orchard. To meet this need, the YOLO (You Only Look Once) 
series of models was developed, which enabled the real-time 
detection of objects in high speed while still maintaining a 
remarkable accuracy. Basically, YOLO models addresses 
object detection as a regression problem where bounding 
boxes and class probabilities are regressed at once. The best 
performing models for the agricultural applications were 
YOLOv3 and YOLOv4 as its real time detection speed and 
accurate fruit counts made the YOLO algorithms ideal for 
counting fruits in an orchard[8]. 
The latest version, YOLOv5, brings more enhancements 
to it which are architectural improvements to its precedent. 
These include improving model efficiency including focus 
layer, CSP (Cross Stage Partial) connections and mosaic data 
augmentation through segmenting the images into small 
portions and augmenting them to form images of different 
sizes. Some of the new investigations incorporating YOLOv5 
have been undertaken in the agricultural domain and its 
usability for fruit recognition and enumeration across apples, 
oranges, and grapes has been shown. These advantages of the 
model – its relatively light weight and stellar inference speed 
– suggest that it’s well-suited for deployment on edge devices 
like drones and mobile phones, so that orchards can be 
monitored in real-time[9]. 
Other than the YOLO family, several object detection 
models have been used in the automated fruit counting. For 
example, Mask R-CNN derived from Faster R-CNN has been 
applied for instance segmentation where not only the fruits but 
also the pixel-level boundaries are identified. This model has 
been particularly useful in counting fruits in clusters where 
fruits tend to obscure one another as in the case of grapevines 
or tomatoes. This way, by segmenting each fruit instance 
separately, Mask R-CNN counts correctly in these scenarios, 
though it took more time to execute as against the time that 
YOLO-based models consumed[10]. 
The enhancement of these object detection models with 
UAV systems has also broadened their suitability in extensive 
orchards. Camera mounted UAVs can capture images of 
entire orchards and such wide-angle images can be rerouted to 
models like YOLOv5 for fruit counting at a more massive 
level. This approach has been used especially for stapling

Furthermore, scholars have studied transfer learning as an 
enhancement technique for applying deep learning techniques 
to fruit counting in orchards. Because deep learning models 
often involve big amounts of labeled data for training, transfer 
learning enables to retrain the models trained on the large scale 
databases such as ImageNet on the small scale target domain 
bases such as fruit detection databases. This approach has 
been demonstrated to yield shorter training time while 
simultaneously providing high detection accuracy especially 
for those species of fruits which are less commonly or rarely 
featured in the training set. Employing transfer learning 
approaches, it has been shown that approaches like YOLOv5 
and Mask R-CNN may be fine-tuned to learn about particular 
orchard arrangements[12]. 
The recent progress in unfolding the capabilities of 
synthetic data for realizing deep learning models has also 
contributed to the creation of better fruit counting systems. 
The process of collecting, and annotating real-world data on 
orchards may be lengthy and expensive; hence, researchers 
have resorted to the creation of synthetic data using tools such 
as GANs and full 3D modeling. Real lighting-conditions and 
occlusion and fruit growth stages can also be emulated in the 
synthesis of the data and this enhances the generalization of 
the machine learning model. This approach has been 
particularly useful in training on low-frequency or hard-to-
photon fruits such that YOLOv5 models can effectively detect 
in various orchard settings[13]. 
III. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY 
The approach for the automated fruit counting in the 
orchards is proposed using the YOLOv5 for the detection and 
count of fruits in the real-time: These steps are designed to 
solve some problems of the orchard environment, for 
example, changes in lightning, and occlusions, and the 
presence of other similarly sized objects, e.g., leaves and 
branches. The methodology is divided into the following 
phases: acquisition and preparation of data, on selection and 
training of the data model, on deployment and final 
assessment of the data model[14]. 
A. Data Collection 
Data acquisition is the foundational step for any machine-
learning pipeline. In this system, high-quality images or video 
streams of orchards are captured using drones, mobile phones, 
or fixed cameras. These devices are deployed to collect data 
under varying environmental conditions such as bright 
sunlight, overcast weather, and during different stages of fruit 
growth[15]. The aim is to ensure that the dataset is diverse 
enough to represent real-world orchard scenarios, including 
occlusions by leaves or branches and overlapping fruits. 
Multispectral or hyperspectral imaging can be optionally 
included to provide additional spectral data for better 
separation of fruits from their surroundings. 
B. Data Preprocessing 
Once the images are collected, preprocessing ensures they 
are suitable for training the YOLOv5 model. First, bounding 
boxes are annotated around the fruits using tools like 
LabelImg. Data augmentation techniques are applied to make 
the dataset robust to variations, such as flipping, rotation, 
scaling, and adjustments in brightness and contrast[16-19]. 
Mosaic augmentation, a feature of YOLOv5, is also used to 
combine multiple images into a single input to improve 
detection of fruits across scales and environments. 
C. Model Training 
The YOLOv5 model is trained using transfer learning, 
starting with weights pre-trained on the COCO dataset. The 
model is fine-tuned on the orchard-specific dataset to learn 
fruit detection effectively. Parameters such as learning rate, 
batch size, and Intersection over Union (IoU) thresholds are 
optimized to achieve the best performance. During training, 
metrics like Precision, Recall, F1-score, and mean Average 
Precision (mAP) are monitored to ensure the model's accuracy 
and efficiency.

D. Deployment 
The trained YOLOv5 model is integrated into edge 
devices like drones or mobile phones for real-time 
deployment. These devices capture video streams or images 
of the orchard and process them using YOLOv5 to detect and 
count fruits. The model’s lightweight architecture and high 
frame-per-second (FPS) processing capability ensure minimal 
latency, making it ideal for real-time applications. 
E. Post-Processing 
Post-processing involves aggregating fruit counts from 
each frame to produce an accurate count. Object tracking 
algorithms like DeepSORT are employed to assign unique IDs 
to detected fruits, ensuring that duplicates are not counted as 
the camera moves. Spatial and temporal data are also used to

F. Evaluation 
The deployed system is evaluated in real-world conditions

F1-score are measured under various environmental 
scenarios, such as bright sunlight and dense foliage. The 
system is also benchmarked against other models like Faster 
R-CNN and SSD to highlight the advantages of YOLOv5 in 
terms of accuracy and speed. 
G. Feedback and Continuous Improvement 
To enhance the model's robustness, new data is 
periodically collected from the orchard and used to retrain the 
model. Synthetic data generated using techniques like GANs 
can supplement real-world data to handle rare or challenging 
scenarios. Additionally, feedback from real-time deployment 
is used to fine-tune the model, ensuring its adaptability to 
evolving orchard conditions.

V. CONCLUSION 
In this work, we discussed how the authors applied and 
utilized the YOLOv5 model to automatically count fruits in 
orchards using deep learning techniques to achieve high fruit 
counting accuracy, and real-time and stable performance 
regardless of weather conditions. The model showed very 
high accuracy in the fruit detection and counting with the 
specificity of 97,5%, sensitivity of 96,1% and F1 coefficient 
of 96,8%. Realtime results with 30 FPS on edge devices show 
how it can be integrated into UAVs and mobile platforms for 
effective orchard management. Compared to other object 
detection models including Faster R-CNN, SSD, and Mask R-
CNN, we found that YOLOv5 had higher accuracy and speed 
while providing reasonable precision for usage in real-time 
applications. However, a few difficulties are still observed 
when there are high levels of concealment through foliage, or 
partial fruit occlusion, in which case detection accuracy is 
slightly lower. Some future directions include applying a more 
novel data augmentation strategy and synthetic data to these 
cases to improve outcomes. In general, YOLOv5 has 
considerable potential in automating fruit counting for 
gardens, promising the enhancement of harvest management, 
work efficiency, and the role of data in current precision 
farming.
