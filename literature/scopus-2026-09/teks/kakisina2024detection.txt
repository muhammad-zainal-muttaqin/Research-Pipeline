Abstract—This paper uses YOLOv7 to detect and collect 
loose oil palm fruit with an autonomous harvesting robot. 
YOLOv7 was selected for its high accuracy and real-time 
detection capabilities. Data from high-resolution cameras in an 
oil palm plantation was annotated to create a robust training set. 
Key hyperparameters like Anchor, Learning Rate, and Weight 
Decay were optimized to enhance performance. The robot used 
YOLOv7 to detect fruit, prioritizing picking based on bounding 
box coordinates. Hyperparameter set A excelled in detection 
accuracy and precision, while set B slightly outperformed in 
bounding box prediction. The mAP50 was 0.866 and 0.823 for 
sets A and B, respectively. The optimized model significantly 
improved the robot's efficiency in real-time harvesting. 
Keywords—Loose Oil Palm Fruit Detection, Autonomous 
Harvesting Robot, YOLOv7, Deep Learning in Agriculture, 
Hyperparameter Optimization  
I. INTRODUCTION 
Oil palm (Elaeis Guineensis) is a plant originally from 
West Africa, but it is now widely cultivated in various 
Southeast Asia countries, including Indonesia and Malaysia. 
This plant is well known for its fruit, which produces palm oil, 
one of the world's most widely produced vegetable oils [1]. In 
the food industry, palm oil is used to produce margarine, 
cooking oil, processed meat products, bakery products, and 
more. Additionally, palm oil is also used in various non-food 
products such as biodiesel, soap, detergents, cosmetics, and 
other personal care products. 
The manual collection of oil palm loose fruits is 
considered inefficient and time-consuming [2] and can cause 
several issues, such as fatigue and symptoms related to unsafe 
working postures[3]. Workers often have to adopt an 
uncomfortable bent-over position for prolonged periods [4]. 
Additionally, loose fruits left behind can negatively impact 
plant growth, as they can become weeds around the trees if not 
harvested promptly[5], which is why the manual harvesting 
process does not yield optimal results[6]. 
Instead of constantly bending over or squatting, workers 
can harvest oil palm loose fruits by using devices like rollers, 
spike collectors, and separator baskets. These instruments still 
can't tell the loose oil palm fruit from surrounding items, 
though, so unwanted elements ontinue to accumulate. 
Workers must, therefore, sort the loose fruits before putting 
them in a container or basket. Loose oil palm fruit can also be 
collected using a suction machine. With a connected blower, 
this machine operates on diesel fuel. It can extract dust and 
other impurities into a separate container and suction loose 
fruits. Research has shown that the use of these devices can 
effectively reduce workers` workloads in collecting loose 
fruits from oil palm trees. However, these tools can damage 
the palm fruits, which may increase the free fatty acid content 
during storage and reduce the quality of the harvested fruit. 
Ads are used to collect loose fruits from oil palm trees. 
However, these tools can damage the palm fruits, which may 
increase the free fatty acid content during storage and reduce 
the quality of the harvested fruit. Additionally, operating the 
machine requires 2-3 workers and fuel, making the effort 
needed at least three times greater than manual methods to 
achieve the same results [7]. 
Therefore, smart robots have become a crucial factor in 
technological innovation across various sectors, including 
agriculture [8]. With the application of computer vision, 
cameras mounted on robots can capture images in real time 
[9]. Computer vision has rapidly become one of the most 
important areas of computer science. It focuses on developing 
machines that can "see" and understand images. This 
technology uses cameras and computers to replace human 
visual abilities and identify, classify, and measure specific 
objects in images, which can then be further processed for a 
variety of applications. 
The camera will be mounted on the robot, and through 
computer algorithms, loose fruits can be automatically 
detected based on visual characteristics such as shape, color, 
and size [10]. One type of smart robot used in the harvesting 
process is the grasping robot. This technology employs robots 
to pick up, hold, and manipulate objects using hands or 
grippers [11, 12]. In the context of harvesting automation, 
accurately measuring the distance for object retrieval with the 
robot is a crucial aspect [13]. 
This study contributes to the detection and collection 
sequence of loose palm fruits using deep learning, 
significantly reducing time and effort. The detection process 
improves fruit handling efficiency by applying computer 
vision, enabling more precise actions. The bounding boxes 
generated during detection provide the coordinates for 
estimating the distance between the collector and the loose 
fruits. The absolute distance is calculated using the center 
points of these boxes, optimizing retrieval and minimizing 
time and costs in the collection process. 
To provide an efficient solution, this study deals with 
detecting and collecting correctly free oil palm fruits by an 
autonomous harvesting robot. The main issues to address are 
how this study effectively detects individual loose fruits in the 
complex environment and what is the most appropriate 

picking sequence to harvest efficiently. Several advantages of 
this proposed method compared to the existing techniques: 
• The study uses the YOLOv7 algorithm based on its 
high accuracy and real-time detection capabilities. 
• The 
hyperparameter

better 
performance for a particular task of loose oil palm fruit 
detection. 
• The proposed model utilizes a newly developed 
picking sequence based on bounding box coordinates 
for the best harvesting. 
II. PROPOSED METHOD 
In this research propose a system for detecting loose 
palm fruits that have fallen to the ground and a distance 
estimation system for retrieving these fruits, intended for use 
with an automated harvesting robot. The dataset is collected 
using a GoPro camera during manual collection of the loose 
palm fruits. The collected images will be processed and 
annotated. Additionally, to obtain a wider variety of images 
covering different lighting conditions, positions, and fruit 
sizes, image capture can also be performed across various 
palm oil plantations. 
A. Dataset Preparation 
 This research proposes a system for detecting loose palm 
fruits that have fallen to the ground and a distance estimation 
system for retrieving these fruits, intended for use with an 
automated harvesting robot. 
The dataset was collected using a GoPro Hero11 Black 
camera with a resolution of 5568 x 4872 pixels in RGB 
format. The dataset was obtained from a palm oil plantation in 
Mamuju, West Sulawesi, during the harvesting process. A 
total of 839 images are used in this study. An example of the

B. Data Pre-Processing 
The pre-processing stage involves several key steps. It 
begins with resizing to adjust the dimensions of the images, 
followed by annotation to label the data. Next, image 
augmentation is performed to increase data diversity. The 
final step is dataset splitting, which divides the data into 
training and testing sets. The block diagram of the pre-
processing stage can be seen in Figure 2. 
Figure 2. Methodology Flow 
1) Resize: the images are resized to 640 x 640 pixels to 
speed up computation and reduce memory usage. This also 
helps accelerate the training process. 
2) Labelling: at this step,  the collected images are annotated 
with bounding boxes to indicated the position and size. Then 
normalized and labelled with categories. 
3) Image Augmentation: During the image augmentation 
stage, various techniques are applied to the original images to 
generate new variations without altering the labels or other 
critical information. The primary goal is to increase the amount of 
training data and enhance the model's ability to recognize objects 
under different conditions. Common techniques include 
rotating images at certain angles, scaling sizes without 
changing proportions, cropping parts of the image, horizontal 
or vertical flipping, and shifting the image in specific 
directions. Additionally, adjustments to brightness, adding 
blur effects, and introducing noise are also frequently 
applied.vertical flipping, and shifting the image in specific

C. Model Hyperparameter Optimization 
To locate and identify the loose oil palm fruit harvesting 
sequence, the YOLOv7 algorithm was chosen. The selection 
of YOLOv7 was based on its high precision real-time object 
detection capacity, which makes it ideal for applications 
demanding quick and accurate identification, including 
gathering loose oil palm fruit. This algorithm helps with the 
automated and effective harvesting process by identifying the 
presence of loose oil palm fruit. 
 
Finding and locating loose oil palm fruit as an object may 
be accomplished with strong and efficient network design, 
such as YOLOv7, and hyperparameter optimization. The 
hyperparameters determine the network structure, such as the 
learning rate and other training parameters. Hyperparameters 
were first chosen before the network's parameters (weights 
and bias) were optimized and trained.

1) Anchor: predefined bounding boxes with specific 
dimensions (height and width) used to enhance the model's 
accuracy and efficiency in object detection. 
2) Learning Rate: a configurable parameter that controls 
the step size during each iteration of model training, aiming 
to minimize the loss function and optimize model 
performance. 
3) Weight Decay: a regularization method applied to 
prevent overfitting by adding a penalty to the loss function, 
encouraging the model to maintain smaller weights during 
training. 
4) Epoch: an epoch refers to one complete cycle through 
the entire training dataset during the model training process. 
In each epoch, the model processes all training examples 
once, allowing it to learn from the data to improve model 
accuracy and performance. 
D. Object Detection 
 In this research, we used images captured by a camera 
mounted on an automatic loose palm fruit harvesting robot. 
 
Figure 3. Loose Oil Palm Fruit Detection 
 
After the images are obtained, detection is carried out 
using the YOLOv7 algorithm to differentiate between loose 
palm fruits and other objects in the surrounding area. The 
detection result, which is composed of the bounding box 
coordinates, confident score, and the object class, is then 
further processed. In YOLO, a bounding box is represented by 
four values: center x-coordinate, center y-coordinate, width, 
and height. X_center and y_center are the normalized 
coordinates of the center of the bounding box. To normalize 
coordinates, we take pixel values of the x and y, which mark 
the center of the bounding box on the x-axis and y-axis. Then, 
we divide the value of x by the width of the image and the 
value of y by the height of the image. Width and height 
represent the width and the height of the bounding box. In this 
research, we used y coordinate to determine the object 
estimation of the oil palm fruit. The value of the coordinates 
is saved in a text file. 
For example, figure 4 contains one bounding box that 
marks a loose palm fruit. The image width is 640 pixels, and 
its height is 640 pixels. The width of the bounding box is 322 
pixels, and its height is 117 pixels. The bounding box has the 
following (x, y) coordinates of its corners: top-left is (x_min, 
y_min) or (98px, 345px), top-right is (x_max, y_min) or 
(420px, 345px), bottom-left is (x_min, y_max) or (98px, 
462px), bottom-right is (x_max, y_max) or (420px, 462px). 
The coordinates of the bounding box's corners are calculated 
based on the image's top-left corner, which has (x, y) 
coordinates (0, 0).

III. RESULT AND DISCUSSION 
In this study, the sequence of harvesting oil palm fruit by 
the automatic robot is organized based on the bounding box 
coordinates obtained from image processing. The images of 
loose oil palm fruits were trained using the YOLOv7 
algorithm, combined with hyperparameter optimization, to 
accurately detect and extract the coordinates of each fruit 
within the images. Key hyperparameters, such as anchor sizes, 
learning rate, and weight decay, were fine-tuned to enhance 
the model's detection capability. This optimization allowed 
the YOLOv7 model to generate precise bounding boxes 
around the detected fruits, providing the x and y coordinates 
of each fruit's location. These coordinates were critical for the 
robot's automated harvesting system, as they enabled efficient 
planning of the pick sequence based on the fruits' positions in 
the field. Through this process, the model achieved high 
accuracy in detecting the fruits and determining their exact 
locations within the image.

The table displays the coordinate values generated by the 
YOLOv7 algorithm for object detection. It includes four key 
parameters: x_center and y_center, which represent the 
normalized center coordinates of the bounding boxes in the 
image, and Width and Height, which define their relative

The sequence of detection results is based on the y-center 
value of each bounding box. This ensures that detections with 
the smallest y-center values are processed first. By sorting the 
detections in this manner, the system prioritizes processing 
the lowest vertical position first, thereby optimizing the 
handling sequence and minimizing disruptions in the 
harvesting process. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Figure  7. The Results of Detection After Sorting 
 
The bounding box results that have been sorted based on 
the smallest minimum value will be labelled with the number 
1. This approach facilitates the automatic robot in determining 
the sequence for harvesting palm fruit bunches in a directed 
and precise manner. By assigning label number 1 to the 
bounding box with the smallest min value, the robot can 
identify which bunches need to be processed first. This 
sequence ensures that the bunches in the lowest vertical 
position are prioritized, making the harvesting process more 
efficient and organized while minimizing the chance of 
disturbing bunches still attached to the palm. This label-based 
sorting system enhances the accuracy and effectiveness of the 
robot’s tasks. 
IV. CONCLUSION 
In conclusion, the YOLOv7 algorithm was chosen 
because of its high precision and real-time object detection 
capabilities, making it ideal for applications such as loose oil 
palm fruit identification. The hyperparameter, which includes 
variables like Anchor, Learning Rate, and Weight Decay, is 
crucial to increasing the model's accuracy and efficiency. 
Fine-tuning these hyperparameters is critical for improving 
the model's performance, but it can be time-consuming due 
to the number of parameters involved. After adjusting these 
hyperparameters, the YOLOv7 model detected loose oil palm 
fruit with high reliability. 
For mAP50, which measures mean Average Precision at 
an IoU threshold of 0.50, hyperparameter set A has a higher 
score of 0.866 than set B, showing superior object recognition 
accuracy for A at the lower IoU threshold. Similarly, in 
mAP50-95, which evaluates the model across a range of IoU 
thresholds from 0.50 to 0.95, set A outperforms set B with a 
score of 0.765 compared to 0.602, indicating superior 
performance across numerous IoU levels. 

Regarding Precision, set A has a slightly better score of 
0.791 than set B, indicating that hyperparameter A produces 
fewer false positives. However, when analyzing the Box 
Loss, which indicates how well the model predicts bounding 
box coordinates, hyperparameter set B performs somewhat 
better, with a loss value of 0.02158 vs 0.02304 for set A.  
Objectness Loss, which measures the model's ability to 
identify if an object exists within a proposed region, shows 
that set A outperforms set B. With a loss score of 0.01677 
compared to 0.01971, set A demonstrates higher object 
detection confidence. In conclusion, hyperparameter set A 
generally demonstrates superior precision and overall object 
detection accuracy, while set B provides slightly more 
accurate bounding box predictions.
