How the AI can still help

The AI remains a welcome assistant. Please use it in this way:

  • Let it prepare the work: pre-fill a sheet, find a page, or draft a sentence.
  • Then check it yourself: open the source, and confirm or correct each value.
  • Record what you saw, and log every change, so the paper can say exactly what the checks changed.

Check 1: A blind sample of the screening

This check tells us how often a person agrees with the AI. The paper will report this number, which the draft does not have yet.

Steps
  1. Draw a random sample with a script and a fixed seed:
    • 300 records from the title stage
    • 100 records from the abstract stage
  2. Make a sheet that shows the title, and the abstract for the second set, but not the AI's decision.
  3. Decide each record yourself, using the draft's screening rules:
    • At the title stage: "go on to the abstract" or "exclude".
    • At the abstract stage: the group of the study, or the reason for excluding it (see the table below).
  4. Compare your decisions with the AI's. For each stage, work out the percentage of agreement and Cohen's kappa. A kappa above 0.6 is usually taken as good agreement.
  5. Send Fatma every disagreement, and she will make the final call.

When the check passes

The most important rule is simple: the sample must not contain a core study (C1) or an oil-palm study (C3) that the AI excluded. If it does, please look for the pattern behind the miss, re-check every record of that kind, and draw 300 more titles, as the flow below shows.

The loop is needed only when a core or oil-palm study was missed. Otherwise, the check ends with Fatma's decisions and the agreement numbers in the manuscript.

Order of work

Two notes
  • Counts that come from the scripts, such as "113 of 182 use M3", do not need a hand check. After the rerun in Check 5, please just make sure each sentence still matches the new output.
  • A general claim should say how many studies support it. For example, "appearance features did not improve tracking" rests on two studies, so the abstract should say so.
